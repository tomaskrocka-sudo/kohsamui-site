Pig Island is Koh Madsum, a small island off Koh Samui's south-west coast, 15–30 minutes by boat from Thong Krut pier, where a family of pigs roams the beach. The island fee is about 50 THB per person in cash, and almost every trip adds snorkelling at neighbouring Koh Tan. Our shared tours start at 1,450 THB and run every day of the year, weather permitting, which makes Pig Island the go-to boat trip while Ang Thong is closed (1 November–15 December).

Map: Thong Krut, Koh Madsum, Koh Tan and Koh Rap

Three islands sit off the south-west tip of Samui. Koh Tan (Koh Taen) is the largest and closest to Thong Krut. Koh Madsum, the pig island, lies just beyond it. Koh Rap is the furthest of the three, about 6 km from Koh Tan, and quieter. The Five Islands (Koh Ha) are the cluster of rocks you see from the Taling Ngam coast at sunset.

The pigs: what to expect and how to behave

The pigs are domestic pigs that live on Koh Madsum's main beach and the shady ground behind it. Numbers change as piglets are born and grow, so expect anything from a few adults to a crowd with piglets. They are used to boats arriving and may trot down to the waterline when one lands, because they have learned that visitors can mean food.

In the heat of the day they retreat to the shade under the trees, and they sometimes wade or swim in the shallows on their own to cool off. They are not trained and there is no show: if they are asleep in the shade, that is what you will see. Morning and late afternoon visits usually find them more active.

Rules we ask every guest to follow, and that our crew enforce:

  • Don't chase the pigs, lift them, ride them or pull them into the water.
  • Give mothers and piglets plenty of space. A sow defending piglets can bite.
  • Feed only if the guide allows it, and only food the guide approves. Never feed bread, crisps, sweets, fizzy drinks or alcohol, and never hand-feed from a bag in front of a crowd.
  • Keep children beside you. Adult pigs are heavy and can knock a small child over by accident.
  • Watch your bag and snacks on the sand; pigs will investigate anything that smells of food.

Watching and photographing the pigs, including in the shallows, is fine. Their hooves are sharp, so wear water shoes if you are standing in the shallows with them.

Koh Tan: snorkelling and the village

Koh Tan is the bigger, greener island next door and the better snorkelling stop of the pair. The classic spot is Ao Ok on the east side, where a long beach has a shallow reef alongside. On the west side, Ao Tok is a sheltered bay with mangroves and rocky shores. Captains choose the spot on the day for wind and swell.

Be realistic about the reef. Dynamite fishing damaged the area until about the 1980s; parts have recovered, but expect patchy coral and reef fish rather than a wall of colour, and turtles are rare. The water is shallow and calm on most days, which makes it ideal for beginners and children. Only a few dozen people live on Koh Tan, mostly in Ban Koh Taen, and there are a few basic bungalow operations. Read the full Koh Tan guide.

Koh Rap and the pink dolphins

Koh Rap is the island most day-trippers miss: a long white beach, calm water, a small bar, toilets and room to spread out, noticeably quieter than Koh Madsum at midday. Koh Rap also charges an island fee of about 50 THB per person in cash. Two of our private trips include it: the Koh Madsum, Koh Tan and Koh Rap private boat (8,450 THB per boat) and the Koh Rap full-day private boat (9,950 THB per boat).

The pink dolphins are Indo-Pacific humpback dolphins, which often turn pink as they age. They feed off the Khanom coast on the mainland opposite Samui, not around Koh Madsum itself. Our Pink Dolphins and Pig Island tour (2,250 THB, about 6 hours) crosses to their feeding grounds in about 45 minutes, then visits Koh Tan and Koh Madsum. They are wild animals, so sightings are frequent but never guaranteed. Boats keep their distance, run engines slowly and never chase the dolphins, and swimming with them is not allowed.

Getting to Thong Krut

All our Pig Island trips include a return hotel transfer from most areas of Koh Samui. The minibus collects you from your hotel lobby or the main road outside it. If the road to your villa is too steep or narrow for the minibus, we send a nearby meeting point with a map pin. A few remote zones carry a 400 THB per person return surcharge, and the booking form tells you if your hotel is in one. Private trips use a private minibus that collects only your group.

If you would rather drive or ride a scooter, Route 4170 runs from the ring road down to Thong Krut. It is steep and narrow in places and slippery after rain, so take it slowly; see Koh Samui roads and driving. Message us before booking if you plan to meet the boat at the pier.

When to go

Pig Island works all year, which is its big advantage over Ang Thong. From January to September the sea on the south-west coast is calm on most days. From October to December the north-east monsoon throws swell and rain at Samui's east coast, but Thong Krut faces south-west and sits in the lee of the island, so trips usually run. Rain can still cancel a day, and a strong westerly can make the crossing choppy in June to September afternoons.

Children and accessibility

Pig Island is the easiest boat trip from Samui for children: the crossing is short, the water is shallow and the snorkelling at Koh Tan suits beginners. Children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn at all times on the boat; non-swimmers can snorkel in a life jacket next to the guide. Infants aged 0–2 travel free.

Landings are wet: you step down from the boat into ankle- to knee-deep water and wade ashore. Crew help, but guests who can't manage steps or uneven ground should choose a private trip, where the captain can take more time. The beaches are sand with no boardwalks, so wheelchairs can't be used on the islands. For more family ideas, see Koh Samui with kids.

On the water, the main risks are sunburn, the occasional jellyfish and boat traffic around the reefs. Snorkel only where the guide shows you, stay close to the boat and keep clear of longtail propellers. Box jellyfish are rare but have caused deaths in the waters off Samui and Koh Phangan; if someone is stung, pour vinegar on the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it or use fresh water, and call 1669. Pig Island in the rainy season

From October to December Samui gets most of its rain and the east coast takes the north-east monsoon swell. This is when Pig Island earns its place. The boats leave from Thong Krut on the south-west coast, cross 10–30 minutes of water in the lee of Samui, and never round the exposed east side. While Chaweng flies red flags, the sea off Koh Madsum is often calm.

What changes: heavy showers can blow through, the water at Koh Tan can turn cloudy after a downpour, and sunsets are more often hidden by cloud. Some days are cancelled, usually when a storm is forecast across the whole Gulf. Book for the first half of your stay so you have spare days to move to, and check the forecast the evening before. If we cancel, you choose a full refund or a new date. In November and the first half of December, when Ang Thong is closed, Pig Island and the Koh Tao day trip are the two boat days we run every day the sea allows. Make a day of the south coast

A morning Pig Island trip has you back by about 14:15, which leaves the afternoon for the quiet south of the island. Everything below is within about 20–30 minutes' drive of Thong Krut:

  • Laem Sor Pagoda: a gold-tiled chedi right on the shore at the end of Bang Kao beach. Free, 07:00–18:00.
  • Wat Khunaram: the temple of the mummified monk, Luang Pho Daeng, on Route 4169. Free; cover shoulders and knees.
  • Na Muang Waterfall 1: a swimmable pool inland, free entry, 09:00–17:00, parking 20 THB for a scooter and 60 THB for a car. Fullest from October to January.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai: the famous rock formations at the south end of Lamai, free.
  • Taling Ngam: a long, quiet beach on the west coast with the Five Islands on the horizon, one of the best sunset spots on Samui.

The afternoon departure works the other way round: temples and waterfalls in the morning, the pigs after lunch, dinner by the sea.

Pig Island and animal welfare: our honest view

Pig Island is not a sanctuary, and the pigs are not wild. They are domestic animals living on a beach that receives boats all day. The welfare risks are real: overfeeding with the wrong food, heat stress at midday, and handling by visitors who want a photo. Visits are only acceptable if people treat the animals as animals rather than props.

What we do about it: our crews brief every group, stop guests from chasing, lifting or riding the pigs, and allow feeding only with food the guide approves. We encourage morning and late-afternoon visits, when the pigs are active by choice rather than being pulled out of the shade. If you see someone mistreating the animals on our trip, tell the guide straight away. If watching animals is not for you, the three-island private trip spends most of its time at Koh Rap and Koh Tan, and our snorkelling guide lists other options.
