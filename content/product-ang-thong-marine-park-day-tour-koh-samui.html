Why go: Ang Thong Marine Park by speedboat

Mu Ko Ang Thong National Marine Park is an archipelago of 42 limestone islands about 30 km west of Koh Samui. The park covers about 102 km², of which only about 18 km² is land, so most of what you see is sheer cliff, cave and reef rising out of shallow green water. Two sights make the day. On Koh Mae Ko, steep wooden steps (15–20 minutes up) lead to a platform above Thale Nai, the Emerald Lake: a saltwater lagoon enclosed by cliffs and fed through underwater tunnels. On Koh Wua Talab (Wua Ta Lap), the park headquarters island, a 500 m trail with ropes near the top climbs to a 360-degree view over the whole chain.

The speedboat is the fastest way in. The crossing takes about 60–75 minutes each way, against about 1.5 hours on the big boat, so you spend more of the day in the park and are back at the pier by 16:00–16:30. That extra time goes into kayaking (45–60 minutes when the sea allows) and a snorkelling stop the guide picks on the day, often around Koh Sam Sao, Koh Wao or Koh Tai Plao. The trade-off is ride comfort: a speedboat is quick but bounces in swell, and a big boat is slower but steadier.

Compared with our other Ang Thong options, this is the standard full day at the standard price. The big boat tour costs 300 THB less but adds about two hours to the day. The VIP tour visits the same stops with only 15 guests on a big speedboat. The private speedboat gives you the boat and the timing. If you want to see Ang Thong at sunset and after the day boats leave, look at the overnight trips.

Who this tour is for

  • First-time visitors who want the Emerald Lake, the Wua Talab viewpoint, kayaking and snorkelling in one day and are happy to be back at the hotel by early evening.
  • Couples, friends and families with children who are fine on a fast boat. Children aged 3–11 pay the child price and infants up to 2 travel free.
  • Reasonably fit walkers. Both climbs are optional, but they are the reason most people come.
  • Travellers short on time: it is the shortest full Ang Thong day from Samui.

Who should choose another tour

  • Prone to seasickness, back or neck problems: the big boat rides more steadily. Read speedboat vs big boat before you decide.
  • Pregnant: we do not advise an open-sea speedboat crossing in pregnancy. A short, sheltered trip such as the Pig Island longtail is a gentler day on the water; check with your doctor first.
  • Want space and a quieter boat: the VIP tour (only 15 guests on a big speedboat).
  • Your own group and timing: the private speedboat.
  • Travelling 1 November–15 December: the park is closed. Take the Koh Tao and Nang Yuan day trip or the Pig Island speedboat tour instead.

What to bring

  • Cash for the park fee in Thai baht: 300 THB per foreign adult, 150 THB per child aged 3–14. There is no card payment in the park.
  • Swimwear, a towel and a dry change of clothes.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses with a strap.
  • Shoes or sandals with grip for the stairs and the rope section of the viewpoint trail.
  • A dry bag or waterproof phone pouch: spray reaches every seat on a speedboat.
  • Seasickness tablets if you need them (take one about an hour before departure).
  • Your passport number for the booking (needed for the accident insurance).

Good to know

  • Season: the park closes every year from 1 November to 15 December and reopens on 16 December. February to April has the calmest sea; May to September is good most days; October is wetter and choppier. December to February is peak season, and park quotas limit numbers, so book early.
  • Weather: rain alone does not cancel the tour; strong wind and waves do. If the captain cancels for safety, we tell you the evening before and you choose a full refund or a new date. Check the swell on windy.com or our sea conditions page.
  • Seasickness: sit towards the back of the boat, where the ride is smoothest, keep your eyes on the horizon and eat a light breakfast.
  • Mobility: the Mae Ko steps are steep and the Wua Talab trail has ropes near the top. Guests who skip the climbs can stay on the beach. Boarding from a pier or beach can involve a step down into the boat.
  • Non-swimmers and children: snorkelling is optional and can be done in a life jacket close to the guide. Children's life jackets are always on board and children wear them at all times on the boat.
  • Park rules: no swimming in the Emerald Lake, no fires, no feeding the dusky langurs or macaques, and take all rubbish back to the boat. Since 2021 sunscreens containing oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-MBC or butylparaben are banned in Thai marine national parks. Don't stand on or touch coral.
  • Drones: flying in a national park needs prior permission from the Department of National Parks, and drones in Thailand must be registered with the CAAT and NBTC. Without both, leave the drone at the hotel.
  • No one-way drop-offs: boats return to the island they left from, so the Samui departure ends on Samui.

Pickup and meeting point

Hotel transfers are included from the main areas of Koh Samui: Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, Maenam, Choeng Mon, Bang Rak and Plai Laem. Remote areas (Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and similar) cost 400 THB per person return, and the booking card adds it automatically. Pickup runs 07:30–08:30; we send your exact time and the pier location by email and WhatsApp the day before. If the minibus can't reach your villa, we send a nearby meeting point with a map pin. You can also come to the pier yourself.
