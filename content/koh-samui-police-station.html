In an emergency, call 191 for the police or 1155 for the Tourist Police, who speak English and answer 24 hours a day. Koh Samui has two main police stations: Koh Samui Police Station on the west side near Nathon (+66 77 421 097) and Bo Phut Police Station, which covers the busy north and east including Chaweng; the Tourist Police have their own office on the island. To make a report, bring your passport (or a copy with your entry stamp), any evidence and, ideally, a Thai-speaking friend.

Last checked: November 2026. The national numbers are confirmed by the Thai government. Local station phone numbers and addresses come from local directories and news reports; if you need to go in person, call 1155 first and ask which station handles your area.

The Tourist Police are a division of the Royal Thai Police set up to help foreign visitors. They can take reports, translate between you and the local police, and mediate disputes, but serious crimes are investigated by the regular station for the area. Our Koh Samui Tourist Police guide explains what they can and can't do.

Filing a police report

What to bring

  • Your passport, or a copy of the photo page and entry stamp if it was stolen.
  • Date, time and exact place of the incident, with a map pin if you can.
  • A list of what was lost or stolen, with approximate values in THB, serial numbers and phone IMEI numbers.
  • Evidence: photos, videos, rental contracts, screenshots of messages, a vehicle's number plate.
  • Your address on Samui and a phone number where officers can reach you.
  • A Thai-speaking friend or hotel staff member, if possible.

Language help and copies

  • Reports are written in Thai. Ask for an English summary attached, or ask the Tourist Police to help translate.
  • Check names, dates and the list of items before you sign. Don't sign anything you don't understand.
  • Ask for a copy for yourself and photograph every page; insurers usually want the report within 24 hours of the incident.

Checkpoints and fines

Police run regular checkpoints near Chaweng Lake Road, near the airport and along the ring road. Officers check helmets, licences and International Driving Permits, and sometimes test for alcohol at night.

Paying a fine officially

  • Officers usually write a ticket. You pay at the police station named on it, or on the spot if the officer is authorised to collect, and in both cases you should receive an official receipt.
  • If you are asked for cash without a ticket or receipt, stay polite, ask for the ticket and offer to pay at the station.
  • Real officers wear uniform or show ID. If in doubt, call 1155 to check.
  • A rental shop may hold your licence or ask you to pay a fine through them; get the receipt either way.

Thailand also lets drivers pay some traffic tickets online or at banks and post offices. [check]

If you or a friend are arrested

The offences that most often land visitors in a Thai police station are drugs, vapes, fights, drink-driving and working without a permit. If it happens:

  1. Stay calm and polite. Arguing or resisting makes everything worse.
  2. Ask for your embassy to be contacted. Consular staff can't get you released, but they can check on your welfare, give you a list of local lawyers and contact your family.
  3. Don't sign a statement you can't read. Ask for an interpreter and a lawyer.
  4. Contact a lawyer before any hearing. Bail in Thailand usually means a cash or property guarantee.
  5. Tell your family or travel companion where you are held.

The UK government warns that lèse-majesté (criticising or insulting the monarchy) carries long prison sentences and is interpreted broadly, that possessing even small amounts of drugs can lead to heavy fines or prison, and that vapes are illegal with no exceptions for tourists. After a road accident

For any crash with injuries, call 1669 for an ambulance and 191 for the police. The police station for the area where the crash happened records it, and your insurer will want that report.

  • Don't move the vehicles until police arrive, unless they block traffic dangerously or someone's safety is at risk; photograph their positions first.
  • Thai police often settle minor crashes on the spot by agreement between the parties. Don't agree to pay anything until you understand what you are paying for and have a receipt.
  • If you were riding without a licence or helmet, expect a fine on top of any settlement, and expect your insurer to refuse the claim.
  • Rental shops will ask you to pay for damage to their bike; keep your photos from the start of the rental.

Lost passports and documents

For a lost or stolen passport you need a police report first, then your embassy in Bangkok, then Thai immigration. The Tourist Police can take the report. Your embassy issues an emergency travel document or a new passport, and Koh Samui Immigration in Maenam (+66 77 423 440) transfers your entry record and permitted stay to the new document. Your permitted stay keeps running while you wait, so talk to immigration before it expires. See the immigration office guide and the Thailand visa guide.

Online scams and fake booking pages

Fake Facebook pages copying hotels and tour companies, and fake visa or arrival-card websites, are a growing problem. If you have paid a fake page, contact your bank at once to try to stop or reverse the payment, keep screenshots of the page and messages, and report it to the Tourist Police on 1155. Thailand's online scam hotline is 1441. [check] Book tours only through websites and WhatsApp numbers you can verify; ours is +66 63 704 0888.

Practical tips

  • Save 191, 1155 and 1669 in your phone, plus your hotel's number.
  • Keep a photo of your passport photo page and entry stamp on your phone; it speeds up any report.
  • If you don't have a Thai SIM, a hotel, shop or bar will call for you.
  • Theft reports are free. If anyone asks for a fee to write a report, call 1155.
  • For scams and rental disputes, read the scams guide first; prevention is easier than a police case.
