Where is the mummified monk on Koh Samui? | At Wat Khunaram, on the ring road (Route 4169) in Na Mueang in the south of the island, between Hua Thanon and the turn-off to the Na Muang waterfalls.
Is it free to see the mummified monk? | Yes, entry to Wat Khunaram is free; there are donation boxes and you can buy flowers, incense or gold leaf as an offering if you wish.
Who was Luang Pho Daeng? | A Samui-born meditation master (1894–1973) who married and raised six children, was ordained again at about 50, and died seated in meditation in 1973; he asked for his body to be displayed if it did not decay.
Can I take photos of the mummified monk? | Respectful photos are generally allowed; follow any signs, never use flash, and don't take selfies or pose beside the glass case.
What should I wear to Wat Khunaram? | Cover shoulders and knees; sarongs are sometimes available on site, but bring your own to be sure.
Why does the mummified monk wear sunglasses? | His eyes decayed while much of the body stayed intact, so the monks covered the eye sockets with sunglasses, partly so children would not be frightened; whether they are on during your visit varies.
What are Wat Khunaram's opening hours? | The temple publishes no fixed times; it is open in daylight hours, and early morning is the quietest time before tour coaches arrive late morning.
How long do I need at Wat Khunaram? | About 20–30 minutes, which makes it an easy stop on the way to the Na Muang waterfalls or Thong Krut pier.
Is the mummified monk suitable for children? | Some children find it upsetting and others are fascinated; explain what they will see beforehand, and anyone can wait outside.
How do I get to Wat Khunaram from Lamai? | It is about 8 km and 12–15 minutes south-west along Route 4169 past Hua Thanon; a Grab costs roughly 150–250 THB, but return cars are scarce in the south.
