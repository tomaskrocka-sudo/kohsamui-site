Koh Samui is a departure point rather than a dive destination: its own reefs are shallow and modest, so most diving is by day boat to Sail Rock (a granite pinnacle between Koh Phangan and Koh Tao) or to the Koh Tao dive sites. A two-dive Sail Rock day from Samui costs from about 4,650 THB in 2026 with gear, lunch and transfers. An Open Water course started on Samui, with two nights on Koh Tao, costs about 18,000 THB, against roughly 11,000–14,000 THB if you start on Koh Tao. The season runs February to October, with the clearest water in March and April.

Map of dive sites and departure piers

The dive sites lie north of Samui, in the open Gulf between Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, with Ang Thong to the west. Dive boats from Samui leave from the north-coast piers around Bangrak, Bophut and Maenam.

Our advice: if you want to learn, or dive more than two days, go to Koh Tao for a few nights. Fast boats take 1.5–2 hours and cost about 700 THB one way (see Koh Samui to Koh Tao). If you are a certified diver with one free day, a Sail Rock trip from Samui is the best single dive day in the Gulf.

Sail Rock

Sail Rock (Hin Bai) is a granite pinnacle that rises from the seabed to above the surface in open water roughly halfway between Koh Phangan and Koh Tao. Dive centres on all three islands often call it the best dive site in the Gulf of Thailand.

  • Depth: 5–40 m, with a ledge at about 12 m used for training and corals down to about 18 m.
  • The Chimney: a vertical swim-through that runs from about 18 m up to about 5 m inside the rock. It is the signature dive and needs good buoyancy.
  • Marine life: big schools of barracuda, batfish, trevally and fusiliers, groupers, and cleaner wrasse and shrimp on the walls.
  • Visibility: often best in March and April; poorer after heavy rain.

A typical Sail Rock day from Samui

One Samui dive centre's published schedule shows the pattern: hotel pickup at 08:00, the boat leaves at 09:00, first dive at about 10:30, lunch on board, second dive at about 13:00, back on Samui at about 15:30 and at your hotel by 16:00. That trip includes two dives, equipment, dive insurance, lunch (with vegetarian and vegan options), soft drinks, towels and transfers from the main areas (Maenam, Bophut, Choeng Mon and Lamai), runs on set days of the week and requires an Open Water certification or equivalent. Prices start at about 4,650 THB.

Whale sharks at Sail Rock

Whale sharks visit Sail Rock and Chumphon Pinnacle, and these are the best places in the Gulf to hope for one. They are never guaranteed, and the "season" depends on who you ask. Koh Tao dive centres point to March to May, and in July 2026 the Koh Tao Tourism Association named June to September as the peak for sightings. In practice they turn up in most months. Book Sail Rock for the site itself, and treat a whale shark as a bonus.

Koh Tao dive sites

Koh Tao has dozens of sites within minutes of its piers. From Samui you reach them on a Koh Tao dive day or by staying on the island. The sites most often named by Koh Tao dive centres include:

  • Chumphon Pinnacle: granite pinnacles to the north-west of Koh Tao, down to about 40 m, with barracuda, giant groupers and occasional whale sharks. For Advanced divers.
  • Shark Island: a small rocky island with coral, sea fans and reef fish, down to about 25 m. Despite the name it is not known for sharks.
  • Japanese Gardens, Koh Nang Yuan: a shallow coral garden off the Nang Yuan sandbar, popular for training and snorkelling. Day visitors pay the 250 THB Nang Yuan island fee.

Boat time from Samui to Koh Tao is 1.5–2 hours. Non-divers in your group can join our Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB), which snorkels the Japanese Gardens and two Koh Tao bays. See our Koh Tao guide for the island itself.

Diving in Ang Thong

Ang Thong Marine Park's diving season runs from February to October, with the clearest water usually in March and April. The reefs are shallow fringing reefs around limestone islands, and visibility drops after rain because run-off from the islands clouds the water. That makes Ang Thong better for snorkelling than diving, and most divers on Samui choose Sail Rock or Koh Tao instead. The park fee of 300 THB per adult (150 THB per child) is paid in cash, sunscreens containing oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-MBC or butylparaben are banned, and the park is closed from 1 November to 15 December. Read the Ang Thong guide, or join our Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB) for the snorkelling.

Diving around Samui itself

Honestly, it is limited. Samui's coastline is mostly sand, mud flats and shallow rocky coves, and the east coast is churned up by surf from October to December. There is no shore dive on Samui that we would send a certified diver to over Sail Rock or Koh Tao. Samui dive centres use swimming pools or calm shallow bays for confined-water training and Discover Scuba sessions, then take students by boat to Koh Tao or Sail Rock for open-water dives. If you only want to look at a reef from the surface, the shore spots in our snorkelling guide are free and fine for an hour.

Courses

Discover Scuba Diving

A try-dive for non-divers: a briefing, skills practice in a pool or shallow water, then a shallow guided dive with an instructor beside you. No certification is issued. PADI's minimum age is 10. You fill in a medical questionnaire first, and some conditions need a doctor's sign-off. On Koh Tao it costs about 2,500–3,500 THB;

Open Water

The entry-level certification: online or classroom theory, confined-water skills, then four open-water dives, normally over 3–4 days. It certifies you to dive to 18 m with a buddy. The 2026 market price on Koh Tao is about 11,000–14,000 THB depending on the school and whether a dorm bed is thrown in. Starting from Samui, one operator's package with two nights on Koh Tao, ferries and accommodation costs about 18,000 THB, and a Samui-based course with transfers, lunch and the speedboat is quoted at about 18,200 THB. Doing the e-learning before you arrive frees up beach time.

Advanced Open Water

Five adventure dives, usually including a deep dive to 30 m and navigation, over two days. On Koh Tao it costs about 11,000–12,000 THB in 2026. It is the course to take if you want Chumphon Pinnacle and the deeper parts of Sail Rock.

Refresher

If you haven't dived for a year or more, most centres will ask you to do a short refresher (skills review in shallow water) before a deep site like Sail Rock. Ask for one rather than waiting to be asked; it is cheap insurance against a bad first dive.

Fun-dive packages

Certified divers who want more than one day have three choices:

  • Sail Rock days from Samui: about 4,650 THB for two dives, with transfers. Repeatable on the days the boat runs.
  • Short package to Koh Tao from Samui: one operator sells 5 dives with 1 night on Koh Tao for about 10,500 THB.
  • Stay on Koh Tao: single fun dives cost about 800–1,200 THB, and 6–20-dive packs about 3,500–15,000 THB. Sail Rock costs extra from Tao because of the longer boat ride.

Diving in November and December

Yes, but plan around it. The north-east monsoon makes November the wettest and roughest month: crossings from Samui are often cancelled, Sail Rock can be unreachable for days, and visibility falls to 5–10 m. Koh Tao's centres keep diving on the sheltered west side of the island. If November is your only window, stay on Koh Tao rather than commuting from Samui, and keep spare days.

How to choose a dive centre

Good to know: the cheapest course is rarely the best value. These checks take five minutes and matter more than 1,000 THB.

  • Agency registration: ask for the centre's PADI or SSI centre number and check it on the agency's website.
  • Instructor ratio: ask how many students each instructor takes on open-water training dives; four or fewer is a good sign.
  • Insurance: confirm the dive insurance in the price and what it covers, including chamber treatment.
  • Equipment: look at the regulators, BCDs and computers. Every diver should have a dive computer, and gear should be serviced and fit you.
  • Briefings: a proper dive briefing before every dive, covering depth, time, gas, entry and exit, and what to do if separated.
  • Weather calls: a good centre cancels or changes sites in bad conditions without pressure, and refunds or reschedules.
  • Emergency kit: oxygen and a first-aid kit on the boat, and a crew member trained to use them.
  • Language: theory and briefings in a language you fully understand.

Medical, insurance and flying after diving

Every course and try-dive starts with a medical questionnaire. Answering yes to certain questions (asthma, heart conditions, recent surgery, some medications, pregnancy) means you need a doctor's written approval before you dive, so if any apply, get it at home before you travel. Don't dive with a cold or blocked ears.

Check that your travel insurance covers scuba diving to the depth you plan, including evacuation and recompression treatment; many standard policies cap depth or exclude diving. See our travel insurance guide.

Flying after diving: the Divers Alert Network (DAN) recommends a minimum surface interval of 12 hours after a single no-decompression dive and 18 hours after multiple dives or multiple days of diving, and substantially longer after decompression dives. Most dive days involve two dives, so plan at least 18 hours, and preferably 24, between your last dive and your flight home. The same applies to driving up high mountain roads straight after diving.

Recompression chamber and emergencies

Know where to go before you need it. If a diver shows symptoms of decompression illness (joint pain, numbness, unusual fatigue, dizziness or breathing difficulty after a dive), give 100% oxygen if it is available, keep them lying down, call for help and contact your dive insurer's emergency line.

Diving etiquette and reef care

  • Master your buoyancy before you dive near coral, and keep fins, gauges and octopus tucked in.
  • Don't touch, stand on or kneel on coral, and don't handle marine life, including "harmless" starfish and sea cucumbers.
  • Give whale sharks at least 3 m of space, never touch or chase them, and don't block their path.
  • No gloves on reef dives; they encourage touching.
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen or a rash vest; the four banned chemicals are illegal in marine national parks.
  • Take your rubbish back, including anything you find on the dive if it is safe to remove.
  • Follow the guide's turnaround times and the deepest-first, ascend-slowly routine; at Sail Rock, watch for currents around the pinnacle.
