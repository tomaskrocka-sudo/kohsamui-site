Every party, festival, public holiday and alcohol-ban day relevant to Koh Samui from November 2026 to December 2027 is on this page: 16 Full Moon Parties, the Half Moon and jungle parties on Koh Phangan, Loy Krathong (Tuesday 24 November 2026), Chinese New Year (Saturday 6 February 2027), Songkran (13–15 April 2027), the Samui Regatta (22–29 May 2027) and more. Each date carries a confidence rating; "provisional" dates are re-checked monthly and confirmed about 30 days ahead.

Dates come from organisers, the Thai government holiday calendar and our own boat schedules, cross-checked against at least two sources where possible. Thai Buddhist holidays follow the lunar calendar, and Full Moon Parties move off them because of the alcohol-sales ban, which is why many calendars online get the 2027 dates wrong. November–December 2026

Full Moon Party and Loy Krathong, Tuesday 24 November

The flagship night of the season. Loy Krathong, when Thais float krathong baskets of banana leaf, flowers and candles on water, falls on the same night as the Full Moon Party. Float yours at sunset at Chaweng Lake, Big Buddha or on the beach, then take the boat to Haad Rin. Some English-language calendars give 25 November; the Thai calendar and the full moon put it on the 24th. Sky lanterns are banned on Samui. See Loy Krathong on Koh Samui.

Half Moon Festival

Tue 3 and Tue 17 November are beach-club day parties only. Wed 2 / Thu 3 December and Wed 16 / Thu 17 December are full two-day editions, with the Magic Forest jungle night on the Thursdays. Our Half Moon package (speedboat plus Magic Forest ticket, from 2,700 THB) runs on 3 and 17 December.

Jungle Experience and Waterfall Party

The Jungle Experience in Ban Tai runs the night before each Full Moon: Mon 23 Nov, Wed 23 Dec and Wed 30 Dec 2026, from about 22:00 to 06:00, age 20+ with ID. The Waterfall Party has November dates on Wed 11, Sun 22 and Thu 26. Both dates come from a single organiser listing each, so confirm a week ahead.

Father's Day, Saturday 5 December

King Bhumibol Memorial Day is a public holiday; banks and government offices close, but there is no alcohol ban.

Christmas and New Year's Eve

Christmas Eve (Thu 24 Dec) is a Full Moon night. Christmas Day is not a Thai public holiday, but resorts run brunches and dinners. New Year's Eve brings the free countdown at Central Samui, ARKbar's beach countdown, fireworks along the east and north coasts, resort galas and the NYE Full Moon Party, with our shared boat at 1,800 THB that night. The Samui Santa Run was held on Sat 13 Dec in 2025; the 2026 date had not been announced at the start of November. Details: Christmas and New Year's Eve on Koh Samui.

Good to know: there are no alcohol-ban days in November or December 2026. Ok Phansa, the last ban of 2026, fell on Monday 26 October.

January–March 2027

  • Fri 22 Jan: Full Moon Party.
  • Sat 6 Feb: Chinese New Year, the Year of the Goat. Lion and dragon dances at the Chinese shrines in Maenam and Nathon and at the Guan Yu shrine near Hua Thanon. See Chinese New Year on Koh Samui.
  • Sun 21 Feb: Makha Bucha. Shops can't sell alcohol; hotels and licensed bars may still serve. Mon 22 Feb is the substitute public holiday.
  • Mon 22 Feb: Full Moon Party, moved off Makha Bucha.
  • February: the Koh Samui Red Cross Fair, traditionally in Nathon (the 2026 edition was around 22 February). Not yet announced for 2027.
  • Mon 22 Mar: Full Moon Party.
  • Late March: Samui Open Beach Volleyball on the beach at Central Samui (2026: 26–29 March). Not yet announced for 2027.

February is Samui's driest month, so this is also the best time of year for boat days: Ang Thong Marine Park is open again from 16 December.

April–June 2027

  • Tue 6 Apr: Chakri Day, public holiday.
  • Tue 13 – Thu 15 Apr: Songkran. The government programme for Samui in 2026 ran 12–15 April in Bo Phut (Chaweng and Fisherman's Village), Maret (Lamai) and Nathon; expect the same pattern. Chaweng Beach Road is the main water fight. See Songkran on Koh Samui.
  • Wed 21 Apr: Full Moon Party.
  • Thu 20 May: Visakha Bucha alcohol-sales ban.
  • Fri 21 May: Full Moon Party, moved off Visakha Bucha.
  • Sat 22 – Sat 29 May: 24th Samui Regatta, Chaweng, with racing in the bay and social evenings ashore.
  • Late May: Central Samui Neon Run, a 5 and 10 km night run (2026: Sat 30 May, about 3,000 runners).
  • June: Samui Half Marathon (2026: Sun 21 June).
  • Sat 19 Jun: Full Moon Party.

July–September 2027

  • Sun 18 – Mon 19 Jul: Asahna Bucha and Khao Phansa, the start of Buddhist Lent, both alcohol-sales ban days. One holiday calendar puts them a day later, so check closer to the date.
  • Tue 20 Jul: Full Moon Party, moved off the holy days.
  • Tue 17 Aug: Full Moon Party.
  • Wed 15 Sep: Full Moon Party (the night before the morning full moon).
  • About Thu 30 Sep: Sart Duean Sip, the southern Thai festival of merit for the dead, at every Samui temple, with krayasat rice sweets. The date is calculated from the lunar calendar.

October–December 2027

  • About Fri 15 Oct: Ok Phansa, the end of Buddhist Lent and an alcohol-sales ban day (calculated).
  • Sat 16 Oct: Full Moon Party, moved off Ok Phansa.
  • Mid/late October: Chak Phra and Thod Pha Pa in Nathon: temple floats from around 16 Samui temples are pulled through the town to the pier over a fair of about nine days. Past editions ran 18–26 Oct 2022 and 6–14 Nov 2023; expect the ferry piers in Nathon to be busy on procession day.
  • Sat 13 Nov: Full Moon Party, probably the same night as Loy Krathong 2027 (to be confirmed when the Thai calendar for 2027 is published).
  • November: the Samui Trail running race (2025: 8–9 Nov). Not yet announced for 2026 or 2027.
  • Mon 13 Dec: Full Moon Party.
  • Fri 31 Dec: New Year's Eve countdowns on Samui and the NYE Full Moon Party.

Half Moon Festival 2027

The organiser had not published its 2027 dates at the start of November 2026. The festival normally runs twice a month, as a beach-club day party followed by the Magic Forest night, around each half moon. We add 2027 dates to this page and to our Half Moon package only once the official list is out, because unofficial lists circulating online don't always match.

Alcohol-ban days explained

On the main Buddhist holy days, alcohol sales are banned across Thailand. Since May 2025 the ban has had important exemptions:

  • Hotels licensed under the Hotel Act may serve.
  • Licensed entertainment venues, pubs and bars in tourist areas may serve.
  • International airports and designated special events are exempt.
  • 7-Eleven, supermarkets and ordinary shops can't sell alcohol.
  • Unlicensed restaurants and street stalls can't sell alcohol.

In practice, Chaweng's beach clubs and bars usually open as normal, while you can't buy a beer at a convenience store. Illegal sales carry up to 6 months in jail and/or a 10,000 THB fine. Election days bring separate bans; none are scheduled in this period. Loy Krathong, Songkran and Chinese New Year are not ban days. More: drugs and alcohol law on Koh Samui.

Events that no longer run

  • Samui Festival (a September festival of sport, music and food): last held in 2019.
  • Samui Jazz Festival: last confirmed in 2022.
  • Samui triathlon: no current edition.
  • Zamna "On The Beach": ran once, on 21 February 2026 at CLUB SEEN in Chaweng; no 2027 date announced.
  • Buffalo fighting: a traditional event historically held at village arenas around New Year and Songkran. We don't list or sell it; see buffalo fighting on Koh Samui for why.

Using this calendar

Book boats for Full Moon nights, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve early: they sell out first. Party boats run only in safe sea conditions, and November and December bring the monsoon swell, so check the forecast on Windy and our sea conditions guide. If we cancel for weather, you choose a full refund or another date. Weekly nights out (pool parties, fire shows, fight nights) aren't listed here; see Koh Samui parties and nightlife.
