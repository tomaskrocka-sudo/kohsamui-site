Koh Tan (also spelled Koh Taen) is the larger island beside Koh Madsum (Pig Island), 4–5 km south of Thong Krut on Koh Samui's south-west coast and 15–30 minutes away by boat. It is the better snorkelling stop of the two, with a shallow reef that suits beginners, and there is no separate landing fee. Our Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) snorkels here, and the small-group longtail (1,750 THB) stops here for lunch.

Snorkelling at Koh Tan

The classic snorkel stop is Ao Ok on the east side, a long beach with a reef running alongside it in shallow water. On the west side, Ao Tok is a sheltered bay with mangroves and rocky shores, used when the wind blows from the east. The captain chooses the spot on the day for wind, swell and tide.

What to expect: patchy hard coral, reef fish and clear water on calm days, at depths where most people can see the bottom without diving. Dynamite fishing damaged reefs in this area until about the 1980s; parts have recovered, but it is not a postcard wall of colour, and turtles are rare. It is the best easy reef snorkelling close to Samui, and better than anything you can reach from Samui's own beaches. For a wider comparison, read our Koh Samui snorkelling guide.

  • Masks and snorkels are provided on all our trips; bring your own fins if you want them.
  • Non-swimmers can snorkel in a life jacket next to the guide.
  • Don't stand on coral or touch anything; wear a rash vest instead of lots of sunscreen.
  • Stay close to the boat and watch for longtail traffic near the beach.

The reef is shallow, so a very low tide can make some areas too shallow to swim over. Calm months from February to April give the clearest water. Check the tide for the south coast in our tides guide.

Beaches and the village

Koh Tan is far quieter than Samui. Only a few dozen people live here, mainly in Ban Koh Taen, and many houses stand empty. Electricity is limited, and life revolves around fishing and the day boats. Our small-group longtail serves lunch at a beach restaurant on the island.

A few basic bungalow operations exist, but several have closed and the ones that remain open change from season to season. If you want to stay overnight, contact the bungalows directly before you go and arrange a longtail from Thong Krut. Don't expect shops, ATMs or nightlife; bring cash, water and anything you need.

Koh Tan with children

Koh Tan is the easiest reef near Samui for a first snorkel. The water off Ao Ok is shallow and usually calm, the crossing from Thong Krut is short, and children can float in a life jacket next to the guide while looking down at fish. Children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn on our boats, infants aged 0–2 travel free, and children aged 3–11 pay the child price (1,250 THB on the speedboat tour). For very young children a private longtail lets you stay as long, or as briefly, as they enjoy it.

What to bring to Koh Tan

  • Swimwear, a rash vest and reef-safe sunscreen
  • Water shoes for the rocky edges and wading ashore
  • Your own fins, if you want them (masks and snorkels are provided)
  • Cash in small notes for the Koh Madsum fee (about 50 THB), drinks and snacks
  • A dry bag for your phone and a towel
  • For an overnight stay: cash for everything, a torch, insect repellent and drinking water

When to go

January to September is the most reliable time, and February to April gives the calmest sea and clearest water. In the north-east monsoon (October to December) Samui's east coast takes the swell, but Koh Tan lies off the sheltered south-west coast, so trips run on most days. Heavy rain can cloud the water and occasionally cancel a day. If we cancel for weather, you choose a full refund or a new date. Check today's sea before you go on our sea conditions page.
