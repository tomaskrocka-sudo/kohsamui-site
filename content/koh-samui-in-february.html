February is the best month to visit Koh Samui for most travellers. It is the driest month, with about 64 mm of rain over just 4 rain days, highs of 29 °C, lows of 25 °C, a 28 °C sea and the calmest sea of the year. Every boat trip runs, including Ang Thong. The trade-offs are peak prices and busy boats. Chinese New Year falls on Saturday 6 February 2027, and the Full Moon Party moves to Monday 22 February because of Makha Bucha.

Good to know: February is peak season for Ang Thong, and park quotas limit visitor numbers. Book the Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB + 300 THB park fee in cash) as soon as your dates are fixed.

The verdict

February suits almost every kind of trip: beach holidays, boat days, honeymoons, families and first visits. It does not suit travellers looking for low prices or full waterfalls.

Good for:

  • The driest weather and calmest sea of the year
  • Ang Thong, Koh Tao and Pig Island, booked with confidence
  • Swimming on Chaweng and Lamai
  • Chinese New Year lion and dragon dances on 6 February 2027
  • Kitesurfing in the steady trade wind
  • Hiking and viewpoints on dry trails

Not for:

  • Bargains: February is peak season
  • Waterfalls, which thin to a trickle from February to April
  • Last-minute rooms around Chinese New Year
  • Booking the Full Moon Party for 21 February: it moves to the 22nd

If you want the same weather at lower prices, March and April are shoulder months, apart from Songkran. See Koh Samui weather by month.

Sea and boat trips in February

February has the calmest sea of the year and is the best month for Ang Thong, snorkelling and island hopping. The wind comes mainly from the south-east and east, and ferries run normally. It is not flawless: cold surges from the north can still bring strong north-east wind for one to three days. On 19 February 2026, red flags went up on Chaweng and Lamai as 1–2 m waves hit the east coast and swimming was banned. Check our sea conditions page before a boat day.

Chinese New Year 2027

Chinese New Year falls on Saturday 6 February 2027, the start of the Year of the Goat. Many of Samui's Chinese-Thai families trace their roots to Hainan, and the celebrations centre on three places:

  • Maenam: lion and dragon dances around the Chinese temple and the short old Chinatown street, the most family-friendly option. See Maenam.
  • Nathon: the more traditional rites at the Hainanese shrine, with processions stopping at offering tables along the waterfront. Past celebrations have included spirit mediums with pierced cheeks, which some visitors and children find disturbing. See Nathon.
  • Guan Yu shrine, above Hua Thanon: a 16 m bronze statue of the general Guan Yu, open daily from about 08:00 to 17:00 with free entry, and busiest at New Year. See Guan Yu shrine.

Expect firecrackers after midnight on Friday 5 February, the Chinese New Year's Eve. Chinese New Year is not a national public holiday in Thailand, so banks, ferries and tours run as normal, though some Chinese-owned shops close for one to three days. The local programme is announced in January. Cover shoulders and knees at shrines, and keep children back from firecrackers. In the days before the holiday, look for khanom kheng, the sticky New Year rice cake, at Chinese shops and markets, and dim sum and roast duck at Chinese-Thai restaurants in Nathon and Maenam. Makha Bucha on Sunday 21 February is a Buddhist holy day. 7-Eleven, supermarkets and ordinary shops can't sell alcohol, while hotels and licensed bars in tourist areas may still serve. Because of the ban, the Full Moon Party moves to Monday 22 February. Many websites still list 21 February: if you book a boat or room for that night, you will arrive on a ban day with no party. Our shared return Full Moon Party speedboat costs 1,400 THB per person with hotel pickup and drop-off from most areas of Samui; entry, about 200 THB, is paid at the beach. Diving, snorkelling, kitesurfing and hiking

February is the start of the Gulf diving season, which runs to October, with the clearest water in March and April. Snorkelling visibility is good from February to April, and Ang Thong, Koh Tan and Koh Nang Yuan are the reefs worth the trip; Samui's own coastline is mostly sand and shallow rocky coves. See diving and snorkelling.

February and March are the peak kitesurfing months. As the monsoon dries out, the wind settles into a steady east to south-east trade wind over the shallow, reef-protected lagoon at Ban Harn and Hua Thanon, one of the best beginner spots in Thailand. See kitesurfing.

On land, December to March is the best period for hiking and viewpoints, with dry trails and lower humidity than April and May. The trade-off is the waterfalls: Na Muang and Hin Lad thin to ribbons of water over dark rock from February to April. See hiking.

Where to stay in February

  • Anywhere works: from February to April the sea is calm on every coast, so choose your area for its vibe rather than the wind.
  • Maenam and Bophut are the best bases for Chinese New Year, with Maenam's processions on the doorstep and Fisherman's Village nearby for dinner. Nathon has few places to stay.
  • Chaweng and Lamai have their best swimming of the year, with Lamai the better value.
  • Choeng Mon has some of the calmest water on Samui and suits families with small children.
  • Hua Thanon and Ban Harn on the south coast put kitesurfers minutes from the lagoon.

Prices in February

February is peak season: room rates run 30–80% above shoulder-season prices from mid-December to February. Chinese New Year adds visitors from China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia, so book rooms and boat trips early. Everyday costs don't change: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB, a scooter 150–350 THB a day. Rates ease from March. See is Koh Samui expensive?

Getting here in February

Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day. A seasonal flight from Chengdu is scheduled to restart on 1 February 2027. From Singapore, Scoot is the only nonstop airline until 28 March 2027, while Bangkok Airways pauses its own flights. January to April is good weather on both of Thailand's coasts, so February is a good time to combine Samui with Phuket or Krabi; see Phuket to Koh Samui and how to get to Koh Samui.

What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses: the UV index is high all day.
  • A rash vest for snorkelling trips, where backs burn fastest.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes and a cover-up for temples and Chinese shrines.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches such as north Lamai, Bang Por and Coral Cove.
  • Seasickness tablets if you are prone to it, even in calm months.
  • A light layer for air-conditioned buses, boats and the airport.
