Koh Samui has every mainstream water sport, but the best of it happens offshore: snorkelling at Koh Tan (from 1,450 THB on the Pig Island tour, all year), kayaking in Ang Thong (included on our tours from 16 December), and diving at Sail Rock and Koh Tao (two dives from about 4,950 THB). On the island, Lamai beach has parasailing (2,000 THB a flight), banana boats, flyboards and jet skis, Maenam has guided jet ski rides, Coral Cove rents kayaks for about 300 THB an hour, and the south-east coast has a kitesurf lagoon from December to April. February to April has the calmest sea; from October to December the east coast is rough and often red-flagged.

Snorkelling

Samui's own beaches have modest snorkelling: Coral Cove, Silver Beach and the Chaweng Noi rocks are the best of it, all free, all best on calm mornings from February to September. The good reefs are a boat ride away. Koh Tan, 10–15 minutes by speedboat from Thong Krut, is the easiest reef for beginners and children, and it is snorkelled all year on the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, 1,250 child). Ang Thong's sheltered reefs reopen to visitors on 16 December, and the clearest water within a day trip is at Koh Nang Yuan on the Koh Tao and Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB plus a 250 THB island fee in cash; fins are banned there). All our boat trips include masks, snorkels and life jackets. Full details in our snorkelling guide.

Scuba diving

Samui is a departure point rather than a dive destination: its own reefs are shallow, so the diving is by day boat to Sail Rock, a granite pinnacle between Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, and to the Koh Tao sites. A fast catamaran runs Sail Rock on Tuesdays, Thursdays, Saturdays and Sundays and Koh Tao on the other days: two dives cost 4,950 THB (Sail Rock), 5,150 THB (Koh Tao) or 5,650 THB (Chumphon Pinnacle), and snorkellers ride along for 2,900 THB. See the catamaran day. A shared speedboat day costs 5,540 THB for certified divers and 6,220 THB for first-timers on instructor-led dives to 12 m (see the speedboat day). The season runs February to October, with the clearest water in March and April. More in our diving guide.

Kayaking and stand-up paddleboarding

The best paddling is in Ang Thong Marine Park, where every one of our Ang Thong tours includes kayaking among limestone islands; the park is closed from 1 November to 15 December 2026 and reopens on 16 December. On Samui, rent a kayak at Coral Cove for about 300 THB an hour, borrow one from your beach hotel, or have a stand-up paddleboard delivered to your beach. Paddle the north and north-east bays (Choeng Mon, Maenam, Bophut) on early mornings, and avoid the east coast from October to December. For a flat-water paddle with children, the jeep safari with mangrove kayak (1,950 THB) ends in a sheltered mangrove channel. See kayak and SUP on Koh Samui.

Jet skis: ride guided, not from the beach

Yes, you can jet ski on Samui, but beach rentals on Chaweng and Lamai are where the island's best-known scam happens: riders are blamed for damage that was already there and pressured to pay, sometimes with their passport held as a deposit. The UK government's travel advice warns against handing over your passport to jet-ski rental businesses. If you rent on the beach, film the jet ski all round first, get the price in writing and never leave your passport. The better way is guided: guided rides from Maenam cost 3,500 THB per jet ski for an hour to Koh Som, or 6,500 THB for 3 hours across to Koh Phangan, and our Jet Ski Safari to Pig Island costs 9,950 THB per jet ski for a driver and a passenger. Read the full jet ski guide before you ride.

Parasailing

A parasail boat launches and lands you from its deck and tows you about 10 minutes above the bay. The flight we sell goes up over Lamai Bay: 2,000 THB for a solo flight or 3,000 THB for a tandem flight for two, priced per flight, with harness, life jacket, crew and accident insurance. Flights run all year in good weather but are often stopped by wind and swell in November and December. Transfer isn't included; a one-way car to Lamai costs 500 THB on request. See parasailing over Lamai. Before you fly with any operator, look at the condition of the harness and towline, and don't go up in gusty wind.

Banana boats, flyboard and wakeboard

Lamai beach has the island's widest choice of towed and powered water toys: banana boat rides, flyboard flights on a water-jet board, boat-towed wakeboarding and jet ski rides, sold singly or as packages, with an instructor, life jackets and accident insurance. Prices vary by activity and time, so we quote on WhatsApp; a van to Lamai costs about 500 THB each way. See the Lamai water sports. For a dedicated wakeboarding scene, Koh Phangan is the island to visit: see wakeboarding on Koh Phangan.

Kitesurfing and wing foil

Samui's kite season follows the monsoon winds. From December to April the north-east and east winds blow across a shallow, reef-protected lagoon at Ban Harn and Hua Thanon on the south-east coast, one of the best beginner spots in Thailand, and a school there teaches IKO courses. From May to November lessons move to Nathon on the west coast, where the wind is lighter and best in the morning. Chaweng and Bophut are kited on windy days but have chop, swimmers and boats, so they suit experienced riders only. Lessons start at about 3,500 THB for a 2-hour discovery session on the islands. Details, wind by month and spots in our kitesurfing guide.

Freediving

Freediving means diving on a single breath. Samui has few dedicated freediving schools; a handful of Samui dive centres run PADI Freediver courses with sea sessions at Sail Rock or Koh Tao, but most schools are on Koh Tao and Koh Phangan, where a 1-day try-out costs about 3,800–4,500 THB and a 2–3 day beginner course about 8,000–8,750 THB. Sail Rock, which drops to about 40 m, is the main deep site. See our freediving guide.

Fishing trips

A shared day on a traditional Thai fishing boat from Big Buddha pier costs 1,800 THB per adult (1,300 THB child) with rods, bait, transfer and lunch cooked from the morning's catch. Night squid fishing under lamps is the easiest fishing for children, and private boats from the sheltered south coast start at 10,500 THB for up to 3 guests with snorkelling at Koh Tan. Fishing isn't allowed inside Ang Thong Marine Park. See fishing trips on Koh Samui.

Boat days that combine several water sports

If you want snorkelling, a paddle and a swim in one day, a private boat is the most flexible. A private longtail to Pig Island and Koh Tan costs 5,950 THB for up to 8 and lets you stay on the reef as long as you like. A small sailing catamaran carries snorkel gear, a paddleboard and floats, from 7,500 THB per boat for a 2.5-hour sail. For bigger groups, our private speedboat charters start at 19,000 THB per boat, and the Ang Thong private speedboat (33,000 THB for up to 10, from 16 December) includes kayaks.

Learn to dive from Samui

If you've never dived, there are two routes. A Discover Scuba session lets you try diving with an instructor, without a course: first-timers can join the shared speedboat day for 6,220 THB, with two instructor-led dives to a maximum of 12 m, or try it on Koh Tao for about 2,500–3,500 THB. For a certificate, the PADI Open Water course takes 3–4 days. Started on Samui with two nights on Koh Tao, it costs about 18,000 THB; done entirely on Koh Tao, about 11,000–14,000 THB, which is why many Samui visitors book a short stay there. You must be comfortable in the water and complete a medical questionnaire, and the minimum age is usually 10. Plan the course for February to October, and leave at least 24 hours between your last dive and your flight. More in our diving guide and Koh Samui to Koh Tao.

Getting to the water: transfers and surcharges

Most of our boat trips and many partner activities include hotel transfer from the main beach areas: Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, Maenam, Choeng Mon and Bang Rak. Remote areas usually pay extra; on our Chaweng-side departures, Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao and Thong Krut add 400 THB per person return, while Pig Island trips leave from Thong Krut, so south-west hotels are close to the pier for them. Some beach activities include no transfer at all: parasailing and the Lamai water toys (a car costs about 500 THB each way), Coral Cove kayaks and the kite lagoon. Hillside villas on narrow roads usually get a nearby meeting point. Check the transfer line on each product before you compare prices.

Water sports in the rainy season

From October to December the north-east monsoon brings rain, wind and swell, mostly to the east coast. Chaweng and Lamai often fly red flags, parasailing and beach water toys stop on many days, and Ang Thong is closed from 1 November to 15 December. That doesn't mean a week without the sea. The south-west coast around Thong Krut is sheltered, so the Pig Island and Koh Tan trip runs on most days and snorkellers still get a calm reef. Fishing boats from the south coast keep going, the Koh Tao day trip runs when the sea allows, and the north-east wind that keeps swimmers out of the water is exactly what kitesurfers want once it steadies in December. Plan water days for the mornings, keep a spare day in your itinerary for weather, and remember that if we cancel for sea safety you choose a full refund or a new date. More in Koh Samui in November.

A water-sports week from Samui

For a week between mid-December and October, this order spreads the big days and leaves room for weather:

  1. Day 1 Settle in; an early kayak or SUP at Choeng Mon or Coral Cove.
  2. Day 2 Pig Island and Koh Tan: an easy reef to find your snorkelling feet.
  3. Day 3 Lamai: parasailing or a flyboard session in the morning, beach in the afternoon.
  4. Day 4 Ang Thong: kayaking, snorkelling and the viewpoint (from 16 December).
  5. Day 5 Spare day for weather, or a guided jet ski ride.
  6. Day 6 Sail Rock or Koh Tao and Nang Yuan: the clearest water of the week.
  7. Day 7 A relaxed fishing morning or a kite lesson in season; no diving within 24 hours of your flight.

Booking tips: per person or per boat

  • Read the price basis. Shared tours are priced per person; jet skis and parasail flights per vehicle or flight; private boats per boat. A 9,950 THB jet ski for two riders works out at about the same per head as many private boats for a family.
  • Check what's included: transfer and its zones, lunch, gear, island and park fees (Ang Thong 300 THB, Koh Nang Yuan 250 THB, Koh Madsum about 50 THB, all cash).
  • Book the day before. Online booking for tomorrow closes at midnight Thai time; same-day requests go to WhatsApp.
  • Keep a weather buffer. Put the trip you care about most early in your stay so a cancelled day can move.
  • Cancellation: our own day tours refund in full 48 hours or more before departure and 50% at 24–48 hours; partner activities show their own terms on each product page.

What to bring for a day on the water

  • Swimwear and a rash vest or long-sleeved top: the sun off the water burns fast.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen; sunscreens with oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-MBC or butylparaben are banned in Thailand's marine national parks.
  • A hat with a strap and sunglasses on a cord.
  • A dry bag for your phone, and a waterproof pouch for photos.
  • Cash in small notes for island fees, drinks and tips.
  • Seasickness tablets, taken before you board, if you're prone to it.
  • Water shoes for rocky landings.

Water sports for non-swimmers

You don't have to be a strong swimmer to enjoy the sea around Samui. On our boat trips, non-swimmers snorkel in a life jacket near the boat while the guide keeps an eye on them, and the reef at Koh Tan is shallow and calm on most days. Parasailing launches and lands from the boat deck, so you stay dry unless the crew dips you on purpose; ask them not to. Kayaking in a double with a life jacket on a calm morning is easy, and riding as a jet ski passenger behind a guide needs no swimming at all, though you should be comfortable if you fall in. Diving and freediving are different: both need you to be comfortable in deep water, and a Discover Scuba instructor will ask about it before you start.

Reef rules for every water sport

  • Don't stand on, touch or kick coral; float in a life jacket if you need a rest.
  • Don't feed fish, collect shells or take anything from the reef.
  • Keep fins, paddles and jet ski hulls away from shallow reef, and anchor boats on sand.
  • Use reef-safe sunscreen or cover up with a rash vest.
  • Take every bit of litter back with you; Koh Nang Yuan bans plastic bottles and cans entirely.

Private boat or shared tour?

Shared tours are the cheapest way onto good water: from 1,450 THB per person to Koh Tan, with gear and a guide. A private boat starts to make sense for a family or group of 5 or more, or when you want to mix activities. A private longtail to the southern islands costs 5,950 THB for up to 8, which is about 750 THB a head when full, and you choose how long to stay on each reef. Private speedboats cost more but reach Koh Tao, Koh Phangan's coast or Ang Thong at your own pace. Groups of 10 or more should always ask for a private quote. Safety: flags, jellyfish and boat traffic

  • Red flags: from October to December, surf and rip currents hit Chaweng and Lamai. Don't swim, paddle or ride when the flag is red. If a current pulls you out, swim parallel to the beach.
  • Box jellyfish: rare but dangerous; 9 of Thailand's 10 recorded box jellyfish deaths since 2002 happened off Samui and Koh Phangan. If someone is stung, pour vinegar on the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it or rinse with fresh water, and call 1669. Many hotels have vinegar stations; a rash vest reduces exposed skin.
  • Boats and swimmers: longtails, speedboats and jet skis share the bays with swimmers. Stay inside buoyed swimming areas, and keep jet skis and boats well away from them.
  • Alcohol: no drinking before any powered water sport or a dive.
  • Diving and flying: leave at least 24 hours between your last dive and your flight home.

Emergency numbers: Tourist Police 1155, Police 191, Ambulance 1669. See beach and swimming safety.

Insurance for water sports

Read the water-sports section of your travel insurance before you book. Many policies exclude jet skis or cover them only with a licensed operator; scuba diving is often limited to a depth (commonly 18 or 30 m) unless you buy an add-on; kitesurfing and parasailing may need an adventure-sports upgrade. Almost all policies refuse claims if alcohol was involved. Our partner activities include basic accident insurance, which is not the same as medical and evacuation cover.
