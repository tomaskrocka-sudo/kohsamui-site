January is a good month for Koh Samui. It averages about 127 mm of rain over 8 rain days, with highs of 29 °C, lows of 24 °C and a 28 °C sea, which makes it one of the most comfortable months of the year. The monsoon's tail can still bring windy spells and swell on the east coast, mostly early in the month. Ang Thong Marine Park is open, the New Year crowds leave after the first week, and the Full Moon Party falls on Friday 22 January 2027.

Good to know: January is Ang Thong's peak season, and park quotas limit visitor numbers. Book the Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB + 300 THB park fee) early, and plan it for the first half of your stay so a windy day can be moved.

The verdict

January suits almost everyone: beach holidays, boat days, sightseeing and families. The catches are peak prices and a few windy days, so it is not the month for a bargain or for a trip with only one possible boat day.

Good for:

  • Beaches and sightseeing in some of the year's most comfortable temperatures
  • Ang Thong, Pig Island and the Koh Tao day trip on most days
  • Swimming on Chaweng and Lamai, as the monsoon surf eases
  • Hiking and viewpoints on drying trails
  • Waterfalls with good flow early in the month
  • The Full Moon Party on Friday 22 January 2027

Not for:

  • Low hotel rates: January is peak season
  • Guaranteed calm sea in the first week or two
  • Diving at its best: the Gulf season runs from February to October
  • Flying out on 1–2 January without an early booking

If you want the driest, calmest sea, February is a little better; see Koh Samui weather by month. If you are flexible on price but not on weather, January is still a safe choice.

January's rain is about double February's and close to July's, but it falls on fewer days. Nights rarely drop below 24 °C, so there is no cool season in the European sense.

What January weather and sea look like

January is the tail of the north-east monsoon. Most days are dry and sunny, but the wind can still blow hard from the east and north-east for a day or three, especially early in the month. On those days the east coast gets swell, Chaweng and Lamai may fly red flags, and the open-water crossings to Koh Tao and Ang Thong get bumpy or are called off. When the red flag is up, stay out of the water.

Real examples show the range. On 8 January 2026 a strong wind warning forecast 2–3 m waves and Ang Thong closed for a day. In early January 2019 Tropical Storm Pabuk suspended ferries and closed marine parks for several days. Storms like Pabuk are rare, and most Januarys bring nothing worse than a few breezy days, but keep a spare day before your flight home.

The west and south coasts stay more sheltered, with typical waves of about 0.5 m. Sunrise is at about 06:40 and sunset at about 18:18: east-coast beaches face the sunrise, and the west coast faces the sunset. Check our sea conditions page the evening before a boat day for live wave height.

Beaches, hiking and waterfalls in January

  • Beaches: the east coast becomes swimmable again as the monsoon fades, and January to September is the best season on Chaweng and Lamai. Still check the flag on windy days.
  • Hiking and viewpoints: December to March is the best period, with drying trails and lower humidity than April and May. Start before 10:00 and carry 2 litres of water. See hiking on Koh Samui.
  • Waterfalls: good flow early in the month, easing later. At Na Muang 2, a visitor died after slipping on an upper tier on 5 January 2026; stay behind the edges and skip it after heavy rain. See waterfalls.
  • Kitesurfing: the lagoon at Ban Harn and Hua Thanon works from December to April, though January wind can still be gusty. See kitesurfing.
  • Snorkelling: Koh Tan on the Pig Island trip and Ang Thong are the easy options; the water gets clearer from February. See snorkelling.

Where to stay in January

  • Chaweng has the most restaurants and nightlife and is 10–15 minutes from the airport. Swimming improves through January.
  • Lamai is the best value on the east coast, at about 1,500–3,500 THB a night mid-range in the shoulder season; expect more in January.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay with some of the calmest water on Samui, our first choice for families with small children.
  • Bophut and Fisherman's Village suit couples and food lovers, but the beach is not for swimming.
  • The west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) has the sunsets and villas, 40–50 minutes from the airport.

Prices in January

January is peak season. Room rates run 30–80% above shoulder-season prices from mid-December to February, and the first few days of January are part of the dearest stretch of the year. The busiest fortnight ends around 5 January, but peak rates continue into February, so book rooms and Ang Thong dates early. Everyday costs don't change: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB, a scooter 150–350 THB a day. See is Koh Samui expensive?

Getting here and around in January

Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day. Flights off the island on 1–2 January were full after New Year 2025, so book early or leave on 3 January. Bangkok Airways has paused its own Samui–Singapore flights from 25 October 2026 to 28 March 2027, so Scoot is the only nonstop airline from Singapore in January. January to April is good on both of Thailand's coasts, so it is a popular time to combine Samui with Phuket or Krabi; Bangkok Airways flies Phuket–Samui about 7–8 times a day (see Phuket to Koh Samui). Ferries run as normal, with occasional storm delays; leave a spare day between an island hop and an international flight. See how to get to Koh Samui and getting around.

What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses: the UV index is high all day.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes and a cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered).
  • A compact umbrella or light rain jacket for the odd shower.
  • Seasickness tablets for boat days, especially if the wind is up.
  • A dry bag for your phone and passport on boats and scooters.
  • A light layer for air-conditioned buses, boats and the airport.
  • A thin wetsuit if you plan several dives a day: the surface can be cooler in January.
