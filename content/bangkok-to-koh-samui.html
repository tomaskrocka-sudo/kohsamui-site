Direct flights from Bangkok to Koh Samui take about 1h05–1h15 from Suvarnabhumi (roughly 20–25 a day) or Don Mueang (about 3 a day) and usually cost 2,800–6,800 THB one way. On a budget, fly a low-cost airline to Surat Thani and take a coach-and-ferry combo: 5–7 hours and about 1,500–3,000 THB all-in. Overland, the night train plus bus and ferry takes 15–17 hours (950–2,000 THB), and joint bus-and-boat tickets cost 620–1,750 THB.

Good to know: prices and times were last checked on 2 November 2026. Train times follow the State Railway of Thailand timetable in force since December 2025. Seatran's Donsak ferry fares rose five times in March–April 2026 with fuel costs, while Lomprayah kept its fares. Re-check on the day.

Direct flights from BKK and DMK

Bangkok Airways is the only airline flying nonstop between Bangkok and Samui. It owns Samui airport, which is why fares are higher than on routes with low-cost competition. Thai Airways sells some of these flights under its own TG numbers, but they are codeshares on Bangkok Airways aircraft.

  • From Suvarnabhumi: about 20–25 flights a day, roughly 06:00–20:00. Flight time 1h05–1h15. This is the airport most long-haul flights use, so it is the easy choice when connecting.
  • From Don Mueang: about 3 flights a day, typically morning, early afternoon and evening. Fares are usually around 5,300–5,800 THB, so DMK only makes sense if you are already there.
  • Fares: 2,800–6,800 THB one way, with a checked bag included. The cheapest seats go first in December–January, Chinese New Year, Easter, Songkran and July–August.
  • When to book: 6–10 weeks ahead for peak dates; 2–4 weeks ahead is usually enough in May, June, September and October. Prices rarely fall at the last minute.
  • Connecting from a long-haul flight: on one ticket Bangkok Airways can check bags through from many partner airlines; on separate tickets allow at least 3 hours at Suvarnabhumi.

Once you land, Chaweng is 10–15 minutes by kiosk taxi. Our Samui airport guide lists taxi zones, Grab rules and lounge access.

Fly to Surat Thani or Nakhon Si Thammarat + ferry

Thai AirAsia, Nok Air and Thai Lion Air fly from Don Mueang to Surat Thani (URT) about 12 times a day, roughly 06:50–19:15, in 1h10–1h20, usually for 1,000–1,650 THB. There are also about 4 daily flights from Suvarnabhumi (roughly 1,250–3,000 THB). At URT arrivals, combo desks sell coach-and-ferry tickets to Samui:

Night train to Surat Thani (Phun Phin)

Southern line trains leave from Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal (the big station formerly called Bang Sue Grand, on the MRT Blue Line), not from the old Hua Lamphong station. "Surat Thani" station is in Phun Phin, 14–16 km west of Surat Thani town and about 80 km from Donsak pier, so allow 3.5–4 hours from the train to Samui.

Good to know: this is a selection of the 10 daily trains. Times are from the timetable in force since December 2025; re-check before you travel. Ticket sales open 90 days ahead for most trains (30 days for some long-distance services), and online resellers add 100–200 THB. Lower berths are longer, wider and further from the lights, so they sell out first.

Express 85 is the best fit for Samui: it arrives at 06:23, in time for the morning coaches and ferries. Trains arriving at 01:00–05:00 leave you waiting in Phun Phin in the dark. On the station forecourt, combo desks sell coach + ferry tickets (Phantip/Seatran about 420 THB, plus about 150 THB for a hotel drop; departures around 06:30, 07:30 and 09:00). Raja coaches leave the station from 05:30; the last one is at 16:00 and lands at Lipa Noi about 20:30.

Is it worth it? In 2nd class the berths are clean, curtained and air-conditioned, and you save a hotel night. Delays of 30–60 minutes are common, the air-con is cold (bring a layer) and the total trip takes most of a day. If you have only a week, fly.

Bus-and-boat joint tickets

Lomprayah

Lomprayah sells bus + catamaran tickets from its office on Khao San Road. The buses go to Chumphon, where you board a catamaran that calls at Koh Tao and Koh Phangan before Samui.

  • 06:00 day bus: arrives Pralarn pier, Maenam, about 17:30.
  • 21:30 bus with slow ferry via Koh Tao: arrives Bangrak about 11:15 the next day, about 1,250 THB.
  • 22:00 night bus: arrives Pralarn pier, Maenam, about 11:30 the next day.
  • Fares 1,250–1,750 THB depending on bus class and boat; hotel drop on Samui about 150 THB extra.

Boonsiri

Boonsiri's 22:00 bus from Khao San Road connects to its catamaran at Chumphon and arrives at Bangrak about 11:30, for about 1,450 THB.

Government coach + Seatran

Transport Co. (the state bus company) and partner operators run overnight coaches from the Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai) and Mo Chit to Donsak, connecting with the Seatran ferry to Nathon. Counter fares are about 620 THB (express) and 960 THB (24-seat VIP); online resellers charge 1,100–1,500 THB. The trip takes 12–16 hours.

Good to know: thefts from hold luggage on overnight tourist buses are a long-standing problem. Keep passports, cards, phones and medication in a small bag with you, and buy tickets from the operator's own office or the official bus terminal rather than a street agent.

Driving to Samui

Bangkok to Donsak pier is roughly 650–700 km, mostly on Highway 35 (Rama II) and Highway 4/41 south via Hua Hin, Chumphon and Surat Thani. Allow 9–11 hours of driving plus stops, then about 1.5 hours on the car ferry.

  • Seatran (Donsak → Nathon) carries cars for about 730 THB or more including the driver after the March–April 2026 fuel-linked rises (580 THB before); extra passengers pay the foot fare, 250 THB since 8 April 2026. It sails roughly hourly.
  • Raja Ferry (Donsak → Lipa Noi) runs roughly hourly 05:00–19:00, and its site lists 220 THB on foot. It does not publish vehicle fares online; ask at the ticket office.
  • Rental cars: most Bangkok rental contracts exclude ferry crossings or island use. Check before you book, or rent on Samui instead. Our car rental guide explains insurance.
  • Break the drive in Hua Hin, Chumphon or Surat Thani if you can. Night driving on the southern highways is tiring and lorries are heavy.

Getting to your departure point in Bangkok

  • Suvarnabhumi (BKK): Airport Rail Link from Phaya Thai (about 30 minutes) or a metered taxi (45–90 minutes depending on traffic, plus expressway tolls).
  • Don Mueang (DMK): SRT Red Line from Krung Thep Aphiwat (about 15 minutes), the A1/A2 airport buses from Mo Chit and Victory Monument, or a taxi.
  • Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal: MRT Blue Line to Bang Sue station, then follow the signs to the long-distance platforms. Arrive 30–45 minutes early; the station is huge.
  • Southern Bus Terminal (Sai Tai Mai): on the Thonburi side, about 30–60 minutes by taxi from Sukhumvit.
  • Mo Chit bus terminal: near BTS Mo Chit and MRT Chatuchak Park; take a short taxi from the station.
  • Khao San Road: Lomprayah and Boonsiri desks are on or near the road; buses collect from there. Allow time for evening traffic.

Coming back: Samui to Bangkok

The return trip needs more planning than the outward one, because many connections leave the island early.

  • Flights: the last Samui–Bangkok flights leave in the evening. Allow 90 minutes at the airport.
  • Combo to Surat Thani airport: combo tickets leave the island from about 05:00 until mid-afternoon. Lomprayah from Nathon at about 08:30 reaches URT around 11:30, so book a flight from 13:00 onwards to be safe.
  • Car ferries: Raja's first boat leaves Lipa Noi at about 05:00; Seatran sails roughly hourly from Nathon.
  • Trains: the 2nd-class railcar from Surat Thani at 09:00 reaches Bangkok around 18:05 (about 524 THB). Overnight sleepers north leave Surat Thani in the evening; check the SRT timetable for times.
  • Bus-and-boat: Lomprayah and Boonsiri sell return bus + catamaran tickets via Chumphon, arriving in Bangkok late evening or early morning.

Good to know: do not plan a Koh Phangan or Koh Tao boat the day before a flight home in October–December. North-east swell can cancel speedboats. A day boat trip from Samui itself, such as Pig Island, carries less risk because it returns to the same island.

Which option suits you?

  • Families: fly direct. Checked bags are included and there are no changes.
  • Budget, arriving the same day: morning low-cost flight to Surat Thani and a Phantip or Raja combo. Aim to land before 13:00.
  • Budget with time: the government coach + Seatran or the Lomprayah night bus. You arrive late morning and save a hotel night.
  • Train lovers: Express 85 in a lower 2nd-class berth. Book as soon as sales open for December–January.
  • Flexible plans: buy combos on the day at URT or Phun Phin; only flights and train berths need booking ahead outside peak weeks.
  • Night travel: keep valuables on you and set an alarm; tourist buses stop for food at 23:00–01:00.
  • Travelling with a pram, car seat or bike: fly Bangkok Airways (bag included) or drive and take the car ferry. Coaches and catamarans have limited luggage space, and gangways at Nathon and Bangrak can be steep at low tide.

Going on to Koh Phangan or Koh Tao

Bangkok to Koh Phangan via Samui is often the fastest route: fly to Samui, then take a 5–10 minute taxi to the Bangrak piers or Big Buddha pier for a 20–50 minute boat.
