Koh Samui has several private hospitals with 24-hour emergency departments: Bangkok Hospital Samui on the ring road near Chaweng Noi (trauma and paediatrics; +66 77 429 500, local hotline 1719), Samui International, Thai International, Bandon International and Wattanapat. The government Koh Samui Hospital is on the outskirts of Nathon. Call 1669 for an ambulance and 1155 for the English-speaking Tourist Police. Private hospitals usually ask for an insurer's guarantee of payment or a deposit before treatment, so carry your policy details.

Private hospitals

Samui's private hospitals are modern, English is widely spoken at reception and by doctors, and most staff are used to dealing with foreign insurers. They are also expensive by Thai standards. All of them treat walk-in emergencies around the clock.

Bangkok Hospital Samui

The largest and best-equipped private hospital on the island, and a member of the BDMS group that runs the Bangkok Hospital network. It is at 57 Moo 3, Thaweerat Phakdee Road, Bophut 84320, on the ring road just before Chaweng Noi, about 15 minutes from the airport. Main line +66 77 429 500; 1719 from a Thai phone. It has a 24-hour emergency department, a trauma unit, paediatrics, orthopaedics, surgery, imaging and maternity care. For major scooter crashes, it is where other clinics often refer patients. Ask at admission for an itemised estimate and whether your insurer has a direct-billing agreement.

Samui International Hospital

At 90/2 Moo 2, Bophut 84320, at the north end of Chaweng Beach Road, a few minutes from the beach hotels of north and central Chaweng. Phone +66 77 300 394 or 395. It runs a 24-hour emergency room, a 24-hour pharmacy and outpatient clinics (08:00–17:00 daily [kp_open spec="Mo-Su 08:00-17:00"]) including internal medicine, paediatrics, dermatology, gynaecology and dentistry, with X-ray, ultrasound and a laboratory. Convenient for stomach bugs, infections and minor injuries if you are staying in Chaweng.

Thai International Hospital

A private hospital in Chaweng on the ring road (route 4169), with a 24-hour emergency department, general practice, minor surgery, dentistry, X-ray and ultrasound, and a pharmacy. It also issues medical certificates (for driving licences or work permits). Some listings place it opposite Lotus's on the Chaweng ring road. [check]

Bandon International Hospital

On the ring road in Bophut, with 24-hour emergency care, surgery and trauma, X-ray, ultrasound, ECG, laboratory and dentistry. It is a good option for guests in Bophut, Fisherman's Village and Maenam. The same group runs a hospital on Koh Phangan, which helps with transfers between the islands.

Wattanapat Hospital Samui

A newer private hospital at 555 Moo 1, Thaweerat Phakdi Road, phone +66 77 965 889. It lists a 24-hour emergency and accident department, orthopaedic and general surgery, internal medicine, ear, nose and throat, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, rehabilitation and CT scanning. Its listed outpatient hours are 09:00–20:00 daily. [kp_open spec="Mo-Su 09:00-20:00"]

Choosing between them

For major trauma, heart symptoms, a sick baby or anything that may need surgery, go to Bangkok Hospital Samui if you can reach it quickly: it has the widest range of specialists on the island. For everyday problems, the nearest private hospital is usually the best choice, because every one of them has a 24-hour emergency room and English-speaking doctors. Ask your insurer's assistance line which hospital they work with before you go, unless it is an emergency. If cost matters more than speed or comfort, Koh Samui Hospital near Nathon treats foreigners at public-hospital prices, and its doctors handle the island's most serious public cases.

Koh Samui Hospital (government)

What it offers

Koh Samui Hospital is the island's only public hospital, at 61 Moo 1, Tambon Ang Thong, Ko Samui 84140, on the outskirts of Nathon on the west coast. Operator +66 77 913 200; office +66 77 420 856. Island residents donated the land and funds, and it has grown into the referral hospital for Koh Samui, Koh Phangan and Koh Tao. It runs the island's 1669 emergency medical service, a 24-hour emergency department and an intensive care unit, and clinics including orthopaedics, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics and gynaecology, eye, skin, neurosurgery and kidney disease. It also offers health check-ups for work permits, retirement visas, driving licences and recreational diving, and yellow fever vaccination for onward travel.

The hospital is on the outskirts of Nathon town. Coming from the east coast, allow 40–50 minutes, and longer in rain. Grab and taxis can take you; tell the driver "rong phayaban Samui" (Samui Hospital) and, if needed, "Nathon".

The hospital is expanding. A four-storey building is under construction as part of plans to become a 300-bed general hospital. In 2026 it reported a shortage of specialist equipment for heart, stroke and intensive care, and a temple fundraiser in Bangkok raised more than 35 million THB towards it.

What to expect as a foreign patient

  • Cost: far lower than private hospitals. Its published inpatient rates are 330 THB a day for a shared ward bed (meals included) and 2,000–3,500 THB a day for a private room, plus treatment.
  • Queues: long in the mornings, as with any Thai public hospital. Bring water and patience.
  • Language: many doctors speak English; nurses and reception staff less so. A Thai-speaking friend helps. The hospital has a service for foreign patients and gives guidance on overseas insurance and direct billing for inpatients.
  • After hours: a special clinic sees patients Monday to Saturday, 16:00–20:00, by appointment, for a higher fee than daytime clinics. [kp_open spec="Mo-Sa 16:00-20:00" note="by appointment; closed on public holidays"]
  • Distance: from Chaweng it is about 25–30 km, roughly 40 minutes, so for an emergency on the east coast a private hospital is usually closer.

Clinics for minor illness and injury

For coughs, ear infections after swimming, stomach upsets, coral cuts and sprains, a private clinic is faster and cheaper than a hospital. You'll find them on the main roads of Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, Maenam and Nathon, many with signs in English and Russian.

  • How to choose: look for a clinic with a doctor (not just a nurse) on duty, a clean treatment room and prices on display or given before treatment.
  • Opening hours: most open in the morning and stay open into the evening, typically until 20:00–21:00. Few are open all night.
  • Ask for: the generic name of every medicine, a written diagnosis and an itemised receipt for your insurer.
  • Escalate to a hospital if there is a head injury, high fever lasting more than 2 days, breathing difficulty, severe abdominal pain, a wound that keeps bleeding, or signs of infection spreading from a cut.

Common holiday problems and what to do

  • Swimmer's ear: pain and itching after days in the sea or pool. A clinic can give antibiotic or antifungal ear drops. Keep the ear dry until it settles, which may mean a break from snorkelling.
  • Stomach upsets: drink bottled or filtered water, use oral rehydration salts, and see a doctor if there is blood, a high fever, or vomiting that stops you keeping fluids down for more than a day.
  • Sunburn and heat exhaustion: Samui's daytime highs are 29–33 °C all year. Rest in the shade, drink water with electrolytes, and go to a clinic for confusion, fainting or a very high temperature.
  • Coral cuts and sea urchin spines: clean coral cuts thoroughly with fresh water and antiseptic, because they infect easily in the heat. Soak urchin punctures in hot water (as hot as is comfortable) and let a clinic remove spines that are deep or near a joint.
  • Sea lice: an itchy rash under swimwear after swimming in some months, mainly on the east coast. Antihistamine tablets and cream from a pharmacy usually help.
  • Infected scooter grazes: the classic "Thai tattoo". Have road rash cleaned and dressed at a clinic on the day, keep it out of the sea and get it checked every couple of days.

Dental emergencies

For a broken tooth, lost filling or abscess, the private hospitals run dental departments and there are dental clinics in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai. An abscess with facial swelling or fever needs a doctor the same day. Our Koh Samui dentist guide covers clinics, licence checks and timing treatment around flights.

Mental health and crisis support

The private hospitals can arrange a psychiatrist or psychologist, and the government hospital can refer you. In a crisis, call 1669 or go to the nearest emergency department. Thailand's Department of Mental Health runs the 1323 hotline, mainly in Thai. Your embassy can also help contact family at home.

Some hotels and villas have an on-call doctor service that visits your room, which is convenient but adds a call-out fee.

Emergency numbers work from any phone with a Thai SIM, and 1669 operators can transfer you to an English speaker. Save these numbers in your phone before your first scooter ride or boat trip. If you don't speak Thai, start with "ambulance, please" and give a landmark (hotel name, 7-Eleven, temple) as well as the road name.

Three Thai phrases help: chuay duay (help), rot phayaban (ambulance) and rong phayaban (hospital). Hotel reception staff can call for you and give directions in Thai, which is often the fastest way to get an ambulance to a villa on a hillside lane. Share your live location from your phone with whoever is helping you.

Ambulances and getting to hospital

You have two choices. Dialling 1669 sends the public emergency service, which takes you to the nearest suitable hospital and is free. Calling a private hospital directly sends its own ambulance, which takes you to that hospital and is billed. If you are insured and conscious, calling the private hospital you want can save a transfer later; if someone is unconscious or not breathing, call 1669 and start CPR.

Costs, deposits and paying

Thai hospitals expect a guarantee of payment before they treat you, and private hospitals on Samui enforce this. In practice:

  • With insurance: show your policy and the insurer's 24-hour assistance number. The hospital contacts the insurer for a guarantee of payment. Some insurers have direct-billing agreements with Samui hospitals, so ask at admission.
  • Without a guarantee: expect to pay a deposit by card or cash before admission or surgery, and to settle the bill before discharge.
  • Outpatients: you usually pay at the end of the visit and claim it back yourself.
  • Ask for: an estimate before planned treatment, an itemised bill, a medical report in English, and copies of scans.
  • Price lists: private hospitals will give you a price estimate for a procedure on request, and you can ask for it in writing before you agree. Their prices are several times higher than the government hospital's.

If you have no insurance

Emergency departments will stabilise anyone in a genuine emergency, but private hospitals expect payment for everything after that. If you are uninsured, say so early and ask for an estimate at each step. For non-urgent care, the government hospital costs a fraction of private rates. Your embassy can't pay medical bills, but it can help you contact family who can transfer money. Don't leave Thailand with an unpaid hospital bill: hospitals can involve the police and immigration.

Keep every receipt and every page of paperwork. Insurers reject claims for missing documents more often than for anything else.

Travel insurance

A single night in a private hospital on Samui, or one scooter crash with surgery, can cost more than your whole holiday. Buy a policy with a high medical limit, cover for medical evacuation, and explicit cover for the activities you plan: riding a scooter, snorkelling, scuba diving to your certification depth, and jet skis. Read the licence clause: most policies refuse scooter claims unless you held a licence valid for motorcycles (an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category plus your home licence). Read our Koh Samui travel insurance guide for a checklist.

Have these ready on your phone and on paper: your policy number, the insurer's 24-hour assistance number, a photo of your passport and entry stamp, your licence and IDP if you ride, and the name of your hotel. If you're travelling as a family, make sure at least two adults have the details. Call the assistance line as soon as you're admitted; many policies limit what they pay if you don't notify them within a set time.

If we cancel a boat trip because of weather, you choose a full refund or a new date, so you don't need to claim for that.

Scooter accidents: the biggest risk

Road crashes are the most common reason tourists end up in Samui's emergency departments. Thailand has one of the highest road death rates in the world, and Samui's ring road mixes trucks, minivans, sand on bends, steep hills and sudden rain. Most injuries are to people who were not wearing a helmet, were riding at night, had been drinking, or were riding a bike they hadn't ridden before.

  • Wear a helmet: it is the law for rider and passenger, with fines of up to 2,000 THB per person.
  • Carry your home licence and an IDP with the motorcycle category; without them, insurance often won't pay.
  • Avoid the steep hill roads, such as the route 4170 connector in the south-west, in the rain.
  • Don't hand over your passport as a rental deposit.

What to do at the scene

  1. Move to safety if you can, and keep injured people still if a neck or back injury is possible.
  2. Call 1669 for an ambulance and 191 for the police; the Tourist Police (1155) can translate.
  3. Photograph the scene, the vehicles, the number plates and the other party's documents.
  4. Don't sign anything you can't read and don't agree a cash settlement under pressure.
  5. Get a police report for your insurer.

Jellyfish stings

Box jellyfish live in the waters around Samui and Koh Phangan. Thailand has recorded 10 deaths from box jellyfish since 2002, and 9 of them were in waters off these two islands. Many hotels have vinegar stations and warning signs on the beach. The risk is higher in the rainy months, after heavy rain, in calm water, at night and outside buoyed swimming areas.

First aid for a box jellyfish sting:

  1. Get the person out of the water and keep them as still as possible.
  2. Pour vinegar (household vinegar, 4–6% acetic acid) over the sting for at least 30 seconds.
  3. Don't rub the area, don't rinse it with fresh water and don't apply ice.
  4. Call 1669. Go to hospital for severe pain, confusion, chest pain, breathing problems or skin colour changes.
  5. If the person stops breathing, start CPR and continue until help arrives.

Other stingers are less dangerous. Smaller jellyfish cause painful red welts that ease within hours; vinegar or seawater (not fresh water) and a pharmacy antihistamine help. Stingrays and stonefish can hide in the shallows of the tidal north and west coasts: shuffle your feet and wear water shoes. A sting from either causes intense pain; immerse the area in hot water and go to hospital.

More on flags, rips and stingers is in our Koh Samui beach safety guide.

Dengue and mosquitoes

Dengue fever is endemic in Thailand, including Samui, with cases all year and peaks in the rainy months. It is spread by day-biting Aedes mosquitoes. There is no specific cure, and a second infection can be more severe than the first.

  • Prevent bites: use a repellent with DEET or picaridin, especially at dawn and dusk; cover up in gardens and at jungle waterfalls; empty standing water around your villa.
  • Symptoms: sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, rash, usually 4–10 days after the bite.
  • What to do: see a doctor and get a blood test. Take paracetamol, not ibuprofen or aspirin, and drink plenty of fluids.
  • Warning signs: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding gums or nose, or drowsiness as the fever falls. Go to hospital at once.

Rabies is also present in Thailand. If a dog, cat or monkey bites or scratches you, wash the wound with soap and water for 15 minutes and go to a hospital the same day for post-exposure vaccination.

Pharmacies and medicines

Pharmacies are everywhere in the tourist areas, and Thai pharmacists can sell many medicines that need a prescription at home. Hospital pharmacies are the most reliable option late at night. If you need a regular medicine, bring enough for your trip in its original packaging with a copy of the prescription; some controlled medicines need a Thai FDA permit to bring in. Cannabis products need a Thai prescription. Read our Koh Samui pharmacies guide before you pack.

Diving, decompression and the recompression chamber

Koh Samui has a recompression (hyperbaric) chamber in Bophut, run by SSS Network / Hyperbaric Services Thailand, which treats divers from Samui, Phangan and Koh Tao. The Samui chamber is listed at 34/8 Moo 4, Bophut, with an office line of +66 77 427 427 and a 24-hour emergency line of +66 81 081 9555. [check]

Signs of decompression illness include joint pain, tingling or numbness, unusual fatigue, dizziness, breathing difficulty or confusion within hours of a dive. Breathe 100% oxygen (dive boats carry it), lie flat, drink water if conscious, and call your dive centre and your dive insurer's emergency line. Don't fly for at least 24 hours after diving, and longer after any symptoms. Check that your travel insurance covers diving to your certification depth. See our Koh Samui diving guide.

Patients from Koh Phangan and Koh Tao

Koh Phangan and Koh Tao have clinics and small hospitals for everyday problems, but serious cases are transferred to Samui, usually by speedboat to a Samui pier and on by ambulance, or by the regular ferries for stable patients. Koh Samui Hospital is the public referral hospital for all three islands, and the private hospitals accept transfers too. Very serious cases go on to Surat Thani or Bangkok. If you are on Phangan for the Full Moon Party, our sister site has a full Koh Phangan hospital guide.

On Full Moon Party nights, Phangan's clinics are at their busiest with injuries, burns from fire shows and alcohol- or drug-related emergencies, and transfers to Samui increase. If you are going from Samui, book a licensed return boat, stay with friends and keep your hotel's address and 1669 in your phone. See Full Moon Party from Koh Samui.

Night-time transfers depend on sea conditions. In the October–December monsoon, boats can be delayed by rough seas.

Children, pregnancy and long-term conditions

  • Children: Bangkok Hospital Samui has a paediatric department, and the other private hospitals and the government hospital run children's clinics. Pack children's paracetamol, oral rehydration salts and a thermometer; heat, sun and stomach bugs are the usual problems. See Koh Samui with kids.
  • Pregnancy: the private hospitals and the government hospital have obstetric care. Check your airline's late-pregnancy rules and whether your insurance covers pregnancy complications abroad. Zika and dengue are both present in Thailand, so take mosquito precautions seriously.
  • Older travellers: heat, dehydration and falls on wet tiles and boat steps are the common problems. Choose accommodation without steep access roads if mobility is limited, and tell boat crews if you need help boarding. Travel insurance for over-70s often has lower limits or excludes existing conditions, so read the small print.
  • Long-term conditions: bring a doctor's letter listing your diagnoses and medicines by generic name, plus enough medication for the trip. Insulin users should keep it cool and carry spare supplies on boat days. Dialysis and other regular treatments must be booked with a hospital in advance.

Medical evacuation to Bangkok or home

For complex surgery, specialist heart or stroke care or burns, patients may be moved to Surat Thani or Bangkok. Your insurer's assistance company arranges this: by scheduled flight with a medical escort, by road and ferry, or by air ambulance for critical cases. Samui's airport takes air ambulance flights. Evacuation is extremely expensive without insurance, which is why "medical evacuation" should be on your policy. Ask the hospital for a full medical report in English and copies of all scans on a disc or USB stick before any transfer; the receiving hospital will need them. Never discharge yourself to fly home without your doctor's agreement and your insurer's approval; flying soon after surgery, a head injury or a dive accident can be dangerous.

Add a boat trip when you're well

Once you're fit again, the sea is the best part of Samui. The Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, all year) is a short, easy half day. Ang Thong National Marine Park is closed from 1 November to 15 December and reopens on 16 December: book the Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB adult) for later dates. The Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip costs 2,200 THB per adult. Tell us about any medical condition, pregnancy or recent dive when you book. Our crews carry first-aid kits and life jackets for every age, and we cancel when the sea isn't safe. Our dentist guide and safety guide cover the rest.
