Koh Samui Immigration is at 333 Maenam Road, Soi 1, Moo 1, Maenam 84330. It moved from Nathon in 2016, so old map pins in Nathon are wrong. Phone +66 77 423 440; it usually opens Monday to Friday, 08:30–16:30, and closes on weekends and Thai public holidays, so call ahead. It handles visa extensions (TM.7, 1,900 THB), 90-day reports (TM.47), TM.30 address notifications and re-entry permits (1,000 THB single, 3,800 THB multiple).

Last checked: November 2026. Since 15 September 2026, visa-exempt visitors from 60 countries get 30 days on arrival, extendable once by 30 days here. Office hours for the Samui unit are not officially published; the times below are the standard provincial immigration hours.

Extensions

Tourists on a 30-day visa exemption can extend once by up to 30 days for 1,900 THB. Tourist visa holders (60 days) can extend once by 30 days. Bring your passport, signed copies, a 4×6 cm photo, the TM.7, proof of address and an onward ticket. Our visa extension guide walks through each step.

90-day reports (TM.47)

If you stay in Thailand for more than 90 days in a row on a long-stay visa or extension, you must confirm your address every 90 days. The window opens 15 days before the due date and closes 7 days after. Your first report must be made in person in Maenam. After that, you can usually report online at tm47.immigration.go.th, or by registered post at least 15 days before the due date. Leaving Thailand resets the 90-day count. Missing a report costs 2,000 THB, and police can fine people who never report.

The online system sometimes rejects reports when the TM.30 address on file doesn't match, so make sure your landlord has filed it first.

See the TM.30 section below for who files and when.

Re-entry permits

An extension of stay (for example retirement, marriage, DTV extension or a Non-B extension) is cancelled if you leave Thailand without a re-entry permit. Apply here on form TM.8 with your passport, a photo and the fee: 1,000 THB for a single re-entry or 3,800 THB for multiple re-entries during the life of your extension. You can also buy a single re-entry permit at Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi Airport before departure, but queues there can be long. Visa-exempt tourists and holders of a multiple-entry visa that is still valid don't need one.

Long-stay extensions (retirement, marriage, DTV)

Annual extensions based on retirement or marriage to a Thai national need far more paperwork than a tourist extension: proof of funds or income, a Thai bank letter issued a few days before, bank book updates, a map to your home, photos, and for marriage cases your spouse in person. Staff may visit your home for marriage extensions. Book well ahead in high season and go with a complete file. The financial thresholds are set nationally and revised from time to time, so confirm them with the office before you prepare documents. For the bigger picture, read our long-stay guide and our Thailand visa guide.

Overstays

If you have already overstayed, go to the office or straight to the airport immigration counter when you leave. The fine is 500 THB per day, capped at 20,000 THB. Overstays of more than 90 days bring a re-entry ban if you hand yourself in (1 year for over 90 days, rising to 10 years for over 5 years); being arrested for overstay means a ban of 5 or 10 years. If a ferry or flight cancellation caused the overstay, bring the cancellation notice.

What this office doesn't do

  • New visas: tourist visas, DTVs and other visas are issued by Thai embassies and the e-Visa portal, not by immigration on Samui. In most cases you can't switch from a visa exemption to a long-stay visa here.
  • Work permits: these come from the Department of Employment, not immigration. A Non-B extension here depends on having one.
  • Passports: a lost or stolen passport is replaced by your embassy in Bangkok. Immigration then links your entry record to the new passport. See our Tourist Police page for the police report you need first.
  • Koh Phangan and Koh Tao residents: check whether your own island's immigration service can handle your case before taking a ferry to Samui.

What to bring

For every visit, bring:

  • Original passport.
  • Signed copies of the photo page, current visa or extension, latest entry stamp and TDAC.
  • One 4×6 cm colour photo taken within 6 months (two if you are doing more than one application).
  • The completed form (TM.7, TM.8 or TM.47), in blue or black ink.
  • The TM.30 receipt for your address, or your hotel booking.
  • Exact cash for the fee.
  • A pen, water and patience. Allow a full morning in high season.

Make copies in advance. Don't rely on a photocopier at the office. Copy shops and photo studios in Maenam, Bophut and Chaweng can do copies and 4×6 cm photos within minutes.

Queues and the best time to go

  • Arrive before 08:30. Queue numbers can run out on busy days.
  • Tuesday to Thursday is usually quieter than Monday or Friday.
  • The busiest months are December to April, when the island is full.
  • Avoid the working day after a long holiday such as Songkran (13–15 April) or New Year.
  • Check the day isn't a public holiday: the office follows the government calendar.

Some agents report an online appointment calendar for the Samui unit. [check]

Bring something to read and a phone charger: once your number is called, you may still wait a while for the passport to come back. Lunchtime counters may slow down. Plan to finish in the morning so you aren't caught by the 16:30 closing time.

TM.30 for landlords, villa owners and tenants

The TM.30 is the notification that a foreigner is staying at an address. The law puts the duty on the owner, householder or manager of the place, not the foreigner.

Where you stayWho files the TM.30
Hotel, resort or licensed guesthouseThe hotel, electronically, at check-in
Rented villa, house or condoThe landlord or the building manager
Condo you ownYou, as the owner
Staying with a Thai friend or partnerThe house owner (the person on the house book)
  • Deadline: within 24 hours of the foreigner arriving at the address.
  • After trips abroad: officially a new notification is due when you return to the address from abroad. Practice varies by office, so ask in Maenam.
  • Online: owners can register once in person and then file online at tm30.immigration.go.th.
  • Fines: up to 2,000 THB for a late or missing notification.
  • Why tenants care: without the TM.30 receipt, extensions and 90-day reports are often delayed or refused.

If you move house on Samui, from a hotel to a villa or between rentals, the new place needs a new TM.30. Keep a photo of each receipt on your phone. Immigration officers in Maenam check the address on your TM.7 or TM.47 against the TM.30 record, and a mismatch is a common reason to be sent home to fix it.

Villa owners who let to foreigners on Samui should build TM.30 filing into every check-in. If you rent through our Koh Samui villas service for a long stay, ask about TM.30 when you enquire.

Online services

Use official portals only. Look-alike websites charge fees for free services and collect passport data.

  • immigration.go.th: Thai Immigration Bureau, forms and announcements.
  • tm47.immigration.go.th: online 90-day reports (after your first in-person report).
  • tm30.immigration.go.th: online TM.30 for registered owners.
  • tdac.immigration.go.th: the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card, required before every arrival.

Visa applications from abroad go through the Ministry of Foreign Affairs e-Visa portal at thaievisa.go.th, not through this office.

Is the immigration office still in Nathon?

No. The Koh Samui immigration unit moved from Nathon, the west-coast port town, to Soi 1 in Maenam in 2016. Some maps, hotel guides and old blogs still show the Nathon address. If you drive to Nathon, you'll lose an hour. Use the Maenam address and phone ahead if your map app shows anything else. Nathon still has the government hospital, the old pier and the district offices, which is partly why the confusion lasts. See our Nathon guide for what the town does offer.

After your visit

Maenam's long, quiet beach is right there, and Fisherman's Village in Bophut is 10–15 minutes east for lunch. Pig Island runs all year: the Pig Island speedboat tour costs 1,450 THB per adult, with hotel pickup included from most areas of Koh Samui (+400 THB per person return from Lipa Noi, Nathon, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and other remote areas).
