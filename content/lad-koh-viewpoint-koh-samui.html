Lad Koh viewpoint is a free, 24-hour roadside lookout on the ring road between Chaweng Noi and Coral Cove, worth a 10–15 minute stop for the view over the granite rocks, the bay and the open Gulf. It is best at sunrise (about 06:00–06:40 depending on the month). Stay on the platform and off the rocks below when waves are up: on 29 November 2024 a 24-year-old visitor was swept off them by 2–3 m waves and drowned.

Map

Lad Koh sits on the headland between Chaweng Noi to the north and Coral Cove to the south, about 1 km from each, on the stretch of ring road that winds along the cliffs towards Lamai.

The view

From the platform you look along a coast of rounded granite boulders, with turquoise water in the coves, the hills behind Coral Cove and Silver Beach to the south, and open sea to the east. On calm days the water below is clear enough to see the rocks under the surface; in the monsoon months, big waves explode against the boulders. Small shrines and benches stand on the grounds, and steps and ramps lead down towards the water.

It is a pleasant, quick stop rather than a destination: most people stay 10–15 minutes for photos and a coconut ice cream. Reviewers who expected more sometimes call it unremarkable; we think it is worth pulling over if you are driving between Chaweng and Lamai anyway.

Safety on the rocks

On 29 November 2024, a 24-year-old Russian visitor walked down from the car park to the rocks below the viewpoint with a yoga mat. A wave of 2–3 m swept her into the sea; a bystander jumped in but could not reach her, the search was suspended after 30 minutes because the waves were growing, and her body was found the next day on a beach about 1 km away. Jet-ski operators had already pulled their machines off the nearby beach that day because of the conditions. Afterwards the Samui City Municipality closed access to the rocky area below the viewpoint.

  • Stay on the platform and the steps. Don't climb over barriers to reach the rocks.
  • Waves on this coast come in sets: a calm minute can be followed by a much bigger wave.
  • From October to December the north-east monsoon drives large swells onto the east coast, and red flags fly at Chaweng and Lamai. Treat every rock at sea level as dangerous in those months.
  • Wet rock is slippery even on calm days.
  • Emergency: ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155.

Parking and traffic

The viewpoint is on a winding section of the ring road with blind bends, steady traffic and tour vans pulling in and out.

  • Slow down well before the pull-out and signal early; don't brake hard on the bend.
  • The parking area is small and fills quickly; if it is full, drive on and come back rather than parking on the road.
  • When leaving, wait for a clear gap: cars come round the bends fast.
  • Don't walk along the road between Lad Koh and the nearby beaches; there is little or no pavement.

Best time to visit

Lad Koh faces the east coast, so it is a sunrise spot. Sunrise is about 06:00 in May–June and 06:40 in January–February; the light is soft until about 09:00, and the tour vans arrive later in the morning. Early evening is quieter for parking and cooler, though the sun sets behind the hills. February to September has the calmest sea and clearest water; October to December brings spectacular surf, best watched from the platform. See sunset and sunrise spots.

An east-coast morning

  1. 06:15 Sunrise at Lad Koh (check the month's sunrise time above).
  2. 07:00 Breakfast in Chaweng Noi or Lamai.
  3. 08:30 Swim and snorkel at Silver Beach before it fills at about 10:00.
  4. 10:30 Coral Cove for a second snorkel, or continue to Hin Ta and Hin Yai and the Overlap Stone above Lamai.

On calm mornings only: from October to December, swap the snorkel stops for a café and the waterfalls.

Photo tips

  • Shoot from the upper platform for the classic view along the boulders to Coral Cove.
  • At sunrise, expose for the sky and let the rocks fall into silhouette.
  • Waves look most dramatic with a fast shutter from a safe distance; never go down to the rocks for the shot.
  • Thailand requires drones to be registered; don't fly over the road or other visitors.

The east coast from the sea

The best view of Samui's boulder coast is from a boat. A private speedboat can run along the east coast and on to the southern islands, at your own pace and on your own schedule.
