Bang Por (also written Bang Po) is a quiet bay of about 5 km on Koh Samui's north-west coast, between Maenam and the Laem Yai headland, 30–40 minutes from the airport. People come for seafood on the sand and sunsets; it is popular with long-stay expats and has almost no tourist crowds. It is very tidal: swim at high tide only, because at low tide the water pulls back 50 m or more over a flat of sand, mud and stones.

The beach, the tides and swimming

Bang Por is a long, gentle bay facing north-west towards the mainland and the outer islands of Ang Thong. The western section runs along the ring road, with seafood restaurants, small resorts and fishing boats; the eastern section, often called Baan Tai, is quieter, prettier and looks across to Koh Phangan.

Sources disagree sharply about swimming here. Some describe soft sand and calm water that is fine for children; others call it rocky and not for swimming at all. Both are describing the same beach at different states of the tide. At high tide Bang Por is a narrow strip of sand with calm, shallow water, pleasant for a swim. At low tide the sea retreats a long way, often 50 m or more, exposing a flat of sand, mud and stones where local people collect shellfish. Then it is a place for a walk, not a swim, and you need reef shoes for the rocks and shells.

  • Swim: within a couple of hours of high tide, in calm conditions. [check]
  • Wear reef shoes: there are stones, rocks and shell fragments, especially at low tide.
  • No lifeguards: swim near others and never alone at dusk.
  • Jellyfish: box jellyfish are rare but recorded in Samui waters, mostly in calm, warm, shallow water like this, after rain and near dusk. First aid is vinegar for at least 30 seconds, no rubbing, no fresh water; call 1669.

The upside of the west coast is the season. While Chaweng and Lamai fly red flags in the north-east monsoon (October–December), the north-west coast is in the lee of the island and is often calm, though still rainy: November averages about 445 mm. See Koh Samui weather.

Sunset at Bang Por

Bang Por is one of the few beaches on Samui where you can watch the sun go down over the sea from a restaurant table with your feet in the sand. The bay faces north-west, so the sun goes down to the west over the Laem Yai headland and the sea, and the whole sky colours over the water; in the winter months the sun sets further south, behind the land towards Nathon, but the afterglow over the bay is still the show. Sunset ranges from about 17:55 in November to 18:45 in July; arrive 45 minutes early for a table at the water's edge. A low-tide sunset, with the wet flats reflecting the sky, is often the most photogenic. More on Koh Samui sunset spots.

Seafood and other places to eat

Seafood is the reason most visitors come. The western end of the bay along the ring road has a row of simple restaurants with tables on or above the sand, with fish, prawns, squid and crab priced by weight.

  • Bang Por Seafood (Takho): listed in the MICHELIN Guide Thailand 2026, southern Thai seafood at modest (฿฿) prices, open daily 10:00–22:00. Its address is Moo 6, Maenam.
  • Khanom Jeen Pla Khiao: a local favourite for khanom jeen rice noodles with southern curries.
  • Beach bars: a few laid-back thatched bars along the sand for a sunset drink.

How to order: check whether the board price is per kilo or per 100 g, choose your fish and agree the cooking style (grilled, steamed with lime, deep-fried with garlic) before it goes on the grill, and ask for the total. Try wai khua, the Samui dish of octopus in coconut milk, if you see it. See Koh Samui seafood and Koh Samui restaurants.

Nightlife is a sunset drink. For more, Maenam's beach bars and Cape Away's Sunday party are 10–15 minutes east; Nathon's daily night market (about 16:00–22:00) is 10–15 minutes south-west.

A sunset evening at Bang Por

  1. 16:00 Check the tide card. If high tide falls in the late afternoon, come early for a swim.
  2. 16:30 Walk the beach east towards Ban Tai; at low tide, watch the shellfish collectors on the flats.
  3. 17:15 Claim a table at the water's edge at one of the seafood restaurants.
  4. 17:30 Choose your fish from the ice, agree the price by weight and the cooking style.
  5. 18:00–18:45 Sunset, depending on the month.
  6. 19:30 Head home; if you came by Grab, book your return before you finish dinner.

Ban Tai: the quiet end of the bay

The eastern part of the bay, towards Maenam, is called Ban Tai. It is a quiet stretch of about 1 km, shallow and calm, with fewer buildings and a view to Koh Phangan. With small children it is the better end, at high tide. Not to be confused with Baan Tai on Koh Phangan, the Half Moon Festival village. To reach the quietest stretch, follow the signs off the ring road for Mimosa Resort down an unmade lane.

Practical tips for Bang Por

  • Cash: the seafood restaurants and beach bars are mostly cash; ATMs charge 220–250 THB per foreign-card withdrawal.
  • Mosquitoes: they bite at dusk on this quiet coast and dengue is present on Samui, so bring repellent for a sunset dinner.
  • Reef shoes: essential at low tide for the stones and shells.
  • Getting home: Grab is thin on the west side after dark; book your return early or keep a taxi driver's number.
  • Weather: showers often clear by sunset in the dry season; in November, check the live forecast before you drive across the island.

Where to stay in Bang Por

Bang Por is a residential and long-stay coast rather than a resort strip. Expect small bungalow resorts, guesthouses, holiday homes and private pool villas, many rented by the month. Prices are generally below the east coast for a similar standard. At the top end, the Four Seasons at Laem Yai, the White Lotus season 3 filming location, sits just west of the bay, with rooms from far above 10,000 THB a night.

  • Good for: long stays, couples who want sunsets and quiet, people with a car or scooter.
  • Not good for: walking to restaurants and nightlife, first-timers who want everything on the doorstep, or anyone relying on Grab (coverage on the west side is thin).

Bang Por is also one of the better places on Samui to see everyday island life away from tourism: fishing boats coming in, families collecting shellfish at low tide and roadside stalls selling fruit and grilled snacks. Be respectful with cameras, ask before photographing people, and don't walk through private gardens to reach the sand; use the public lanes or a restaurant.

Things to do near Bang Por

  • Laem Yai: the headland at the north-west tip, very quiet with clear water for snorkelling around the rocks, best seen from a boat. See White Lotus filming locations.
  • Nathon: the old capital and west-coast ferry port, with a daily night market and a free sunset from the pier.
  • Hin Lad Waterfall: a jungle waterfall about 2 km inland from Nathon; allow about 2 hours on a steep trail.
  • Maenam: Chinatown, the Pralarn ferry pier, Santiburi golf (about 5,600 THB) and the Tree Bridge zipline (about 1,590 THB).
  • Lipa Noi: the very shallow west-coast beach, 20 minutes south, for toddlers at high tide.

Getting there and parking

Bang Por is on the ring road (Route 4169) on the north-west side, between Maenam and Nathon. Parking is easy at the restaurants and along the road. Grab coverage on the west side is thin, especially in the evening: if you come for dinner from the east coast, keep your driver's number or arrange the return in advance. Songthaews run along the ring road in daylight and mostly stop by 20:00–22:00.
