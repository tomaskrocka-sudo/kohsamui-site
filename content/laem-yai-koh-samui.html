Laem Yai is the far north-west tip of Koh Samui, about 5 km (10–15 minutes) north of Nathon and 22 km (30–35 minutes) from Chaweng. The shore is rocks, pebbles and patches of sand, partly fronted by private resorts including the Four Seasons, where much of The White Lotus season 3 was filmed. Swimming is poor at low tide, but at high tide the water is clear enough to snorkel, and it faces west for sunsets over the sea. It is very quiet, with almost no shops or restaurants.

Map and directions

Laem Yai is the headland where the coast turns from the west side to the north side of the island. From Nathon, drive about 5 km north on the ring road and turn left just before the road climbs steeply and bends east. From Maenam or Chaweng, follow the ring road west past Bang Por, and turn right at the bottom of the hill where the road angles left. The lanes down to the shore are narrow and some are private resort roads, so check signs.

The beach

Laem Yai is not a postcard white-sand beach, and it helps to know that before you drive out. The shore is a mix of rocks, pebbles and coarse sand, backed by coconut palms and the gardens of a few resorts and villas. Much of the frontage belongs to private resorts, and the stretches in between are narrow. What it offers is space and quiet: you can walk for long periods without meeting anyone, the water is clear on calm days, and the view west over the open sea is unobstructed. It is the kind of place people come to for a sunset and a walk, not for a sunbed day.

Swimming and snorkelling

  • Low tide: the sea pulls back over rocks and stony flats, and swimming is poor. Don't try to wade out over the rocks.
  • High tide: the water covers the rocks and you can swim and snorkel close to shore in clear water. Expect small fish around the boulders, not a coral garden.
  • Footwear: water shoes are essential on this shore.
  • Gear: there are no rentals on the public shore; bring your own mask and snorkel.

For proper reef snorkelling, take a boat: Koh Tan off the south-west coast and the Ang Thong islands are the nearest good reefs. See Koh Samui snorkelling.

Safety at Laem Yai

  • Remote: there are no lifeguards and few people about; don't swim alone, and tell someone where you are going.
  • Rocks and urchins: slippery, sharp rocks and occasional sea urchins in the shallows.
  • Box jellyfish: present in Samui waters, with risk higher in the monsoon months and in calm, hot weather after rain. Avoid swimming at dusk; if stung, pour vinegar for at least 30 seconds, don't rub, call 1669.
  • Roads at night: the ring road over the north-west hill is steep and unlit in places. Ride slowly after sunset.
  • Medical help: the government Koh Samui Hospital is on the edge of Nathon, about 10 minutes south (+66 77 913 200); the private hospitals are in the Chaweng area, 30+ minutes away.

The Four Seasons and The White Lotus

Season 3 of The White Lotus, aired from February to April 2025, was filmed largely at the Four Seasons Resort Koh Samui on Laem Yai. The resort is private: you cannot wander through it, and access to its restaurants or spa depends on the resort's own booking policy for outside guests, so contact the resort directly before you go. Don't enter resort grounds or private roads without permission. The easiest way to see this stretch of coast is from the water, on a sunset cruise or a private boat along the north-west and west coasts. Everything about the filming locations, prices and visiting is on our White Lotus Koh Samui guide.

Where to stay at Laem Yai

Accommodation here is mostly upscale: the Four Seasons (one of the island's most expensive resorts), The Passage Samui (formerly a Mövenpick resort, with three pools and a beachfront), and a scattering of private pool villas on the hillsides. There are almost no budget options. Staying here means sea-view sunsets, quiet nights and easy access to Nathon's ferries, but you will need a car or scooter for nearly every meal, and Chaweng is 30–35 minutes away. Mid-range travellers usually stay in Maenam (20 minutes east) and drive out for the sunset. See where to stay on Koh Samui, Koh Samui villas and honeymoon ideas.

Eating and nightlife

Laem Yai itself has a small minimart and the resorts' restaurants and bars. For local food, drive:

  • Bang Por, about 3 km east: a string of seafood restaurants with tables on or near the sand and sunset views. Ask the price per kilo before ordering.
  • Nathon, about 5 km south: the daily night food market near the pier, 16:30–22:00, the cheapest dinner on the west coast.
  • Maenam, 11 km east: J and J Street Food Market (daily 12:00–24:00, most stalls in the evening) and beach restaurants.

There is no nightlife at Laem Yai beyond resort bars. See Koh Samui seafood and restaurants.

Nearby sights

  • Nathon: the old capital and ferry port, with a waterfront night market and a pier that is a free sunset spot.
  • Hin Lad waterfall: a jungle waterfall about 2 km inland from Nathon, roughly two hours round trip on a steep trail.
  • Lipa Noi: the shallow, calm sunset beach 6 km south of Nathon, good for toddlers at high tide.
  • Maenam: a long, palm-lined swimming beach on the north coast.
