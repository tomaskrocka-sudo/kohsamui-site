Lamai is Samui's second beach town: about 4 km of fine sand on the south-east coast, 15 km and 25–30 minutes from the airport and 12 km south of Chaweng. Central Lamai is the place to swim, but the seabed drops steeply; the north end is shallow and stony, and the Hin Ta and Hin Yai rocks sit at the south tip. It is cheaper and calmer than Chaweng (mid-range rooms about 1,500–3,500 THB a night), has a night market every evening, and is rough with red flags on many days from October to December.

Map and GPS points in Lamai

Lamai runs from the rocky headland below Silver Beach in the north to Hin Ta and Hin Yai in the south. The ring road (Route 4169) runs behind the town, and a separate beach road loops through the centre. Most shops, bars and the night market sit along that beach road between the river bridge and the south end.

The beach, north to south

Lamai is one long arc of sand, but the three sections feel like different beaches. Pick your hotel by section, not just by "Lamai".

North Lamai

The north end is quiet and lined with resorts. The seabed is shallow and covered with stones and broken coral, so at low tide you wade a long way before the water is deep enough to swim. Reef shoes make it bearable. It is the calmest part of Lamai on windy days because the headland gives some shelter, and it suits people who want a quiet room more than a swimming beach.

Central Lamai

This is the stretch to swim from. The sand is fine and pale, and the water gets deep quickly: you are out of your depth a few metres from the shore, and low tide barely changes that. Strong swimmers like it; parents of small children need to stay close. Sunbed rentals, massage huts, water-sports operators and most beach bars are here. There is little to see underwater, so leave the mask at home and save snorkelling for Silver Beach or Coral Cove.

South Lamai

The south end is the least developed part. Fishermen's houses still stand on the sand, rocks and coral line the waterline, and the beach ends at Hin Ta and Hin Yai, the Grandfather and Grandmother rocks. Swimming is poor here, but the walk along the sand at sunrise is the best in Lamai.

Swimming and safety at Lamai

Lamai faces east, straight into the north-east monsoon. From October to December, and during cold-air surges in January and February, onshore wind builds waves and rip currents. On 19 February 2026, lifeguards and hotels raised red flags along Chaweng and Lamai with waves of 1–2 m and banned swimming for several days. Outside those periods the sea is usually flat from March to September.

  • Red flag means no swimming. People drown on Samui every year after ignoring flags. Yellow means caution; no flag does not mean safe.
  • Rip currents: if you are pulled out, swim parallel to the beach until you are out of the current, then swim back in. Do not fight it head-on.
  • Steep shelf: in central Lamai, children can step out of their depth suddenly. Use a life jacket for weak swimmers.
  • Box jellyfish: most of Thailand's recorded box jellyfish deaths have happened in waters off Samui and Koh Phangan. Risk rises in the monsoon months and in calm, hot weather after rain. Avoid swimming at dusk and at night, use netted areas where a hotel has one, and if someone is stung pour vinegar on the sting for at least 30 seconds, do not rub it or rinse with fresh water, and call 1669.
  • Rocks: stay off wet rocks at Hin Ta and Hin Yai and at the Lad Koh viewpoint when waves are up. A visitor was swept off the rocks at Lad Koh in November 2024.
  • Jet skis: rent only with a written damage agreement and never leave your passport as a deposit. See common Samui scams.

Lamai Night Market and the fresh market

Lamai's evening food scene is centred on the night market on the beach road, just before the bridge over the river. It runs every evening from about 17:00 until late, with the most stalls open 18:00–20:00. In 2025 it was expanded with more seating and a covered bar with a stage for live music. It is cash only.

The Sunday walking street: status in 2026

Older guides send people to a Lamai Sunday walking street, opposite the McDonald's in central Lamai, from about 17:00 to 22:00. Sources disagree in 2026: some listings still show it on Sundays (one says Wednesdays and Sundays), while a careful 2025 market survey says the Lamai and Maenam walking streets disappeared after the pandemic. We have not confirmed that it runs every week, so treat it as a bonus rather than a plan. The daily night market by the bridge is the reliable option. For the markets that definitely run, such as the Friday walking street at Fisherman's Village, see Koh Samui walking streets.

Eating in Lamai

Lamai is good for cheap and mid-priced Thai food and has fewer high-end restaurants than Chaweng or Fisherman's Village. Rough guide to 2026 prices: market dishes 40–80 THB, a local restaurant main 80–180 THB, a beachfront restaurant main 200–400 THB, and a beer in a bar about 80–120 THB.

  • Night market and fresh market: the cheapest meals in Lamai (see above).
  • Sabeinglae: a long-running seafood restaurant with sea-view tables near Hin Ta and Hin Yai, listed in 2026 as open daily 10:00–22:00 at about 300–500 THB per person. Busy at dinner.
  • Krua Chao Baan: a long-standing local Thai seafood restaurant in Lamai, still listed in 2025 guides.
  • Saffron at Banyan Tree Samui: the upmarket Thai option, on the headland at the north end of Lamai.
  • Hua Thanon: five minutes south for halal food, roti and southern curries.

Lamai nightlife

Lamai nights are relaxed: a few long-running pubs, reggae and beach bars, live music at the night market, and the bar strip on the beach road. It attracts an older and more settled crowd than Chaweng, and things wind down earlier. If you want clubs that run past 03:00, take a 15–20 minute Grab to Chaweng.

  • Shamrock Irish Pub: open since 2005, with a resident live band listed nightly 21:30–01:30.
  • Rock Bar: reggae and rock, listed as open 11:00–24:00.
  • Teddy Weed Beach: beach parties and fire shows, listed as open 08:30–02:00.
  • Beach Republic (south Lamai): the pool bar and much of the resort were reported closed in 2025–2026, with only a beach café left. Its Sunday brunches and parties are no longer advertised, so don't plan a night around it; we'll update this if it reopens.

Where to stay in Lamai

Lamai suits couples, budget-minded families and long stays: the sand is as good as Chaweng's, rooms cost less and nights are quieter. The trade-off is fewer restaurants and a longer ride to the airport and the Koh Phangan piers.

Getting to Lamai, getting around and parking

Lamai is on the ring road, Route 4169, 12 km south of Chaweng. Grab works well in Lamai, with typical waits of 5–20 minutes and surge pricing in rain and late evening. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) run the ring road in the daytime and thin out after 20:00; Chaweng to Lamai costs 30–40 THB for locals and 50–60 THB is a fair tourist fare. Taxis have no meters: agree the price before you get in.

Distances are road distances; times are for normal daytime traffic, so allow more in rain and in Chaweng's evening rush. Fares are typical 2026 ranges, not fixed tariffs. Grab cannot use the airport pickup zone, so you walk to a meeting point outside.

Parking: street parking in Lamai is free. In the evening, spaces near the night market and the bridge fill up, so a scooter is easier than a car. Hin Ta and Hin Yai has a paid lot at about 30 THB. Scooter rental in Lamai costs about 150–350 THB a day for a 125 cc; you need an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category, and helmets are compulsory for rider and passenger, with fines of up to 2,000 THB per person. Events in Lamai 2026–2027

  • New Year's Eve (31 December 2026): fireworks along the beach; after Chaweng and Fisherman's Village, Lamai has the island's third-biggest display. See Christmas and New Year on Koh Samui.
  • Chinese New Year (Saturday 6 February 2027): the nearest celebrations are at the Guan Yu Shrine in Hua Thanon. See Chinese New Year on Koh Samui.
  • Songkran (public holidays 13–15 April 2027): Maret subdistrict, which includes Lamai, was one of three official Songkran zones in 2026, alongside Chaweng and Nathon, with events from 12 April. Expect water fights on the beach road. See Songkran on Koh Samui.
