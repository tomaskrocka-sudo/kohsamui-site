Ang Thong National Marine Park is an archipelago of 42 limestone islands about 30 km west of Koh Samui. A day trip covers the Emerald Lake on Koh Mae Ko, the 360° viewpoint on Koh Wua Talab (a steep 25–30 minute climb), kayaking along the cliffs and a snorkelling stop. The park fee is 300 THB per foreign adult and 150 THB per child, paid in cash at the park. The park closes every year from 1 November to 15 December, and the season reopens on 16 December.

Good to know: Ang Thong is closed from 1 November to 15 December 2026. The 2026–27 season reopens on Wednesday 16 December 2026, and you can already book any date from then on. Until it reopens, our two boat trips that run every day are the Pig Island and Koh Tan speedboat tour (1,450 THB) and the Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB), sea conditions permitting.

Most of the 42 islands are uninhabited, steep and covered in forest. Only a handful have landing beaches, and day boats use the same few: Koh Mae Ko, Koh Wua Talab, and one or two snorkelling reefs chosen on the day.

What you see on a day trip

Every shared Ang Thong tour from Samui follows roughly the same route. The order changes with the tide, the wind and how busy each landing is, and the captain makes that call on the day.

The Emerald Lake (Thale Nai) on Koh Mae Ko

The Emerald Lake is an enclosed saltwater lagoon in the middle of Koh Mae Ko, ringed by cliffs. You land on the beach and walk up wooden stairs to a viewing platform above the lake: 15–20 minutes up at an easy pace, with handrails most of the way. The water changes from jade to turquoise with the light, and late morning sun gives the strongest colour. Swimming in the lake is not allowed, so treat this stop as a short climb and a photo.

The viewpoint on Koh Wua Talab

This is the photo most people come for: the whole archipelago spread out below a rocky summit. The trail starts behind the headquarters beach and climbs about 500 m. Allow 25–30 minutes up and a little less down. It is steep, uneven and slippery after rain, with ropes and rough rock steps near the top. Wear shoes with grip, carry water and start early in the stop, because the summit is in full sun. The climb is optional: if you skip it, stay on the beach and swim.

Kayaking

Kayaks are included on our day tours when the sea allows. You paddle for 45–60 minutes along the cliffs and into small coves, usually from the Koh Wua Talab beach. Two-seater sit-on-top kayaks are used, so beginners and children with an adult manage fine in calm water. The guide cancels kayaking if the wind picks up, because the channel between the islands can turn choppy quickly.

Snorkelling

Ang Thong's reefs are shallow fringing reefs on the sheltered sides of the islands. The guide picks the spot on the day for the calmest, clearest water; Koh Sam Sao, Koh Wao and Koh Tai Plao are the usual choices. Visibility is best from February to April. After heavy rain, run-off from the islands clouds the water, and snorkelling is then more about fish near the surface than coral. Masks and snorkels are provided; non-swimmers can float with a life jacket.

Lunch and beach time

Our day tours serve a Thai buffet lunch with a vegetarian option, around 12:30–13:30, with drinking water and soft drinks through the day. The headquarters beach on Koh Wua Talab has toilets, shade and a small park restaurant. Many other operators lunch on Koh Paluay instead. Either way there is time for a swim.

Crowds and how to time your visit

Day boats from Samui and Koh Phangan arrive in the park from about 10:00 and leave by about 15:30, so the landings are busiest in the middle of the day. The Emerald Lake stairs and platform are small and get crowded when several boats land together. December to February and July to August are the busiest months; May, June and September are noticeably quieter.

Three ways to see Ang Thong with fewer people around:

  • Stay overnight. After 15:30 Koh Wua Talab empties, and you get the viewpoint at sunset and sunrise with a handful of other campers.
  • Go private. A private speedboat can leave as early as you like and reorder the stops to dodge the day-boat rush.
  • Choose the VIP tour. With only 15 guests on board, getting on and off at each landing is quick, and the guide has time for everyone.

Avoid Thai public holidays and long weekends if you can, because domestic visitors add to the numbers. Park quotas limit visitor numbers in peak season, so book early for December to February.

Photography tips

The Wua Talab viewpoint faces the whole archipelago, so there is no bad direction, but the light matters. Midday sun flattens the islands and turns the sea pale; the overnight trips give you the low, warm light at 17:00–18:00 and again at sunrise. At the Emerald Lake, the colour of the water is strongest when the sun is high enough to reach the lagoon, so the late-morning stop on the day tours works in your favour. Carry your phone or camera in a dry bag on the boat, because spray reaches every seat on a speedboat. A wide-angle lens helps on the summit, which is small and rocky; watch your footing near the edge.

Park fees and rules

The fee is set by the Department of National Parks, Wildlife and Plant Conservation and collected by rangers when your boat enters the park. It is cash only, in Thai baht, and it is not included in any of our tour prices. Bring the exact amount in small notes.

Note that the park counts children up to 14 years for its fee, while our tour prices count children as 3–11. A 13-year-old pays the adult tour price and the child park fee.

Park rules that matter on a day trip:

  • Sunscreen: sunscreens containing oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-methylbenzylidene camphor (4-MBC) or butylparaben are banned in Thailand's marine national parks, with fines of up to 100,000 THB. Check the label, or cover up with a rash vest.
  • Coral and wildlife: don't stand on or touch coral, don't feed fish or monkeys, and don't collect shells, coral or sand.
  • Litter: take everything back to the boat. There are no bins on most islands.
  • Alcohol and fires: no alcohol is sold in the park and fires are not allowed.
  • Emerald Lake: no swimming.

Staying overnight in the park

Koh Wua Talab is the only island where visitors can sleep. The park runs a small cluster of bungalows and a campsite behind the headquarters beach, with a restaurant that closes at 19:00 and electricity until 21:00. Wi-Fi is limited.

The appeal is simple: once the day boats leave at about 15:30, the beach and the viewpoint are almost empty. You can climb for sunset (17:00–18:00), go back up with a torch for sunrise at 05:30–06:00, and kayak the next morning before the first day boats arrive.

Booking park accommodation yourself is hard. The park's published rates are 600 THB a night for a two-person bungalow, 1,500 THB for an eight-person bungalow and 80 THB per person to camp, plus 450 THB to rent a three-person tent. But the national parks' online booking system was down for long stretches in 2026, phone booking needs Thai, and you still need a boat there and back. Our overnight camping trip (3,600 THB) and overnight bungalow trip (4,200 THB) bundle the boat, the day tour, the accommodation and transfers from most areas, and the bungalow bathrooms are kept very clean. Bungalows are limited and allocated by the park, so book early for December to February.

When to go

Samui's weather runs the opposite way to Phuket's. The north-east monsoon brings most of the year's rain and the roughest sea from October to December, and November is the wettest month. That is why the park closes from 1 November to 15 December. February to April is the calmest, clearest and driest period, and May to September is good on most days.

The annual closure and storm closures

The Department of National Parks closes Ang Thong every year from 1 November to 15 December. The reasons are visitor safety in the north-east monsoon and a rest period for the reefs and islands. The last operating day is 31 October and the first day of the new season is 16 December. During the closure no tourist boats may land, and our booking calendar for every Ang Thong tour starts on 16 December.

Outside that window the park can still close at short notice when the Thai Meteorological Department warns of high waves. Tropical Storm Pabuk shut Ang Thong and the ferries in early January 2019. More recently, the park closed for one day on 8 January 2026, when a strong wind warning forecast 2–3 m waves in the Gulf. If the park or the sea closes your trip, you choose a full refund or a new date.

Sea conditions and seasickness

The crossing is the part of the day that varies most. Between February and May the Gulf is often flat and the speedboat ride is quick and smooth. On windy days, especially in December, January and October, a speedboat bounces over the swell for most of the hour. The big boat rolls more gently and takes longer.

[kp_sea_now]

Practical tips: take a seasickness tablet 30–60 minutes before departure, not on the boat; sit towards the back of a speedboat, where the motion is smallest; look at the horizon; and eat a light breakfast. If you are pregnant, have back or neck problems or know you get seasick, choose the big boat. Tour boats always return to the island they left from, so there is no option to finish on Koh Phangan. Wildlife and landscape

The islands are limestone, eroded into sheer cliffs, sea caves and overhangs, with forest wherever soil can hold. Park surveys have recorded 16 mammal species, about 50 bird species, 14 reptiles and 5 amphibians, and say more remain undocumented. On a day trip most of what you see is the landscape itself, plus reef fish on the snorkelling stops. Don't feed any animals, and keep food in your bag on the beach.

Underwater, the reefs are shallow and patchy, with hard corals and reef fish. They are good for relaxed snorkelling rather than dramatic diving. The park's main diving season runs from February to October, with the clearest water usually in March and April. For dive trips from Samui, see our Koh Samui diving guide.

A short history of the park

Ang Thong was declared a marine national park in 1980 to protect the archipelago's reefs, islands and forests. Today the Department of National Parks, Wildlife and Plant Conservation manages it from the ranger station on Koh Wua Talab. Day trips from Samui and Koh Phangan have run for decades, and the park's visitor facilities (landing beaches, stairs, the viewpoint trail, bungalows and campsite) are concentrated on Koh Wua Talab and Koh Mae Ko so the other islands stay largely untouched.

  • Seasickness tablets if you need them
  • Your booking confirmation and passport number (for the insurance)
  • For the overnight trips: a torch, insect repellent, long sleeves, a power bank and cash for meals

Mistakes to avoid

  • Booking a date between 1 November and 15 December. The park is closed every year in that window, whatever the weather on the day. Our booking calendar won't offer those dates; be wary of anyone who does.
  • Arriving without cash. The rangers take the park fee in Thai baht only: 300 THB per adult, 150 THB per child. Don't count on finding an ATM in the park.
  • Climbing in flip-flops. The Wua Talab trail is steep rock and roots, with ropes near the top. Wear shoes with grip, or skip the climb.
  • Leaving it to the last day. Online booking for tomorrow closes at midnight Thai time, there is no same-day online booking, and December to February fills up because of park quotas. Book early in your stay, so a weather cancellation can move to another date.
  • Using the wrong sunscreen. Sunscreens with oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-MBC or butylparaben are banned in marine national parks. A rash vest solves it.
  • Expecting to swim in the Emerald Lake. It is a viewing platform only; swim at the beaches and on the snorkelling stop.
  • Not telling us where you stay. Transfers are included from most areas; hotels in Lipa Noi, Nathon, Taling Ngam, Bang Por, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and similar remote areas cost 400 THB per person return, and the right pickup time depends on your exact hotel.
  • Planning to finish on Koh Phangan. Tour boats return to the island they left from. To move on to Phangan, take a ferry the next day.

Children, pregnancy and mobility

Children of any age can join the day tours. Infants aged 0–2 travel free and children aged 3–11 pay the child price. Children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn at all times on the boat. Children who are confident on stairs usually enjoy the Emerald Lake walk. The Wua Talab viewpoint is steep enough that we suggest it only for children who can climb unaided for 30 minutes in the heat.

Pregnancy, back or neck problems and limited mobility are reasons to avoid the speedboat, which can slam in a swell. The big boat is steadier. Both climbs are optional, but you need to step on and off boats from the beach, sometimes in knee-deep water. Wheelchair users can't land at most stops. The basic accident insurance covers ages 3 months to 75. For more family advice, see Koh Samui with kids.
