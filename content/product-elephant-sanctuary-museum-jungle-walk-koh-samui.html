Why go: learning, not just looking

This sanctuary combines elephant care with something no other Samui visit offers: an elephant museum. It tells the story of the island's working elephants, which hauled coconuts and timber long before tourism, and what happened to them afterwards. The museum programme pairs the exhibits with a guided jungle walk to see the herd, plus a short workshop making clay seed bombs for reforestation. The half-day programmes spend more time with the elephants: preparing herbal food balls and fruit, feeding, walking beside the herd into the forest and watching them play and dust-bathe on their own.

The fourth option, the waterfall and wellness day, has no elephants. It is a 25–30 minute rainforest hike to Hin Lad waterfall with a swim, a herbal-ball workshop, lunch and a stop at the Secret Buddha Garden. It is useful if part of your group would rather not do an animal visit.

Who it suits, and who should choose something else

  • Good for: families with children aged 5 and over, travellers who want to understand Samui's history, and anyone who wants an ethical visit with real time near the elephants.
  • Choose something else if: you only have a short slot near Chaweng. The Chaweng Noi sanctuary visit takes 3 hours and includes lunch.
  • Limited mobility: the museum is easy, but the jungle walk and the waterfall hike are not. Tell us when you book.

Good to know

  • Ethics: keep to the guide's distances, don't feed the elephants anything you brought yourself, and don't pressure staff for close-up selfies.
  • Timing: elephants are most active in the cooler morning. Book the 07:45 half day if you can face the early start.
  • Rainy season: October to December paths are muddy. Wear shoes you don't mind getting dirty.
