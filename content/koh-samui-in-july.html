July is a good month for Koh Samui's weather and a busy one for everything else. Expect highs of 32 °C, lows of 25 °C, about 117 mm of rain over 11 days in sunny spells and brief showers, and a 30 °C sea that is calm on most days. Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao trips all run. European school holidays begin, so room rates rise to peak levels and boats should be booked a few days ahead. The Full Moon Party moves to Tuesday 20 July 2027, after Asahna Bucha and Khao Phansa.

Good to know: many websites list the July 2027 Full Moon Party on Sunday 18 July. That is Asahna Bucha, an alcohol-ban day, followed by Khao Phansa on the 19th. The party is on Tuesday 20 July; our shared return speedboat costs 1,400 THB.

The verdict

July suits families on school holidays and anyone who wants reliable boat days in summer. It does not suit a bargain hunter or a last-minute planner.

Good for:

  • A school-holiday beach trip while Phuket and Krabi are in monsoon
  • Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao, best in the morning
  • Calm swimming on the east coast
  • Diving, with whale sharks possible
  • Long evenings: the latest sunsets of the year, about 18:45

Not for:

  • Low prices: rooms run 30–80% above shoulder rates
  • Booking boats on the day: book a few days ahead
  • Calm afternoon crossings: July afternoons are often windy
  • Buying alcohol in shops on 18–19 July 2027

If you can travel in June instead, you get the same weather at lower prices. See Koh Samui weather by month.

Weather and sea in July

July sits in Samui's hot season with short showers, which runs from May to September. The wind blows from the west and south-west, across the Thai mainland, so the island is largely sheltered from the south-west monsoon that soaks the Andaman coast. Expect sunny spells, brief showers and humid afternoons.

On the water, July mornings are smoother than July afternoons. The breeze tends to build after lunch, which brings chop to the west and south-west coasts and can make the Pig Island crossing from Thong Krut bumpier. The east coast (Chaweng, Lamai, Choeng Mon) is sheltered and calm, good for swimming.

The tide matters on the north and west beaches. In July low tide usually falls in the morning, around 09:00, and the flats at Maenam, Bang Por and Lipa Noi are exposed. Walk the flats in the morning and swim in the afternoon. See Koh Samui tides.

Box jellyfish risk is higher in the rainy months (the UK government says May to October nationally), in calm, warm water and in the evening. Swim inside netted areas where they exist and wear a rash vest. For a sting, pour vinegar on it for at least 30 seconds and call 1669. Mosquitoes are worse after rain, so use repellent at dusk. See beach safety.

Boat trips in July

Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao trips all run in July, and ferries run normally. July and August are among the busiest months at Ang Thong, together with December to February, and the Emerald Lake platform gets crowded when several boats land at midday.

Asahna Bucha marks the Buddha's first sermon, and Khao Phansa the start of the three-month rains retreat, when people offer candles and robes at temples. Both fall on Sunday 18 and Monday 19 July in the official 2027 holiday list; one calendar puts them a day later, so check closer to the date. On both days 7-Eleven, supermarkets and unlicensed restaurants can't sell alcohol, but since May 2025 licensed hotels and bars in tourist areas may still serve. Koh Samui with children in July

July is the start of the main family season. On our boat trips children aged 3–11 pay a child rate, infants aged 0–2 travel free, and children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn on the boat. The best first boat day is the Pig Island speedboat tour: 4–5 hours door to door, with the pigs on Koh Madsum and a shallow reef at Koh Tan. Bring about 50 THB per person in cash for the Koh Madsum island fee, and book the morning departure for the calmer sea.

On land, go early to Big Buddha and Wat Plai Laem, where children can feed the fish, before the tour vans and the midday heat. Na Muang 1 waterfall has a swimmable pool 5–10 minutes' walk from the car park. See Koh Samui with kids.

Where to stay in July

  • Book early. July and August are school-holiday months, and rooms and boats fill up.
  • Chaweng and Lamai are calm and sheltered in July. Lamai is the better value.
  • Choeng Mon is the easiest beach for toddlers, 5–10 minutes from the airport.
  • Maenam and Lipa Noi are good for small children at mid tide, but the water is a long walk away at the morning low tide.
  • For sunsets, Lipa Noi on the west coast and Fisherman's Village in Bophut, with its views towards Koh Phangan, are the June–August picks. Sunset is at about 18:45 in mid-July. See sunset spots.
  • Two-bedroom pool villas in Choeng Mon, Maenam and the west coast often cost less than two hotel rooms.

Diving and snorkelling in July

Visibility around Koh Tao and Sail Rock is typically 10–25 m in July. Afternoons are windy, so mornings are smoother for the crossing. Whale sharks are possible: in July 2026 the Koh Tao Tourism Association named June to September as the peak for sightings, but they are never guaranteed. For snorkelling, go in the morning at Ang Thong, Koh Tan and Koh Nang Yuan. See diving and snorkelling.

Prices and booking in July

July and August are peak season, alongside mid-December to February, with room rates 30–80% above shoulder prices. Book flights, rooms and villas early. Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day; the route via Surat Thani by low-cost flight, coach and ferry takes 5–7 hours door to door, and can save a couple 2,000–6,000 THB each way against a peak-date direct flight. Daily costs don't change with the season: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage about 300–500 THB, and a compact car 800–1,200 THB a day, usually cheaper than taxis for a family of four. What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), hats and sunglasses for everyone.
  • Rash vests for children and adults on snorkelling days.
  • A compact umbrella for brief showers.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches and tidal flats.
  • Seasickness tablets for afternoon crossings.
  • A cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered, including for older children).
