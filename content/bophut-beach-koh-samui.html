Bophut Beach is a bay of about 3 km on Koh Samui's north coast, with Fisherman's Village in the middle and Koh Phangan on the horizon. It is calm, sheltered and lovely at dusk, but we don't recommend it for swimming: the water is often cloudy, an outflow is reported near the east end of the village and jellyfish are reported. Use it for a beachfront dinner and stay here for the food; swim at Choeng Mon, 15 minutes east.

The beach

Bophut Bay curves for about 3 km along the north coast, between the low headland towards Maenam in the west and the point before Bang Rak in the east. It faces north across a shallow channel to Koh Phangan, which is close enough to see its hills clearly on a dry day. The sand is narrow, brownish and slightly coarse, with a few pebbles, and much of it is backed by resort gardens, restaurants and the old shophouses of Fisherman's Village.

The village stretch

The central part, in front of Fisherman's Village, is the busiest. In the evening the restaurants set tables on the sand, Coco Tam's lays out its beanbags and fire dancers perform at about 19:15–19:30 and 21:00. By day it is surprisingly empty. The nicer, cleaner-looking part of the beach ends around The Wharf at the west end of the village.

East and west of the village

East of the village the beach is fronted mostly by larger resorts, including Anantara Bophut. West of The Wharf it becomes quieter, more local and more residential, with small resorts and villas, and runs on towards the Maenam side. Neither end has the clear water of the east coast.

Swimming at Bophut: our honest view

Look at Bophut from a table and the water seems perfect: calm, warm (28–30 °C), sheltered and shallow. Some guides describe it as inviting for swimming with a gentle slope. We still don't send guests in, for these reasons:

  • Water quality: the water is often cloudy, and visitors report an outflow near the eastern entrance of the village.
  • Jellyfish: jellyfish are reported here, and box jellyfish, though rare, favour calm, warm, shallow water like this, especially after rain and at dusk.
  • Tides: it is shallow and tidal; at low tide you wade a long way for little depth.
  • Boats: longtails moor and pass close to the shore.

If you do go in, do it in daylight, near high tide, away from any drain or stream mouth, and never alone. If anyone is stung by a jellyfish: get them out, pour vinegar over the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub and don't rinse with fresh water, and call 1669. Most beachfront resorts keep vinegar on hand. More in Koh Samui beach safety.

Tides and seasons

The north coast sits on a broad, shallow shelf, so the tide makes a big difference. At high tide the water comes up close to the restaurants and the strip of sand narrows; at low tide it retreats and the bottom is exposed in places. Check the tide before a beach walk or a paddle.

Weather-wise, Bophut follows the Samui pattern: dry and calm January–September, wettest October–December with the peak in November (about 445 mm of rain on average). Because the bay faces north and is partly sheltered by Koh Phangan, it doesn't get the big surf that hits Chaweng and Lamai in the monsoon, but evenings can be rained out and the Friday market cut short. Smoking ban and beach rules

Ao Bo Phut was one of the 24 Thai beaches where smoking was banned from February 2018 under the Marine and Coastal Resources Management Act. The penalty is a fine of up to 100,000 THB, up to one year in prison, or both. Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal everywhere in Thailand, and smoking cannabis in public is banned with fines up to 25,000 THB. Take litter with you, keep music down on the sand at night, and cover up when you walk back up into the village.

Dusk and the view to Koh Phangan

Bophut faces north, not west, so you don't watch the sun sink into the sea as you do at Lipa Noi or Taling Ngam. What you get is the sky colouring over Koh Phangan, the longtails turning dark against the water and the lights of the village coming on. Get a beachfront table by 17:30 to catch it; sunset falls between about 17:55 in November and 18:45 in July. For a true sea sunset, see Koh Samui sunset spots.

Eating and nightlife on the beach

Bophut's beachfront is lined with restaurants that set tables on the sand at night, from simple Thai seafood places to upscale international dining. Street food on market nights runs from about 60–80 THB for pad thai to 80–120 THB for grilled prawns. The full run-down of the village's restaurants, market nights and prices is on our Fisherman's Village guide, and the Friday Walking Street (about 17:00–23:00) has its own page, Fisherman's Village Walking Street.

Nightlife is relaxed: beach bars, happy hours and fire shows rather than clubs. Chaweng is 15–20 minutes away for later nights; the pool beach clubs of Bang Rak are 10 minutes east. See Koh Samui beach clubs.

Where to stay on Bophut Beach

Bophut suits couples and food lovers who want to walk to dinner and don't mind swimming in a pool. Mid-range rooms cost about 2,500–5,000 THB a night in the shoulder season; peak season adds 30–80 %.

  • Beachfront resorts: Anantara Bophut and NH Collection Samui Peace Resort (reopened under the NH Collection name in January 2025) are the best-known.
  • Boutique hotels: in and behind the shophouses of Fisherman's Village; ask for a room away from the street on Fridays.
  • Quieter stays: small resorts west of The Wharf.
  • Villas: in the Bophut hills behind the bay, with views to Koh Phangan; see Koh Samui villas.

Things to do near Bophut Beach

  • Big Buddha (07:00–18:30, free) and Wat Plai Laem (about 06:00–18:00, free): 15 minutes east.
  • Bang Rak piers: ferries to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, 10 minutes east.
  • Maenam: Chinatown, the Pralarn pier and a quieter beach, 10–15 minutes west.
  • Elephants: Samui Elephant Sanctuary has a no-riding site in Bophut; see elephant sanctuary.
  • Hospitals: Bandon International Hospital is on the ring road near Big C in Bophut.

Getting there and parking

Bophut Beach is reached from the ring road (Route 4169) by several lanes down to the village. Grab covers Bophut well. On Fridays the beach road through the village closes to traffic from about 16:30, so plan to park and walk.
