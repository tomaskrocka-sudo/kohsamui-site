We aim for this website to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA, and we fix reported problems as a priority. Our boat trips are harder: none of our shared trips is step-free, because boarding means stepping across a moving gap from a pier, pontoon or beach, and some stops include steep stairs, such as the 500 m Ang Thong viewpoint trail that takes 25–45 minutes up. A private boat is usually the best option for guests with limited mobility. Message us on WhatsApp +66 63 704 0888 before booking and we'll give you an honest answer for your trip.

Our accessibility commitment

Everyone should be able to plan a trip to Koh Samui, read our guides and book a tour, whatever device, browser or assistive technology they use. This statement covers this website. The boat-trip sections below cover the trips we sell, because a booking is only accessible if the trip itself works for you.

What we have done on this website

  • Colour contrast meets WCAG AA in light and dark mode, including darker text where our lime accent colour is used on light backgrounds.
  • Keyboard use: every menu, button, form and the booking date picker can be used with a keyboard, with visible focus outlines.
  • Screen readers: pages use real headings in order, labelled form fields, menus that announce whether they are open, and descriptive alt text on images.
  • Tables, not pictures of tables: prices, timetables and fees are HTML text, so they can be read aloud, zoomed and translated.
  • Tap targets are at least 44 px on phones, and the site works when zoomed to 200%.
  • Moving content: the news ticker can be paused.
  • Language: pages declare British English so screen readers pronounce them correctly.
  • Light and dark themes: choose the one that is easier for you with the theme switch in the header.

Known limitations

  • Maps: interactive maps are hard to use with a screen reader. Pages with a map also name the places in the text, and each place page has a Google Maps directions link.
  • Third-party services: the card payment window, WhatsApp, Google Maps and the cookie banner are provided by other companies. We choose accessible options, but we don't control their code.
  • Automatic translation: translated pages are machine translations and may read awkwardly. Prices, times and policies in English are the official version.
  • Photos: while we add photos across the site, a few older images may have short alt text. Tell us and we'll fix it.
  • Live widgets: weather, sea and tide readings update automatically; each has a text value for screen readers, and we're testing how often updates are announced.

Report a problem

If something on this site doesn't work for you, tell us the page link, what you were trying to do and the device, browser or assistive technology you use. Message us on WhatsApp +66 63 704 0888 (daily 08:00–20:00 Thailand time) or use the email address on our Contact page. If you can't complete a booking online, we'll take it on WhatsApp or by email at the same price, with the same terms and cancellation policy.

Boat trips: what to expect

Thailand's piers and islands were not built for step-free access, and we would rather tell you that now than on the pier. These points apply to most of our trips:

  • Boarding: you step down from a pier or across from a floating pontoon onto a boat that moves with the swell. The crew holds the boat and gives you a hand, but there are no ramps or lifts.
  • Landings: some stops land on a beach, which can mean stepping off the bow into shallow water and walking up the sand.
  • Speedboat motion: speedboats bump hard in waves. Guests with back, neck or heart conditions, recent surgery or a pregnancy should not ride a speedboat; ask your doctor and ask us about calmer options.
  • Heat: shade on deck is limited and daytime highs are 29–33 °C all year. Bring water, a hat and sun protection.
  • Swimming: snorkelling is always optional. Life jackets are on every boat, and children's life jackets must be worn on board.
  • Season: the sea is calmest from February to April. From October to December the north-east monsoon makes the east coast rough, and calm-water trips such as Pig Island from Thong Krut on the south-west coast are the easier choice. Check live sea conditions before you go.

Wheelchair users

None of our shared boat trips is wheelchair accessible, and we won't pretend otherwise. If you can transfer from your wheelchair with help and manage a step down into a boat, a private boat from a pier with a fixed walkway is the most realistic option. Talk to us before booking so we can check the pier, the boat and how much help the crew can give.

Guests with sight, hearing or other needs

  • Safety briefings are given out loud in English. If you are deaf or hard of hearing, tell us when you book and we'll send the key safety points in writing.
  • Blind and partially sighted guests are welcome with a companion who can guide on deck and on landings.
  • Dietary and medical needs: tell us when you book; where lunch is included, we pass your needs to the crew.
  • Older travellers and young children: the Koh Samui with kids guide and our safety guide help you choose.
