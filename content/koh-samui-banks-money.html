Most Thai ATMs charge foreign cards 220–250 THB per withdrawal (AEON ATMs inside Big C and Lotus's charge 150 THB), with limits of 20,000–30,000 THB per withdrawal. Always decline the "conversion" offer (dynamic currency conversion) and choose to be charged in THB. Cards work in malls, 7-Eleven, supermarkets and most hotels, but carry cash for street food, songthaews, many taxis, markets and the cash-only island fees on boat trips.

Last checked: November 2026. ATM fees and limits are set by each Thai bank and change from time to time; your own bank may add its own fee and exchange margin on top.

Thai QR payments (PromptPay) are everywhere, even at food stalls, but they need a Thai bank account. Contactless Visa and Mastercard work at card terminals, not at PromptPay QR codes.

ATM fees and limits

Every Thai bank charges a flat fee for foreign cards, on top of anything your own bank charges. The fee is the same whether you take out 1,000 or 30,000 THB, so fewer, larger withdrawals cost less.

  • Where: ATMs are everywhere in Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut and Maenam, and at Central Samui, Big C and Lotus's. They are scarcer on the west and south coasts (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Thong Krut, Bang Kao), so withdraw before you go.
  • Safer machines: use ATMs inside bank branches or malls. The UK government notes ATM skimming incidents in Thailand.
  • Don't walk away early: wait until you have both your cash and your card back; forgotten cards are a common holiday mistake. If a machine swallows your card, contact that bank's branch the next working day with your passport, and block the card in your banking app meanwhile.
  • Your own bank: cards from fee-free travel banks or multi-currency accounts avoid your bank's charges, but not the Thai ATM fee.

Bank opening hours on Samui

High-street branches in Nathon, Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut and Maenam generally open Monday to Friday, roughly 08:30–15:30, and close on weekends and Thai public holidays. Branches inside Central Samui, Big C and Lotus's keep shopping-mall hours, usually late morning to early evening, seven days a week, which makes them the easiest place to exchange money or sort out a problem with a card. Hours vary by branch, so check the bank's app or the sign on the door.

How much cash to carry

For a typical beach day on Samui, 1,000–2,000 THB per person in cash covers street food, drinks, a songthaew or two and a massage. Indicative 2026 prices at the Fisherman's Village market: pad thai 60–80 THB, mango sticky rice 50–80 THB, grilled prawns 80–120 THB. Add the island fees on boat days, and keep a separate 1,000 THB note somewhere safe for emergencies.

Travel cards and phone wallets

Multi-currency travel cards and fee-free debit cards work well on Samui: they give a fair exchange rate at card terminals, though Thai ATMs still add their own 150–250 THB fee. Apple Pay and Google Pay work at contactless terminals in malls, 7-Eleven and many restaurants. They don't work with PromptPay QR codes, which need a Thai banking app.

Dynamic currency conversion: always decline it

When an ATM or card machine offers to charge you in your home currency "at a guaranteed rate", that is dynamic currency conversion (DCC). The rate is set by the machine's bank and is usually several percent worse than your own bank's or card network's rate. Always choose:

  • "Without conversion", "Decline" or "THB" at the ATM.
  • "Pay in THB" or "Thai baht" on card terminals in shops, hotels and restaurants.

Some staff press the home-currency option for you. Check the screen or the slip before you enter your PIN; if the amount shows in euros, pounds or dollars, ask them to cancel and redo it in baht. Our scams guide covers other card tricks.

Exchanging money

  • Bank exchange booths: every major Thai bank runs exchange counters along Chaweng Beach Road, in Lamai and at Central Samui. Rates are posted on boards outside. Bring your passport; banks need it for every exchange.
  • Private money changers: licensed exchange shops sometimes offer better rates than bank booths. Look for the licence displayed, compare two or three boards and count your money at the counter.
  • Bring clean notes: US dollars, euros, pounds and Australian dollars are easy to change. Torn, marked or old-series notes get poor rates or are refused. Large denominations (USD 100, EUR 50) usually get better rates than small ones.
  • At the airport: exchange rates at airports are usually poorer. Change only what you need for the first day, or use an ATM.

Carrying some cash in your home currency as a back-up is sensible in case your card is blocked or lost.

Service charge, VAT and tipping

Hotel and restaurant prices shown with "++" add a 10% service charge and 7% VAT on top. Tipping is not expected at street stalls, but rounding up or leaving 20–100 THB for good service in restaurants, massage shops and on boat trips is appreciated.

Opening a Thai bank account

Long-stayers want a Thai account to pay rent and bills, use PromptPay QR payments, avoid ATM fees, and (for retirement and marriage extensions) to show funds in a Thai bank. Tourists on a 30-day visa exemption are usually refused.

  • Requirements vary by bank and by branch. Most ask for your passport with a long-stay visa or extension (such as Non-O, Non-B with a work permit, or a DTV), proof of your Samui address (rental contract, TM.30 receipt or a residence certificate from immigration), and a Thai phone number. Some ask for a letter from your employer or embassy.
  • Checks have tightened since 2024 as banks fight scams and "mule" accounts. Expect questions about why you need the account and where your money comes from.
  • Where: the big banks all have branches in Nathon, Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut and Maenam, plus mall branches with longer hours at Central Samui, Big C and Lotus's.
  • Go in person, ideally with a Thai-speaking friend or your landlord, and ask the branch what it needs before you queue.

Transferring money

  • International transfers into a Thai account are received in foreign currency and converted by the bank. Online transfer services often give better rates than a high-street bank.
  • Buying a condo: to register a condo in a foreigner's name, the purchase money must come into Thailand from abroad in foreign currency. Ask the receiving bank for the Foreign Exchange Transaction form (FET) or credit advice for every transfer and keep it for the Land Office. Talk to a lawyer before you send money. See buying property on Koh Samui.
  • Tax: Thailand taxes residents (180+ days in a calendar year) on foreign income brought into the country. Get advice if you live on Samui for long periods.

You may also be asked at immigration to show at least 10,000 THB per person (20,000 per family) in cash or equivalent; see our Thailand visa guide.

Money safety tips

  • Split cash and cards between your wallet, bag and the hotel safe.
  • Turn on transaction alerts and know how to freeze your card in your banking app.
  • Take a photo of your cards' customer service numbers.
  • Don't leave phones and wallets on the sand while you swim.
  • If a card is stolen, report it to your bank at once and to the Tourist Police (1155) for an insurance report.
