Silver Beach, also called Crystal Bay, is a small cove framed by granite boulders on Samui's east coast, 4.6 km north of Lamai and 8 km south of Chaweng. It has soft pale sand, clear water, good swimming and easy snorkelling around the rocks at both ends. It is free, but the sand fills up by about 10:00, so come early; expect waves and red-flag days from October to December.

Map and directions

Silver Beach sits in a bend of the ring road where it climbs over the headlands between Chaweng Noi and Lamai. The turning is easy to miss because there is no public sign: look for the signs for the two beach resorts, Crystal Bay Beach Resort and Silver Beach Resort, and walk down from there. Non-guests are allowed through to the sand.

The beach

Silver Beach is short, a few hundred metres from boulder to boulder, and enclosed by smooth granite rocks at each end. The sand is fine, pale and soft underfoot, and it shelves gently into clear water that turns turquoise on sunny mornings. Coconut palms and the two resorts' trees give some shade at the back. It is one of the most photographed small beaches on the island, and on a calm morning it is easy to see why.

It is a public beach, but the land behind it belongs to resorts and restaurants. You walk through their grounds to reach the sand; be considerate, and buy a drink or lunch if you use their sunbeds.

Swimming and snorkelling

From February to September the water is usually calm, clear and good for swimming. The entry is gentler than central Lamai, which makes it easier for families with confident children. Underwater rocks are scattered across the bay, some of them sharp, especially towards the middle, so swim with care at low tide and consider water shoes.

Where to snorkel

  • North and south boulders: the best snorkelling is along the rocks at both ends, in shallow water. Expect small reef fish around the boulders rather than a coral garden.
  • Low tide: the water near the rocks gets very shallow; swim out a little further and watch your knees and fins on the rocks.
  • Gear: bring your own mask and snorkel. Rentals at the beach are limited and not always available.

For real reefs, Koh Tan off the south-west coast is the best shallow snorkelling near Samui, and our Pig Island speedboat stops there. All snorkel spots are compared on Koh Samui snorkelling.

Swimming safety at Silver Beach

  • October–December: the north-east monsoon brings waves straight into the cove. On 19 February 2026, during a cold-air surge, red flags were raised along Chaweng and Lamai with 1–2 m waves; the same swell reaches Silver Beach. Do not swim when the flag is red or the shore break is dumping.
  • Rocks: do not climb the boulders when waves are breaking over them. A visitor was swept off the rocks at the nearby Lad Koh viewpoint in November 2024.
  • Box jellyfish: rare but dangerous in Samui waters, with risk higher in the monsoon months and in calm, hot weather after rain. Avoid swimming at dusk; if someone is stung, pour vinegar on the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it, and call 1669.
  • Sun: the cove is a sun trap after 11:00. Bring water and reef-safe sunscreen.

Crowds and timing

Silver Beach is small, so a handful of tour vans changes everything. The pattern is the same most days: near-empty at sunrise, filling from about 10:00, busiest from late morning to mid-afternoon, then emptying again after 16:00.

Access and parking

The beach is reached on foot from the ring road through the resort grounds; the walk takes 2–3 minutes down a slope with some steps, so it is not ideal for wheelchairs or heavy prams. There is no public car park. Small private lots near the entrance charged about 20 THB for a scooter and 80 THB for a car in late 2025. A few free spaces exist along the main road, but the road is narrow and curving here, so park well off it and never on a bend.

If you are coming from Lamai or Chaweng, a scooter is the easiest option. Grab drops off at the resort entrances; for the return, book before you leave the beach because coverage on this stretch is patchier than in town. Songthaews pass on the ring road in daylight and can be flagged down, but they run no fixed timetable.

Facilities

  • Beachfront restaurants and bars (resort-run), open in the daytime
  • Sunbeds and umbrellas, about 100 THB
  • Massage huts on the sand
  • Customer toilets and showers at the cafés if you buy something
  • No public toilets or showers
  • No lifeguard tower
  • No ATM at the beach; the nearest are in Lamai
  • Very limited snorkel rental

What to bring and a morning plan

  • Mask and snorkel (rentals are limited), water shoes for the rocky edges, reef-safe sunscreen and a hat.
  • Cash for parking, sunbeds (about 100 THB) and drinks; there is no ATM at the beach.
  • A light bag: the path down from the road is short but has steps.
  1. 06:30 Sunrise at the Lad Koh viewpoint, 3 km north.
  2. 07:30 Swim and snorkel at Silver Beach while it is empty.
  3. 09:30 Coffee at a beachfront café as the first visitors arrive.
  4. 10:30 Move on to Coral Cove or Lamai before the cove fills up.

Where to stay near Silver Beach

Only a few properties front the cove itself: mainly Crystal Bay Beach Resort and Silver Beach Resort, both mid-range bungalow resorts, plus pool villas and boutique hotels on the hillside above the ring road with sea views. Prices are in line with mid-range rooms on the rest of the east coast (roughly 1,500–4,500 THB a night in season), with pool villas costing more. Staying here means a quiet base with a great morning swim, but you need a scooter or taxis for dinner in Lamai (5–10 minutes) or Chaweng (10–15 minutes). Most visitors base themselves in Lamai or Chaweng and come here for a morning. See where to stay on Koh Samui and Koh Samui villas.

Eating and drinking

The resort restaurants on the sand serve Thai and Western dishes, smoothies and cold drinks at resort prices, noticeably higher than in Lamai town. There is no street food at the cove. For cheaper meals and an evening out, Lamai's night market runs daily from about 17:00 on the beach road, 10 minutes south; Chaweng's restaurants and bars are 10–15 minutes north. There is no nightlife at Silver Beach itself, and the beach is unlit after dark. See Lamai nightlife and Koh Samui restaurants.

Nearby sights

  • Coral Cove, 2 km north: the easiest shore snorkelling on Samui, with kayaks at about 300 THB an hour.
  • Lad Koh viewpoint, 3 km north: a free ring-road pull-off with a view over Chaweng Noi; stay off the rocks in waves.
  • Chaweng Noi, 4 km north: a quiet cove next to Chaweng.
  • Lamai, 4.6 km south: night market, bars and a long swimming beach.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai, 7 km south: the famous rock formations at the end of Lamai.

Combine them in one morning: Lad Koh at sunrise, a swim at Silver Beach before 10:00, snorkelling at Coral Cove, lunch in Lamai. More beaches on Koh Samui beaches.
