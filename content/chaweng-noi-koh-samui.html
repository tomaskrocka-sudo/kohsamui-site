Chaweng Noi ("little Chaweng") is the small cove just south of Chaweng's rocky point, 3–4 km from central Chaweng and about 15–20 minutes from the airport. It is quieter than Chaweng and mostly resort frontage, with coarser sand, a little snorkelling off the rocks and occasional sea lice. The water is good for swimming from January to September; expect surf and red flags from October to December.

The beach, swimming and snorkelling

Chaweng Noi is a short, curved bay of a few hundred metres, held between the rocky headland at the south end of Chaweng and the hills that run towards Coral Cove. The sand is coarser and slightly more golden than Chaweng's powder, and the beach is narrower. Because most of the frontage belongs to resorts, there are no bar strips and no jet-ski ranks; you hear the sea rather than music.

The water is clear and warm (28–30 °C). The bottom slopes gently in the centre and becomes rocky at both ends, which is where the snorkelling is. Do not expect a reef: you will see small fish, sea urchins and some coral around the boulders, best on a flat morning at mid to high tide. Bring your own mask and wear water shoes on the rocks. Better shore snorkelling is a few minutes south at Coral Cove and Silver Beach, and the real reefs are offshore; see Koh Samui snorkelling.

Sea lice explained

Every so often swimmers at Chaweng Noi come out with an itchy, red rash in the shape of their swimwear. These are "sea lice": not lice at all, but the tiny larvae of jellyfish and sea anemones, which get trapped under fabric and sting. They are harmless but annoying for a day or two.

  • Shower with fresh water and take off wet swimwear as soon as you leave the sea.
  • Wash the swimwear before wearing it again; larvae can stay in the fabric.
  • An antihistamine tablet or hydrocortisone cream from any pharmacy eases the itch.
  • If you see others itching, swim at Chaweng North instead that day.

Season and safety

Chaweng Noi faces the open Gulf like Chaweng, so it takes the same north-east monsoon surf from October to December and during winter wind surges. On 19 February 2026, 1–2 m waves closed swimming along the whole east coast. Never swim on a red-flag day. Box jellyfish are rare but recorded in Samui waters; avoid swimming at dusk and at night, and treat a sting with vinegar for at least 30 seconds, without rubbing or fresh water, then call 1669. Getting onto the beach

All Thai beaches are public up to the high-water line, so you may use Chaweng Noi even if you are not staying here. Getting onto the sand is the awkward part: resorts and villas take up most of the road frontage. The usual ways in are the lanes beside the beach restaurants off the ring road, or through a resort restaurant where you buy lunch or a drink. Public access paths change with construction, so check on the day. [check]

Be polite: resort sunbeds and towels are for guests unless the staff offer them, and gardens are private. You can always walk south along the sand from Chaweng at low tide and round the point when the sea is calm; don't attempt it in surf.

Where to stay in Chaweng Noi

Accommodation here falls into two groups. On the sand, a handful of mid-range and upper-mid-range resorts own almost all of the beachfront. On the hillside behind and above the ring road, there are private pool villas and small villa developments with sea views.

  • Beachfront resorts: roughly in line with Chaweng's mid-range (2,000–4,500 THB a night in the shoulder season), more for sea-view rooms. Peak season adds 30–80 %.
  • Hillside villas: good value for groups and families who want a private pool; prices vary widely with size and view. Browse Koh Samui villas.
  • The catch: the hillside roads are very steep, narrow and slippery when wet. If you are not an experienced rider, don't rent a scooter here; use Grab or a car with a confident driver.

Families with small children usually do better at Choeng Mon, where the water is calmer and the beach is easy to walk onto. Compare areas in where to stay on Koh Samui.

Eating and nightlife

Chaweng Noi itself has a few beach restaurants and resort dining rooms, nothing more. That is its appeal. For variety, Chaweng's restaurants, markets and nightlife are 5–10 minutes away by car or Grab (about 80–120 THB). The Jungle Club, a hillside restaurant and bar above Chaweng Noi, is known for its view; it runs a truck up the steep road for about 50 THB, and it is worth booking a few days ahead in high season. For the night markets and bars, see Koh Samui restaurants and Chaweng nightlife.

Things to do near Chaweng Noi

  • Lad Koh viewpoint (9.4990, 100.0600): a free pull-off on the ring road just south of the cove, with a view back over Chaweng Noi and Chaweng. Look from the platform; stay off the rocks when waves are up. In November 2024 a tourist was swept off them by a wave.
  • Coral Cove (9.4906, 100.0613): a tiny rocky bay for snorkelling, kayaks about 300 THB an hour; wear water shoes on the coarse sand.
  • Silver Beach (9.4778, 100.0663): a boulder cove with pale sand and clear water; arrive before 10:00.
  • Samui Elephant Sanctuary: its Chaweng Noi site is listed by welfare organisations and runs no riding and no bathing; half-day visits cost about 3,000 THB. See Koh Samui elephant sanctuary.
  • Vikasa: the clifftop yoga retreat between Chaweng Noi and Coral Cove. See Koh Samui yoga.
  • Lamai, Hin Ta and Hin Yai: 15 minutes further south. See Lamai.

Getting there and parking

Chaweng Noi sits on the ring road (Route 4169) as it climbs south out of Chaweng. Bangkok Hospital Samui is on the same road just before the cove, which makes a handy landmark. Grab works well here; songthaews pass along the ring road during the day (about 30–60 THB to Chaweng or Lamai). Parking is limited to the roadside and beach-restaurant lots, so a scooter is easier than a car.
