Coral Cove is a small rocky cove right on the ring road between Chaweng Noi and Lamai, 6.5 km from both, and the easiest place on Samui to snorkel straight from the beach. Entry is free, kayaks cost about 300 THB an hour, and the beach facilities are listed as open 08:00–20:30. The sand is coarse and crystalline, so wear water shoes, and be realistic about the reef: a January 2026 visitor report described dead coral and litter washed in by storms.

Map and directions

Coral Cove sits directly below the ring road where it winds over the headlands south of Chaweng Noi. Driving south from Chaweng, you pass the Lad Koh viewpoint first, then Coral Cove, then Silver Beach before dropping into Lamai. The beach is a short walk down from the road.

The cove

Coral Cove is a small bay hemmed in by granite boulders, with coconut palms for shade at the back. The "sand" is really fine gravel: white, coarse and crystalline, pretty to look at and uncomfortable to walk on barefoot. It suits a snorkel and a sit in the shade more than long walks or sandcastles. There is a beach bar and rentals on the sand and a resort on the slope above.

Snorkelling at Coral Cove

Coral Cove is popular because you can swim straight from the beach to the rocks with no boat. On a calm morning the visibility is good, and the boulders on both sides of the bay are the places to look.

  • Where: follow the rocks on the left and right of the bay. The centre is mostly sand and rubble.
  • What you'll see: small reef fish around the boulders, plus hard-coral patches in varying condition. Don't expect the colour of Koh Tao or Ang Thong.
  • Depth: shallow near the rocks, so watch for coral and sea urchins at low tide and never stand on the reef.
  • Gear: snorkel sets are rented on the beach, but stock is limited; bring your own mask if you have one.
  • When: February–September before about 10:00, when the water is flat and the visitors haven't stirred up the sand.

For the best snorkelling in reach of Samui, go by boat: Koh Tan near Pig Island has better shallow reefs, and Ang Thong and Koh Tao are further but clearer. Compare all options on Koh Samui snorkelling.

Kayaking

Kayaks are rented on the beach for about 300 THB an hour. In calm weather a short paddle along the rocks to the north brings you towards the Lad Koh headland, and south towards Silver Beach. Stay close to shore, wear the life jacket provided, and don't go out on days with an onshore wind: the waves bounce off the boulders and make the cove choppy. Longer paddles and stand-up boarding are covered on kayak and SUP on Koh Samui.

Reef condition: an honest note

Coral Cove's name promises more than it delivers today. A visitor review dated 6 January 2026 reported dead coral, while friends of the same reviewer had seen live coral there the previous May; storms and warm-water bleaching events can change a shallow reef within months. The same report described litter washed into the bay by currents and not cleared, and very dirty toilets. Other recent visitors were happy with the clear water and shade. Our advice: treat Coral Cove as an easy, free swim among rocks with some fish, not as a reef trip, and help by taking your rubbish with you and not touching or standing on coral.

Swimming and safety

  • Monsoon swell: October–December is the roughest period on this coast. On 19 February 2026 red flags went up along Chaweng and Lamai with 1–2 m waves during a cold-air surge, and the swell reaches Coral Cove too. On those days, don't snorkel.
  • Rocks: waves can push you onto the boulders. Keep a few metres off the rocks when there is any swell, and stay off them on foot when waves are breaking. A visitor was swept from the rocks at nearby Lad Koh in November 2024.
  • Sea urchins and coral cuts: water shoes protect your feet; clean any coral cut with fresh water and antiseptic.
  • Box jellyfish: uncommon but dangerous in Samui waters, with risk higher in the monsoon months and in calm, hot weather after rain. If stung, pour vinegar for at least 30 seconds, don't rub, and call 1669.

Facilities

  • Rental of sunbeds, towels, snorkel sets and kayaks (kayak about 300 THB per hour)
  • Beach bar with drinks and simple food
  • Toilets (reported as poorly kept in 2026)
  • Shade from coconut palms
  • No lifeguard
  • No ATM; bring cash
  • No public showers; rinse at the bar if offered

Access and parking

Coral Cove has no formal car park. Scooters and a few cars use pull-ins along the ring road near the entrance; this is a narrow, curving stretch with fast traffic, so park fully off the tarmac and walk carefully on the road edge. A scooter is the easiest way to come. Grab can drop you off, but book the return before you finish, as coverage between Chaweng Noi and Lamai is patchier than in town. Songthaews pass in daylight and can be flagged down on the ring road.

What to bring to Coral Cove

  • Water shoes or reef sandals: the most useful item for this beach.
  • Your own mask and snorkel, as rental stock is limited.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen and a rash vest; you will float face-down in strong sun.
  • Cash for the kayak (about 300 THB an hour), drinks and sunbeds; there is no ATM.
  • A bag for your rubbish, since litter washed in by storms is not always cleared.
  • A dry bag if you plan to kayak with a phone.

Where to stay near Coral Cove

The headlands around Coral Cove are lined with hillside resorts and pool villas with sea views, a few minutes from both Chaweng and Lamai by scooter. Rooms in this stretch are mostly mid-range to upscale; expect east-coast mid-range prices of roughly 1,500–4,500 THB a night in season and much more for private pool villas. The roads are steep, so a scooter or car is close to essential, and you will drive to dinner. The Vikasa yoga retreat sits on the cliffs just south of the cove. See where to stay on Koh Samui, Koh Samui villas and yoga on Koh Samui.

Eating and nightlife

Food at the cove is limited to the beach bar's simple menu, and reviews of it are mixed. Better options are 10–15 minutes away: Chaweng Noi's resort restaurants and Chaweng's full restaurant scene to the north, or Lamai's night market (daily from about 17:00 on the beach road) to the south. The Jungle Club, a hillside restaurant above Chaweng Noi known for its sunset view, is close by. There is no nightlife at Coral Cove; head to Chaweng nightlife or Lamai nightlife.

Nearby sights

  • Lad Koh viewpoint, 1 km north: free, open 24 hours, best at sunrise.
  • Silver Beach (Crystal Bay), 2 km south: soft sand and clear water.
  • Chaweng Noi, 2.5 km north: a quiet cove with snorkelling off the rocks.
  • Lamai, 6.4 km south: night market, bars and a long beach.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai, about 9 km south.
