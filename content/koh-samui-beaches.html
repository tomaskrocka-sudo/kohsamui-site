For swimming on Koh Samui, choose Chaweng North, central Lamai, Choeng Mon or Silver Beach. For toddlers, choose Choeng Mon, or Lipa Noi at high tide. For sunsets, go to the west coast at Lipa Noi, Bang Por or Taling Ngam. The sea is calm January–September and calmest February–April; October–December brings surf, rip currents and red flags to Chaweng and Lamai, so in those months swim on the sheltered north-east bays or stay out of the water on flag days.

Koh Samui has 18 beaches worth naming, spread around a 51 km ring road, plus two piers (Nathon and Thong Krut) that people often mistake for beaches. They are not alike. The east coast has the long white-sand swimming beaches and the sunrises. The north coast is calm, shallow and tidal, and it is where you eat on the sand. The west coast is quiet, very shallow at low tide and the place for sunsets. The south is local, rocky in places and empty. This page compares all of them, honestly, including the ones we would not swim at. We are a local Australian-Thai team, on the islands since 2006, and we update this page every month. [kp_updated].

Good to know: November is Samui's wettest month. It averages about 445 mm of rain and the east coast often flies red flags. Ang Thong National Marine Park is closed 1 November–15 December and reopens on 16 December 2026. Pig Island trips run all year when the sea allows.

How to choose a Samui beach

The short rule: the east coast is for swimming and sunrise; the north coast is calm, shallow, tidal and best for dinner; the west coast is for sunsets and tidal flats; the south is quiet and local. Then match the beach to your trip.

First-timers: Chaweng

Chaweng has the island's best-known fine white sand, the most hotels, restaurants and bars, and it is 10–15 minutes from the airport. Stay at the north end if you want to swim; stay central if you want to walk to the nightlife. See our Chaweng guide and Chaweng or Lamai.

Families with small children: Choeng Mon and Lipa Noi

Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay with a gentle slope, 5–10 minutes from the airport. Lipa Noi on the west coast is very shallow with no coral, calm for much of the year, and best for toddlers at high tide. Read more in Koh Samui with kids.

Couples: Chaweng Noi, Silver Beach and Bophut for dinner

Chaweng Noi is a quiet cove a few minutes south of Chaweng. Silver Beach is a small boulder-framed cove that fills by about 10:00. For an evening, nothing beats dinner on the sand at Fisherman's Village in Bophut.

Strong swimmers: Chaweng North and central Lamai

These are the two longest stretches of open water with sand underfoot. Central Lamai shelves quickly, so you are out of your depth sooner than at Chaweng. Both are dangerous on red-flag days.

Snorkellers: Coral Cove, Silver Beach and Laem Yai

Shore snorkelling on Samui is modest. Coral Cove, Silver Beach, the rocks at Chaweng Noi and Laem Yai on the north-west tip are the best options. The good reefs are offshore at Koh Tan and Koh Nang Yuan; see Koh Samui snorkelling.

Sunset: Taling Ngam, Lipa Noi and Bang Por

The sun sets on the west and north-west coasts. Taling Ngam has the Five Islands on the horizon; Bang Por adds seafood restaurants on the sand. More in Koh Samui sunset spots.

Budget and long stays: Maenam

Maenam has the cheapest beachfront rooms on the island (mid-range about 1,000–2,500 THB a night), a long quiet beach and the Lomprayah ferry pier for Koh Phangan and Koh Tao.

East coast: Chaweng, Chaweng Noi, Silver Beach, Coral Cove and Lamai

The east coast faces the open Gulf. That gives it the cleanest water and the best sand on Samui, and it also means it takes the full force of the north-east monsoon. All five beaches below can fly red flags from October to December and during winter wind surges, like the one on 19 February 2026 when waves of 1–2 m closed the water island-wide.

Chaweng

Best for: first-timers, nightlife, families who want restaurants nearby · Sand: fine, white, gentle slope · Swimming: good, best at the north end · Facilities: everything · Parking: hard on Beach Road · Sun: sunrise.

Chaweng is Samui's busiest beach, a long arc of white sand on the east coast; figures of 5–7 km are quoted for its length depending on where you start counting. North Chaweng is the quietest part and the best for swimming: the sand shelves gently and you can wade a long way out on calm days. Central Chaweng is where the action is, with ARKbar, the beach clubs and nightly fire shows, and Soi Green Mango a few minutes' walk inland. The south end runs down to the rocky point that separates it from Chaweng Noi. Jet skis and parasailing operate off central Chaweng; agree the price and check the craft for damage before you ride. Beach Road is congested from late afternoon, so walk or use Grab. Full details are in our Chaweng area guide and Chaweng nightlife.

Chaweng Noi

Best for: couples, quiet · Sand: coarser than Chaweng · Swimming: good in season; snorkelling off the rocks · Facilities: resort bars · Parking: limited · Sun: sunrise.

Chaweng Noi ("little Chaweng") is the cove just south of Chaweng's rocky point. It is mostly resort frontage, so it is calm in atmosphere even when Chaweng is packed, and it is three minutes by car from Chaweng's restaurants. There is a bit of snorkelling off the rocks at either end. Occasionally swimmers get sea lice, tiny larvae that cause an itchy rash under swimwear; rinse off and change out of wet clothes quickly. The Lad Koh viewpoint is just south on the ring road; stay off the rocks there when waves are up.

Silver Beach (Crystal Bay)

Best for: couples, snorkelling, photos · Sand: pale and soft · Swimming: good swim and snorkel · Facilities: a beach restaurant and a few loungers · Parking: small and roadside; the turn-off is easy to miss · Sun: sunrise.

Silver Beach is a small cove framed by granite boulders between Chaweng Noi and Lamai. The water is clear on calm days and there are fish around the rocks. It is small, so it fills by about 10:00 in high season; come early or late afternoon. In the monsoon the waves break hard here and the rocks make it a poor place to be in the water.

Coral Cove

Best for: snorkellers · Sand: coarse and crystalline; wear water shoes · Swimming: snorkelling rather than lengths · Facilities: kayak hire about 300 THB an hour · Parking: roadside · Sun: sunrise.

Coral Cove is a tiny rocky bay just off the ring road, south of Lad Koh. It is the most-visited shore snorkelling spot on Samui, but be realistic: a January 2026 visitor report described dead coral and litter. Go in the morning when the water is flat, bring your own mask, and do not stand on coral.

Lamai

Best for: value, couples, swimming · Sand: fine, white · Swimming: the centre is best but shelves steeply; the north end is shallow and stony · Facilities: plenty · Parking: street parking · Sun: sunrise.

Lamai is Samui's second beach town: about 4 km of sand on the south-east coast, 25–30 minutes from the airport. It is cheaper and calmer than Chaweng. In the centre the bottom drops quickly, which suits swimmers but means children are out of their depth fast. The north end is shallow with rocks, so wear reef shoes. The Hin Ta and Hin Yai rocks are at the south end. Lamai is as exposed as Chaweng in the monsoon. Read Lamai and Hin Ta and Hin Yai.

North-east: Choeng Mon, Tongson Bay and Plai Laem

The north-east tip is a string of small bays between the airport and the sea. They are sheltered by headlands, close to the airport and quieter after dark.

Choeng Mon

Best for: families with small children, honeymooners · Sand: soft and white, rocky at the west end · Swimming: among the calmest on Samui; best in the centre · Facilities: beach restaurants, loungers (about 200 THB for non-guests at some resorts) · Parking: public entrance opposite the Café Amazon by Tops Daily · Sun: sunrise.

Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay about 1 km long on Samui's north-east tip, 5–10 minutes from the airport. The slope is gentle and the water is usually flat, which is why most families end up here. At low tide you can wade across a sandbar to the islets of Koh Fan Noi and Koh Fan Yai off the south-eastern end; wear water shoes for the rocks and walk back before the tide turns. Shade is limited, so bring a hat or take a lounger.

Tongson Bay

Best for: a quiet half day · Swimming: clear and shallow December–April, rougher May–October · Facilities: one or two restaurants · Parking: limited, at the end of a sand road · Sun: sunrise side.

Tongson (Thongson) Bay is a small, white-sand bay between Choeng Mon and the Plai Laem headland. The northern part is better for swimming; the rocky southern part is better for snorkelling. It is rarely busy. The last stretch in is a sand track, so take it slowly on a scooter. See Tongson Bay.

Plai Laem

Plai Laem is the headland around Wat Plai Laem, the colourful lake temple with the 18-armed Guanyin. Most of its shoreline is resort frontage and the water is shallow at low tide. Visit for the temple (about 06:00–18:00, free) rather than the beach. See Plai Laem and Wat Plai Laem.

North coast: Bang Rak, Bophut, Maenam and Ban Tai

The north coast faces Koh Phangan across a shallow channel. The water is calm most of the year, but it is shallow and tidal, often a little murky, and two of these beaches are not good for swimming at all. Come for the food, the ferries and the views.

Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach)

Best for: short stays near the airport and piers, seafood on the sand · Swimming: not recommended (muddy shallows, murky water in April–May, glass reported in the sand) · Facilities: restaurants, beach clubs · Sun: dusk over the bay.

Bang Rak is the bay between Bophut and Big Buddha, right next to the airport; planes fly low overhead. It is the island's ferry hub: the Lomprayah pier, the Seatran pier, Petcherat Marina and the Big Buddha pier for the Haad Rin Queen are all here, and they are all loosely called "Bangrak pier". The beach clubs (Chi Samui, Tembo, Malika, 79 Beach Club) have pools, which is the point. See Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach).

Bophut

Best for: dinner, couples · Sand: narrow, browner, a few pebbles · Swimming: not recommended (an outflow is reported near the east end of the village, the water is often cloudy and jellyfish are reported) · Sun: dusk views across to Koh Phangan.

Bophut's beach runs about 3 km along the north coast, with Fisherman's Village in the middle. The water looks calm and some guides call it fine for a dip, but we tell guests to swim at Choeng Mon or Chaweng and come here for the evening. Bophut is one of the 24 Thai beaches where smoking on the sand has been banned since 2018. See Bophut Beach and Fisherman's Village.

Maenam

Best for: budget, long stays, quiet families · Sand: golden, coarse in places · Swimming: calm in the dry season; the bottom drops to chest depth about 5 m out · Facilities: beach bars and restaurants · Parking: easy · Sun: not a sunset beach.

Maenam is a long, quiet, palm-lined beach on the north coast, 20–30 minutes from the airport. It is the cheapest place on Samui to sleep on the sand. High tide narrows the beach to a strip in places. Watch small children: it is shallow at the edge, then deepens quickly.

Ban Tai (north coast)

Ban Tai is a quiet stretch of about 1 km between Maenam and Bang Por, on the eastern part of the same bay. It is shallow and calm, with few facilities, and good for small children at high tide. Do not confuse it with Baan Tai on Koh Phangan, the Half Moon Festival village.

North-west and west: Bang Por, Laem Yai, Lipa Noi and Nathon

The west side of Samui faces the mainland and Ang Thong. It is sheltered from the north-east monsoon, which makes it the calmest coast from October to December, but it is very tidal: at low tide the water can retreat 50 m or more over sand, mud and stones.

Bang Por

Best for: seafood and sunsets · Swimming: high tide only · Sun: sunset.

Bang Por (also written Bang Po) is a quiet bay of about 5 km on the north-west coast, popular with long-stay expats. Sources disagree on the beach itself: some call it soft sand that is fine for children, others call it rocky and not for swimming. Both are true at different tides. At low tide the water pulls back over a flat of sand, mud and stones where locals collect shellfish; at high tide it is a narrow strip with calm water. The seafood restaurants along the ring road are the reason to come. See Bang Por and Koh Samui seafood.

Laem Yai

Best for: snorkelling, sunset · Swimming: poor at low tide; clear water around the rocks · Sun: sunset.

Laem Yai is the headland at Samui's north-west tip, home to the Four Seasons resort where White Lotus season 3 was filmed. The shoreline is very quiet and partly private resort frontage, with rocks and pebbles rather than sand. Treat it as a snorkelling and sunset spot, ideally seen from a boat. See White Lotus filming locations on Koh Samui.

Lipa Noi

Best for: families with toddlers, sunsets · Swimming: very shallow and calm; no coral · Sun: sunset.

Lipa Noi is a quiet, local beach on the west coast, calm for much of the year. It is so shallow that toddlers can paddle safely at high tide; at low tide it becomes wide flats. The Raja car ferry from Donsak lands nearby, and Nikki Beach is on this stretch. It is 40–50 minutes from the airport and has few restaurants.

Nathon

Nathon is Samui's old capital and main west-coast ferry port. It has a town waterfront and a pier, not a swimming beach. Walk the pier at sunset (free) and eat at the daily night market (about 16:00–22:00). See Nathon.

South-west and south: Taling Ngam, Thong Krut, Bang Kao and Laem Set

The south is the least developed part of Samui: fishing villages, temples, wellness resorts and long empty beaches that are shallow and tidal.

Taling Ngam

Taling Ngam is a long, quiet village beach with fishing boats on the sand and luxury resorts on the cliffs above. The water is shallow and tidal. It has the best sunset on Samui, with the Five Islands (Koh Ha) silhouetted offshore, and Samui's MICHELIN Bib Gourmand 2026, Baan Suan Lung Khai, which needs a reservation.

Thong Krut

Thong Krut is the pier village where boats leave for Pig Island (Koh Madsum) and Koh Tan, about 15–30 minutes away. It is not a swimming beach. See Thong Krut and Pig Island.

Bang Kao

Bang Kao is a quiet local beach on the south coast with few facilities. Swim at high tide only; it is very shallow with pieces of coral underfoot, so wear water shoes. Litter has been reported. The gold-tiled Laem Sor Pagoda (07:00–18:00, free) is at the western end. See Bang Kao and Laem Sor Pagoda.

Laem Set

Laem Set is a rocky headland on the south-east coast between Hua Thanon and Bang Kao, known for wellness resorts such as Kamalaya. It is a place to stay quietly, not a beach to drive to for a swim. See Koh Samui wellness resorts.

Beaches you reach by boat

Samui's best sand and coral are on the islands around it. These are the ones you can reach on a day trip.

  • Ang Thong National Marine Park (Koh Mae Ko for the Emerald Lake, Koh Wua Talab for the viewpoint and the white beach by the park office): 42 limestone islands about 30 km west. Park fee 300 THB adult, 150 THB child, cash. Closed 1 November–15 December; the season reopens on 16 December 2026. Our Ang Thong speedboat tour costs 1,800 THB (child 1,400) and the big boat tour 1,500 THB. See Ang Thong Marine Park.
  • Koh Madsum (Pig Island) and Koh Tan: off Thong Krut in the south-west, running all year. Koh Madsum charges about 50 THB a person to land; Koh Tan has the best shallow-reef snorkelling near Samui. Our Pig Island speedboat tour costs 1,450 THB (child 1,250). Please don't chase the pigs or feed them junk food.
  • Koh Nang Yuan: the three islets joined by sandbars next to Koh Tao. Landing fee 250 THB, and plastic bottles, fins and water shoes are not allowed on the island. Our Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip costs 2,200 THB (child 1,600).
  • Your own route: a private speedboat from 19,000 THB a boat can take you to Koh Som, Tongson Bay, Pig Island or Koh Phangan's beaches.

Tides: why the north and west beaches empty at low tide

Samui's north and west coasts sit on a wide, shallow shelf. When the tide goes out, the water at Maenam, Bophut, Bang Rak, Bang Por, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam and Bang Kao can retreat tens of metres, leaving sand, mud and stones. The east coast drops off faster, so Chaweng and Lamai are swimmable at any state of the tide. The practical rule: on the north and west coasts, plan your swim around high tide and check the time before you go.

Swim within a couple of hours either side of high tide on the tidal beaches. [check]

Beach safety

Most beach days on Samui are uneventful. The people who get hurt usually ignored a flag, swam alone at dusk or climbed onto wave-washed rocks. These are the real risks.

Red flags and rip currents

Chaweng and Lamai are the danger beaches from October to December and during winter wind surges. On 19 February 2026, a cold air mass and strong easterly winds pushed 1–2 m waves onto the east coast, and swimming was banned along the whole shoreline. Drownings on Samui have followed ignored red flags. A red flag means no swimming, full stop. If a rip pulls you out, do not swim against it: float, raise an arm, and swim parallel to the beach until you are out of the current. In November 2024 a tourist was swept off the rocks at the Lad Koh viewpoint by a single wave; stay well back from rocks in big surf.

Box jellyfish

Box jellyfish are rare but real here: of the 10 recorded deaths in Thailand since 2002, 9 were in the waters off Samui and Koh Phangan. Risk is higher in calm, warm water and after rain, and jellyfish come closer to shore at dusk and at night. In August 2025 officials warned of sightings around both islands and told swimmers to use netted swimming areas where they exist. Many hotels have vinegar stations and multilingual signs.

  • Get the person out of the water and call 1669 (ambulance).
  • Pour vinegar (household 4–6 % acetic acid) over the sting for at least 30 seconds.
  • Do not rub the sting, and do not rinse it with fresh water.
  • Go to hospital even if the pain eases; serious reactions can follow.

Sea lice

Sea lice are tiny jellyfish and anemone larvae that cause an itchy, red rash, mostly under swimwear. They turn up occasionally at Chaweng Noi and other east-coast coves. Shower and change out of wet swimwear soon after swimming; an antihistamine or hydrocortisone cream from a pharmacy helps.

Jet skis, theft and the sun

Jet-ski "damage" scams still happen: photograph the craft before you ride, agree the price and the time in writing, and leave a copy of your passport, never the passport itself. Read Koh Samui jet ski and Koh Samui scams. Don't leave phones and wallets on sunbeds while you swim. The UV index is extreme around midday all year, so cover up between 11:00 and 15:00.

Emergency numbers

Ambulance 1669 · Tourist Police 1155 · Police 191 · Fire 199. The nearest large hospitals to the beaches are in Chaweng and Bophut; see Koh Samui hospitals and Koh Samui beach safety.

Beach rules and etiquette

  • Beaches are public. Thai beaches are public land up to the high-water line. Resorts can control their sunbeds and gardens, not the sand.
  • Smoking. Since February 2018, smoking is banned on 24 Thai beaches, including Bophut, with fines of up to 100,000 THB or up to a year in prison. Many resorts ban smoking on their beach frontage too. Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal everywhere in Thailand.
  • Cannabis. Buying it now requires a prescription, and smoking in public is banned, with fines of up to 25,000 THB. Never smoke it on a beach.
  • Dress. Topless or nude sunbathing is not accepted in Thailand and public indecency can be fined. Cover up when you leave the beach for shops, cafés and temples.
  • Drones. Thai rules require drones to be registered with the NBTC and the CAAT before you fly, and many resorts ban them over their grounds. The airport zone around Bang Rak and Chaweng is a no-fly area without permission. [check]
  • Litter. Take everything home, including cigarette ends and bottle caps. Don't take coral or shells from the islands.

Facilities and costs

  • Entry: every beach on Samui is free to use.
  • Sunbeds: on the busy beaches, loungers and umbrellas belong to the resorts and beach restaurants. Some let non-guests rent them (about 200 THB at Choeng Mon) or use them if you buy food and drinks.
  • Beach massage: a one-hour Thai massage costs about 300–500 THB island-wide and 400–600 THB in Chaweng. See Koh Samui spa.
  • Toilets and showers: use the beach restaurants; buy a drink. There are few public toilets.
  • Parking: no paid public beach car parks. Chaweng and Fisherman's Village are the hard ones; elsewhere, park on the roadside lane down to the sand. Never block a resort gate.
  • Kayaks: about 300 THB an hour at Coral Cove and similar on the busy beaches. See kayak and SUP and water sports.
  • Cash: beach vendors and small restaurants take cash. Foreign-card ATM withdrawals cost 220–250 THB each.
