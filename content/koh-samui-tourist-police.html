Call 1155, the national Tourist Police hotline, for theft, scams, disputes or any emergency where you need an English speaker; it answers 24 hours a day. The Tourist Police can take a police report for your insurance, help you through a lost or stolen passport, and mediate jet-ski, scooter-rental and taxi disputes. For a crime in progress or violence, call 191; for an ambulance, call 1669.

Last checked: November 2026. The national numbers on this page are confirmed by the Thai government. The local Samui office number and address come from local directories, so if you need to visit in person, call 1155 first and ask where to go.

What the Tourist Police do, and don't do

The Tourist Police are a division of the Royal Thai Police set up to help foreign visitors. Officers speak English, and some speak other languages. In many Thai resort areas, trained foreign volunteers also assist the Tourist Police with translation. On Samui, the network includes volunteers from countries such as the UK, Russia, France, China, Canada, Austria, Finland and Israel, who met senior Tourist Police Bureau officers on the island in August 2026 to expand their work against crime targeting tourists.

  • Take reports of theft, loss, fraud and assault, and give you a written report for insurance.
  • Help with lost or stolen passports, including the police report your embassy needs.
  • Mediate disputes with rental shops, jet-ski operators, taxi drivers, hotels and tour sellers.
  • Translate between you and the local police, hospital or business.
  • Give safety advice and help in emergencies until other services arrive.
  • They are not a court: they can't force a business to refund you, though mediation often works.
  • They don't replace the regular police for serious crimes; cases are passed to the local police station.
  • They can't issue passports or visas; that is your embassy and Thai immigration.
  • They won't help you avoid a legitimate fine (for example riding without a helmet).

When you call, say where you are (hotel name, beach, nearest 7-Eleven or landmark), what happened and whether anyone is hurt. Keep your phone charged and nearby: the operator may call you back or send a local officer. If you don't have a Thai SIM, ask your hotel, a shop or a bar to call for you.

Office location and hours

The Tourist Police on Koh Samui work from an office on the island and patrol the main tourist areas, especially Chaweng, Lamai and Bophut, with extra presence during big events and high season. The hotline 1155 runs 24 hours; desk hours at the local office are not published.

Getting a police report for insurance

Insurers almost always need a police report for theft or loss claims, and they usually want it made within 24 hours of the incident.

What to bring

  • Your passport (or a copy and your entry stamp if the passport was stolen).
  • Details of what happened: date, time, place, and a list of items with approximate values in THB.
  • Any evidence: photos, videos, receipts, rental contracts, screenshots of messages, the number plate of a vehicle.
  • Your Samui address and a phone number where officers can reach you.
  • A Thai-speaking friend, if you have one; it speeds things up.

Tips

  • Ask for the report in English, or ask for an English summary attached to the Thai report.
  • Check names, dates and item lists before you sign.
  • Ask for a copy for yourself and take photos of every page.
  • For a lost phone, include the IMEI number (it's on the box or in your phone account online).
  • Report card theft to your bank first, then to the police.

After a theft: the first hour

  1. Block stolen cards in your banking apps and call your banks' 24-hour lines.
  2. Use "find my phone" to locate, lock or wipe a stolen phone, and change your email and banking passwords from another device.
  3. Ask your mobile operator to block your SIM so it can't be used for verification codes.
  4. Check nearby shops, bars and hotels for CCTV and ask them to keep the footage; the police can request it.
  5. Call 1155 and arrange to make your report the same day.

Bag snatching from passing scooters happens occasionally on Samui's busier roads. Carry bags on the side away from the traffic, and don't put your phone in a scooter's front basket.

Lost or stolen passport, step by step

  1. Day 1 Report the loss to the Tourist Police (1155) or the local police and get a written police report. Search the place you lost it and ask your hotel; passports are often handed in.
  2. Day 1 Contact your embassy in Bangkok. Most countries let you apply online or by appointment for an emergency travel document or a new passport. Have digital copies of your passport photo page and entry stamp ready.
  3. Days 2–7 Attend your embassy appointment in Bangkok if required. Samui has direct flights to Bangkok, about 1 hour 5 minutes. Some countries have honorary consuls in the south who can help with paperwork.
  4. Before you fly Take the new document and the police report to Koh Samui Immigration in Maenam, or to immigration at your departure airport, so your entry record and permitted stay can be transferred to the new document.
  5. Departure Allow extra time at airport immigration and carry the police report with you.

Your permitted stay doesn't pause while you wait for a new passport. If it runs out, talk to Koh Samui Immigration (+66 77 423 440) before the date. See our Koh Samui immigration office guide and Thailand visa guide.

Disputes: jet skis, scooters, taxis

Most disputes visitors bring to the Tourist Police on Samui involve damage claims after a jet-ski or scooter rental, or taxi fares.

Evidence first

  • Film the jet ski or scooter from every angle, with the operator in shot, before you ride, and again when you return.
  • Keep the rental contract and your deposit receipt, and photograph the shop front and the owner's phone number.
  • For taxis, agree the price before you get in and photograph the number plate.

Don't hand over your passport

Never leave your passport as a deposit with a rental business; the UK government warns that businesses holding passports use them to force payment for claimed damage. Leave a cash deposit with a receipt instead.

If a dispute starts

  1. Stay calm, don't argue in a crowd, and don't pay under pressure.
  2. Call 1155 and ask an officer to attend or to speak to the operator by phone.
  3. Show your videos and contract. Officers will usually broker a settlement.
  4. If you do pay, get a written receipt with the business name, and report it afterwards.

Assault, sexual assault and serious crime

If you or a friend are attacked or sexually assaulted:

  1. Get to a safe place, with people you trust.
  2. Call 191 (police) or 1155 (Tourist Police, English). For injuries, call 1669 or go to the nearest hospital emergency department.
  3. If you can, go to hospital before showering or changing clothes, so evidence can be collected. Hospitals can also give emergency contraception and medication to prevent HIV, which works best within 72 hours.
  4. Contact your embassy in Bangkok. Consular staff can help you deal with the police, find an interpreter and contact family.
  5. Tell your travel insurer; most policies cover medical costs and changes to travel plans.

You decide whether to make a formal complaint. The Tourist Police can explain the process and stay with you while you speak to the investigating officers.

People posing as police

Very occasionally, people pretend to be police to demand on-the-spot "fines", for example from tourists caught vaping. Real officers wear uniform or show official ID and issue receipts for fines. If in doubt, stay polite, ask for ID and a receipt, and call 1155 to check.

Staying safe on Samui

Koh Samui is generally safe for visitors. The real risks are scooter crashes, the sea (rip currents from October to December and box jellyfish) and alcohol-related incidents on party nights, more than crime. Keep valuables in the hotel safe, don't leave phones on the sand when you swim, and watch your drinks in bars. Read our Koh Samui safety guide and beach safety guide for more.
