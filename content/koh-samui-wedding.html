Hold a Koh Samui wedding between February and April, when the sea is calmest and rain is least likely; avoid October to December, the rainy season. Most couples have a symbolic ceremony here on a beach, in a pool villa, at a resort or on a private boat, and either register the marriage at home or do the legal part in Thailand, which takes an embassy affirmation, a certified Thai translation, legalisation by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Bangkok and registration at a district office. The best months for a beach wedding

Samui's rain follows the north-east monsoon, so it falls mostly from October to December, the opposite of Phuket's wet season. For an outdoor ceremony, the dry months give you a far better chance of the sunset you planned.

Beach weddings

Choose the coast by the light you want. East-coast beaches (Chaweng, Chaweng Noi, Choeng Mon, Lamai) face sunrise and have the best sand, so ceremonies there work in the late afternoon with soft light rather than a sun-into-sea sunset. The west and south-west (Taling Ngam, Lipa Noi) face the sunset directly, but the beaches are tidal: check the tide table for your date, because low tide can expose wide sand flats behind your arch. On the north coast (Bophut, Maenam), the sky glows over Koh Phangan at dusk. Beaches in Thailand are public, so a ceremony on a busy beach will have passers-by; resort frontages and small coves are quieter.

Villa weddings

A large pool villa lets your guests stay together and gives you a private garden or terrace for the ceremony and party. Low-season rates for a 6–10-bedroom villa run from about 30,000 to 120,000 THB a night for the whole house. Ask the owner in writing whether events are allowed, the maximum guest number, the latest time for music, and whether a security deposit or event fee applies. Check the access road for a catering van and guests in heels. Browse Koh Samui villas.

Resort weddings

Most large resorts on Samui sell wedding packages with a set-up, a celebrant for a symbolic ceremony, a cake and dinner, and rooms for guests. That is the simplest route from overseas. Read the package for what is fixed (the venue's own florist and photographer, corkage, music curfews) and what counts as extra. Resorts we're often asked about are covered in our honeymoon guide.

Symbolic or legal marriage in Thailand?

This is the part most couples misunderstand. In Thailand a marriage is legal only when it is registered at a district office (amphoe in the provinces, khet in Bangkok). A ceremony on a beach, in a villa or on a boat, even with a celebrant and a certificate, is symbolic. Many couples marry legally at home before or after the trip and hold the celebration on Samui. Others do both here.

How foreigners register a marriage in Thailand

  1. Affirmation of freedom to marry. Each foreign partner gets a document from their own embassy or consulate in Thailand (or, for some countries, from the authorities at home) confirming they are free to marry. Book embassy appointments well ahead.
  2. Thai translation. The affirmation and passport details are translated into Thai by a certified translator.
  3. Legalisation. The translated documents are legalised by the Legalization Division of the Department of Consular Affairs, Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in Bangkok. See the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for current rules and opening hours.
  4. Registration. Both partners register the marriage in person at any district office in Thailand with their passports and the legalised documents, plus an interpreter if needed. The officer issues a Thai marriage certificate the same day.
  5. Recognition at home. Get the certificate translated and, if your country requires it, legalised, so it can be recorded at home.

Because the embassy and legalisation steps happen in Bangkok, most couples spend a few working days there before flying to Samui. Allow 3–5 working days in Bangkok for the embassy and legalisation steps [check]. On Samui, the district office for the island is the Ko Samui District Office in Nathon [check]. Requirements differ by nationality, so check your own embassy's current instructions before you book flights.

Same-sex couples

Thailand's Marriage Equality Act took effect on 23 January 2025, so same-sex couples, including foreigners, can register a marriage at a district office using the same process. Check how your home country recognises a Thai same-sex marriage before relying on it for legal purposes.

Age and other conditions

Both partners must be at least 18 to marry without court or parental consent [check]. If either partner has been married before, bring the divorce or death certificate, as embassies usually ask for it when issuing the affirmation.

A Thai Buddhist blessing

Many couples add a traditional Buddhist blessing, either as the whole ceremony or in the morning before an evening celebration. It is not a legal marriage, but it is a moving, very Thai start to the day.

  • Monks' blessing: usually in the morning. Monks are invited (traditionally an odd number, often nine), the couple offers food and requisites, and the monks chant blessings. Monks must eat before noon, so the blessing is early.
  • Water-pouring ceremony (rod nam sang): the couple sit with hands pressed together over flower-filled bowls while elders and guests pour lustral water over their hands from a conch shell and offer good wishes.
  • Sacred thread (sai sin): a white cotton thread links the couple's heads to symbolise their union.

Etiquette: women must not touch monks or hand anything to them directly; dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) for the blessing, and sit with your feet pointing away from the monks. A temple, a resort or a villa can host it. Read more about local customs in our temples guide.

Guest logistics

Flights and ferries

Most guests fly to Samui via Bangkok: about 20–25 flights a day from Suvarnabhumi, taking about 1 hour 10 minutes, with one-way fares roughly 2,800–6,800 THB including a checked bag. There are also direct flights from Singapore and Hong Kong. Budget-minded guests can fly to Surat Thani on a low-cost airline and take a coach and ferry (5–7 hours in total, about 1,500–3,000 THB). Full routes are in how to get to Koh Samui.

Entry rules

Since 15 September 2026, most nationalities get 30 days visa-free. Every arriving guest must complete the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card at tdac.immigration.go.th within 3 days before landing; warn guests about paid look-alike websites. See our Thailand visa guide.

Where guests stay and how they get around

Book a block of rooms near the venue, or a large villa for family. Taxis on Samui don't use meters and are expensive for groups, so plan shared transport on the wedding day: a private van takes up to 10 guests. Ask guests not to ride scooters to and from the party.

Things for guests to do

Give guests a list of easy boat days around your dates: the Pig Island speedboat runs every day of the year, and Ang Thong runs from 16 December to 31 October. All trips are on our Koh Samui tours page.

Weather plan B

  • Agree a covered fallback (a sala, a marquee or an indoor room) in your contract, and the time by which you'll decide to move.
  • Showers outside October–December usually pass in an hour; a flexible start time often saves the outdoor ceremony.
  • For boat weddings, the captain makes the safety call on the day; agree a back-up date or a dockside ceremony option in advance.
  • Have umbrellas and fans for guests: heat is a bigger problem than rain from March to May.

Photographers, drones and permissions

Samui has many experienced wedding photographers and videographers, and hiring locally saves flights and excess baggage. Foreign photographers working in Thailand need the right permission to work [check]. Drones need care: Thailand requires camera drones to be registered with the aviation and telecoms authorities before flying [check]. Samui's airport sits between Bophut, Bang Rak and Chaweng, and flying near it is prohibited, which rules out much of the north-east. Ask your photographer to show their drone registration. For a ceremony on a public beach, ask your planner or venue whether the municipality needs notice; resorts handle this for their own grounds.

How far ahead to book

For February–April and the Christmas and New Year period, book the venue first, as soon as you have a date, then accommodation for guests, then the boat and suppliers. Boats can take only one charter at a time, so a sunset slot on a specific boat is often the scarcest item. In the low season (May–September), a few months' notice is usually enough. Leave at least a month for embassy appointments if you plan to marry legally in Thailand.
