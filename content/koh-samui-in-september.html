September is one of the best-value months on Koh Samui. It averages about 118 mm of rain over 11 rain days, with highs of 32 °C, lows of 25 °C and a 29 °C sea, and the sea is good for boat trips on most days. The school-holiday crowds have gone, room rates fall to shoulder-season levels, and Ang Thong is open. It is the last reliable month before the north-east monsoon arrives in October. The Full Moon Party falls on Wednesday 15 September 2027.

Good to know: September is a good month for Ang Thong. The Ang Thong big boat tour costs 1,500 THB and the speedboat tour 1,800 THB, both plus the 300 THB park fee in cash. The Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) runs every day, weather permitting.

The verdict

September suits couples, divers and anyone who wants summer weather without summer prices. It does not suit travellers who want a busy nightlife scene or the fullest waterfalls.

Good for:

  • Shoulder-season room rates after the school holidays
  • Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao, with the sea good on most days
  • Swimming on Chaweng and Lamai, which are calm from January to September
  • Diving in the Gulf, while the Andaman coast has its wettest month
  • Quieter beaches, roads and restaurants
  • The Full Moon Party on Wednesday 15 September 2027

Not for:

  • Nightlife at full strength: some venues cut their hours
  • Guaranteed dry afternoons: showers come on about 11 days
  • Midday swims on the north and west coasts, which are shallow at low tide
  • Waterfalls at full flow (they run fullest from October to January)

If your dates are flexible between September and October, choose September: it has well under half of October's rain and a calmer sea for boat days. See Koh Samui in October and Koh Samui weather by month.

What September weather looks like

The south-west wind still blows across the mainland in September, and the Thai peninsula keeps the worst of the Andaman monsoon away from Samui. The result is warm weather with mixed sun and showers: rain falls on about 11 days, and the sea stays at 29 °C. Most showers are short and clear within an hour or two. Phuket and Krabi, on the other coast, are at their wettest this month.

Highs of 32 °C and high humidity make midday heavy going. Plan temples, viewpoints and walks for the morning, spend 12:00–15:00 in the shade or the sea, and drink more water than you think you need.

Tides: on the north and west coasts (Maenam, Bang Por, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam), low tides still fall in daylight until the end of September. At low tide you may walk 50 m or more before the water is deep enough to swim, so plan swims there for the late afternoon or evening, around high tide. Check Koh Samui tides.

Box jellyfish: the risk is higher in the rainy months (the UK government says May to October across Thailand), after heavy rain, in calm, warm water and in the evening. Swim inside netted areas where they exist and wear a full-length rash vest. For a sting, pour vinegar over it for at least 30 seconds and call 1669. See beach safety.

The date, about Thursday 30 September 2027, is calculated from the lunar calendar, so confirm it closer to the time. Dress for a temple visit, with shoulders and knees covered; see Koh Samui temples.

What not to look for: the old September "Samui Festival" of sport, music and food has not been held since 2019. September falls inside Buddhist Lent (19 July to about 15 October 2027), but none of the 2027 alcohol-ban days falls in September. Where to stay in September

  • Chaweng and Lamai are still among the best swimming beaches on Samui, and September is their last calm month before the monsoon. Chaweng has the most restaurants; Lamai is the best value on the east coast.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay 5–10 minutes from the airport, good for families and honeymooners, and quieter once the school holidays end.
  • Bophut and Fisherman's Village suit couples who want dinners and the Friday market. From June to September the Friday market is noticeably less crowded and evenings are usually dry. The beach is not for swimming.
  • The west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) has the sunsets and the villas, 40–50 minutes from the airport. Swim at high tide.
  • Maenam has the cheapest beachfront rooms, about 1,000–2,500 THB mid-range, and many monthly rentals.

Prices and quiet-season closures

September is a shoulder month, with March–April and May–June. Room rates drop back from the July–August peak, when they run 30–80% higher. In the shoulder season a mid-range double costs about 2,000–4,500 THB in Chaweng, 1,500–3,500 THB in Lamai and 2,500–5,000 THB in Bophut. Flights are easier too: 2–4 weeks ahead is usually enough.

Daily costs do not change: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB (400–600 THB in Chaweng), a scooter 150–350 THB a day. The catch is that September and October are the quietest months. Some bars cut their hours, some cafés close for a break, and some restaurants shorten their hours, so call ahead before driving across the island. Getting here and around in September

Direct flights from Bangkok Suvarnabhumi take about 1h05–1h15, with roughly 20–25 a day. The budget route is a flight to Surat Thani and a ferry; see how to get to Koh Samui. Ferries to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao run normal timetables. If you are island-hopping on a long trip, aim to finish the boat legs before the rougher October to December crossings.

Grab costs about 80–120 THB for a 5 km hop, and songthaews run along the ring road by day for 30–80 THB. If you hire a scooter, carry an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles and wear a helmet (fines up to 2,000 THB). See getting around Koh Samui.

Diving and snorkelling in September

September is a good diving month. The Gulf season runs from February to October, and in July 2026 the Koh Tao Tourism Association named June to September as the peak for whale shark sightings at sites such as Sail Rock and Chumphon Pinnacle. Treat a whale shark as a bonus, not a promise. If diving is the priority and you are choosing between coasts, the Gulf is the better choice from February to September. Snorkellers do best offshore: Koh Tan on the Pig Island tour, Ang Thong, and Koh Nang Yuan by Koh Tao (250 THB landing fee). See diving and snorkelling.

What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes and a cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered).
  • A compact umbrella for afternoon showers.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin: dengue is present all year and peaks in rainy periods.
  • A full-length rash vest for snorkelling, against sunburn and jellyfish.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches and low-tide flats.
  • Seasickness tablets for boat days.
