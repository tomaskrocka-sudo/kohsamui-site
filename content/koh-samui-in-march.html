March is one of the best months to visit Koh Samui, and often the best value of the dry season. Rain falls on only about 5 days (about 116 mm in all), highs reach 30 °C, lows 25 °C, and the sea warms to 29 °C. The sea is calm, every boat trip runs, and visibility for snorkelling and diving improves towards April. Hotel prices start to ease after the February peak. The Full Moon Party falls on Monday 22 March 2027.

Good to know: March is one of the best months for Ang Thong (1,800 THB + 300 THB park fee in cash) and the Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB + 250 THB Nang Yuan fee). If you are coming for Songkran (13–15 April 2027), book rooms now.

The verdict

March suits beach holidays, boat days, divers and anyone who wants dry-season weather without February's prices. It suits waterfall lovers least, and the heat builds through the month.

Good for:

  • Calm sea and long sunny days
  • Island hopping: Ang Thong, Koh Tao and Pig Island
  • Diving and snorkelling, with visibility improving towards April
  • Kitesurfing in the steady trade wind
  • Hiking and viewpoints on dry trails
  • Lower room rates than in February

Not for:

  • Waterfalls, which run low until the rains return
  • Travellers who struggle in heat: highs reach 30 °C, rising to 32 °C in April
  • Festivals: March is a quiet month for events

If you can only move by a few weeks, February is a little drier and April a little calmer and hotter. See Koh Samui weather by month.

Sea and boat trips in March

March is the start of Samui's calmest stretch: February to May has the calmest sea, with typical daily highest waves of 0.3–0.5 m. The wind comes mainly from the south-east, east-coast waves average about 0.5 m and the west and south about 0.4 m, and ferries run normally. Boat trips rate excellent, and the long crossings to Koh Tao and Ang Thong are usually smooth. Afternoon thunderstorms are more typical of May and October, but captains still make the final call each morning. Our sea conditions page shows live wave height.

Diving, snorkelling and other outdoor plans

March and April usually give the clearest water of the year in the Gulf. Sail Rock, with its schools of fish and the Chimney, is the most photogenic dive site within reach of Samui, and its visibility is usually best in these two months. For snorkellers, the good reefs are offshore: Koh Tan on the Pig Island trip, Ang Thong and Koh Nang Yuan by Koh Tao. Samui's own coastline is mostly sand and shallow rocky coves. See diving and snorkelling.

  • Kitesurfing: February and March are the peak months, with a steady east to south-east trade wind over the lagoon at Ban Harn and Hua Thanon. See kitesurfing.
  • Hiking: December to March has the driest trails and lower humidity than April and May. Start early and carry plenty of water. See hiking.
  • Waterfalls: low in March; a storm can refresh them for a day or two. See waterfalls.

Heat, sun and sea safety

Samui's heat is steady rather than extreme, but it catches people out: nights stay warm and the humidity is high all year. March is warming up towards April and May, the hottest and hardest months for walking and sightseeing. Plan temples, viewpoints and waterfalls for the morning, and spend 12:00–15:00 in the shade or the sea.

The sun is strong at 9.5° north. Use a high-factor, reef-safe sunscreen, reapply after swimming and wear a rash vest when snorkelling. Warm, calm water after rain is when jellyfish are most likely near the shore: on a sting, pour vinegar over it for at least 30 seconds and call 1669. Even in calm months, swim where you can stand, never after drinking, and leave the water at the first thunder. More in our beach and swimming safety guide.

Where to stay in March

  • Anywhere works: from February to April the sea is calm on every coast, so choose your area for its style and budget.
  • Chaweng and Lamai have good swimming; Lamai is the better value, at about 1,500–3,500 THB a night mid-range.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay with some of the calmest water on Samui, our first choice for families with small children.
  • Tongson Bay is small, clear and shallow from December to April.
  • The west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) has the sunsets and villas; swim at high tide, as the water is very shallow.
  • Hua Thanon and Ban Harn on the south coast are minutes from the kitesurfing lagoon.

Prices in March

March is shoulder season. Peak rates, 30–80% above shoulder-season prices, run from mid-December to February, and hotel prices start to ease in March, which makes it one of the best months for good weather at lower rates. Mid-range doubles cost about 1,000–2,500 THB in Maenam, 1,500–3,500 THB in Lamai and 2,000–4,500 THB in Chaweng in the shoulder season. Everyday costs don't change: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB, a scooter 150–350 THB a day. See is Koh Samui expensive?

Getting here in March

Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day. Its own Samui–Singapore flights are paused until 28 March 2027, so Scoot is the only nonstop airline from Singapore for most of the month. January to April is good weather on both of Thailand's coasts, so March is a good time to combine Samui with Phuket or Krabi; Bangkok Airways flies Phuket–Samui about 7–8 times a day. What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses: the UV index is high all day.
  • A rash vest for snorkelling trips; on boat days the breeze hides the burn until evening.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes and a cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered).
  • Water shoes for stony beaches such as north Lamai, Bang Por and Coral Cove.
  • Plenty of water: drink more than you think you need; bottled water is sold at every 7-Eleven.
  • A light layer for air-conditioned buses, boats and the airport.
