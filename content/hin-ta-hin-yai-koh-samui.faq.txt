Is Hin Ta Hin Yai free? | Yes, entry to the Grandfather and Grandmother Rocks is free. You pay only for parking in the lots by the stall lane, about 20 THB for a scooter and 30–40 THB for a car.
What are the Hin Ta Hin Yai opening hours? | Roughly 08:30–18:30 Sunday to Friday and 08:00–19:00 on Saturday, set by the stalls and car parks. Holiday hours can differ; the best light and fewest people are before 10:00 or after 16:00.
What is the legend of Hin Ta and Hin Yai? | In a widely told version, an elderly couple sailing to Prachuap Khiri Khan to ask for a bride for their son were wrecked by a storm in Lamai Bay; their bodies washed ashore and became the two rocks. It is folklore with several versions.
Is Hin Ta Hin Yai suitable for children? | Yes, with supervision. The stall lane is flat and children enjoy the coconut ice cream, but the final section is uneven rock with gaps and no railings, and it is not suitable for pushchairs or wheelchairs.
How do I get to Hin Ta Hin Yai from Chaweng? | Drive or take a taxi or Grab south through Lamai towards Hua Thanon, about 13 km and 20–30 minutes, and follow the signs to the car parks at the south end of Lamai Bay.
Is Hin Ta Hin Yai worth visiting? | As a 20–45 minute stop combined with Lamai, the Red Temple or Overlap Stone, yes: it is free, quirky and the stall lane is good for kalamae. It is not worth a special trip across the island on its own.
Is it safe to walk on the rocks at Hin Ta Hin Yai? | On calm days, yes, if you wear shoes with grip and stay off the wet, dark rock. From October to December waves can wash over the lower rocks; stay on the dry upper rocks and never turn your back on the sea.
What can I buy at Hin Ta Hin Yai? | The lane is known for kalamae, Samui's sticky sweet made from glutinous rice flour, palm sugar and coconut cream, plus coconut ice cream in the shell, fresh coconuts, dried fruit and souvenirs. Bring small notes; few stalls take cards.
