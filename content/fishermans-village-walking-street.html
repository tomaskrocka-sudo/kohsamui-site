Fisherman's Village Walking Street fills Bophut's old beach road every Friday from about 17:00 to 23:00, with smaller, quieter markets on Monday and Wednesday evenings. It is Samui's best-known night market: street food from about 40 THB a dish, handicrafts, clothes and live music between The Wharf and Coco Tam's beach bar. Arrive by 17:30–18:00 to park and eat before the crowds peak around 19:00–20:30.

Stalls go up and come down with the weather, so hours are approximate. Friday is the night to go if you only have one; Monday and Wednesday suit people who want to browse without the crush. In high season (December to April) the Friday market spills into the side lanes and the area around The Wharf.

Where it is, and parking

The market runs along Bophut's narrow beach road, the old Chinese-Thai fishing village on the north coast of Samui, from The Wharf shopping area at one end to Coco Tam's beach bar at the other. The road is closed to traffic for the market from about 16:30 on Fridays. Directions to Fisherman's Village Walking Street.

  • Cars: the car park at The Wharf is the easiest option, but it fills by about 18:00. Paid lots on the lanes between the ring road and the beach road also fill early.
  • Scooters: park only in marked bays or paid lots. Scooters left on the closed road or blocking driveways are regularly towed or clamped on Fridays, and getting them back costs time and money.
  • Best option: come by Grab or taxi, and you won't need to park at all.

Parking costs about 20 THB for a scooter and 40 THB or more for a car in the paid lots. [check]

What to eat, and what it costs

Food is the reason most people come. The stalls are a mix of Thai street food, southern snacks, seafood grills and sweets, with a few international stands. Prices are a little higher than at the daily markets in Lamai and Nathon, but still good value.

Pay in cash; very few stalls take cards. Seating is limited on Fridays, so many people eat as they walk or take food down to the beach. For more on Samui's food, see Koh Samui restaurants and Koh Samui seafood.

What to buy

  • Clothes: linen shirts, beach dresses, elephant trousers and kaftans.
  • Handicrafts: carved soap, wooden bowls, coconut-shell homeware, woven bags and baskets.
  • Jewellery and accessories: silver, shell and bead pieces from independent sellers.
  • Local products: coconut oil, herbal balms, soaps and Thai spices that pack well.
  • Art: paintings and prints of Samui scenes from local artists.

Haggling is normal for clothes and souvenirs: start at about 60–70 % of the asking price, stay friendly and walk away if the price isn't right. Don't haggle over food. Skip anything made from coral, shells taken from reefs or endangered animals, and leave counterfeit brands alone. Buddha images may need an export permit, so ask the seller before buying a large one.

Taxis on Samui don't use meters: agree the fare before you get in, and expect 20–30 % more after 22:00. Songthaews thin out after 20:00 and mostly stop by 22:00, so plan your ride home. Grab works well in Bophut, but waits get longer after 22:00 on Fridays. More: getting around Koh Samui.

Nearby: fire shows and sunset bars

Fisherman's Village is also one of Samui's best evenings out without a big party. Coco Tam's, the beanbag beach bar at the end of the walking street, runs fire shows on the sand most evenings, at around 19:15 and 21:00 (some nights about 19:30); there are minimum spends on some seating. Beach-front restaurants along the village serve dinner with Koh Phangan on the horizon. Watch the sunset from the beach before the market gets busy: Sunset on Samui is between about 17:50 and 18:45 depending on the month. A suggested Friday evening

  1. 17:00 Arrive by Grab or park at The Wharf while there are still spaces.
  2. 17:30 Walk the market from The Wharf end while stalls are setting up and the light is soft; browse clothes and crafts before the crowds.
  3. 18:00 Sunset drink on the beach or at one of the beach-front bars.
  4. 18:45 Street-food dinner: start with som tam and satay, then pad thai or grilled prawns, then roti or mango sticky rice.
  5. 19:15 or 21:00 Fire show on the sand at Coco Tam's.
  6. 21:30 Last browse as stalls start to discount, then a pre-booked ride home before the 22:00 rush.

Where to stay nearby

Staying in Bophut means you can walk to the market every Monday, Wednesday and Friday. The old village has small boutique hotels and guesthouses on the beach road, with larger resorts at each end of the bay; Bang Rak, Plai Laem and Choeng Mon are a short ride east, and Maenam a short ride west. Bophut is also close to Samui Airport and to the Bang Rak piers for ferries to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao. See where to stay on Koh Samui.

Safety tips

  • Keep phones and wallets in a front pocket or zipped bag in the Friday crush.
  • Watch small children near the fire shows and the open seafood grills.
  • Don't ride a scooter home after drinking; police checkpoints are common on the ring road at night, and the roads are dark.
  • Carry water: even after dark it is about 25 °C and humid.

Bringing children

The walking street works well with children: it is car-free, there is plenty of cheap food they will eat (pancakes, satay, fruit shakes, ice cream), and the fire shows are a hit. Go early, between 17:00 and 18:30, when it is cooler and less crowded. Strollers struggle in the Friday crush after 19:00, so use a carrier for toddlers. Agree a meeting point (The Wharf entrance is easy to find) in case you get separated. See Koh Samui with kids.

Rain and season

The market runs all year, but outdoor stalls are often rained off during the north-east monsoon from October to December. November is the wettest month, with about 445 mm of rain on average, and a heavy downpour on a Friday evening can close half the stalls. Showers are often short, so check the radar before you set off. December to April is the busiest and driest period. Christmas Day 2026 falls on a Friday, so the market should be extra busy that week.

Not here on a Friday? Chaweng Walking Street, beside Chaweng Lake, runs on Thursday, Friday and Saturday evenings; the Lamai and Nathon night markets open every day; and the Monday and Wednesday markets in Fisherman's Village are a calmer version of the Friday one. The Maenam Thursday and Lamai Sunday walking streets may have stopped since the pandemic, so ask locally before you plan around them.
