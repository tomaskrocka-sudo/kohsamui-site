Na Muang has two waterfalls on the same hillside in south-central Koh Samui. Na Muang 1 is an 18 m fall with a swimmable pool 5–10 minutes' walk from the car park; Na Muang 2, 1 km further up, is a steep 20–40 minute climb with fixed ropes and is busy by about 10:30. Both are free (parking about 20 THB for a scooter and 60 THB for a car), open about 09:00–17:00 (Na Muang 1) and 09:00–18:00 (Na Muang 2), run fullest October–January and thin to a trickle February–April.

Map

Na Muang is in the island's south-central interior, inland from the ring road (Route 4169) between Hua Thanon and Nathon. The map shows both falls and Wat Khunaram, 2.5 km away on the ring road; the Secret Buddha Garden is 2 km north, high on the ridge.

Na Muang 1

Na Muang 1 is the island's best-known waterfall and the easiest to visit. A single drop of about 18 m falls over purple-grey rock into a wide, round pool shaded by forest. It is the swim stop on most jungle tours, which is why it gets busy from late morning.

  1. 0 min Turn off Route 4169 at the signed Na Muang junction and follow the road to the Na Muang 1 car park. Pay the attendant (about 20 THB scooter, 60 THB car).
  2. 2 min Walk past the toilets, food stalls, café and souvenir shop onto the shaded path.
  3. 5–10 min The path ends at the pool. Leave bags well back from the water's edge.
  4. Swim Enter from the low rocks at the side. The bottom shelves quickly and the depth varies through the year, so check before jumping.

The rocks around the pool are slippery, and souvenir sellers in the car park can be pushy. A swim and a drink take about an hour.

Na Muang 2

Na Muang 2 is Samui's tallest waterfall: a chain of tiers about 80 m high in total. It is a proper climb, not a stroll. Drive up the steep hill road past Na Muang 1 to the car parks at the top, take the left-hand path signed as a free jungle walk, go about 100 m downhill past a small shop and cross a wooden bridge. From there it is 20–40 minutes up roots, mud and boulders, with fixed ropes on the steepest sections, to small pools and views over the forest. The right-hand route leads through a private café and garden with coloured water slides; reviewers rate its views lower and swimming at the top is not allowed there.

At least four visitors have died in falls at Na Muang 2 since 2019. Read our dedicated Na Muang 2 climb guide for the full route, the tiers and the safety rules before you go.

Our advice: everyone should see Na Muang 1. Add Na Muang 2 only if you are fit, have grippy shoes and the rock is reasonably dry. If you have time for one waterfall only and want a forest walk rather than a rope climb, consider Hin Lad near Nathon instead.

Parking, and what we don't recommend

As you drive in from the ring road, you pass animal attractions before you reach the waterfalls. Attendants at the first car park, beside an elephant camp, may wave you in. Drive on and use the waterfall's own car park. Visitor reports from 2026 describe elephants kept in cramped enclosures near the car parks, with rides still offered. The old Namuang Safari Park address now trades as Samui Elephant Kingdom, which says it no longer offers riding; we have not been able to confirm the current set-up.

We don't endorse any elephant attraction on the Na Muang road and never recommend riding. If you want to see elephants, read our elephant sanctuary guide and choose a no-riding, no-bathing venue.

Safety

  • Falls on wet rock: almost every serious accident at Na Muang has happened on the upper tiers of Na Muang 2, often while someone was stepping back for a photo. Stay behind the edge of every drop.
  • Flash floods: heavy rain on the ridge can raise the stream quickly, especially October–December. If the water rises or turns brown, get out of the stream bed.
  • No jumping at Na Muang 2: the pools are shallow and rocky. At Na Muang 1, jump only after checking the depth yourself.
  • Mosquitoes: dengue is present in Thailand year-round. Use repellent.
  • Valuables: keep your phone and money in a zipped bag you can see while you swim; lock helmets and bags in the scooter seat.
  • Emergency: ambulance 1669; Tourist Police 1155. The nearest hospitals are covered on Koh Samui hospitals.

A good half day: Na Muang 1 at 09:00, Na Muang 2 at 10:00, lunch on Route 4169, then Wat Khunaram. The waterfalls hub has a full-day route.

Best time of day and crowds

Tour minibuses from Chaweng and Lamai arrive from about 10:00, and both falls are busiest until about 14:00. Peak months are July–August and December. Arrive at opening time and you will often have the Na Muang 1 pool almost to yourself, with cooler air for the climb to Na Muang 2. Late afternoon is quieter again, but leave enough daylight to come down from Na Muang 2 before the forest gets dark.

What to wear and bring

  • Swimwear under your clothes and a quick-dry towel
  • Closed shoes with grip for Na Muang 2; strapped sandals are enough for Na Muang 1
  • 1–2 litres of water per person
  • Insect repellent and sunscreen
  • A small backpack to keep your hands free on the ropes
  • 20 and 100 THB notes for parking and drinks
  • A sarong or shirt for Wat Khunaram afterwards

Is Na Muang worth it?

Na Muang 1 is worth an hour on almost any trip: it is free, close to the ring road and has the best waterfall pool on Samui. Go between October and January for a full fall, or in February for a quieter, clearer pool; in March and April the fall is a thin ribbon and the visit is mainly about the swim. Na Muang 2 is worth it for fit walkers in December and January, but not after heavy rain and not as a family outing with young children. If you are only on Samui for a few days and the sea is calm, a boat day will give you more than either fall; if it is wet and windy on the east coast, Na Muang is one of the best things to do.
