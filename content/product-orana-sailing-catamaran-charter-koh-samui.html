Why go: a proper sailing catamaran to the southern islands

The Orana is a 44 ft (13.1 m) Fountaine Pajot Orana 44, a popular French cruising catamaran. It has 3 cabins, 2 bathrooms and an air-conditioned saloon, so there is always somewhere cool to sit out of the midday sun. A captain and 2 crew handle the sailing; you swim, snorkel and eat.

Every option goes to the same pair of islands off Samui's south coast. Koh Madsum, better known as Pig Island, has a shallow sandy beach where the island's pigs wander and wade. Koh Tan, next door, has the most reliable snorkelling close to Samui, over a fragile, recovering reef. The 8-hour day adds Koh Rap, a quieter island with a long beach. Under sail, with the engines off, the crossing is quiet; when the wind is light the twin diesels keep it at 8–10 knots.

Who it suits, and who should choose something else

It suits families, couples celebrating something and small groups who want a comfortable boat with shade, a shower and cabins for changing. The air-conditioned saloon is a real advantage with toddlers or older guests. The 6-hour afternoon option, which starts after a late breakfast and ends near sunset, is the best balance of time and price.

If you only want to see the pigs and are on a tight budget, a shared or private speedboat is far cheaper: see our private speedboat to Pig Island. If your group is larger than 20, the Super King or Serenity Max take more people; compare them in our catamaran charter guide.

Good to know

  • The pigs are wild-ish animals. Do not chase, ride or pick them up, and only feed them the fruit and vegetables the crew or beach staff hand out.
  • Koh Tan's reef is fragile. Use reef-safe sunscreen, stay off the coral and do not take shells.
  • The south coast is sheltered from the northeast monsoon, so this route often runs when east-coast trips are cancelled. November is still the wettest month; check the Koh Samui weather before you go.
  • Half-day sails do not include lunch, so eat before a morning sail or bring snacks.
