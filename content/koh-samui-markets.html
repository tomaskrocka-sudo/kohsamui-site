The firm weekly fixtures on Koh Samui are Fisherman's Village Walking Street in Bophut every Friday (about 17:00–23:00, with smaller markets on Monday and Wednesday) and Chaweng Walking Street beside Chaweng Lake from Thursday to Saturday. Every night you can eat at the Lamai Night Market, the Nathon Night Market (busiest on Tuesday), Si Khao and Central Samui in Chaweng, and the newer Free Wave Market in Bophut. The Maenam Thursday and Lamai Sunday walking streets may have stopped, so check locally before you go.

Fisherman's Village on Friday

Samui's best-known market fills Bophut's old beach road from The Wharf to Coco Tam's beach bar. The road closes to traffic from about 16:30; arrive at 17:30–18:00 to park and eat before the peak at 19:00–20:30. Expect street food, clothes, handicrafts, live music and fire shows on the beach at Coco Tam's. Park at The Wharf or in marked bays, because scooters left on the closed road are towed or clamped. Full guide: Fisherman's Village Walking Street.

Chaweng Walking Street

Chaweng Walking Street sets up beside Chaweng Lake, opposite Central Samui, from Thursday to Saturday, from about 16:00–17:00 until 22:00–23:00. It is mostly food: grills, noodles, desserts, fruit shakes, with some clothes and souvenirs. It is easy to combine with the Central Samui market or a Muay Thai night at the Chaweng stadiums: Samui International Muay Thai Stadium has fights on Friday, and Phetchbuncha Stadium on Saturday, both from about 21:00. Park at Central Samui rather than on the lakeside road, and walk across. It is also the most practical walking street for families staying in Chaweng, because the mall's toilets, air-conditioning and covered areas are a few steps away. See our Chaweng guide and Muay Thai fight nights.

Maenam Thursday (uncertain)

Maenam's walking street in the old Chinatown, near the Chinese gate, used to run on Thursday evenings. A careful local source reported in late 2025 that it had disappeared after the pandemic, while some 2026 guides still list it or describe a smaller Monday market in Maenam. Until we confirm it on the ground, treat it as uncertain. J&J Street Food on the ring road opposite the Chinese gate is open daily. See Maenam.

Lamai Sunday (uncertain)

The Lamai Sunday Walking Street, in central Lamai on the beach road, is in the same position: listed by some 2026 sources, reported gone by others. The daily Lamai Night Market, before the river bridge, is the reliable choice. See Lamai.

Nathon Saturday (discontinued)

We found no current evidence of a Saturday walking street in Nathon; treat it as discontinued. The daily Nathon Night Market by the pier is the place to go. See Nathon.

Daily night markets

Lamai Night Market

Lamai's main market, on Lamai Beach Road just before the river bridge, opens daily from about 17:00 and has expanded in recent years and now has a live-music bar. It is the best everyday market on the east coast for cheap southern Thai food, at about 40–70 THB a portion. Best between 18:00 and 20:00. Cash only.

Nathon Night Market

On the waterfront near Nathon pier, from about 16:00–16:30 until 22:00 daily, busiest on Tuesday. It is mostly local, with pork skewers at 10–15 THB a stick, grilled corn and Thai dinners for 100–200 THB per person. Good for the sunset over the sea on the west coast.

Si Khao Night Market, Chaweng

Off Chaweng Beach Road, west of the Central Samui roundabout, open daily from 17:00 to 23:00. Prices are generally lower than on the walking streets, and it is a useful late-dinner option in Chaweng.

Central Samui market

The square at Central Samui, facing Chaweng Lake, has food stalls every day from late afternoon to about 22:00–23:00, with more on walking-street nights. Air-conditioning and toilets are a few steps away in the mall, which helps with children and in the rain.

Food Village, Chaweng (formerly Chaweng Night Market)

Across the road from Chaweng Lake, open from late afternoon until about midnight. Older guides call it the Chaweng Night Market. Opening days vary between sources, so treat it as a bonus rather than a plan.

Free Wave Market, Bophut

A newer market, opened in 2025, next to the Fisherman's Village sign and the 7-Eleven, open daily from about 16:00 to midnight. It is less crowded than the Friday walking street.

Plaza The Green Market, Bang Rak

Opposite the Bang Rak fresh market near the Seatran pier, daily from about 15:00 to 22:00. Handy if you are staying around Big Buddha or catching an evening ferry.

J&J Street Food, Maenam

On the ring road opposite Maenam's Chinese gate, open daily from about 12:00 to midnight, with most stalls trading in the evening.

Lamai Seafood Market

In central Lamai on the beach road, open daily from about 17:00 to midnight, on the site of the old Lamai Night Plaza. Seafood is sold by weight; check whether prices are per kilo or per 100 g before your fish goes on the grill. See Koh Samui seafood.

Fresh and morning markets

  • Lamai fresh market: open from about 06:00 to 20:00, best before 09:00 for fruit, vegetables, curry pastes and breakfast snacks.
  • Hua Thanon fish market: the Muslim fishing community south of Lamai lands its catch in the early morning. Go at dawn to see the boats come in. See Hua Thanon.
  • Bang Rak fresh market: a local morning market near the Seatran pier, opposite Plaza The Green Market.

Fresh markets are where locals shop, so prices are fixed and low. Bring small notes, and go early: the best produce is gone by mid-morning.

Special and occasional markets

  • Samui Green Market: organic and local products, last Sunday of each month, about 15:00–19:00, at a café in Fisherman's Village.
  • White Market, Bophut: a newer evening market near Fisherman's Village; its schedule is not fixed, so check locally.
  • The moving market: a travelling fair that has rotated around the ring road, the Bang Rak temple and Chaweng Lake every week or so; reports are old, so treat it as uncertain.
  • Chak Phra and temple fairs in Nathon: around the end of Buddhist Lent (in October or November), temples from across Samui pull decorated floats through Nathon, with a fair of several days.
  • Koh Samui Red Cross Fair: usually in February (the 2026 fair was on 22 February), traditionally in Nathon, with a costume parade, stalls and games.

Tips for your first Samui market

  • Go early. Stalls are fully set up by about 17:30, food is freshest, and it is cooler before the crowds arrive.
  • Walk the whole market before you buy. The same dish can cost 20 THB less at the far end, and the best stalls have the longest local queues.
  • Share plates. Portions are small, so a group can try six or eight dishes for the price of one restaurant main.
  • Ask about spice. Southern Thai food is the hottest in Thailand. "Phet mai?" means "Is it spicy?".
  • Carry wet wipes and a small bag for rubbish. Bins and napkins run out on busy nights.
  • Allergies: peanuts, shrimp paste, fish sauce and shellfish are in many dishes. A card in Thai explaining your allergy is worth carrying.
  • Check prices on seafood. Ask whether the price is per kilo or per 100 g, and confirm the total before the fish is cooked.

What to eat at a Samui market

Khanom jeen with southern curry

Fresh rice noodles you top yourself with fiery southern fish curry (kaeng tai pla), green curry or a milder coconut sauce, plus a tray of raw vegetables and herbs. A local breakfast and lunch staple.

Gai tod and moo ping

Fried chicken in the southern style, often with crispy fried shallots, and sweet grilled pork skewers with sticky rice. Ten baht a stick and very hard to stop eating.

Grilled seafood

Squid, prawns and whole fish grilled over charcoal, served with a sharp green chilli and lime dip (nam jim seafood). Ask the price by weight before you order.

Som tam

Green papaya salad pounded to order. Say "phet nit noi" for a little spicy, or "mai phet" for not spicy.

Roti

Thin pan-fried pancakes from the Muslim stalls, filled with banana and egg or drizzled with condensed milk.

Khanom krok

Small coconut-milk puddings cooked in a dimpled pan, crispy outside and soft inside.

Mango sticky rice

Sweet sticky rice with coconut cream and fresh mango, best in the main mango season from about March to June.

Fresh fruit and shakes

Pineapple, watermelon and mango cut to order, and shakes blended with ice. Tropical fruit is at its cheapest in season; rambutan and mangosteen appear in the middle of the year.

The south and west coasts have few evening markets, so if you are staying in Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam or Thong Krut, plan a Friday trip to Fisherman's Village or a Tuesday evening in Nathon. Taxis don't use meters on Samui, so agree a return fare or book a Grab for the ride home before 22:00.

Money, etiquette and getting there

  • Cash: almost every stall is cash only. Bring 100 and 20 THB notes; vendors struggle to change 1,000 THB notes early in the evening. Foreign-card ATMs charge 220–250 THB per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often. See banks and money.
  • Bargaining: haggle politely for clothes and souvenirs, never for food or drinks.
  • Bags: bring a reusable bag; many stalls still hand out plastic.
  • Parking: at Fisherman's Village use The Wharf car park or a marked bay, because scooters on the Friday road are towed. In Chaweng, park at Central Samui.
  • Getting there: Grab works best in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai; short hops cost about 80–120 THB. Songthaews charge locals 30–80 THB along the ring road but mostly stop by 22:00. See getting around Koh Samui.
  • Rubbish: use the bins; markets are next to beaches and rivers.

Markets in the rainy season

Samui's rainy season runs from October to December, peaking in November at about 445 mm of rain. Heavy evening downpours can close outdoor markets, or leave only a handful of covered stalls. Rain often comes in short bursts, so a market rained off at 17:00 may be trading by 19:00. Central Samui, with its covered areas and the mall next door, is the safest choice on a wet night. From January to April evenings are drier and markets are at their busiest. More: Koh Samui weather by month and Koh Samui in November.
