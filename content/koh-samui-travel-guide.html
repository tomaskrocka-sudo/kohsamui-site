Koh Samui is Thailand's second-largest island (228.7 km²), with its own airport (USM, about 1 h 05 min from Bangkok) and a 51 km ring road that links every beach. The driest, calmest months are February to April; the rainy season runs from October to December and peaks in November with about 445 mm of rain, the opposite of Phuket. Most visitors stay in Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut or Choeng Mon and spend at least one day on the water: Ang Thong Marine Park (closed 1 November–15 December) or Pig Island, which runs all year.

Good to know: Ang Thong Marine Park is closed every year from 1 November to 15 December. Season reopens 16 December. Book now for later dates. Until then the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) and the Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB) run as normal, sea permitting.

What's changed in 2026

Several rules that affect visitors changed in 2025 and 2026. Older guides and forum posts often get these wrong.

  • Visa-free stay is now 30 days, not 60. Royal Gazette notices dated 31 August 2026 cut the exemption to 30 days from 15 September 2026 for 60 countries and territories, including the UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan, India and all EU states. Travellers who entered before 15 September keep the stay stamped on entry.
  • TDAC is still mandatory. The Thailand Digital Arrival Card must be filed online, free, within 3 days before you arrive.
  • The tourist entry fee is not being collected. A flat 450 THB fee for all arrivals has been proposed and is moving through hearings and approvals. An online public hearing ran from 24 August to 28 September 2026, but the fee still needs Cabinet approval and is not being collected as of 2 November 2026. Air arrivals would only start paying about 180 days after it is published in the Royal Gazette.
  • Alcohol can be sold from 11:00 to 24:00. Since 29 May 2026 the old 14:00–17:00 afternoon ban has gone. Hotels and licensed entertainment venues are exempt from the hours; the legal drinking age is 20.
  • Helmet fines are up to 2,000 THB per person, rider and passenger, and police run checkpoints around Chaweng and the airport.
  • Cannabis needs a prescription (since 25 June 2025), and smoking it in public is banned. Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal to bring in, carry or use.
  • The airport is expanding: the plan raises capacity to 6 million passengers a year and adds gates (7 to 11), with construction from 2026 and completion targeted for 2030.
  • Donsak ferry fares are up. Seatran raised its walk-on fare to Samui five times between 14 March and 8 April 2026, from 170 to 250 THB, as diesel prices rose; a car now costs about 730 THB or more. Raja's site still lists 220 THB, and Lomprayah kept its island fares unchanged.
  • Fewer flights from Singapore this winter. Bangkok Airways was reported in June 2026 to be pausing its Samui–Singapore route from 25 October 2026 to 28 March 2027, leaving Scoot as the only nonstop airline on it.

Fly direct to Samui (USM)

Bangkok Airways operates every domestic route into USM: about 20–25 flights a day from Suvarnabhumi, about 3 from Don Mueang, 7–8 from Phuket, 1–2 from Krabi and about one a day from Chiang Mai and U-Tapao (Pattaya). Internationally there are direct flights from Singapore (Scoot only until 28 March 2027, while Bangkok Airways pauses its own flights) and Hong Kong, and a seasonal Chengdu service that is scheduled to restart on 1 February 2027. Fares rise sharply around Christmas, New Year and Songkran, so book those dates early. The full route guide is how to get to Koh Samui, and the airport itself is covered in our Samui Airport guide.

Fly to Surat Thani, then take the ferry

Low-cost airlines fly from Don Mueang to Surat Thani (URT). Combined coach and ferry tickets are sold in the arrivals hall, with the last combinations leaving around 16:00. Expect 5–7 hours and roughly 1,500–3,000 THB all-in. Details and operators are in Surat Thani to Koh Samui.

Overland from Bangkok

The night train to Surat Thani (Phun Phin), for example Express 85 leaving Krung Thep Aphiwat at 19:50 and arriving at 06:23, plus a bus and ferry takes 15–17 hours door to door. A second-class sleeper costs 700–900 THB and first class 1,300–1,500 THB. Combined bus and boat tickets from Khao San run about 1,250–1,750 THB. Compare all options in Bangkok to Koh Samui.

From Koh Phangan or Koh Tao

Phangan is 20–50 minutes away and Koh Tao 1 h 30–2 h by fast boat. Several different piers on Samui are all called "Bangrak pier", so check the exact pier on your ticket. Timetables and fares are in Koh Samui to Koh Phangan and Koh Samui to Koh Tao; coming from the Andaman side, see Phuket and Krabi to Koh Samui.

Arriving: airport, piers and your first transfer

Samui Airport sits in the north-east of the island, about 10–15 minutes from Chaweng and 5–10 minutes from Choeng Mon. Taxis do not use meters. You pay a fixed fare at the airport counter, roughly 400–700 THB per car depending on the zone (Chaweng is usually 400–600 THB). Shared minibuses cost about 100–250 THB per person but wait to fill up. Grab works, but drivers cannot use the airport pickup lane, so you meet them at a point just outside; expect about 300–600 THB to the main resort areas.

Arriving by sea, you will land at one of these piers. Each one suits a different part of the island.

When to go: seasons and weather

Samui sits in the Gulf of Thailand and is largely sheltered from the south-west monsoon that soaks Phuket and Krabi from May to October. Its rain comes with the north-east monsoon instead. That gives three seasons:

  • Dry season, January–April: the best weather and the calmest sea, especially February to April. Ideal for Ang Thong snorkelling and kayaking.
  • Hot season with short showers, May–September: hot (highs 32–33 °C), sunny most days, calm sea; a good time when Phuket is wet.
  • North-east monsoon, October–December: the wettest months, peaking in November. Big surf and red flags on the east coast. Ang Thong closes 1 November–15 December.

Getting around

There is no metered taxi system and no public bus network beyond the shared songthaews on the ring road. Most visitors use a mix of tour pickups, Grab and the occasional taxi, or rent a scooter or car.

To ride legally you need your home licence plus an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles; without it most travel insurance will not pay after a crash. Helmets are compulsory for rider and passenger, with fines up to 2,000 THB each. Don't leave your passport as a rental deposit. Roads in the hills are steep and slippery in the rain, and the south-west connector (Route 4170) has blind bends. Entry rules: visas, TDAC and the entry fee

Entry rules are the facts most likely to be out of date elsewhere, so here is the position on 2 November 2026.

  • Visa exemption: 30 days for 60 countries and territories (UK, US, Canada, Australia, Japan, India, all EU states and more) since 15 September 2026. Mauritius and Seychelles get 15 days. Some nationalities have separate bilateral agreements.
  • Extension: once, by up to 30 days, at the Koh Samui Immigration Office in Maenam (form TM.7, 1,900 THB, at the officer's discretion). The office moved from Nathon in 2016, so ignore old map pins.
  • TDAC: file the Thailand Digital Arrival Card free at tdac.immigration.go.th within 3 days before arrival. Paid look-alike sites exist; you never need to pay.
  • Proof at the border: officers may ask for 10,000 THB per person (20,000 THB per family) in funds and an onward ticket.
  • Overstay: 500 THB per day up to 20,000 THB, plus possible detention and re-entry bans.
  • Entry fee: a flat 450 THB fee has been proposed, but it is not being collected as of 2 November 2026.
  • Land borders: visa-exempt entries by land are limited to 2 per calendar year (some ASEAN nationals excepted). This does not limit arrivals by air.

Full details, including the Destination Thailand Visa for remote workers, are in our Thailand visa guide, Koh Samui visa extension and Koh Samui immigration office pages.

Money, SIM cards and Wi-Fi

Foreign cards pay 220–250 THB per ATM withdrawal at most Thai banks. AEON machines inside Big C and Lotus's charge 150 THB. Limits are usually 20,000–30,000 THB per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts less often. Always decline "dynamic currency conversion" and choose to be charged in THB.

Visa and Mastercard work in malls, supermarkets, 7-Eleven, hotels and most restaurants. Carry cash for street food, songthaews, many taxis and the island fees paid on boat trips: Ang Thong 300 THB per adult and 150 THB per child, Koh Madsum about 50 THB and Koh Nang Yuan 250 THB.

The two mobile networks are AIS and True (True merged with dtac in 2023). Tourist SIMs cost 499 THB for 8 days, 699 THB for 15 days and 1,199 THB for 30 days with unlimited data, and you need your passport to register. Travel eSIMs use the same networks. Wi-Fi is standard in hotels and cafés. Read banks, ATMs and money and SIM cards and Wi-Fi.

Health and safety

Samui has the best healthcare of the Gulf islands. The main private hospitals are Bangkok Hospital Samui (on the ring road before Chaweng Noi; +66 77 429 500, hotline 1719), Samui International Hospital (north end of Chaweng Beach Road), Thai International Hospital (Chaweng, opposite Lotus's) and Bandon International Hospital (Bophut, near Big C). The government Koh Samui Hospital is on the outskirts of Nathon. Private care is expensive, so travel insurance matters; see travel insurance.

The main risks are on the road and in the sea:

  • Rip currents: from October to December the north-east monsoon pushes big surf onto Chaweng and Lamai. When red flags are up, stay out of the water; swimmers have drowned after ignoring them. Waves also sweep the rocks at Lad Koh viewpoint, where a tourist was lost in November 2024.
  • Box jellyfish: of the first 10 recorded deaths in Thailand since 2002, 9 were in waters off Samui and Phangan, and a 13-year-old boy died after a sting at Sichon on the mainland coast opposite Samui on 1 August 2026. If stung, pour vinegar over the area for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it, don't rinse with fresh water, and call 1669. Many hotels keep vinegar stations on the beach.
  • Scooters: most serious tourist injuries involve rental bikes. Ride sober, with a helmet, and avoid hill roads in heavy rain.
  • Drugs and vapes: vapes are illegal; cannabis is prescription-only and banned in public. See drugs, alcohol and the law.

Things to do on Koh Samui

Samui is easy to explore in short trips from your hotel. Our things to do hub lists every activity; these are the essentials.

Beaches

Chaweng has the longest stretch of fine white sand (about 7 km) and the busiest scene. Lamai (about 4 km) is slower, with a steep drop in the centre. Choeng Mon is the calmest bay for children, Silver Beach (Crystal Bay) is a small boulder cove that fills by 10:00, and Lipa Noi and Taling Ngam on the west coast have the sunsets. Bophut and Bang Rak are not swimming beaches. North and west-coast beaches are tidal, so swim at high tide. Compare them all in Koh Samui beaches.

Temples and viewpoints

  • Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai): on Koh Fan, a small islet joined by a causeway near Bang Rak; free, 07:00–18:30. See Big Buddha.
  • Wat Plai Laem: about 1 km east, with an 18-armed Guanyin on a lake platform; free, about 06:00–18:00.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai: the Grandfather and Grandmother rocks at the south end of Lamai; free, parking about 30 THB.
  • Wat Khunaram: the mummified monk Luang Pho Daeng on Route 4169; free. Cover shoulders and knees.
  • Lad Koh viewpoint: a free roadside stop between Chaweng Noi and Coral Cove.

Read temples, hiking and viewpoints and sunset spots.

Waterfalls

Na Muang 1 has a swimmable pool a short walk from the car park (free; parking 20 THB scooter, 60 THB car; 09:00–17:00). Na Muang 2 is a steep 20–40 minute climb with ropes in places. Both flow best from October to January and thin to a trickle from February to April. See waterfalls and Na Muang Waterfall.

Markets and nightlife

Fisherman's Village Walking Street in Bophut runs on Friday from about 17:00 to 23:00, with smaller markets on Monday and Wednesday. Chaweng Walking Street, beside Chaweng Lake opposite Central Samui, runs Thursday to Saturday. Muay Thai fights are on at Samui International Stadium (Tuesday, Friday, Sunday) and Phetchbuncha Stadium (Monday, Wednesday, Saturday), both in Chaweng, with tickets at 1,500, 2,000 (ringside) and 2,500 THB (VIP); there are no fights on Thursdays. See markets, nightlife, beach clubs and Muay Thai fight nights.

Food

Try Samui's own dish, wai khua (octopus in coconut milk), plus southern sour curry (gaeng som), khua kling (dry minced-pork curry) and khanom jeen noodles with southern curry. Street dishes cost 50–120 THB. Baan Suan Lung Khai in Taling Ngam holds a MICHELIN Bib Gourmand for 2026 for its southern seafood; book ahead. More in restaurants.

Ang Thong Marine Park

A national park of 42 islands about 30 km west of Samui, with the Emerald Lake on Koh Mae Ko, the viewpoint on Koh Wua Talap and sea kayaking among limestone cliffs. The park fee (300 THB per foreign adult, 150 THB per child, cash) is not included in tour prices. The park closes 1 November–15 December every year. Our Ang Thong guide explains the tours, and speedboat or big boat? helps you choose.

Pig Island and Koh Tan

Boats leave from Thong Krut on the south-west coast for Koh Madsum, where the island's pigs wander the beach, and Koh Tan for snorkelling. Pig Island runs on any date of the year, weather permitting, which makes it the first choice from November to mid-December. Options include a sunset tour (1,550 THB), a longtail tour (1,750 THB) and private boats. See Pig Island guide.

Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan

A full day by speedboat to the three islets joined by a sandbar at Koh Nang Yuan and the snorkelling bays of Koh Tao. The Nang Yuan landing fee is 250 THB per adult and 120 THB per child in cash, day visitors may stay from 10:00 to 15:00, and plastic bottles, cans, fins and drones are not allowed on the island.

Koh Phangan and the Full Moon Party

Our shared return speedboat to the Full Moon Party costs 1,400 THB per person with hotel pickup and drop-off included from most areas of Samui; remote areas such as Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and Laem Yai add 400 THB per person return. We sell the boat only; party entry (about 200 THB) is paid at the gate. For a day on Phangan, a private speedboat to Koh Phangan is the flexible option. Read Full Moon Party from Koh Samui.

Khao Sok, honestly

Khao Sok National Park is 4–6 hours each way from Samui (ferry to Donsak, then about 190 km by road). It does not work as a day trip. Do it as a 2-day, 1-night trip or at the start or end of your holiday.

All boat tours return to the island they left from; there is no one-way island hopping on tour boats. Cancellation: full refund 48 hours or more before departure, 50% at 24–48 hours, none under 24 hours (Ang Thong tours: full refund 48 hours or more before, none under 48 hours). If we cancel for weather or sea safety, you choose a full refund or a new date. See all trips in Koh Samui tours and islands near Koh Samui.

A 7-day Koh Samui plan

One week covers the island, two boat days and a slow day. Swap Ang Thong for Pig Island between 1 November and 15 December.

  1. Day 1 Arrive, check in, dinner at Fisherman's Village (on a Friday, the walking street).
  2. Day 2 Ang Thong Marine Park by speedboat: pickup 07:30–08:30, back at your hotel 16:30–17:00.
  3. Day 3 Round-island loop: Big Buddha, Wat Plai Laem, Lad Koh, Hin Ta and Hin Yai, Wat Khunaram, Na Muang 1.
  4. Day 4 Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan by speedboat.
  5. Day 5 Beach morning, then the Pig Island sunset tour from Thong Krut.
  6. Day 6 Koh Phangan, or the Full Moon Party if your dates match.
  7. Day 7 Cooking class, spa or an ethical elephant sanctuary, then a sunset on the west coast.

Day-by-day 3, 5, 7 and 10-day versions, with timings and rainy-day swaps, are in our Koh Samui itinerary.

Koh Samui vs Koh Phangan vs Koh Tao

  • Koh Samui: easy flights, the widest choice of hotels, private hospitals and family comfort.
  • Koh Phangan: the Full Moon and Half Moon parties, yoga and wilder beaches, 20–50 minutes by boat.
  • Koh Tao: diving and snorkelling, 1 h 30–2 h by fast boat.
