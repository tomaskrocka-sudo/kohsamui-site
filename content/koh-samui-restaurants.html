Eat southern Thai food and seafood on Koh Samui: order wai khua (octopus in coconut milk), southern sour curry and khua kling. For a dinner out, go to Fisherman's Village in Bophut; for seafood by the sea, Bang Por and Bang Rak; for cheap eats, the Lamai and Nathon night markets, where dishes cost 40–80 THB. The MICHELIN Guide Thailand 2026 gives Samui two Bib Gourmands, Baan Suan Lung Khai in Taling Ngam and Jun Hom in Maenam, plus ten more recommended restaurants, but no stars.

Samui's food clusters in a few places: Chaweng and Lamai on the east coast; Bophut, Fisherman's Village, Bang Rak and Choeng Mon in the north-east; Maenam and Bang Por on the north coast; and a scattering of seafood restaurants and resorts on the quieter west and south coasts. Samui dishes to try

Samui cooking belongs to the southern Thai tradition: hotter, saltier and more sour than central Thai food, built on fresh turmeric, shrimp paste (kapi), coconut and seafood. These are the dishes to look for:

Wai khua

Samui's own dish: octopus gently cooked in coconut milk with shrimp paste, chilli and herbs until tender, served with rice. Local restaurants and some resorts cook it; it is rarely found on the mainland. Order it where octopus is fresh, at seafood restaurants in Bang Por, Maenam and Taling Ngam.

Gaeng som and gaeng lueang

The southern sour curry, yellow from fresh turmeric and sharp with tamarind, usually cooked with fish and coconut shoots or green papaya. It has no coconut milk and is fiery and thin, closer to a soup. Eat it with plenty of rice.

Gaeng tai pla

The most southern curry of all, made with fermented fish innards, pumpkin, bamboo shoots and beans. It smells strong and tastes deep and salty; locals eat it with khanom jeen noodles. Try a small portion first.

Khua kling

Minced meat stir-fried dry with a turmeric-heavy curry paste until each grain is coated, finished with shredded kaffir lime leaves. Very hot; order it with cucumber and rice to cool down.

Moo hong

A mild, sweet-savoury braise of pork belly with garlic, black pepper and soy, from the Hokkien-influenced kitchens of southern Thailand. A good choice for children and anyone avoiding chilli.

Pad sataw goong

Prawns stir-fried with sataw, a flat green bean with a bitter taste and a strong smell, with shrimp paste and chilli. People either love it or never order it again.

Khanom jeen with southern curry

Thin fermented rice noodles you top yourself with curry (fish curry, green curry or gaeng tai pla), then add raw vegetables, herbs, pickles and boiled egg from the shared tray. It is a breakfast and lunch dish, sold at markets and roadside shops until the curry runs out, usually by early afternoon.

Crab curry with betel leaves

Blue swimming crab in a rich coconut curry with wild betel leaves, eaten with rice noodles or rice. Jun Hom in Maenam, one of Samui's two Bib Gourmands, is known for its crab coconut-milk curry, cooked with shrubby basil or wild betel leaves.

How to order spice levels

  • Mai phet: not spicy.
  • Phet nit noi: a little spicy. In southern restaurants this is still hot for most visitors.
  • Phet mak: very spicy.
  • Mai sai prik: no chilli at all; best for children.

Southern curries are made from a paste, so the kitchen cannot always take the chilli out. If you can't handle heat, order moo hong, grilled fish or seafood, fried rice and stir-fries, and add chilli from the table condiments yourself.

MICHELIN on Koh Samui

The MICHELIN Guide Thailand 2026 lists 12 restaurants on Koh Samui, all checked against the Guide's own listing on 1 November 2026. There are no MICHELIN-starred restaurants on Samui. Two are Bib Gourmands, the Guide's award for good food at moderate prices; the other ten are "Selected" (recommended). The 2027 selection is expected to be announced in the coming months, and we will update this list when it is.

Bib Gourmand 2026

  • Baan Suan Lung Khai (Taling Ngam, west coast): a garden house on a coconut plantation serving three southern Thai seafood set menus, built on the catch of local fishermen, including blue crab and grilled prawns. Open daily 11:00–14:00 and 16:30–19:00. Reservations are required, so call ahead; it is not a walk-in restaurant. About 30–40 minutes from Chaweng by car.
  • Jun Hom (Maenam, north coast): an open-air beachside seafood restaurant serving hearty, low-priced southern dishes cooked to order, so expect a wait at busy times. Signature dish: crab coconut-milk curry. Open daily, about 12:00–20:00.

Restaurants by area

Restaurants open and close often on Samui. Every name below was listed as open in 2026 sources; we name them as context, not endorsements, and none of them pays us. Hours change by season, so call ahead in the low season.

Chaweng

The busiest dining area, with everything from 40 THB noodles to resort dining rooms, but many beach-road places serve generic tourist menus. Go a street or two back for local food.

  • Kapi Sator (MICHELIN Selected): southern Thai curries, ฿฿.
  • Phensiri (MICHELIN Selected): Thai, on Chaweng Beach Road, ฿฿.
  • Baitong (Banana Fan Sea Resort): Thai and seafood, daily 07:00–23:00, about 350–700 THB per person.
  • Si Khao Night Market: daily 17:00–23:00 for cheap street food.
  • Chaweng Walking Street: Thursday to Saturday evenings beside Chaweng Lake.

See our Chaweng guide.

Fisherman's Village and Bophut

Samui's best area for an evening out: a beach-front lane of restaurants in old Chinese shophouses, with Koh Phangan on the horizon. Prices run from street food to fine dining.

  • Khao Horm (MICHELIN Selected): southern Thai, ฿฿.
  • Krua Bophut: Thai and seafood on the beach, about 300–600 THB per person; arrive before 19:00 for a beach table.
  • Salt & Oak: open about 13:00–23:00.
  • Chez François: French fine dining, Tuesday to Saturday, 18:30–23:00, top price level; book ahead.
  • The Shack: steakhouse and grill, daily 18:00–22:00, about 500–900 THB per person.
  • 2 Fishes: Italian, evenings, about 600–1,200 THB per person; reservations recommended.
  • Also open in 2026: The Thai Tapas, Rice x De Pier, Charm Samui, Ban Thai and Ran Khang Non (Tuesday to Sunday).

On Friday the Fisherman's Village Walking Street takes over the lane, and restaurants are packed: book or eat early. See Fisherman's Village.

Lamai

Lamai has cheaper, more local eating than Chaweng, and the island's best everyday night market.

  • Saffron at Banyan Tree Samui (MICHELIN Selected): Thai dinner in the hills above Lamai Bay, about 18:00–23:00, ฿฿฿.
  • Sabeinglae: seafood sold by the kilo near the Hin Ta Hin Yai rocks, daily from about 10:00 to 22:00, about 300–500 THB per person.
  • Lamai Night Market: daily from about 17:00, before the river bridge, with southern Thai dishes at about 40–70 THB.
  • Lamai Seafood Market: daily from about 17:00 to midnight in central Lamai; seafood by weight.

See our Lamai guide.

Maenam

Quiet Maenam has the best concentration of serious local seafood on the island, including a Bib Gourmand and two MICHELIN Selected restaurants.

  • Jun Hom (Bib Gourmand 2026): open-air beachside southern seafood, about 12:00–20:00.
  • Ko Seng (MICHELIN Selected): southern seafood for over 50 years, daily 10:00–20:00, ฿฿.
  • Sala Thai at Santiburi: Thai and seafood dinner, 18:00–22:00 (last orders 21:30), about 300–600 THB per person.
  • J&J Street Food: daily street-food court opposite the Chinese gate, busiest in the evening.

See our Maenam guide.

Choeng Mon and Plai Laem

The north-east tip is resort country, with three MICHELIN-recommended restaurants at five-star properties and fewer independent places.

  • FishHouse at Kimpton Kitalay (MICHELIN Selected): European and seafood, ฿฿฿.
  • The Ranch at the Ritz-Carlton (MICHELIN Selected): steakhouse, dinner Wednesday to Sunday, ฿฿฿฿.
  • Long Dtai at Cape Fahn (MICHELIN Selected): southern Thai on a small private island, reached by a crossing that depends on the tide.

See Choeng Mon and Plai Laem.

Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach)

Bang Rak has a strip of beach-front seafood restaurants where the catch is displayed on ice and cooked to order. Typical 2026 prices: whole grilled fish 300–500 THB, tiger prawns 300–400 THB, steamed blue crab 250–350 THB and a large Chang beer 60–70 THB. Plaza The Green Market, near the Seatran pier, has cheap street food every evening. Bang Rak is also where the beach clubs Chi Samui and Tembo serve food with the sunset. See Big Buddha Beach.

West coast: Bang Por, Lipa Noi and Taling Ngam

The west coast has the sunsets but few restaurants, and long drives between them, so plan ahead.

  • Baan Suan Lung Khai (Bib Gourmand 2026), Taling Ngam: reservation required.
  • Bang Por Seafood Takho (MICHELIN Selected), Bang Por: seafood with sunset views; Bang Por beach itself is best at high tide.
  • Koh Thai Kitchen at the Four Seasons (MICHELIN Selected): regional Thai set dinners at about 1,500–3,000 THB or more per person; reservations essential.
  • Nikki Beach, Lipa Noi: beach club by day, restaurant 18:00–23:00.
  • Nathon Night Market: daily about 16:00–22:00 on the waterfront, the cheapest dinner on the west coast.

See Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam and Nathon.

South and Hua Thanon

The south coast (Hua Thanon, Bang Kao, Thong Krut) is the least developed part of Samui for dining. Hua Thanon is a Muslim fishing village with an early-morning fish market, and halal food is easy to find there. Beyond that, we could not verify many independent restaurants in 2026, so most visitors eat at their resort or drive to Lamai. Our Pig Island trips leave from Thong Krut, and the Pig Island sunset tour includes dinner. See Hua Thanon and Thong Krut.

Night markets and street food

Markets are the cheapest and often the tastiest way to eat on Samui. The weekly highlights are Fisherman's Village Walking Street every Friday (about 17:00–23:00) and Chaweng Walking Street from Thursday to Saturday; the Lamai and Nathon night markets run every day. Typical 2026 prices: pad thai 60–80 THB, som tam about 40, satay about 10 per stick, grilled prawns 80–120, mango sticky rice 50–80, and a full market dinner 100–250 THB per person in Lamai or Nathon and 300–500 THB in Fisherman's Village. Day-by-day guide: Koh Samui night markets.

Seafood

Samui's seafood is best where the boats land: Bang Por and Maenam on the north coast, Bang Rak, Taling Ngam in the south-west and the Lamai Seafood Market. Seafood is usually sold by weight from a display on ice. Before anything goes on the grill:

  • Ask whether the price is per kilo or per 100 g, and how much your chosen fish weighs.
  • Agree the cooking style (grilled, steamed with lime, deep-fried with garlic) and whether it costs extra.
  • Confirm the total, and check the bill for items you didn't order.

Skip shark fin, and ask what is local and in season. Full guide: Koh Samui seafood.

Vegan, vegetarian and healthy food

Samui is easy for vegetarians and manageable for vegans, with care. Many cafés in Bophut, Lamai and Chaweng serve plant-based bowls, smoothies and salads aimed at the wellness crowd, and most Thai restaurants will cook stir-fries and curries with tofu and vegetables. The catch is that southern Thai cooking relies on fish sauce and shrimp paste, which are in most curry pastes.

  • Jay (เจ) stalls: a yellow flag with red Thai lettering marks "jay" food, which is plant-based and also avoids garlic and onion. They are most common around the Chinese shrines in Nathon, Maenam and Hua Thanon, and during the annual Vegetarian Festival in October.
  • Useful phrases: "kin jay" (I eat vegan), "mangsawirat" (vegetarian), "mai sai nam pla" (no fish sauce), "mai sai kapi" (no shrimp paste), "mai sai kai" (no egg).
  • Safe bets: fruit, som tam made without fish sauce or dried shrimp, vegetable fried rice, roti with banana, and morning-glory stir-fries without oyster sauce.

International food

Samui has a large international dining scene, mostly in Fisherman's Village, Chaweng and the resorts. In 2026 you can find French fine dining (Chez François in Fisherman's Village), Italian (2 Fishes in Bophut), steakhouses (The Shack in Bophut and The Ranch at the Ritz-Carlton), European seafood (FishHouse in Choeng Mon), tapas nights (Tembo in Bang Rak on Tuesdays), pizza at beach bars such as Coco Tam's, plus Indian, Japanese, Middle Eastern and burger restaurants in Chaweng and Lamai. Expect to pay two to five times the price of a Thai meal, with "++" at resorts. Book Western Christmas and New Year's Eve dinners early: see Christmas and New Year's Eve.

Coffee and breakfast

The local breakfast is khanom jeen with curry, jok (rice porridge) or patongko (fried dough sticks) with sweet soy milk, from market stalls and roadside shops from about 06:00. Cafés serving espresso, smoothie bowls and Western breakfasts are everywhere in Bophut, Chaweng, Lamai and Maenam. 7-Eleven toasties and iced coffee are the budget fallback. Our Koh Samui cafés guide covers the best coffee by area.

Dinners with a view

  • The Jungle Club: a hillside hotel restaurant and bar above Chaweng Noi with views over Chaweng Bay, open about 10:00–22:00. Expect about 1,500 THB for two for dinner. The road is steep; a local pick-up truck runs guests up for a small fee, and booking about three days ahead is advised in high season.
  • Resort sunset bars: on the west and north-west coasts, such as Air Bar at the InterContinental (about 17:00–22:00) and Jahn at the Conrad, open to non-guests with a smart-casual dress code.
  • Fisherman's Village beach front: tables on the sand looking across to Koh Phangan.
  • Bang Por and Lipa Noi: seafood with the sun setting over the sea.
  • Long Dtai at Cape Fahn: dinner on a small island off the north-east coast.

Tipping and paying

  • Tipping is not expected but is appreciated. In local restaurants, leave the coins or round up by 20–50 THB; in mid-range places 5–10 % if service was good. Where a 10 % service charge is already on the bill, an extra tip is optional.
  • Cash vs card: street stalls, markets and many local restaurants are cash only. Resorts, malls and most mid-range restaurants take Visa and Mastercard; ask before you order if you plan to pay by card.
  • Always decline DCC: if the card machine offers to charge you in your home currency, choose THB. The conversion rate is always worse.
  • ATMs charge foreign cards 220–250 THB per withdrawal (150 THB at AEON machines in Big C and Lotus's). See banks and money.

Food safety

  • Water: don't drink tap water; bottled and filtered water is cheap and everywhere.
  • Ice: factory-made tube ice, with a hole in the middle, is made from purified water and is safe; be wary of crushed block ice at very basic stalls.
  • Seafood: eat it where it is busy, cooked to order and hot all the way through. Shellfish that has sat in the sun is the commonest cause of stomach trouble.
  • Allergies: peanuts, shellfish, shrimp paste and fish sauce are in a large share of Thai dishes, often invisibly. Carry a card in Thai describing your allergy, and know where the nearest hospital is: see hospitals on Koh Samui.
  • Spice: southern Thai food is the hottest in Thailand. Start mild and build up.
  • Heat: food goes off quickly at 30 °C, so eat market food when it is freshly cooked.

Booking and seasons

From mid-December to February, and around Songkran in April, popular restaurants fill up: book Fisherman's Village beach tables, resort restaurants and the MICHELIN-listed places a few days ahead, and Christmas and New Year's Eve dinners weeks ahead (many sell out by mid-December). Baan Suan Lung Khai always needs a reservation. In the rainy season (October to December) and the quiet months of September and October, some restaurants shorten their hours or take a break, so call ahead before driving across the island. Beach tables are moved inside on wet nights. More on the seasons: Koh Samui weather by month.

Taste Samui on the water

Some of the best meals on Samui come with a boat ride. The Pig Island sunset tour (1,550 THB adult, 1,350 THB child) runs all year from Thong Krut and includes dinner. With a private speedboat you can plan the day around a long seafood lunch, and the Koh Madsum, Koh Tan and Koh Rap private boat (8,450 THB per boat) visits the quiet southern islands. To cook the dishes yourself, join a Thai cooking class (enquire on WhatsApp). Hotel pickup is included from most areas of Koh Samui; Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao and Thong Krut add 400 THB per person, return.
