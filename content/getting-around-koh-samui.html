Koh Samui's taxis don't use meters, so agree the fare before you get in; the airport to Chaweng costs roughly 400–600 THB. Grab works best in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai, where short hops cost about 80–120 THB, while songthaews (shared pickup trucks) charge locals 30–80 THB along the ring road by day, take cash only and mostly stop by 20:00–22:00. Scooters cost about 150–350 THB a day, but you need an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category as well as your home licence.

Good to know: prices on this page were last checked on 1 November 2026. Fares on Samui are negotiated, so treat them as guides, not fixed tariffs.

Taxis: unmetered fares and zones

Samui taxis are painted yellow and red or yellow and green, and most have a "Taxi-Meter" sign on the roof. The meters are not used. You either pay a fixed price from a taxi rank board (at the airport, some malls and hotel ranks) or agree a price with the driver before you get in.

  • Within one area (for example central Chaweng to Chaweng Noi): about 200–300 THB.
  • Neighbouring areas (Chaweng to Bophut, Chaweng to Lamai): about 300–500 THB.
  • Across the island (Chaweng to Nathon, Lamai to Maenam): about 600–1,000 THB.
  • After 22:00, fares are typically 20–30% higher.

Confirm whether the price is per car or per person, and settle it before loading bags. If a driver refuses a fair price, wait for the next taxi or open Grab to compare. For known tricks, read our Koh Samui scams guide.

Airport transfers

At Samui airport, taxis are sold at a fixed-price kiosk by zone: approx. 400–700 THB per car for most areas (Chaweng is at the bottom of the range, the west coast above it). Shared minibuses cost about 100–250 THB per person and leave when full. Songthaews do not serve the airport. The full zone table is on our Koh Samui airport page.

Grab on Koh Samui

Grab (cars and some motorbike taxis) operates on Samui, with upfront prices shown in the app. Coverage is good in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai, patchy in Maenam and Choeng Mon, and thin on the west and south coasts.

  • Prices: about 80–120 THB for a 5 km hop, 200–350 THB across the island, and 300–600 THB from the airport to resort areas.
  • Waits: usually 5–20 minutes. Prices surge in rain and in the evenings.
  • Airport and piers: local transport groups resist app pickups at the airport, piers and some hotels. In May 2026 visitors reported walking more than a kilometre with luggage to meet Grab drivers outside the airport. Message your driver and agree a meeting point.
  • Payment: card in the app or cash to the driver.

Bolt and other apps

Bolt operates in Thailand's major cities and ran a 2026 rider promotion that includes Central Samui. Driver numbers on Samui are much lower than on Grab, so expect longer waits or no cars outside Chaweng. [check] Install both apps before you arrive; registering a foreign phone number works, but you may need Wi-Fi to verify. Hotel desks can also call a taxi or book a driver.

Songthaews: routes, fares and hours

Songthaews are pickup trucks with two benches in the back. They circle the ring road (Route 4169) between Nathon, Maenam, Bophut, Chaweng and Lamai, with a few branching to Choeng Mon and Big Buddha. Flag one down anywhere, ring the buzzer or tap the roof to stop, and pay the driver through the passenger window when you get out. Most are blue or green; the colour and destination are painted on the side.

  • Cash only: carry small notes and coins.

  • Night songthaews: late at night in Chaweng, some songthaews work as shared taxis at higher prices (100–200 THB or more).

Renting a scooter

A 125 cc automatic scooter costs about 150–350 THB a day (cheapest in Maenam, Nathon and Lipa Noi, dearest in Chaweng and Lamai), 150–160 cc models 250–450 THB, and a month 2,000–4,500 THB. You legally need your home licence plus an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle (A) category, a helmet for rider and passenger, and you should never leave your passport as a deposit. Full details, deposits, insurance and a pre-ride checklist are on our scooter and motorbike rental guide.

Renting a car

A compact car costs about 800–1,200 THB a day and an SUV 1,500–2,500 THB. A car is the comfortable choice for families, rain and villas on steep hill roads. You need your licence plus an International Driving Permit. See our Koh Samui car rental guide for insurance and where to rent.

Bicycles and e-bikes

Cycling works on the flatter north coast between Maenam and Bophut and for short trips within an area, but the heat, hills and fast ring-road traffic make it hard work elsewhere. E-bikes help on the climbs. Our bicycle and e-bike rental guide covers routes and safety.

Licences, helmets and insurance

  • Licence: carry your home licence and an International Driving Permit (1949 or 1968 convention) showing the right category: motorcycle (A) for scooters, car (B) for cars. A car-only licence does not cover a scooter, even a small one. ASEAN licences are accepted without an IDP.
  • Helmets: compulsory for rider and passenger. The fine has been up to 2,000 THB per person since 1 June 2025.
  • Checkpoints: police run regular checks near Chaweng Lake Road, near the airport and on the ring road. Riding without a valid licence or IDP is typically fined 500–1,000 THB.
  • Insurance: the UK Foreign Office warns that driving without a valid licence can invalidate travel insurance. Check your policy's motorcycle clause before you ride.

More on rules of the road and checkpoints is on our Koh Samui roads guide.

Getting around at night

After about 20:00 songthaews become scarce, and after 22:00 you rely on taxis, Grab or night songthaews in Chaweng. Agree the fare first and expect a 20–30% night premium. The drink-drive limit is 0.05% blood alcohol for most drivers and 0.02% for drivers under 20 or without a full licence, and police set up checkpoints at night. If you have been drinking, do not ride: take a taxi or Grab.

The rainy season

From October to December, and especially in November, heavy rain floods low-lying roads, mainly inland and in the south-west, for hours at a time. Hill roads such as Route 4170 in the south-west become slippery, and sand and gravel wash onto bends. Ride slowly, avoid flooded dips (water hides potholes) and consider a car or taxi on wet days. Boat days: hotel pickup

Our boat tours include hotel pickup from most areas of Koh Samui, so you don't need transport on tour days. Remote areas (Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and similar) carry a surcharge of 400 THB per person return on Chaweng-side departures. Pig Island tours leave from Thong Krut on the south coast, so south-west hotels are close for those. Popular choices: the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, every day of the year) and, from 16 December when the park reopens, the Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB).

Island hopping from Samui

For Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, use the ferries: see Koh Samui to Koh Phangan and Koh Samui to Koh Tao, or book a private speedboat to Koh Phangan. Tour boats always return to the island they left from; there are no one-way island hops on tours. Rental scooters and cars must stay on Samui.

Getting around with children

Child car seats are rarely available in taxis or Grab cars. If you need one, ask your hotel or a private transfer company to provide it in advance, or rent a car with a seat from an international agency. [check] Minibuses and private drivers are the most comfortable for families with luggage. Never carry a child on a scooter without a properly fitting helmet; police fine riders and passengers. More family advice is on Koh Samui with kids.

Getting around on a budget

  • Use songthaews by day and pay the local fare: say your destination, and hand over the usual price without asking.
  • Stay in a walkable area such as Chaweng, Lamai or Fisherman's Village and walk to dinner.
  • Compare Grab against taxi quotes in the evening.
  • A monthly scooter rental (2,000–4,500 THB) is the cheapest transport for long stays, if you have the right licence.
  • See the Koh Samui budget guide for daily costs.

Accessibility

Samui is hard work with limited mobility. Pavements are narrow, broken or missing, side roads are steep, and songthaews have a high step. Taxis and private vans with a step or sliding door are the practical choice; ask a transfer company in advance for a vehicle with space for a folded wheelchair. The airport terminal is largely on one level. [check] Piers have steep gangways at low tide.

Private driver and round-island day

A taxi or private driver for a full day costs about 2,000–3,000 THB per car. The ring road is about 51 km and takes roughly 90 minutes non-stop. A classic clockwise day from Chaweng:

  1. 09:00 Lad Koh viewpoint, then Hin Ta and Hin Yai rocks at Lamai.
  2. 10:30 Na Muang waterfall (a short walk to Na Muang 1).
  3. 12:00 Lunch near Nathon or Lipa Noi on the west coast.
  4. 14:00 Wat Khunaram or the Secret Buddha Garden (4x4 or tour only up the hill).
  5. 16:00 Big Buddha and Wat Plai Laem in the north-east.
  6. 18:00 Sunset and dinner at Fisherman's Village, Bophut.

Agree the stops, the hours and whether waiting time and fuel are included before you set off.

Safety checklist before you ride

  • Licence with the right category plus IDP, and a photocopy of your passport.
  • Helmet on and fastened for rider and passenger.
  • Photos and video of every panel before you leave the shop.
  • Brakes, tyres, lights and horn checked.
  • Travel insurance that covers the vehicle you are riding.
  • No riding after drinking; no riding in heavy rain on hill roads.
  • Emergency numbers saved: ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155, police 191.

Our advice after 20 years on the island

Our team has run boats and transfers on Samui since 2006. The single biggest risk for visitors is not scams but scooter crashes, usually on wet hill roads or at night. If you do not ride a motorbike at home, do not learn on Samui. Stay in a walkable area, use songthaews by day and Grab or taxis at night, and book tours that include hotel pickup from most areas.
