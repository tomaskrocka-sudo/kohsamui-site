Can you cycle around Koh Samui? | Yes. The ring road loop is 51 km, and a fit cyclist can ride it in about 3–4 hours of riding time, plus stops. It has busy traffic and steep hills between Lamai and Chaweng and in the south-west, so start at first light and carry plenty of water.
Where can I rent a bicycle on Koh Samui? | Ask your hotel first: many resorts lend simple bikes to guests. A few bike and sports shops in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai rent mountain bikes, road bikes and e-bikes, and some scooter shops keep electric bikes. Always test the brakes before you ride off.
Are e-scooters legal on Koh Samui? | Stand-up electric kick scooters can't be registered as vehicles in Thailand, so they aren't legal on public roads. Seated electric mopeds count as motorcycles and need plates, insurance, a helmet and a motorcycle licence with an IDP.
How long does it take to cycle around the island? | Around 3–4 hours of riding for a fit cyclist on a road or hybrid bike, and a full day with beach stops and lunch. On an e-bike, check the battery range first, because the hills drain it quickly.
Is it safe to cycle on the ring road? | It is busy and has no cycle lanes, with trucks, minivans and scooters pulling out of side roads. Ride it only at first light if you are confident in traffic, wear a helmet and bright clothes, and use lights. The back lanes of Maenam and the west coast are far calmer.
What is the best time of day to cycle on Koh Samui? | From dawn until about 10:00, before the heat and traffic build, or for a short ride in the late afternoon with lights for the ride home. Sunset is between about 18:00 and 18:45 depending on the month.
Where is the flattest cycling on Koh Samui? | The north coast lanes around Maenam and Bophut and the west coast between Lipa Noi and Nathon. The interior, the Lamai hills and Route 4170 to Taling Ngam are steep.
