Why go: riding on the sand at the quiet end of the island

This is one of the few ways to ride a horse along a Samui beach. The ride starts at stables on the south coast, follows jungle trails and then reaches the sand, where the horses walk in the shallows. Small groups of riders go out with a guide, and every rider has their own horse. Nobody shares a saddle and nobody is led on a rope line unless they want to be.

There are four start times. The 07:00 ride is the coolest and the beach is empty. The 08:30 and 16:00 rides suit families who don't want an early start. The 17:30 ride is timed for the evening light and finishes as the sun goes down over the south-west coast. If you have never ridden, book a group or private lesson. Lessons spend more time on how to sit, steer and stop before you go out on the trail.

Who it suits, and who should choose something else

  • Good for: families with children from about 5–6, couples who want a sunset activity that isn't a boat, and riders of any level. Experienced riders should say so at booking, so they get a livelier horse.
  • Choose something else if: you are pregnant, have back or hip problems, or are well above average weight. The stables ask for weight and height to protect the horses, and they can refuse a booking. A jungle hike or a sunset cruise is a better choice in that case.
  • Staying in Chaweng or the north: the stables are 40–60 minutes away by road, so add the transfer cost and time when you compare options.

Good to know

  • Heat: Samui is above 30 °C most afternoons from March to September. Horses and riders do best at 07:00 and 17:30.
  • Weather: October to December brings heavy showers. Trails turn muddy and rides can be shortened. Book for a morning when the forecast is better.
  • Horse welfare: look at the horses before you mount. They should be alert, with clean tack and no saddle sores. Report anything that worries you to us on WhatsApp.
  • Insurance: check that your travel insurance covers horse riding. Some policies exclude it.
