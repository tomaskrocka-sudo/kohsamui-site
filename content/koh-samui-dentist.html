For toothache on Koh Samui, the private hospitals in Chaweng and Bophut run dental departments, and private dental clinics on the main roads of Chaweng, Bophut, Lamai and Maenam see walk-in patients during the day. For planned work, check that the dentist is registered with the Dental Council of Thailand and get a written, itemised quote. Multi-visit treatment such as implants usually needs months between stages, so it rarely fits a 30-day visa-exempt stay, even with the 30-day extension.

Good to know: this page is general information, not medical advice. We don't recommend individual clinics; prices are indicative and change, so always ask for a written quote.

Samui International Hospital (north end of Chaweng Beach Road), Thai International Hospital (Chaweng) and Bandon International Hospital (Bophut) all list dentistry among their services, alongside 24-hour emergency departments. Bangkok Hospital Samui, near Chaweng Noi, is the island's largest private hospital. Addresses, phone numbers and a map are on our Koh Samui hospitals page, and late-opening pharmacies on pharmacies.

Until you see a dentist

  • Paracetamol or ibuprofen, taken as the packet says, for pain (if they are safe for you).
  • Rinse with warm salt water after meals.
  • Avoid very hot, cold or sugary food and drink on the painful side.
  • Don't put aspirin on the gum; it burns the tissue.
  • Pharmacies sell temporary filling kits for a lost filling.

Choosing a clinic

  • Licence: every dentist practising in Thailand must be registered with the Dental Council of Thailand (Thanta Phaetthayasapha). Its website has a public "Check Dentists" search where you can look up a dentist by name. Ask the clinic for the full name of the dentist who will treat you, and look them up before you agree to anything more than an emergency visit.
  • Clinic licence: private clinics must also hold a licence under Thailand's medical facilities law, usually displayed in reception. [check]
  • English: many Samui clinics work with foreign patients every day, but ask whether the dentist, not only the receptionist, speaks English well enough to explain options and risks.
  • Written quote: ask for an itemised quote in THB before treatment, with the materials and brands (implant system, crown type), the number of visits and what happens if a stage fails.
  • Sterilisation: look for sealed instrument pouches opened in front of you, an autoclave, gloves changed between patients, and a clean surgery.
  • Records: ask for copies of X-rays, photos of the work and a treatment summary in English; your dentist at home will need them.
  • Specialists: for implants, root canals on molars and surgical extractions, ask whether a specialist does the work and how many such cases they do.

Be wary of high-pressure selling: a check-up that turns into a plan for ten crowns is a reason to get a second opinion, either on the island or at home.

Prices vary with the dentist's experience, the materials and the complexity of the case. A cheap implant quote may use an unknown implant system that dentists at home can't service; ask which brand it is.

Planning dental work around your trip

Most Western visitors now get 30 days visa-free (since 15 September 2026), which can be extended once at Koh Samui Immigration for up to 30 more days (form TM.7, 1,900 THB, at the officer's discretion). Fit the treatment to the time you have:

TreatmentFits a 30-day stay?
Cleaning, fillings, simple extractionsYes, in one or two visits
Crowns and bridgesUsually, with about a week between impressions and fitting; allow time for adjustments
Root canal treatmentUsually, if started early in the trip
VeneersSometimes, for a small number of teeth; allow two weeks
ImplantsRarely: the implant usually needs 3–6 months to heal before the crown, so you need two trips or a long-stay visa

Book the first appointment for your first days on the island, not the last, so there is time to fix problems before you fly. For longer treatment, read our visa extension guide and the Thailand visa guide; the Destination Thailand Visa lists medical treatment among its eligible purposes. For a stay of a month or more, a villa with a kitchen is easier on a sore mouth than restaurant food: see our long-stay guide and villas.

Flying, diving and boat trips after dental work

  • Flying: after a routine filling or cleaning you can fly the same day. After an extraction or surgery, ask your dentist; many advise waiting at least 24 hours, longer after surgery, because pressure changes can cause pain or bleeding.
  • Diving: divers are generally advised not to dive for several days to a week after an extraction, and longer after implant or sinus surgery, because of the risk of infection and pressure problems. Air trapped under a temporary filling or crown can cause pain at depth. Ask your dentist for a specific date before you book a dive trip.
  • Snorkelling and swimming: keep an extraction site out of the sea for at least a few days to reduce infection risk.
  • Speedboat trips: fine after routine work. After surgery, a bumpy crossing and a day in the sun can make swelling worse; a calm half day is easier.
  • Alcohol and smoking: avoid both for at least 24–72 hours after an extraction; they slow healing and raise the risk of a dry socket.

When you are ready, the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, all year) is a short, sheltered half day. Diving plans: diving from Koh Samui.

Insurance and receipts

  • Travel insurance usually covers only emergency dental treatment for sudden pain, often up to a low limit. Planned work is almost never covered.
  • Call your insurer before treatment if the bill will be large, and ask whether a guarantee or pre-approval is needed.
  • Ask for an itemised receipt in English showing each tooth, the treatment and the price, plus the dentist's name and licence number.
  • Some home health insurers and tax systems reimburse treatment abroad; keep X-rays and the treatment plan for them.

Is dental work on holiday worth it?

It can be, for straightforward treatment you have planned with your own records in hand. The honest trade-offs:

  • Lower prices than most Western countries for cleaning, fillings and crowns.
  • Short waiting times; appointments are often available the same week.
  • Modern equipment in the better clinics and hospital departments.
  • Follow-up care is far away if something goes wrong after you fly home.
  • Implants and large bridges need several stages and months of healing.
  • Your dentist at home may be reluctant to repair work done abroad, especially implants from an unfamiliar system.
  • Sore teeth, antibiotics and no swimming can take a bite out of a beach holiday.

If you go ahead, keep the treatment simple, start early in the trip and leave a few days free before your flight.

Practical tips

  • Bring your recent X-rays and treatment history from home, especially for crowns, bridges or implants.
  • Take a list of your medicines, including blood thinners; tell the dentist before any extraction.
  • Ask whether the quote includes follow-up checks, stitches removal, X-rays and medicines, or whether they are charged separately.
  • Card payments are widely accepted at clinics and hospitals; some add a card fee. See banks and money.
  • Get there by Grab or taxi after sedation or surgery, not by scooter; see getting around Koh Samui.
