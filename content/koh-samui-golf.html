Koh Samui has one full golf course, Santiburi Samui Country Club in the hills above Mae Nam: 18 holes, par 72, 6,930 yards, where a round costs about 5,600 THB for 18 holes or 2,825 THB for 9, with caddie and cart included. There is also the 9-hole Royal Samui Golf & Country Club in the hills between Bophut, Chaweng and Lamai, with no caddies and unpublished fees. Tee off at about 07:00 to beat the heat, and play February to April for the driest weather. For racket sports, the island now has at least four padel clubs at about 600–1,200 THB per court per hour; tennis courts are mostly at resorts.

Santiburi Samui Country Club

Samui's only championship course opened in 2003 on the slopes behind Mae Nam and was designed by Edward Thiele and Pirapon Namatra. It hosted the Bangkok Airways Open in 2006. The course is hilly, with big elevation changes, water hazards and several holes looking out over the Gulf of Thailand towards Koh Phangan; from the back (gold) tees it plays 6,930 yards, with blue tees at 6,506, white at 5,792 and red at 4,717. It is open to non-members every day, all year, from about 06:00 to 18:30, with first tee times around 07:00.

  • Green fees: about 5,600 THB for 18 holes and 2,825 THB for 9 holes, the same on weekdays and weekends, with caddie and cart included. One 2026 listing quotes a range of 3,500–6,000 THB depending on tee time and season, so ask about early and late rates when you book.
  • Carts and caddies: compulsory, because of the terrain; both are included in the green fee.
  • Rental clubs: available at the pro shop.
  • Driving range: 5 bays; a bucket of 30 balls costs about 100 THB.
  • Clubhouse and pro shop on site.

Royal Samui Golf & Country Club

A 9-hole hillside course founded in 2006 by a small group of golf enthusiasts, in the hills between Bophut, Chaweng and Lamai, with spectacular sea views. Play it twice for 18 holes. There are no caddies: you drive your own cart and pick your own clubs and lines, and clubs can be borrowed. Visitors are welcome daily, but the fee isn't published; reviewers paid about 3,800–5,000 THB for two players in 2019–2020, including cart. Honest notes from those reviews: the course is short and very hilly with blind shots, the greens and bunkers were poorly kept, an unfinished clubhouse stands on site, and the access road is best tackled in a 4x4. A golf tour operator still lists it in its Samui packages and advises checking conditions first, especially in the rainy season. Current fees and opening days weren't published at the time of writing, so ring ahead before you go.

Tee times and the best season

Book the first tee times, around 07:00, or the late-afternoon slots. From May to September midday temperatures reach 32–33 °C with high humidity, and an 18-hole round in the hills takes about 4–4.5 hours. February to April is the driest period, with the calmest weather; it is also the hottest by April. October to December is the rainy season, with the most rain in November: rounds are often interrupted by downpours, and the hill fairways get slippery. Golf tour operators call November to March the coolest period. Check the sky before you leave: [kp_weather_now]. See Koh Samui weather by month.

Playing in the heat and the rain

  • Drink before you're thirsty: a litre every 4–5 holes is not too much from April to September, and add electrolyte sachets from any 7-Eleven.
  • Cover up: a wide-brimmed hat or cap with a neck flap, long-sleeved sun shirt and reef-safe sunscreen; the hill holes have little shade.
  • Use the cart: walking Santiburi's slopes in the heat is not an option, which is why carts are compulsory.
  • Lightning: from October to December, storms build fast in the afternoon. Leave the course at the first thunder; caddies know the shelters.
  • Wet fairways: after heavy rain, expect little roll, plugged lies and slippery cart paths on the steep holes.
  • Spare glove and towel: the humidity soaks both by the turn.

Planning a golf trip to Samui

With one 18-hole course and one 9-hole course, Samui suits a holiday with a few rounds rather than a golf marathon. A typical plan is two or three rounds at Santiburi, perhaps one at Royal Samui, and the days between on the beach or the water. Golf tour operators sell 5-day, 4-night Samui packages with two rounds from about 18,000 THB per golfer, including hotel and transfers. If you are travelling on to Bangkok, Hua Hin or Phuket, keep the serious golf for there and treat Samui as the relaxing part of the trip. Stay in Mae Nam, Bophut or Bang Rak to be close to Santiburi: see where to stay on Koh Samui.

What to bring for a round

  • Your own clubs if you plan more than one round; check your airline's sports-equipment allowance.
  • Soft-spike golf shoes (required at Santiburi) and a spare pair of socks.
  • Two gloves, a towel and a hat; rain gear from October to December.
  • Cash for the caddie tip and drinks.
  • Your handicap card, if you have one.

Golf etiquette and dress

  • Collared shirt, tailored shorts or trousers; no swimwear, vests or denim.
  • Soft spikes only on Santiburi's course.
  • Bring a hat, sunscreen and at least two litres of water per player; drinks carts and halfway stops may be limited.
  • Let your caddie read the greens: they know the slopes.
  • Tip your caddie in cash at the end of the round; ask the pro shop what is usual.
  • Pace of play: keep up with the group ahead, especially on busy high-season mornings.

Getting there

Santiburi's course is in the Ban Don Sai hills above Mae Nam on the north coast. Allow about 10–15 minutes from Maenam, 15–30 minutes from Bophut or Chaweng depending on traffic, and 40 minutes or more from Lamai or the west coast. There's no public transport to the clubhouse: take a taxi or Grab, drive yourself, or book a private van, which can wait while you play (our van with driver costs 2,500 THB for 4 hours or 3,200 THB for 6, for up to 10 people). The road up to Royal Samui is steep; go by car or 4x4, not scooter. More options in getting around Koh Samui and car rental.

Practice, lessons and golf for non-golfers

Santiburi's driving range is the easy way to try golf without a full round: a bucket of 30 balls costs about 100 THB. The 9-hole option there suits beginners and anyone who wants to be back at the pool by lunchtime. Ask the pro shop about lessons with the resident professional when you book. Non-playing partners can ride in the cart with a player, though Santiburi's views are best enjoyed from the clubhouse terrace.

Tennis

Most tennis courts on Koh Samui belong to resorts and private villas, including several large resorts (the Four Seasons at Laem Yai is one), and some larger villas have their own court. Public, pay-by-the-hour tennis courts are rare. If tennis matters on your trip, choose a hotel or villa with its own court, and ask other resorts whether non-guests can book a court and hire rackets; many padel clubs below also run social sessions. Play before 10:00 or after 16:00: hard courts get very hot in the middle of the day.

Padel

Padel has taken off on Samui, with clubs on the north, east and west coasts. Courts are usually booked through the Playtomic app or on WhatsApp, and most clubs run beginner classes and social "Americano" tournaments.

Golf on a budget

Golf is one of the pricier activities on Samui, but there are ways to keep the cost down. Play 9 holes at Santiburi (about 2,825 THB, caddie and cart included) rather than 18; ask about twilight or early rates, which some listings quote from about 3,500 THB; share a private van between four golfers rather than taking four taxis; and bring your own clubs if you play more than one round. Practice at the range costs about 100 THB a bucket. Royal Samui's reported fees for two players made it the cheaper option in recent years, though at a lower standard of course.

Other sports and fitness on Samui

Between rounds, Samui has gyms in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai, Muay Thai camps for complete beginners, yoga studios and retreats, and calm early-morning water for paddleboarding. See Muay Thai on Koh Samui, retreats and kayak and SUP.

Rainy-day and family plans

When a downpour stops play, the covered padel courts in Bophut and Lamai keep going, and a Thai cooking class or a Muay Thai session fills a wet afternoon: see our cooking class guide and Muay Thai on Koh Samui.
