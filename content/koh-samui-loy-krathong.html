Loy Krathong falls on Tuesday 24 November 2026, the full moon of the twelfth Thai lunar month and the same night as the Full Moon Party on Koh Phangan; some English-language sites wrongly give 25 November. On Samui, people float krathongs on Chaweng Lake, at the Big Buddha and on beaches and hotel ponds from about 19:00. Sky lanterns are banned on Samui, so float a bread or banana-leaf krathong instead, never a foam one.

Loy Krathong is not a public holiday: shops, banks and government offices stay open, and there is no alcohol-sales ban. Celebrations happen in the evening, after dark.

What Loy Krathong means

A krathong is a small floating basket, traditionally made from a slice of banana trunk wrapped in folded banana leaves and decorated with flowers, three incense sticks and a candle. On the night of the twelfth full moon, Thais float them on rivers, lakes and the sea to thank Phra Mae Khongkha, the goddess of water, and to ask forgiveness for polluting it. Floating a krathong also means letting go: of bad luck, grudges and the past year's troubles. Many people add a coin, or a strand of hair or a nail clipping, before setting it on the water. Couples who float a krathong together believe they will stay together if it drifts away without sinking.

The festival is usually traced to the old kingdom of Sukhothai in the 13th–14th centuries. On Samui it is a family night, quieter than the big northern celebrations in Chiang Mai and Sukhothai, and much more local. More background: Thai culture and traditions.

Where to celebrate on Samui

Chaweng Lake

Chaweng Lake, behind central Chaweng and opposite Central Samui, is the island's main public Loy Krathong spot. Locals gather on the banks from early evening, stalls sell krathongs and food, and the lake fills with candles. It is easy to reach from Chaweng, Bophut and Choeng Mon, and it is busiest from about 19:00 to 21:00. Directions to Chaweng Lake.

Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai)

The temple of the 12 m golden Big Buddha, on a small island joined to Samui by a causeway near Bang Rak, holds a quieter, more spiritual celebration. Families make merit at the temple and float krathongs in the sea from the beach around the causeway. Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) if you go up to the temple. Beaches

Any calm beach works. Lamai, Bophut (in front of Fisherman's Village), Maenam and Bang Rak all see locals floating krathongs at the water's edge. In November the east coast can have strong waves; if the sea is rough, krathongs wash straight back and the shore can be dangerous, so choose a sheltered north-coast beach or the lake. Never wade out after dark in the monsoon.

Hotel events

Many resorts hold their own Loy Krathong evening, usually with a krathong-making session in the afternoon, Thai dancing and a buffet, and floating on the hotel pond or beach. They are a good choice with young children or in heavy rain. Ask at reception what is planned for 24 November, and whether outside guests can join.

How to float a krathong

  1. Afternoon Buy a krathong from a stall, a market or your hotel, or make one at a workshop. Bread and banana-leaf ones are the best choice.
  2. Before you go Add a small coin and, if you like, a strand of hair or a nail clipping for luck.
  3. At the water Light the candle and the three incense sticks.
  4. Make a wish Hold the krathong, close your eyes and make a wish or say thank you.
  5. Let it go Set it gently on the water and give it a small push. Don't throw it.
  6. Watch Stay until it drifts away. Don't fish out other people's krathongs, even for photos.

Eco krathong and the lantern ban

Every year Thai cities collect hundreds of thousands of krathongs from their waterways the next morning, and foam ones take decades to break down. On Samui, choose:

  • Bread krathongs: fish eat them within hours.
  • Banana-trunk and banana-leaf krathongs: fully biodegradable; pick ones fixed with toothpicks rather than metal pins.
  • One krathong per family or couple: the tradition does not need one each.
  • Foam (polystyrene) krathongs: avoid them completely.
  • Plastic decorations and glitter: they end up on the reef.
  • Sky lanterns (khom loi): banned on Koh Samui because of the fire risk. Don't buy or release one, even if someone offers.

Loy Krathong and the Full Moon Party on the same night

In 2026 Loy Krathong and the Full Moon Party are both on Tuesday 24 November, and they are expected to coincide again on Saturday 13 November 2027. From Samui you can do both:

  1. 18:30–20:00 Float a krathong at Chaweng Lake, the Big Buddha or your hotel.

Weather on Loy Krathong night

November is Samui's wettest month: about 445 mm of rain over 16 days on average, with the north-east monsoon bringing waves to the east coast. Plan for rain. Celebrations go ahead in light showers, and people float krathongs under umbrellas; in heavy rain the lake crowds thin out, and hotel events move indoors. Bring a light rain jacket and shoes that can get wet. If the sea is unsafe, Full Moon boats are rescheduled, and we offer a full refund or a new date.

Loy Krathong etiquette

  • It is a respectful festival, not a party: keep your voice down at the water's edge and at temples.
  • Cover shoulders and knees at temples, and take your shoes off before entering any temple building.
  • Don't step over or kick krathongs on the shore, and don't take them out of the water.
  • Children throw firecrackers on Loy Krathong night; keep a distance and watch small children's feet.
  • Ask before photographing people making wishes, and don't use a flash in their faces.
  • Don't swim among the krathongs: candles, incense and pins make it unsafe.
  • Collect your own litter: plates, cups and plastic bags from the food stalls end up in the lake and the sea if left on the bank.
