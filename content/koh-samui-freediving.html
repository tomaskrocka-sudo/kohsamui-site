You can freedive from Koh Samui, but there are few dedicated freediving schools on the island: most are on Koh Tao (1.5–2 hours by fast boat) and Koh Phangan (20–50 minutes). A 1-day try-out costs about 3,800–4,500 THB in 2026, and a 2–3 day beginner course that certifies you to 10–20 m costs about 8,000–8,750 THB. A few Samui dive centres run the PADI Freediver course with pool training on Samui and sea sessions at Sail Rock or Koh Tao. Sail Rock, a pinnacle that drops to about 40 m between Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, is the region's main deep site. Never freedive alone.

Can you freedive on Koh Samui?

Yes, but be realistic about where. Samui's own reefs off Coral Cove, Silver Beach and Chaweng Noi are shallow, which is fine for breath-hold practice while snorkelling but not for depth training. The deep water that freediving schools need, with a buoy line over 20–40 m of clear sea, is around Sail Rock, Koh Tao and the north of Koh Phangan. So you have three choices: take a course with one of the few Samui operators that runs sea sessions at Sail Rock or Koh Tao; base yourself on Koh Tao or Koh Phangan for 2–4 nights to do a course; or, if you're already certified, join a boat day to Sail Rock from Samui and freedive with a buddy. If you only have a day, a snorkel trip with breath-hold dives on a shallow reef is the honest best.

What a beginner course involves

  1. Day 1, morning Theory: how breath-hold works, relaxation and breathing, equalising your ears, safety and buddy rules.
  2. Day 1, afternoon Pool: static breath-hold lying face down, dynamic swims with fins, rescue drills.
  3. Day 2 Open water: line diving on a buoy, duck dives, free immersion down the rope, constant-weight dives towards 10–16 m, rescue from 5 m.
  4. Day 3 (if needed) More sea time to reach the depth requirement and a short exam.

Many beginners hold their breath far longer than they expected by the end of the first pool session. Depth is limited by equalisation more than by breath, so practise clearing your ears on the way down.

Freediving day trips from Samui

If you're already certified, or want breath-hold practice while snorkelling, these day boats from Samui reach the best water:

  • Sail Rock or Koh Tao by fast catamaran: 2,900 THB for a snorkeller, with two stops, lunch and transfer from Bophut, Chaweng, Bang Rak and Choeng Mon; Sail Rock days are Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday. See the catamaran day.
  • Shared dive and snorkel speedboat: 2,950 THB for snorkellers, with breakfast, lunch and transfer from the main beach areas; the site rotates between Koh Tao, Sail Rock and Chumphon Pinnacle. See the speedboat day.
  • Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan: our speedboat day trip (2,200 THB) snorkels the clear, shallow Japanese Gardens and two Koh Tao bays. Fins are banned at Nang Yuan, so it's breath-hold practice without fins.
  • Private speedboat: your own boat and timing, from 22,000 THB for up to 4 to Koh Phangan's reefs or 32,000 THB to Koh Tao. See the private snorkel boat.

On shared dive boats, freedivers share the site with scuba groups. Bring your own buoy and line only with the skipper's agreement, stay away from divers' bubbles and the boat's propeller, and always dive with a certified buddy.

Season and visibility

The water is 28–30 °C all year, so a thin wetsuit is for sun and stingers, not warmth. Visibility is the variable, and local sources disagree a little because conditions change with plankton, rain and wind:

Safety: never alone

Freediving is safe when done with the rules and dangerous without them. The main risk is a blackout near the surface at the end of a dive, which is silent and can happen to fit, experienced divers.

  • Always dive with a trained buddy who watches you from the surface for your whole dive and the 30 seconds after.
  • One up, one down. Never dive at the same time as your buddy.
  • Never hyperventilate before a dive; it delays the urge to breathe without adding oxygen.
  • Don't freedive after scuba on the same day; ask your instructor about the gap before a flight.
  • Use a buoy and line in open water so boats can see you.
  • Stop at the first sign of ear pain and never force equalisation.
  • Medical: you'll fill in a health questionnaire; asthma, heart conditions, epilepsy and recent ear or sinus problems need a doctor's sign-off.

Emergency: ambulance 1669. Samui has private hospitals with 24-hour emergency rooms; see hospitals on Koh Samui. Check that your travel insurance covers freediving; many policies limit depth.

Where to stay for a course

For a course on Koh Tao, stay 2–4 nights near your school (Sairee or Chalok Baan Kao are typical) and come back to Samui afterwards; schools often help with accommodation. For a Koh Phangan school, the north coast near Chaloklum is closest to the boats. If you'd rather stay on Samui, choose the north or east coast (Bophut, Bang Rak, Chaweng, Choeng Mon), where the day boats to Sail Rock and Koh Tao collect from: see where to stay on Koh Samui. Remember there is no one-way island hopping on our tour boats; book a ferry for a stay on another island.

What to bring

  • Swimwear and a rash vest or thin wetsuit (schools usually lend wetsuits, long fins, low-volume masks and weights).
  • Your own low-volume mask if you have one that fits.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen, a towel and a warm layer for the boat.
  • A water bottle and a light breakfast: train on an empty-ish stomach.
  • Seasickness tablets that don't make you drowsy, if needed, cleared with your instructor.
  • Your certification card or app for fun freedives.

Freediving, snorkelling or scuba?

Snorkelling is the easiest way to see the reef and costs least. Freediving teaches you to go down and stay calm on one breath; it is quiet, needs little gear and lets you get close to fish that scuba bubbles scare away. Scuba keeps you down longest and is the only way to spend 45 minutes at Sail Rock's deeper walls. Many people combine a snorkel day from Samui with a freediving course on Koh Tao or Koh Phangan.
