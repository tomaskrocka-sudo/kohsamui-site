Is there buffalo fighting on Koh Samui? | Traditionally yes: buffalo fights have been held at small village arenas on Samui, especially around New Year and Songkran. However, we found no reliable source confirming a venue or date in 2025 or 2026, so any listing you see online is likely to be out of date.
Is buffalo fighting legal in Thailand? | The Animal Welfare Act of 2014 exempts traditional local contests such as bullfighting and cockfighting from the cruelty offence, provided they follow the official rules, so traditional animal fights can be legal. Betting on them is allowed only under official permits; informal betting is illegal.
When are buffalo fights held on Koh Samui? | Historically on special occasions, particularly New Year's Day and Songkran in April, and announced locally only a few days ahead on banners, loudspeaker trucks and Thai social media. There is no fixed public schedule.
Can tourists attend a buffalo fight? | Historically visitors could pay to watch, and fights were open to anyone who turned up. We don't recommend it: betting is central to the event, safety at village arenas is basic, and animal welfare groups oppose animal fighting. Samui has many other ways to see local culture.

Are buffalo fights the same as bullfights in Thailand? | They are similar. In both, two animals of the same kind push head to head until one runs, with no matador and no weapons, and betting is central. Bullfighting with cattle is better known in the southern mainland provinces; on Samui the tradition used water buffalo.
