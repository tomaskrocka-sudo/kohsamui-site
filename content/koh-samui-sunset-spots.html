The sun sets over the sea on Koh Samui's west and north-west coasts: Taling Ngam (with the Five Islands offshore), Lipa Noi, Nathon pier, Laem Yai and Bang Por. On the north coast, Bophut and Big Buddha Beach watch the sky colour over Koh Phangan while the sun drops behind the headlands. Chaweng and Lamai face sunrise, not sunset. Sunset falls between about 17:56 (mid-November) and 18:45 (mid-July), and February–April gives the clearest evenings.

We compare 12 places below: 10 on land, one hotel sky bar and one from a boat. Each entry says whether you can swim there too, whether it costs anything and how hard it is to get home afterwards, which on the quiet west coast matters more than most people expect.

Sunset time by month

Why the sun sets in a different place each month

The point on the horizon where the sun sets swings about 47° through the year, from roughly 246° (west-south-west) in December to 294° (west-north-west) in June and July. This decides which beaches get a true "sun into the sea" view:

  • West coast (Taling Ngam, Lipa Noi, Nathon): faces west, so the sun sets over open water all year.
  • North-west (Laem Yai, Bang Por): best April–September, when the sun sets furthest north over the sea. In December–January it sets further south and can drop behind the Laem Yai headland.
  • North coast (Maenam, Bophut, Bang Rak): the sun usually sets behind land or headlands. You come here for the pink and orange sky over Koh Phangan, plus dinner on the sand.
  • East coast (Chaweng, Lamai, Choeng Mon): sunrise only.

West coast

This is where the sun goes into the sea. The west coast is also Samui's quietest side: fewer restaurants, almost no nightlife and few taxis, so plan the ride home before you go.

Taling Ngam and the Five Islands

Taling Ngam (9.4554, 99.9347) is a long, quiet village beach with fishing boats pulled up on the sand and the Five Islands (Koh Si Koh Ha), where swiftlet nests are harvested, offshore. It is our pick for the best sunset on Samui: the sea is usually calm, the islands make a silhouette, and there is nothing between you and the horizon. Swimming depends on the tide and the water is shallow. It is 35–45 minutes from Chaweng. See Taling Ngam.

Lipa Noi

Lipa Noi (9.5007, 99.9344) has very shallow, calm water and wide sand flats at low tide, which turn into a mirror at sunset. It is the best sunset beach for families with toddlers, because you can paddle while you watch. Swim at high tide; at low tide you walk a long way to get wet. The Raja car-ferry pier and a large beach club are nearby. See Lipa Noi.

Nathon pier (free)

Nathon, the island's original capital and main ferry port, has a free sunset from its long town pier, where local families and fishermen gather in the evening. Combine it with the daily night food market (about 16:00–22:00) on the seafront. It is the easiest west-coast sunset to get home from, because Nathon is a town with some taxis and songthaews in the early evening. See Nathon.

Laem Yai

Laem Yai (9.5696, 99.9163) is the north-west cape: very quiet, with rocks and pebbles, clear water for snorkelling and part of the shore taken by resort frontage. From April to September the sun sets over open water; in mid-winter it may set behind the headland. Swimming is poor at low tide. See Laem Yai.

Bang Por

Bang Por (9.5781, 99.9369) is a quiet north-west beach known for local seafood restaurants with tables near the sand, which makes it the classic "sunset and dinner" stop. Swim at high tide only: at low tide a stony flat reaches 50 m or more, so wear reef shoes. It is 10–15 minutes from Maenam. See Bang Por.

North coast

The north coast faces Koh Phangan. The sun usually sets behind the island's own headlands, but the sky over Phangan turns pink and orange, the restaurants are close by, and getting home is easy.

Bophut and Fisherman's Village (view to Phangan)

Bophut's old wooden shophouses line the beach, with restaurant terraces right above the sand. Watch the afterglow over Koh Phangan, then stay for dinner; on Fridays the Walking Street starts around 17:00. The sea here is not good for swimming (cloudy water, jellyfish reported). See Fisherman's Village.

Bang Rak and Big Buddha

Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach, 9.5572, 100.0533) is the pier beach next to the airport, with seafood tables on the sand and the golden Big Buddha at the east end of the bay. The Big Buddha terrace (open 07:00–18:30, free) looks west across the bay; from November to January sunset falls between 17:56 and 18:18, so you can catch it from the steps before closing time. Not a swimming beach (muddy shallows). See Big Buddha and Big Buddha Beach.

Maenam

Maenam's long, palm-lined beach faces north. From April to September the sun sets to the west-north-west along the curve of the bay; in winter it drops behind land. It is calm for swimming in the dry season and has cheap beachfront restaurants. See Maenam.

South-west: Thong Krut and Laem Sor

Thong Krut, the pier village where boats leave for Pig Island and Koh Tan, is on the sunset side of the island and has a few simple restaurants by the water. It is quiet and authentic, but not a swimming beach. Laem Sor Pagoda on the south coast closes at 18:00, before sunset for most of the year, so visit it for late-afternoon light and then drive 10–15 minutes to Thong Krut or Taling Ngam for the sunset itself. See Thong Krut and Laem Sor Pagoda.

Hilltop and rooftop sunsets

The cliff-top resorts above Taling Ngam and the north-west coast have sunset bars that non-guests can use, usually from about 17:00. The best known is the Air Bar at the InterContinental on Taling Ngam: the bar deck is first come, first served, while its sunset sofa beds require a booking at least 4 hours ahead with a prepaid minimum spend of 4,000 THB++. The Conrad, also above Taling Ngam, and the W in Maenam have sunset bars too. Expect hotel prices for drinks, a smart-casual dress code and a hotel taxi home. We don't link to hotels; book directly with them.

Sunset bars on the sand

Laem Yai, Lipa Noi and Thong Krut have small, family-run beach bars and restaurants that set out tables for sunset. They change name, owner and opening days often, and many close on one weekday or during the November rains, so we don't name them here until we have checked them in person for the current season. Practical tip: message or call ahead before driving 40 minutes across the island for a particular bar, and have a back-up beach nearby.

Sunset dinners: seafood at Bang Por and Bang Rak

The two easiest sunset-and-dinner combinations are the local seafood restaurants at Bang Por on the north-west coast and the tables on the sand at Bang Rak near the Big Buddha. Fish is sold by weight: ask the price per kilo before you choose, and check whether rice and vegetables are extra. Our guides to Koh Samui seafood and restaurants have more.

Sunset and the tide

On the shallow west coast the tide changes the view completely. At low tide Lipa Noi and Bang Por expose wide wet flats that reflect the sky, which is beautiful for photos but means a long walk to swimmable water. At high tide the water comes up to the sand and you can swim until the sun goes down. Check the tide before you choose: [kp_tide_now] Full tide tables are on Koh Samui tides.

Prefer sunrise?

If you are staying on the east coast, the sunrise is right outside your door. Sunrise is about 06:00 in May–June and 06:40 in January–February. The best sunrise spots are Chaweng beach, Lamai, the boulders of Silver Beach, the Lad Koh viewpoint (stay on the platform) and Choeng Mon. Sunrise also means empty beaches and cooler air for a walk; see hiking and viewpoints.

Best months for sunsets

February to April gives the clearest skies and the calmest sea, with only 4–6 rain days a month. May to September is good too, with occasional afternoon showers that often clear for dramatic skies. October to December is the north-east monsoon: November averages about 445 mm of rain over 16 rain days, and many evenings are clouded out. January is a good compromise, with 8 rain days and cooler air. More in Koh Samui weather by month.

Rainy-season sunsets

From October to December the west coast is actually the sheltered side, because the monsoon wind blows from the north-east onto Chaweng and Lamai. On the west coast the sea can be flat while the east coast flies red flags. Clouds are the problem, not waves: watch for gaps on the western horizon around 17:00 and have a back-up plan, such as the Nathon night market, if the sky closes in. Rain often comes in short, heavy bursts, followed by some of the most colourful skies of the year.

Sunset on special nights

Loy Krathong falls on Tuesday 24 November 2026, the full-moon night of the 12th lunar month. After sunset, people float small decorated baskets (krathong) on the sea, rivers and ponds to make a wish; Nathon pier and the beaches are good places to watch. The Full Moon Party on Koh Phangan falls on the same night; if you are going, see our Full Moon Party speedboat (1,400 THB). Choose krathongs made of banana leaf or bread, not foam.

Free sunset spots on a budget

Every beach and pier on Samui is public, so a sunset costs nothing but the ride. The best free options are Nathon pier (with cheap food at the night market a few steps away), Lipa Noi and Taling Ngam beaches, the Big Buddha terrace before 18:30 and the sand at Bang Rak. Bring your own drinks from a 7-Eleven if you like; alcohol sales are allowed from 11:00 to 24:00, but don't leave bottles or cans on the beach. A beach-bar cocktail costs far less than a hotel sky bar, and the view is the same sea.

Dusk practicalities: mosquitoes, light and swimming

  • Mosquitoes: they bite most around dusk. Wear repellent and light long sleeves for the walk back; dengue is present in Thailand year-round.
  • Light: twilight is short this close to the equator, about 20–25 minutes after sunset. Bring a phone torch for the path back to the road.
  • Swimming: don't expect lifeguards on the quiet west-coast beaches. Don't swim alone after dark, and check for jellyfish warnings; vinegar is the first aid for box jellyfish stings.
  • Valuables: keep bags in sight when you go into the water at golden hour.

Sunrise coast or sunset coast: where to stay

If watching the sunset every evening matters, stay on the west coast at Lipa Noi or Taling Ngam, or on the north-west at Bang Por or Laem Yai. You'll get quiet beaches and spectacular evenings, but you'll be 40–50 minutes from the airport, with fewer restaurants and almost no nightlife. Stay on the north coast (Bophut, Maenam, Bang Rak) for a compromise: a sunset-coloured sky over Koh Phangan and plenty to do. Chaweng, Lamai and Choeng Mon give you sunrises, the most restaurants and the easiest transport. Compare areas on where to stay on Koh Samui.

Sunset with children

Lipa Noi is the best choice for young children: shallow, calm water, flat sand and space to run. Bang Rak and Bophut are easy too, with restaurants a few steps from the sand. The west coast's long drive home after dark is the main drawback with tired children, so book a driver for a fixed time. Bring repellent: mosquitoes are most active around dusk. A west-coast sunset afternoon

  1. 14:30 Drive to the south-west via Hua Thanon and Route 4169.
  2. 15:15 Laem Sor Pagoda in the afternoon light (closes 18:00).
  3. 16:15 Swim at Taling Ngam or Lipa Noi at high tide.
  4. 17:30 Find a spot on the sand or at a beach bar; golden hour starts.
  5. 17:56–18:45 Sunset, depending on the month (see the table above).
  6. 18:45 Dinner at the Nathon night market or a seafood restaurant at Bang Por, then your pre-booked driver home.

Getting back after dark

This is the part most visitors get wrong. West-coast taxis are scarce, Grab coverage is weak outside Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai, and songthaews mostly stop between 20:00 and 22:00. The hilly road down to Taling Ngam (Route 4170) is steep and slippery when wet, and long sections are unlit, with potholes after rain.

  • Book a driver for a fixed return time before you leave; a full-day taxi costs about 2,000–3,000 THB.
  • If you ride a scooter, don't drink, wear a helmet (fines up to 2,000 THB per person) and ride slowly.
  • Choose Nathon, Bophut or Bang Rak if you want an easy ride home.

Sunset photo tips

  • Use the table above to see where the sun will set and pick a beach with a foreground in that direction: fishing boats at Taling Ngam, the pier at Nathon, wet sand at Lipa Noi.
  • Arrive 45 minutes early. The best colour is often 10–20 minutes after the sun has gone.
  • On a phone, tap the bright sky to lock exposure, then lower it slightly to keep the colours.
  • Thailand requires drones to be registered. Don't fly over people, temples or resorts.
  • Keep your gear dry: west-coast showers arrive fast in the wet season.
