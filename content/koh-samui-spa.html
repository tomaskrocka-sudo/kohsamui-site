A one-hour Thai massage costs about 300–500 THB on Koh Samui in 2026: 400–600 THB in Chaweng, 300–450 THB in Lamai and 300–400 THB in Bophut and Maenam. An oil massage runs 400–700 THB and a foot massage 300–500 THB. Resort spas charge about 1,800–3,500 THB for an hour and the top spas 2,500–10,000 THB or more. Book a resort or forest spa for the setting and specialist treatments, and a good street shop for value and a daily massage after the beach.

Where the spas are

Street shops cluster along every beach road, in Fisherman's Village and on the lanes behind Chaweng and Lamai. Day spas are mostly in Chaweng and the Lamai hills. The big resort spas sit on the headlands: Taling Ngam and Laem Yai in the west, the north-east tip around Choeng Mon, the Lamai headlands and Laem Set in the south-east.

Types of massage on Koh Samui

Traditional Thai massage

Done fully clothed on a floor mattress, usually in loose cotton trousers and a top that the shop provides. The therapist uses palms, thumbs, elbows, knees and feet to press along energy lines and then moves you through assisted stretches. It is firm and active rather than relaxing in the spa sense, and it is the best choice after a long flight or a day of walking. Say "bao bao" for softer and "nak nak" for harder.

Oil and aromatherapy massage

A Western-style full-body massage with oil on a bed, with long gliding strokes. It is the most relaxing option and the best one if you are new to massage or have any stiffness you don't want stretched.

Foot reflexology

Thirty to sixty minutes in a reclining chair, with pressure on points of the feet and a short lower-leg massage. Good after a day in sandals and easy to fit in while someone else shops.

Herbal compress

Cotton bundles of herbs such as lemongrass, turmeric and kaffir lime are steamed and pressed onto the body, usually after a Thai massage. It is soothing for sore muscles and smells wonderful. Ask for it as a 90-minute combination.

Aloe vera after-sun

Cool aloe gel smoothed onto sunburnt skin without pressure. If you are burnt, ask for this rather than a Thai or oil massage.

Specialist spa treatments

Resort and day spas add hot-stone massage, four-hand massage, body scrubs and wraps with coconut or local herbs, facials, lymphatic drainage and pregnancy massage. For these, a spa with trained therapists is worth the higher price.

Honest trade-offs: some of the best Thai massages on the island are done in plain shops for 400 THB, and some resort treatments are more about the setting than the hands. Shops can be noisy, and the cheapest places reuse towels and mats too quickly. Resort spas are calmer and more consistent, but you pay several times as much for the same hour. Many visitors do both: a shop massage every other day and one spa ritual.

Notable spas on Koh Samui

We don't sell spa treatments yet, so these are named for orientation only. Prices were checked in late 2026.

  • Tamarind Springs (Thong Takian, in the hills behind Lamai): a forest spa built among boulders, with herbal steam caves and plunge pools. Open 09:00–18:00. The Steam and Dream session costs 1,500 THB++ per person; packages with a long massage run from 5,500 THB++, and a two-hour pregnancy massage costs 5,000 THB++. Book ahead.
  • Kamalaya (Laem Set, south-east): a wellness resort whose day passes in 2026 started at 1,800 THB++ with access to the pools and wellness facilities, rising to 5,300 THB++ with a spa treatment. Day-pass offers change with the season, so check before you go.
  • Absolute Sanctuary (near Choeng Mon): a wellness resort 5 minutes from the airport with a detox and spa centre.
  • Resort spas at Six Senses Samui, Banyan Tree, the Four Seasons and the other large resorts offer full menus and spa suites for couples; most accept outside guests by booking.

Saunas and herbal steam

Samui doesn't have a sauna culture like Finland's, but herbal steam is part of Thai traditional medicine. The reliable places in 2026 are the spas: Tamarind Springs' herbal steam caves (the Steam and Dream session, 1,500 THB++), Kamalaya's herbal steam caverns on its day passes, and steam rooms at several wellness resorts and larger hotels for their guests. Some gyms and resorts have dry saunas and ice baths for recovery. We could not confirm a public village herbal sauna operating on Samui in 2026, so treat any older listing with caution. Steam is not advised in pregnancy, with heart conditions or after alcohol; drink plenty of water before and after.

How to choose a good massage shop

  • Clean linen and covered mats. Look for fresh towels and covers on every mattress, and a sink where therapists wash their hands and feet between clients.
  • A price board. Reputable shops show prices by treatment and duration at the door.
  • Training. Many therapists display certificates from recognised schools, such as the Wat Pho school in Bangkok. Ask how long the therapist has worked; experience matters more than décor.
  • Licence. Massage and spa businesses in Thailand must be licensed by the provincial public health office, and good shops display the licence [check].
  • A short health question. A good therapist asks about injuries, pregnancy and pressure before starting.
  • Clear boundaries. Most Samui shops are entirely professional. If a shop has staff calling out from the doorway late at night or offers "special" services, it isn't a massage shop: walk on.

Health cautions

  • Pregnancy: don't have a traditional Thai massage while pregnant. Book a dedicated pregnancy massage at a spa with trained therapists, and check with your doctor first.
  • Injuries and conditions: tell the therapist about recent surgery, slipped discs, osteoporosis, varicose veins, blood clots or blood-thinning medication; skip deep pressure and stretching if in doubt.
  • Sunburn: ask for aloe after-sun, not a Thai or oil massage.
  • After eating or drinking: wait an hour after a big meal, and don't book a massage or steam after alcohol.
  • Pain: Thai massage can be firm but should never be sharply painful. Say "jep" (it hurts) and the therapist will ease off.

Beach massage

On Chaweng and Lamai, women with mats and oils work from shaded salas or under the trees at the back of the beach, at similar prices to the shops (about 300–500 THB an hour). It is a lovely way to spend an afternoon, with two catches: wind blows sand onto oil, and mats are cleaned less often than in a shop. Bring your own sarong to lie on, choose Thai massage over oil on windy days, and avoid the midday sun.

Spa etiquette and tipping

  • Shower before a treatment if you've come from the beach; shops have showers or will wipe your feet.
  • For oil massage you'll be given disposable underwear; therapists drape a towel and uncover only the area being worked on.
  • Arrive 10 minutes early at spas; late arrivals usually lose time from the treatment.
  • Tipping is customary at shops: about 50–100 THB after an hour, or 100–200 THB at mid-range spas. Hand it directly to your therapist, because their basic pay is low. At resort spas the "++" already includes a 10% service charge, so an extra tip is optional.
  • Keep phones on silent, and speak quietly in shared rooms.

Thai massage courses

Several schools and spas on Samui teach Thai massage, from a half-day introduction to multi-day courses, and the best-known certificate courses in the country are run by the Wat Pho school in Bangkok and schools in Chiang Mai. If you want to study for weeks, Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV) covers some courses under its "soft power" track; details are in our Thailand visa guide. Rainy-day wellness

From October to December, when the east-coast sea is rough, Samui's indoor wellness is at its best: a long spa ritual, a yoga class in a covered shala or a few days at a wellness resort. See yoga on Koh Samui, meditation and the retreats hub.

Combine a massage with a day out

A massage is the perfect end to a boat day. The Pig Island speedboat is back by about 14:15 on the morning trip, in time for an afternoon massage, and runs every day of the year. From 16 December, a full day at Ang Thong on the speedboat tour or the boutique yachting day (4,500 THB, park fee included) leaves you ready for a Thai massage in the evening. Ang Thong is closed from 1 November to 15 December. For a hands-on afternoon instead, try a Thai cooking class.
