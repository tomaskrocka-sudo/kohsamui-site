Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday 6 February, the start of the Year of the Goat. Many of Samui's Chinese-Thai families trace their roots to Hainan island, and the celebrations centre on three places: Maenam's Chinese temple and old Chinatown for family-friendly lion and dragon dances, Nathon's Hainanese shrine for the more traditional rites (which have included face-piercing), and the Guan Yu shrine above Hua Thanon. The local programme is announced in January, so confirm times on the island.

Chinese New Year is a family festival first and a public show second. Expect red lanterns on Chinese-owned shops, firecrackers in the early hours, offerings set out on tables in front of homes and businesses, and processions that stop at each one. February is also Samui's driest month, so it is a good time to combine culture with boat days.

Chinese New Year is not a national public holiday in Thailand: banks, government offices, ferries and tours run as normal. Exact procession times are set locally by each shrine committee and usually announced only in late January, on banners and by word of mouth. [check]

Where to celebrate on Koh Samui

[kp_places_map]

Maenam: lion dances and the family option

Maenam, on the north coast, has a Chinese temple near the pier and a short old Chinatown street. In recent years it has hosted the island's most colourful street celebrations: a dragon procession, lion dances, acrobats, music and fireworks, with evening performances. It is the most family-friendly of the three. J&J Street Food on the ring road opposite the Chinese gate is open daily until about midnight for dinner afterwards. See Maenam.

Nathon: the Hainanese shrine

Nathon, the island's old port and administrative town on the west coast, is where Samui's Hainanese trading families settled, and its Chinese shrine holds the most traditional celebration. Processions of lion and dragon dancers stop at offering tables outside homes and shops along the waterfront. Past celebrations have included spirit mediums with pierced cheeks, similar to Phuket's Vegetarian Festival, which some visitors and children find disturbing. Nathon's night market by the pier opens daily from about 16:00. See Nathon.

Guan Yu shrine, Hua Thanon

The Guan Yu shrine stands on the hillside above Hua Thanon, just south of Lamai, crowned by a 16 m bronze statue of the red-faced general Guan Yu with his halberd. It is open daily from about 08:00 to 17:00 with free entry, and it is busiest with worshippers at New Year, when it also holds lion dances and blessings. The statue can frighten small children. See Guan Yu shrine and Hua Thanon.

Resort dinners and galas

Several of Samui's large resorts hold Chinese New Year dinners and lion-dance performances for guests, and some accept outside bookings. Expect set menus with "++" (10 % service and 7 % VAT), and book ahead: Chinese New Year brings a wave of visitors from China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia.

Samui's Hainanese heritage

Samui appears on Chinese sea charts from the 17th century and has traded with Chinese merchants for a long time. Many Samui families have Chinese ancestors, believed to come mostly from Hainan, the large island off southern China, which is why Nathon's shrine is known as the Hainan shrine. The shrines in Nathon and Maenam, and the shophouses of Nathon's old waterfront, are the clearest signs of that history today. Hainanese trading families built much of old Nathon, and many of the town's shops are still run by their descendants. [check]

For the island's wider story, Buddhist and Muslim as well as Chinese, see temples on Koh Samui and Hua Thanon, Samui's Muslim fishing village.

Etiquette at shrines

  • Dress: cover shoulders and knees, as at a Buddhist temple. Red is the lucky colour; black and white are associated with mourning.
  • Incense: you can buy a set at the shrine for a small donation. Light it from the candle, not your own lighter, hold it with both hands, bow three times and place it in the urn.
  • Lion dances: give the dancers space. Offering a red envelope (ang pao) or a green vegetable for the "lion" to take is a lucky gesture.
  • Photos: fine at processions, but ask before close-ups of worshippers, and never photograph spirit mediums in trance with flash.
  • Firecrackers: they are loud and thrown close to crowds. Keep children back and protect their ears.

Food to try

  • Hainanese chicken rice (khao man gai): poached chicken over rice cooked in chicken fat, with ginger and soy sauce; sold all over Samui every day.
  • Khanom kheng (nian gao): the sticky, sweet New Year rice cake, sold at Chinese shops and markets in the days before the holiday.
  • Oranges and tangerines: exchanged as lucky gifts, especially in pairs.
  • Dim sum and roast duck: at Chinese-Thai restaurants in Nathon and Maenam.
  • Vegetarian "jay" food: some families eat plant-based on the first day; look for the yellow flags.

Weather and sea in February

[kp_weather_now]

February is the driest month on Koh Samui: about 64 mm of rain over just 4 days, highs of about 29 °C, the calmest sea of the year and a steady breeze. Every boat trip runs. It is the ideal time for the Ang Thong speedboat tour (1,800 THB adult, 1,400 THB child), open again since 16 December, and the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, 1,250 THB child). Hotel pickup is included from most areas of Koh Samui. Month-by-month detail: Koh Samui weather.

Plan a Chinese New Year weekend

  1. Fri 5 Feb Dinner at a Chinese-Thai restaurant in Nathon; firecrackers after midnight.
  2. Sat 6 Feb, morning Guan Yu shrine above Hua Thanon before the heat, then the fish market and Lamai.
  3. Sat 6 Feb, evening Maenam's lion and dragon dances and street food.
  4. Sun 7 Feb A day at sea: Ang Thong or Pig Island.

Where to stay for Chinese New Year

Maenam and Bophut are the best bases: Maenam's processions are on your doorstep, Fisherman's Village is a short ride away for dinner, and both are quieter than Chaweng. February is peak season and Chinese New Year adds visitors from Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia and China, so book rooms and boat trips early. Nathon has few places to stay; visit for the evening and return to the north coast. See where to stay on Koh Samui.

Makha Bucha alcohol ban, 21 February 2027

Two weeks after Chinese New Year, Sunday 21 February is Makha Bucha, a Buddhist holy day. Shops and 7-Eleven can't sell alcohol, while hotels and licensed bars in tourist areas may still serve. The Full Moon Party on Koh Phangan moves to Monday 22 February because of it. Chinese New Year itself has no alcohol ban. See the party and festival calendar and Koh Samui events.
