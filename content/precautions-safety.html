Koh Samui is generally safe for visitors, and violent crime against tourists is rare. The real risks are scooter crashes, the sea (rip currents and red flags on the east coast from October to December, and box jellyfish) and alcohol-related incidents on party nights. Wear a helmet, swim where the flags say it is safe, and book licensed transport to and from the Full Moon Party. Save two numbers: 1155 for the English-speaking Tourist Police and 1669 for an ambulance.

Good to know: this page was checked in November 2026 against the UK government's Thailand travel advice and Thai official sources. Laws are summarised for general information; they are not legal advice.

Road safety

The roads are the biggest risk on Samui by a wide margin. The UK government notes that Thailand has a high number of road accidents, particularly with motorcycles, and the WHO ranks it among the world's deadliest countries for motorcycle deaths. Hospitals on the island treat scooter injuries every day.

  • Helmets: compulsory for rider and passenger, with fines up to 2,000 THB per person since June 2025. Fasten the strap.
  • Licence and IDP: you need your home licence plus an International Driving Permit, both showing the motorcycle category. Without them you can be fined and your travel insurance is unlikely to pay after a crash.
  • Wet hills: Route 4170 in the south-west and the roads to Na Muang, Overlap Stone and the Secret Buddha Garden are steep, and wet concrete is slippery.
  • Night riding: roads are poorly lit, dogs run out and other riders may be drunk or without lights. Use Grab or a taxi after dark.
  • Passports: never leave your passport as a rental deposit; the UK government warns that businesses use them to force payment for claimed damage.

Sea and beaches

Samui's east-coast beaches, Chaweng and Lamai, take the full force of the north-east monsoon from October to December. Waves of 1–2 m, rip currents and red flags are normal then, and red flags also go up during winter wind surges, as on 19 February 2026. People drown every year in Thailand after ignoring them. The north and west coasts are calmer but tidal, with shallow flats at low tide.

  • Flags: red means no swimming. Follow all warning signs, as the UK government advises.
  • Rip currents: if you are pulled out, don't swim against the current. Float, raise an arm and swim parallel to the beach once it eases.
  • Rocks and viewpoints: stay back from the edge at Lad Koh and other rocky points in big waves; a visitor was swept off the rocks there in November 2024.
  • Box jellyfish: of the first 10 deaths from box jellyfish recorded in Thailand since 2002, 9 were in waters off Samui and Koh Phangan. Swim in netted areas where they exist. For a sting, pour vinegar on it for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it or rinse it with fresh water, and call 1669.
  • Children: Choeng Mon and Lipa Noi are the gentlest beaches for small children.

Boat trips

The UK government links fatal boat accidents in Thailand to overloading, poor maintenance and rough seas, especially in the monsoon. That matches what we see. Our approach on every trip we run:

  • We cancel or change the route when the sea isn't safe, and you choose a full refund or a new date.
  • Life jackets for every age, including children's sizes; children wear them at all times on board.
  • Licensed boats and experienced local captains; we have run trips from Samui since 2006.
  • Ang Thong Marine Park trips stop every year from 1 November to 15 December, when the park closes for the monsoon; it reopens on 16 December.

When choosing any operator, ask what happens in bad weather, look at the life jackets before you board, and don't get on a boat that looks overloaded. Pig Island and Koh Tan sit in the sheltered south and run all year: the Pig Island speedboat tour costs 1,450 THB per adult. Nightlife and Full Moon nights from Samui

Samui's nightlife centres on Chaweng, with smaller scenes in Lamai and Fisherman's Village. Thousands of people also cross to the Full Moon Party on Koh Phangan each month. The UK government warns that sexual assaults are most common during full moon parties or similar events and near bars late at night, and advises against leaving drinks unattended or accepting drinks from strangers.

  • Spiking: buy your own drinks, watch them being poured and don't leave them. If a friend suddenly seems far drunker than they should, get them to a safe place and medical help.
  • Methanol: cheap spirits and buckets of unknown origin carry a risk of methanol poisoning, which can kill and cannot be detected by taste or smell. Blurred vision or severe sickness after drinking needs a hospital at once.
  • Getting home: pre-arrange transport so you don't accept lifts in unlicensed vehicles or boats. Never ride a scooter after drinking.
  • Full Moon night: book a licensed return boat before you go. Our Full Moon Party speedboat costs 1,400 THB return with hotel pickup and drop-off from most areas of Koh Samui; entry to the party (about 200 THB) is paid at the gate. Next party: [kp_next_party]
  • Drugs: the UK government warns that possessing even small quantities can lead to heavy fines or imprisonment. Don't buy anything at a party.

Crime and theft

Most crime affecting visitors is opportunistic theft. The UK government advises beware of bag snatchers, especially on motorbikes.

  • Carry bags on the side away from the road and don't put your phone in a scooter's front basket.
  • Use the hotel or villa safe for passports, spare cards and cash. Carry a photo of your passport and entry stamp.
  • Don't leave phones and wallets on the sand while you swim.
  • Lock scooters and take the key; thefts happen overnight outside bungalows.
  • Use bank ATMs inside banks, malls or 7-Eleven, and cover your PIN.

Report a theft to the Tourist Police (1155) or the police station for the area, and get a written report for your insurer; see police stations on Koh Samui. Money-focused tricks, from jet-ski damage claims to fake hotel pages, are covered in our scams guide.

Women and solo travellers

Many women travel alone on Samui without problems, and the island's resort areas are busy and well lit in the evening. The UK government notes that violent sexual assaults and unprovoked attacks can happen in tourist areas across Thailand, and advises avoiding walking through isolated areas alone, especially at night.

  • Take a registered taxi or Grab rather than walking long, dark stretches of road or beach at night.
  • Share your live location with a friend when you go out, and tell your hotel where you are going.
  • Be wary of strangers who are very keen to drive you somewhere or to buy you drinks.
  • Choose accommodation with a staffed reception or secure villa access.
  • If anything happens, go to a safe place and call 191 or 1155, then go to hospital before showering if you can, so evidence can be collected. The Tourist Police page explains the next steps.

Health

  • Heat: daytime highs are 29–33 °C all year, with high humidity. Drink water through the day, use high-factor sun cream and avoid long walks between 11:00 and 15:00.
  • Drinking water: don't drink tap water. Bottled water and refill stations are cheap and everywhere.
  • Dengue: present all year in Thailand and peaks in the rainy months. Use repellent with DEET or picaridin, especially at dawn and dusk. See a doctor for a sudden high fever and take paracetamol, not ibuprofen.
  • Dogs and rabies: rabies is present in Thailand. If a dog, cat or monkey bites or scratches you, wash the wound with soap and water for 15 minutes and go to hospital the same day for vaccination.
  • Hospitals: private hospitals in Chaweng and Bophut have 24-hour emergency departments, and all hospitals ask for an insurer's guarantee of payment or a deposit before treatment. See Koh Samui hospitals, pharmacies and travel insurance.

Natural hazards

Samui's rainy season runs from October to December, with November the wettest month at around 445 mm of rain on average. It is the opposite of Phuket's monsoon, so many visitors don't expect it.

  • Flooding: low-lying inland and south-west roads can flood in heavy rain; avoid driving through water you can't see the bottom of.
  • Storms: tropical storms can reach the Gulf in December and January. Tropical Storm Pabuk in January 2019 closed ferries and Ang Thong Marine Park for several days.
  • Waterfalls: flash floods can make jungle waterfalls dangerous after heavy rain; don't swim in a rising, brown torrent.
  • Warnings: follow official forecasts from the Thai Meteorological Department and check Windy for wind and waves. If a storm is forecast, ferries and tours stop; plan a buffer day before international flights.

Families with children

Samui is an easy island for families, and most of the risks above are simple to manage with children.

  • Beaches: Choeng Mon and Lipa Noi are shallow and sheltered. Chaweng and Lamai can be rough from October to December, and Lamai shelves steeply in the centre.
  • Pools: many villa pools have no fence or alarm. Agree who is watching the children at all times.
  • Transport: don't carry children on scooters. Car seats are not standard in taxis or rental cars, so ask for one in advance or bring a travel seat.
  • Sun and heat: children burn quickly; use rash vests, hats and shade between 11:00 and 15:00.
  • Boats: on our trips children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn; infants aged 0–2 travel free.
  • Animals: teach children not to approach dogs, cats or monkeys; any bite or scratch needs a hospital visit the same day.

Before you travel

  • Entry: most Western nationalities now get 30 days visa-free (since 15 September 2026). Fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card at tdac.immigration.go.th within 3 days before arrival, and avoid paid look-alike sites. Details: Thailand visa guide.
  • Insurance: check medical cover, evacuation, the motorcycle clause and water sports before you fly.
  • IDP: you can only get an International Driving Permit in your home country.
  • Medicines: bring prescriptions in original packaging, no more than 30 days' supply, and check whether any need a Thai permit; see pharmacies.
  • Copies: keep digital copies of your passport, insurance policy and emergency numbers in your email or cloud storage.
  • Money: carry two cards from different banks and some cash; ATMs charge foreign cards 220–250 THB per withdrawal. See banks and money.

Our safety checklist

  1. Buy travel insurance that covers scooters (if you'll ride), water sports and medical evacuation.
  2. Carry your IDP if you plan to ride, and always wear a helmet.
  3. Check the flags before you swim and stay out of the sea at night.
  4. Keep your passport in the safe and a photo of it on your phone.
  5. Leave vapes at home.
  6. Book licensed boats and pre-arrange your ride home from parties.
  7. Save 1155 and 1669 in your phone.
