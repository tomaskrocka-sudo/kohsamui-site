A compact car on Koh Samui costs approx. 800–1,200 THB a day and an SUV 1,500–2,500 THB, with discounts for weekly and monthly rentals. You need your home licence plus an International Driving Permit. Choose full first-class (Class 1) insurance with a low excess, and check whether the contract allows the car on the Donsak ferry before you plan a mainland trip.

Good to know: prices were last checked on 1 November 2026 and rise in December–January and July–August. Book ahead for those weeks.

Licence and IDP requirements

  • Carry your full national driving licence and an International Driving Permit (1949 Geneva or 1968 Vienna format) issued in your home country. UK drivers are told to carry the 1968 IDP.
  • Agencies may rent to you on a home licence alone, but police and insurers can still treat you as unlicensed without the IDP. The UK Foreign Office warns that driving without a valid licence could invalidate your travel insurance.
  • Most agencies require drivers to be at least 21 (some 23) and to have held a licence for a year or more. [check]
  • ASEAN national licences are accepted without an IDP.
  • Long-stay residents should get a Thai licence; see our long-stay guide.

Insurance and excess

Thai car insurance comes in layers, and the words on your contract matter:

CoverWhat it pays forWhat to check
Compulsory motor insuranceBasic medical costs for people injured in a crash; every registered car has itIt does not cover damage to cars or property
Class 1 ("first-class") voluntary insuranceDamage to the rental car, third-party property and injury, theft and fireThe excess (deductible) per claim, and whether it covers single-vehicle accidents
Class 2+ / 3+ and lower classesNarrower cover, often only collisions with another vehicle or third-party onlyAvoid for a rental unless you have separate cover
Excess reduction ("super cover")Lowers your excess, sometimes to zeroExclusions for tyres, glass, underbody and keys
  • Deposits: international brands put a hold on a credit card; local agencies may ask for cash. Get a receipt that shows the amount.
  • Passports: do not leave your passport as a deposit; show it and leave a copy.
  • The ferry clause: many contracts forbid taking the car off Samui or void insurance while it is on a ferry. Ask in writing.
  • Accidents: call the rental company first. In Thailand the insurer usually sends an assessor to the scene; do not move the car or agree fault until they arrive, unless it is dangerous. Ambulance 1669, police 191, Tourist Police 1155.

Airport pickup vs local agencies

International brands, including Hertz and Budget, have desks at or near Samui airport, alongside Thai national firms. Local agencies operate along the ring road in Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut and Maenam, and many deliver cars to hotels and villas.

Which car for Samui's hills

  • Automatic: almost all rentals are automatic; you will be grateful on steep starts.
  • Ground clearance: villa roads in Chaweng Noi, Bophut hills, Choeng Mon and the south-west are steep concrete with sharp humps. A small SUV handles them better than a low saloon.
  • 4x4: only needed for unpaved hill tracks. The road up to the Secret Buddha Garden is steep and partly rough; most visitors go by 4x4 or on a tour.
  • Engine: a 1.2-litre compact struggles with four adults on the steepest climbs. Choose 1.5 litres or more for hilly villas.
  • Brakes: use a low gear on long descents such as Route 4170 to avoid overheating the brakes.

Driving and parking tips

  • Thailand drives on the left. The ring road (Route 4169) is about 51 km and takes roughly 90 minutes non-stop.
  • Scooters filter on both sides and pull out without looking. Check mirrors constantly and leave space.
  • Many drivers flash headlights to mean "I'm coming through", not "go ahead".
  • Seat belts are compulsory for the driver and passengers, front and back.
  • Give way to emergency vehicles and slow right down near schools and temples.
  • The drink-drive limit is 0.05% blood alcohol, and 0.02% for drivers under 20 or without a full licence. Police run night checkpoints.
  • Parking is tight in central Chaweng in the evening; use hotel or mall car parks.
  • At Fisherman's Village, the main road closes for the Friday walking street from late afternoon; park outside the village and walk in.
  • Beach car parks at viewpoints and Hin Ta and Hin Yai charge small fees.
  • From October to December, low roads flood after heavy rain. Do not drive through water if you cannot see the road.

Drives worth doing

  • The round-island loop: about 51 km and 90 minutes non-stop; a full day with stops at Lad Koh viewpoint, Hin Ta and Hin Yai, Na Muang waterfall, Nathon and the Big Buddha.
  • The south-west coast: Route 4170 through Thong Krut, Taling Ngam and Lipa Noi for quiet beaches and sunsets; steep and narrow in places, so take it slowly.
  • The north-east headland: Route 4171 to Plai Laem, Wat Plai Laem, the Big Buddha and Choeng Mon.
  • Sunset on the west coast: Taling Ngam and Lipa Noi face west; leave Chaweng an hour before sunset.

Most Samui rental contracts do not allow the car to leave the island, and those that do may charge extra or void insurance during the crossing. If you want to explore Khao Sok or the mainland, ask in writing before you book, or rent a separate car in Surat Thani. Ferry times and the Donsak piers are on Surat Thani to Koh Samui.

Common rental disputes

  • Pre-existing damage: the classic problem. Your timestamped photos and video from the start of the rental settle most arguments.
  • Fuel charges: return the car with the same fuel level as noted, and keep the receipt from the last fill-up.
  • Cleaning fees: sand and wet towels in the car can trigger a cleaning charge; shake out mats before returning.
  • Late returns: agree the return time and grace period in writing; some agencies charge a full extra day after an hour.
  • Deposit refunds: card holds can take 1–3 weeks to release; ask for written confirmation that the car was returned without damage.

If a dispute cannot be settled, the Tourist Police (1155) can mediate.

Rental checklist

  1. Licence plus IDP, and a credit card in the driver's name.
  2. Class 1 insurance; note the excess in writing.
  3. Photos and video of every panel, the wheels, the windscreen and the interior.
  4. Fuel level and mileage noted on the contract.
  5. Spare tyre, jack and warning triangle present.
  6. Registration book or copy and tax disc in the car.
  7. Emergency number for the agency saved in your phone.
  8. Written confirmation of the ferry and off-island rules.
  9. Child seats booked in advance if needed; they are not always available.

Do you need a car at all?

If you are staying in Chaweng, Lamai or Fisherman's Village, you can manage with walking, songthaews by day and Grab or taxis at night; see getting around Koh Samui. A car makes most sense for families, villa stays on hill roads, rainy-season visits and exploring the quiet south and west coasts. A full-day driver costs about 2,000–3,000 THB and removes the insurance and parking worries.

On boat days you can leave the car at your hotel: our tours include hotel pickup from most areas of Koh Samui. Try the Pig Island speedboat tour (runs every day of the year) or, from 16 December when the park reopens, the Ang Thong speedboat day tour.
