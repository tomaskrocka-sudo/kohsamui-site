Chaweng is the centre of Samui nightlife: the clubs on Soi Green Mango (open nightly from about 21:00 to 04:00), ARKbar's free beach fire shows from 20:00, live-band bars and two Muay Thai stadiums with fights six nights a week (none on Thursday). Lamai is smaller and older, while Fisherman's Village, Bang Rak and the west coast do beach clubs and sunset bars. Since 29 May 2026 shops may sell alcohol from 11:00 to 24:00, and licensed venues stay open later.

We are a local team that has worked on Samui and Koh Phangan since 2006, and we run the party boats from Samui to the Full Moon Party. This guide covers every area, what each night of the week is good for, what things cost and how to get home safely.

Chaweng

Chaweng has more bars and clubs than the rest of the island combined. The night usually runs in three stages: sunset or a fire show on the beach, dinner and drinks on Chaweng Beach Road, then the clubs on Soi Green Mango after midnight.

  • Soi Green Mango: a short lane off Chaweng Beach Road lined with clubs and bars. The Green Mango Club, open-air on several levels with two dance floors, opens nightly from about 21:00 (some listings say 22:00) and gets busy after midnight, closing at about 02:00–04:00. Entry is usually free; you must be 20 or over, so bring ID. Sound Club next door runs event nights.
  • ARKbar Beach Club: the island's best-known beach club, in the middle of Chaweng Beach, with a free fire show from 20:00 and a beach party until about 02:00; adults only from 21:00.
  • Reggae and live music: Reggae Pub, on its own soi, runs from the afternoon until about 02:30; Bar Solo on the beach road has live cover bands most nights.
  • Beach clubs: SEEN at Avani Chaweng and Elephant Beach Club host DJ nights and pool parties at the weekend.

Saturday is the peak night, Friday a close second, and Sunday to Tuesday the quietest. Full street-by-street detail: Chaweng nightlife and our Chaweng guide.

A Saturday night in Chaweng

  1. 17:30 Sunset drink on Chaweng Beach; walk-in sunbeds at ARKbar have a 300 THB minimum spend.
  2. 19:00 Dinner at Chaweng Walking Street by the lake, or a restaurant on Chaweng Beach Road.
  3. 20:00 Free fire show on the sand at ARKbar.
  4. 21:00 Muay Thai at Phetchbuncha Stadium (Saturday card), or live music at Bar Solo.
  5. Midnight Soi Green Mango as the clubs fill up.
  6. 02:00–04:00 Grab or a pre-agreed taxi home; late food at Food Village on the way.

Lamai

Lamai, south of Chaweng along the ring road, is quieter and attracts an older crowd. Shamrock Irish Pub, open since 2005, has a show band playing nightly from about 21:30 to 01:30. Rock Bar plays reggae and rock from late morning until midnight, and Teddy Weed runs beach parties and fire shows until about 02:00. Beer is cheaper than in Chaweng, at about 80–120 THB. Beach Republic, once Lamai's pool-party venue, has been largely closed in 2025–26, so don't plan around it. Read more: Lamai nightlife and our Lamai guide.

Fisherman's Village and Bophut

Bophut's old fishing village is Samui's best evening out without a club. On Friday the beach road becomes the Fisherman's Village Walking Street from about 17:00 to 23:00, with smaller markets on Monday and Wednesday. Coco Tam's, the beanbag beach bar at the end of the village, runs fire shows on the sand at about 19:15 and 21:00 (some nights 19:30), and cocktails cost about 250–400 THB; pop-up market bars sell cocktails for about 99 THB. Beach-front restaurants and bars stay busy until about midnight. It is the best area for couples and for anyone who wants a lively evening and a quiet night's sleep, and it is a short Grab ride from Chaweng if you want to carry on. See Fisherman's Village.

Bang Rak and Plai Laem

The north-east bays around Big Buddha are Samui's sunset beach-club strip. Chi Samui has a swim-up bar, sunset DJ sets and fire shows; Tembo Beach Club runs sunset DJs on Monday and Friday, with a tapas night on Tuesday and a happy hour on Thursday; Malika is the quiet option (closed Monday); and 79 Beach Club, opened in 2024, sells day passes and runs themed nights. It is a good area for an early evening before dinner in Fisherman's Village. See Plai Laem and Big Buddha Beach.

Maenam and the west coast

Maenam is quiet after dark, with one exception: Cape Away Beach Bar's Sunday party, with DJs, dancers and fire performers, open from about 10:00 to 22:00. At the W Koh Samui in Maenam, Woobar has a resident DJ in the evening and cocktails from about 350 THB. On the west coast, Nikki Beach at Lipa Noi holds a Saturday party and a Sunday brunch, and the big resorts run sunset bars open to non-guests, such as Air Bar at the InterContinental and Jahn at the Conrad. Expect resort prices and a smart-casual dress code. See Maenam, Lipa Noi and sunset spots.

Beach clubs and pool parties

ARKbar runs a pool party every day from 14:00, peaking from 16:00 to 19:00, with live performers on Wednesday and Friday; entry is free, a walk-in sunbed has a 300 THB minimum spend and full pool access costs 500 THB. SEEN at Avani Chaweng held its pool party on Saturdays in 2025–26 and debuted the Zamna "On The Beach" festival on 21 February 2026 (no 2027 date announced). Nikki Beach is the west-coast weekend venue. Most clubs charge no entry and set minimum spends instead. Full guide, with a night-by-night calendar: Koh Samui beach clubs.

Live music and cabaret

  • Bar Solo, Chaweng: live cover bands most nights.
  • Shamrock Irish Pub, Lamai: show band nightly, about 21:30–01:30.
  • Rock Bar, Lamai: reggae and rock, until midnight.
  • Cabaret, Chaweng: two ladyboy cabarets are listed in 2026 guides on Chaweng Beach Road. Paris Follies Cabaret, under the Eiffel Tower sign, has run three shows a night with free entry if you buy a drink, and Divas Cabaret lists shows at about 20:30, 21:30 and 22:30. Show times change, so check at the door on the day.
  • Starz Cabaret closed in November 2021 and has not reopened, although some old guides still list it.

LGBTQ+ nightlife

Thailand legalised same-sex marriage in January 2025, and Samui is relaxed and welcoming. There is no gay district as in Bangkok or Phuket, but Chaweng has a small scene: Pride Bar on Soi Solo opens in the evening until about 01:00, and the cabaret shows on Chaweng Beach Road have a mixed crowd. Most beach clubs and bars are openly gay-friendly. Public displays of affection, gay or straight, are best kept low-key outside the party areas, as anywhere in Thailand.

A typical card has six to eight bouts over about three hours, each starting with the wai kru ram muay, the ritual dance fighters perform to honour their teachers, to live pipe-and-drum music. The early bouts often feature young local fighters; the main events come later in the evening. There are no fights on Thursday. Quieter evenings

Not every night has to end at 04:00. Some of Samui's best evenings are low-key:

  • Sunset: the west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) and the Big Buddha area have the best sunsets; see sunset spots.
  • Night markets: Fisherman's Village on Friday, Chaweng Walking Street from Thursday to Saturday, and Nathon every evening.
  • Fire shows: free on the beach at ARKbar from 20:00 and at Coco Tam's at about 19:15 and 21:00.
  • A fight night: a Muay Thai card lasts about three hours and children aged 6–10 pay half price at Samui International.
  • Dinner with a view: hillside and beach-front restaurants; see Koh Samui restaurants.
  • An evening massage: many massage shops in Chaweng and Lamai stay open into the late evening; see spas on Koh Samui.

Dress codes and door policies

  • Clubs and bars on Soi Green Mango are casual, but wear a shirt: bare-chested customers may be turned away.
  • You must be 20 to drink, and clubs check: carry a passport copy or a driving licence.
  • Resort sunset bars and Nikki Beach in the evening are smart-casual: no beachwear after dark.
  • Beach clubs in the daytime are swimwear-friendly by the pool, with a cover-up for the restaurant.
  • Big events (NYE, festival nights) sell tickets in advance and can refuse entry once full.

Nights out for couples and families

Samui works well for couples who want a nice evening rather than a big night: sunset at a beach club, dinner in Fisherman's Village, a fire show at Coco Tam's and a pre-booked ride home. Families can join much of the early evening too: the Friday walking street, the free fire shows at ARKbar (before 21:00, when it turns adults only) and Coco Tam's, the night markets and a Muay Thai fight night, where children under 6 go free at Samui International. The cabaret shows are adult entertainment. See Koh Samui with kids and Koh Samui for honeymooners.

Alcohol laws and holy-day bans

  • Sales hours: since 29 May 2026, shops may sell alcohol from 11:00 to 24:00. The old 14:00–17:00 afternoon ban has ended.
  • Exempt venues: hotels licensed under the Hotel Act and licensed entertainment venues are exempt from the hours and may stay open until about 02:00–04:00, depending on zoning and licence.
  • Drinking age: 20. Clubs check ID.
  • No alcohol is sold at temples, government offices, petrol stations, public parks or on public transport.
  • Holy-day bans: on Buddhist holy days shops and 7-Eleven can't sell alcohol, but since May 2025 hotels, licensed bars and entertainment venues in tourist areas may still serve. Illegal sales carry up to 6 months in jail and/or a 10,000 THB fine.

Scams and tricks to know

  • Padded bills: drinks you didn't order, or a "service charge" that wasn't on the menu. Pay per round in busy bars, or check the bill line by line.
  • Ticket touts: street sellers offering Muay Thai or party tickets at a mark-up, sometimes for the wrong night. Buy from the box office, the venue or us.
  • "Full Moon boat" bargains: cheap tickets sold in bars for unlicensed boats, or with no return. Ask which pier, which boat and what time you come back.
  • Taxi fares: no meters on Samui, so agree the price before you get in, especially after midnight.
  • Scooter "damage": rental shops claiming old scratches as new damage the next day. Photograph the bike before you ride, and better still, don't ride at night.
  • Lost phones: phones left on bar tables or in the sand disappear fast. Use a lanyard or a zipped pocket.

Staying safe at night

  • Drink spiking and assault: the UK Foreign Office warns of drink spiking and sexual assaults in island party areas. Watch your drink being made, never leave it, and look after friends.
  • Vapes are illegal in Thailand, including for tourists: expect confiscation, fines, possible detention and, in reported cases, extortion demands.
  • Cannabis: since June 2025, buying it requires a prescription, and smoking in public can bring fines of up to 25,000 THB. Never smoke in public.
  • Other drugs carry very heavy penalties, and police run checks in party areas.
  • Scooters: never ride after drinking. Samui's roads are dark, hilly and busy with drunk riders at night, police checkpoints are common, and insurance won't pay out for drink-riding.
  • ID: carry a copy of your passport; leave the original in the safe.
  • Swimming: don't swim at night or after drinking; Chaweng and Lamai have rip currents from October to December.
  • Emergency numbers: Tourist Police 1155 · Police 191 · Ambulance 1669.

Getting home

  • Grab works best in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai; expect waits of 5–20 minutes and surge pricing late at night and in rain. Cover on the west coast is weak.
  • Taxis don't use meters. Agree the fare before you get in, and expect 20–30 % more after 22:00.
  • Songthaews (shared pick-ups) are cheap but thin out after 20:00 and mostly stop by 22:00.
  • Walking: staying within walking distance of Chaweng or Lamai Beach Road is the simplest plan for big nights.
  • From Koh Phangan: there are no public boats back to Samui in the middle of the night, so book a return boat before you go.

Where to stay for nightlife

For big nights, stay in Chaweng, ideally within walking distance of Chaweng Beach Road and Soi Green Mango, so you never need a ride home at 03:00; the north and south ends of Chaweng are quieter if you want to sleep. Lamai suits people who want bars and live music without clubs. Bophut and Fisherman's Village give you the Friday walking street, beach bars and a short Grab ride to Chaweng. Maenam and the west coast are quiet bases, best if nightlife is an occasional treat. Full comparison: where to stay on Koh Samui.

Koh Samui or Koh Phangan for nightlife?

Samui's nightlife is broader but more mainstream: clubs, beach clubs, live bands, cabaret and Muay Thai every week, with better restaurants and hotels around them. Koh Phangan has the famous parties (Full Moon, Half Moon, Jungle Experience) and a stronger underground and festival scene, but quieter normal nights outside Haad Rin and Ban Tai. Many people stay on Samui and cross for one party night. If you plan to base yourself on Phangan, our sister site covers Koh Phangan nightlife.

Party at night, the sea by day

After a late night, a gentle afternoon on the water helps. The Pig Island sunset tour (1,550 THB adult) and the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) run all year from Thong Krut. Ang Thong Marine Park is closed from 1 November to 15 December; from 16 December the Ang Thong speedboat tour (1,800 THB) is back, best booked for two days after a big night, not the morning after.
