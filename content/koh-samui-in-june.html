June is a good month for Koh Samui. It is hot and mostly sunny, with highs of 33 °C, lows of 25 °C and about 134 mm of rain over 10 rain days, most of it in short showers. The sea is 30 °C. While Phuket and Krabi are in the south-west monsoon, Samui is sheltered and the boats run normally, Ang Thong included. Rooms are at shoulder prices before the July rush, and the Full Moon Party is on Saturday 19 June 2027.

Good to know: June is one of the best-value months for a beach holiday on Samui. Ang Thong is open and quieter than in high season: the Ang Thong speedboat tour costs 1,800 THB plus the 300 THB park fee in cash. For a family first boat day, the Pig Island speedboat tour costs 1,450 THB.

The verdict

June suits families, couples and anyone who wants a summer beach holiday in Thailand without the Andaman monsoon. It does not suit a trip built around wind sports or cool weather.

Good for:

  • Families: calm sea, short showers and lower rates than July
  • Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao, best in the morning
  • Swimming on the east coast, which is sheltered from the south-west wind
  • Diving, with whale sharks possible
  • A Saturday Full Moon Party on 19 June 2027

Not for:

  • A guaranteed dry week: about one day in three has some rain
  • A kitesurfing holiday: Koh Phangan has stronger summer wind
  • Morning swims on the north and west coasts, where June brings the lowest water of the year
  • Midday walks at 33 °C

If you are choosing between June and July, the weather is much the same; June is quieter and cheaper. See Koh Samui weather by month.

Weather, sea and tides in June

From May to September the wind blows from the south-west and west, across the Thai mainland. Samui sits in its lee, so the sea around the island is calm on most days and showers are usually short, followed by sunshine. Plan beach time for mornings and late afternoons; midday at 33 °C with high humidity is heavy going.

The south-west wind changes which coast is calm. The east coast (Chaweng, Lamai, Choeng Mon) is sheltered and calm, the opposite of the monsoon months. The west and south-west coasts get some westerly chop in the afternoon, which can make the Pig Island crossing from Thong Krut a little bumpy after lunch. Morning trips are smoother.

June also brings the lowest water of the year. Low tide falls in the morning, around 10:00, and the north and west coast flats (Maenam, Bang Por, Lipa Noi) are exposed for tens of metres. Swim there in the late afternoon and evening, when the tide is high. See Koh Samui tides.

Inland, the waterfalls have a modest flow that rises after afternoon showers. Na Muang 1 has a swimmable pool 5–10 minutes' walk from the car park; the rocks are slippery, so wear sandals with grip. If you hike, go early: sunrise is around 06:00, and the mornings are the best part of a June day. See waterfalls.

Mosquitoes are worse after rain; use repellent at dusk. Box jellyfish risk is higher in the rainy months (the UK government says May to October nationally) and in calm, warm water in the evening. Swim inside netted areas where they exist, and keep vinegar to hand. See beach safety.

Where to stay in June

  • Chaweng and Lamai are sheltered from the south-west wind, so June is a good month to swim there. Lamai is the better value.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay with gentle water, a good family base 5–10 minutes from the airport.
  • Maenam on the north coast is mostly calm, with some afternoon chop. It is tidal, so swim in the afternoon or evening. Neighbouring Bophut is a base for Fisherman's Village rather than for swimming.
  • The west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) has the sunsets but also the afternoon westerly chop, and very shallow water at the morning low tide.
  • Villas: check that the access road is paved, as steep concrete drives get slippery after showers.

Diving, snorkelling and kitesurfing in June

Visibility around Koh Tao and Sail Rock is typically 10–25 m in June, with some wind. Whale sharks are possible: in July 2026 the Koh Tao Tourism Association named June to September as the peak for sightings, but they are never guaranteed. Snorkelling at Ang Thong, Koh Tan and Koh Nang Yuan is good; go in the morning, before the afternoon breeze. See diving and snorkelling.

Kitesurfing in June is fair at best. The south-west wind is lighter on Samui than on Koh Phangan, and lessons move to Nathon on the west coast, where the wind is best from about 09:00 to 12:00. Watch for squalls. If kiting is the point of the trip, Phangan's summer wind is stronger. See kitesurfing.

Samui or Phuket in June?

Thailand's two coasts have their rains at different times. The Andaman side (Phuket, Krabi, Koh Lanta, Khao Lak) gets the south-west monsoon from about May to October, and its national marine parks such as the Similan and Surin Islands close from mid-May. The Gulf side, with Samui, Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, gets its rain from October to December. That makes Samui the better choice of the two for a beach and boat holiday in June. Bangkok Airways flies Phuket–Samui about 7–8 times a day and Bangkok–Samui about 20–25 times a day. See Phuket to Koh Samui and how to get to Koh Samui.

Prices in June

June is shoulder season, with rooms well below the peaks of mid-December to February and July to August, when rates run 30–80% higher. Along with May and September, it is one of the best months for good weather at moderate prices. Daily costs don't change: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage about 300–500 THB, a scooter 150–350 THB a day, and a compact car 800–1,200 THB a day, which is often cheaper than taxis for a family. What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses.
  • A compact umbrella for short showers.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin.
  • A rash vest or stinger suit for snorkelling and evening swims.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches and the tidal flats at low tide.
  • Seasickness tablets if you are prone to it; afternoon chop is common.
  • A cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered).
