Why go: elephants, a waterfall and the hills in one day

This is the full-day version of the classic Samui jungle safari: an open 4x4 up into the island's interior, a swim at a waterfall, an hour with rescued elephants and lunch, then the hill roads to a temple viewpoint and the Secret Buddha Garden. The elephant part is hands-on feeding at a sanctuary that does not offer riding, which is the main reason we chose this safari over the many cheaper ones that still do.

What you'll do

The truck collects guests from hotels in the main areas and heads south to Na Muang waterfall for a swim. At the sanctuary you help prepare the elephants' food and feed them by hand while a keeper explains where each animal came from. After a Thai lunch the 4x4 climbs to a hilltop temple with views over the south coast, then on to the Secret Buddha Garden, a sculpture garden built by a local fruit farmer high in the hills.

Who it suits

  • Families with children of any age: under-4s are free and the stops are varied.
  • Visitors who want to meet elephants without riding them.
  • Anyone who wants to see the jungle interior, which most visitors never reach.

Who should choose something else

  • If you only want the elephants, the elephant sanctuary skywalk visit is a 3-hour programme focused on observation.
  • Pregnant travellers, wheelchair users and anyone with back problems: the 4x4 roads are rough.

Good to know

  • Ethics: follow the keepers' instructions, don't feed elephants anything you brought yourself and keep your distance from mothers with calves.
  • Weather: the waterfall is fullest from October to January; in a dry April or May the flow can be weak.
  • Clothing: a scarf or sarong covers shoulders at the temple stop.
