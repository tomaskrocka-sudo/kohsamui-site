Bang Rak, also called Big Buddha Beach, is the north-coast bay between Bophut and Big Buddha, right next to Samui Airport and the main piers for Koh Phangan and Koh Tao. Four different piers here are all loosely called "Bangrak pier", so check which one is on your ticket. It is convenient and good for seafood on the sand and beach clubs, but the water is shallow and muddy and not for swimming, and planes fly low overhead.

Which "Bangrak pier"?

This is the single most useful thing to know about Bang Rak. Ferry companies, hotels and taxi drivers all say "Bangrak pier", but there are four separate piers along this bay, run by different operators. Go to the wrong one and you can miss your boat. Always check the operator name on your ticket and tell your driver the pier by name.

Good to know: in the north-east monsoon (October–December) crossings can be rough and are occasionally cancelled. If you have a flight to catch, leave Koh Phangan or Koh Tao the day before.

The beach, honestly

Bang Rak is a long, gently curving bay of narrow, brownish sand looking north to Koh Phangan, with Big Buddha's golden statue at its eastern end. It looks pleasant from a restaurant table at dusk. It is not a swimming beach, for several reasons:

  • The water is shallow and the bottom is soft mud in many places; at low tide it retreats a long way.
  • It is often murky, especially in April–May.
  • Broken glass has been reported in the sand; don't walk barefoot at the water's edge.
  • Ferries, speedboats and longtails use the bay all day.
  • Planes on approach to Samui Airport pass low overhead.

If you want to swim, Choeng Mon is 10 minutes east and Chaweng 10–15 minutes south. Or use a beach-club pool (below). Compare all beaches on Koh Samui beaches.

Eating at Bang Rak

Bang Rak's strength is seafood with your feet in the sand. A strip of simple restaurants along the beach road grills fish, prawns, squid and crab, priced by weight. Check whether the price on the board is per kilo or per 100 g before the fish goes on the grill, and agree the cooking style in advance. For more on ordering, see Koh Samui seafood.

For cheap eats, Plaza The Green Market opposite the Bangrak fresh market, near the Seatran pier, runs daily from about 15:00 to 22:00. There is no Big Buddha walking street; for a proper night market go to Fisherman's Village on Friday (10 minutes west) or Chaweng's walking street from Thursday to Saturday. See Koh Samui markets.

Beach clubs and nightlife

Because the sea is poor, the beach clubs here are built around pools. All of these were operating in 2025–2026:

  • Chi Samui: swim-up bar, sunset DJ sets and fire shows.
  • Tembo Beach Club: sunset DJ sets on Mondays and Fridays, tapas on Tuesdays, happy hour on Thursdays.
  • Malika Beach Club and 79 Beach Club, the latter known for its New Year's Eve party.

Expect a minimum spend on day beds at weekends. For the full scene see Koh Samui beach clubs.

Staying near the airport

Bang Rak and neighbouring Plai Laem are the most practical places to sleep before an early ferry or a morning flight: the airport is about 5 minutes away and the piers are on the doorstep. There is a mix of budget guesthouses, mid-range resorts and pool villas. Prices tend to be lower than in Chaweng for the same standard, because of the beach and the noise.

  • Good for: one or two nights in transit, Full Moon Party nights (you can walk to the Haad Rin Queen), divers on early Koh Tao boats.
  • Not good for: a beach holiday, light sleepers, anyone who wants to swim from the hotel.
  • Check: that your room is not directly under the flight path, and whether the hotel has a pool.

Things to do near Bang Rak

  • Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai): the golden seated Buddha on the Koh Fan islet, reached by a causeway at the east end of the bay; 07:00–18:30, free. Cover shoulders and knees and take your shoes off on the stairs.
  • Wat Plai Laem: about 1 km east of Big Buddha, with the 18-armed Guanyin on a lake platform; about 06:00–18:00, free (fish food a few baht).
  • Choeng Mon: the nearest good swimming beach, 10 minutes east.
  • Fisherman's Village: 10 minutes west, with the Friday Walking Street and Samui's best dining street.
  • Plai Laem: the headland beyond Big Buddha, with more beach clubs and resorts.

Getting there and parking

Bang Rak sits on Route 4171, which leaves the ring road near the airport and loops around the north-east tip. Most ferry tickets with "hotel transfer" include a minibus to the right pier. Grab works well here, but drivers can't use the airport pickup zone, so you meet them outside. Each pier has its own car park; on Full Moon days and in high season they fill early, so take a taxi or Grab.
