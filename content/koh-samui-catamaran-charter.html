Why charter a catamaran

Stability is the big one: a catamaran sits on two hulls and moves far less than a monohull or a speedboat, so it suits families, older guests and anyone who gets seasick. There is room to move around, shade from the sun and nets at the bow to lie on over the water. Blue Coco has three double cabins, each with its own bathroom, plus a master cabin; Kia Ora has a bathroom and two showers plus an outdoor shower. The trade-off is speed: catamarans are much slower than speedboats, so they are best for routes close to Samui. Ang Thong by catamaran is a 10-hour day (08:30–18:30) with a large part of it spent in transit; if Ang Thong is the priority, a private speedboat gives you more time there.

Catamaran itineraries

Pig Island and the southern islands

The most popular catamaran day: the beach pigs on Koh Madsum, snorkelling on the reef at Koh Tan, and on longer days the quieter beach at Koh Rap. Victoria runs it as a 4-hour half day (09:00–13:00 or 14:30–18:30), a 6-hour day or an 8-hour day with Koh Rap (10:30–18:30); Blue Coco and Coco Paradise run 4-hour mornings from 08:30 and full days to 18:30. Island fees are paid in cash: about 50 THB on Koh Madsum and about 100 THB on Koh Rap.

Koh Som, Tongson Bay and Choeng Mon

Short, sheltered water off the north-east coast. Kia Ora's sunset programme goes to Koh Som and Tongson Bay, and its 4-hour half day to Koh Som and Choeng Mon Bay (09:30–13:30 or 14:30–18:30). Good for young children: little time in transit and calm, clear water from December to April.

Sunset sail

Victoria and Kia Ora both run 2.5-hour sunset programmes from 16:00 to 18:30, and the Red Baron junk runs a 3-hour sunset dinner cruise for bigger groups. Sunset is between about 17:55 in November and 18:45 in July. More options are on our sunset cruise page.

Koh Phangan beaches

Kia Ora's full day (10:30–18:30) crosses to Haad Rin and Haad Yuan on Koh Phangan's south coast, with lunch ashore paid separately. Blue Coco and Coco Paradise also run full days to Koh Phangan (09:00–17:00).

Ang Thong by catamaran

Blue Coco and Coco Paradise run 10-hour Ang Thong days (08:30–18:30) from 16 December to 31 October; the park closes every year from 1 November to 15 December. The park fee (300 THB per foreign adult, 150 THB per child) is paid in cash. Coco Paradise's sea pool and water slide are not allowed inside the park.

Events on board

Catamarans are made for celebrations: the deck is yours, the boat is steady enough for a buffet, and there is room to dance. Kia Ora and Coco Paradise take up to 45 guests, Blue Coco up to 25 with a bartender. Birthdays, hen and stag days, anniversaries, wedding parties and company outings all work. Tell us the occasion, numbers and catering you want when you enquire, and give us at least a few days' notice for food and decorations (more over Christmas, New Year and Songkran). For 46–70 guests, the Red Baron junk on our yacht charter page is the boat.

Best season for a catamaran

February to April is the best: the calmest sea of the year and the clearest water. May to September is good on most days, as Samui is sheltered from the south-west monsoon. The north-east monsoon brings stronger wind and swell to the east and north-east coasts from about October to December, peaking in November (about 445 mm of rain on 16 days), when we use the sheltered south-west route to the southern islands. Ang Thong is closed from 1 November to 15 December. Check the forecast on Windy and our sea conditions page.
