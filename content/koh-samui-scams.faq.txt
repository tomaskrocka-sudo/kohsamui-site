What are the most common scams on Koh Samui? | Damage claims after jet-ski and scooter rentals, taxis quoting inflated fares, paid look-alike TDAC websites, card machines that charge in your home currency, and touts selling overpriced fight tickets or Full Moon "entry tickets". Most are avoided with a video, a written price and no passport deposit.
How do I avoid the jet-ski scam? | Film the jet ski slowly from every angle with the operator in shot before and after riding, get a written agreement, never leave your passport, and check your insurance covers jet skis. A guided jet-ski safari with a licensed operator avoids most of the risk.
Should I leave my passport when renting a scooter? | No. The UK government advises against leaving your passport with rental businesses. Leave a cash deposit with a receipt instead, and photograph the scooter before you ride.
Do taxis overcharge on Koh Samui? | Often, yes. Samui taxis rarely use their meters, so agree the full fare before you get in. Use the fixed-price counters at the airport, or Grab where it operates in Chaweng, Bophut and Lamai.
Is the TDAC free? | Yes. The Thailand Digital Arrival Card is free on the official site, tdac.immigration.go.th. Thai immigration says at least 10% of arrivals have used paid look-alike sites, so type the address yourself.
Should I pay in my home currency or THB? | Always THB. Paying in your home currency (dynamic currency conversion) uses a worse exchange rate set by the machine's bank. Check the screen before you tap or enter your PIN.
How can I check a tour company is licensed? | Ask for its Tourism Authority licence number and check it on the Department of Tourism's online licence search. Our licence is TAT 43/01467. Pay through a secure checkout in THB and read the cancellation policy first.
Who do I call if I've been scammed? | Call the Tourist Police on 1155 (English-speaking, 24 hours). For consumer complaints about shops or services, the Office of the Consumer Protection Board hotline is 1166. Keep photos, receipts and messages as evidence.
