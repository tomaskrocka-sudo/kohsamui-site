Samui International Airport (USM) is a small, open-air airport in the north-east of Koh Samui, privately owned and run by Bangkok Airways since it opened in 1989. Bangkok Airways flies every route except Scoot's Singapore service, with about 20–25 flights a day from Bangkok Suvarnabhumi alone. Chaweng is 10–15 minutes away, and taxis charge fixed zone prices from the arrivals kiosk, roughly 400–700 THB per car depending on where you are staying.

Airlines and routes

In late 2026 two airlines have scheduled flights at USM: Bangkok Airways on every domestic route and Hong Kong, and Scoot (Singapore Airlines' low-cost arm) on Singapore. Bangkok Airways has paused its own Samui–Singapore flights from 25 October 2026 to 28 March 2027, so Scoot is the only nonstop option from Singapore this winter. Arriving step by step

Domestic arrivals

Flights from Bangkok, Phuket, Krabi, Chiang Mai and U-Tapao are domestic. You walk or ride a shuttle from the aircraft to the arrivals pavilion, collect your bag from the belt (usually within 15 minutes) and walk out to the transport desks. There are no passport checks.

International arrivals

Flights from Singapore, Hong Kong and Chengdu clear immigration and customs at Samui. Before you fly, complete the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card at tdac.immigration.go.th within 3 days before arrival. Since 15 September 2026, visa-exempt visitors from the UK, US, EU, Australia and around 56 other countries receive 30 days on arrival. Officers may ask for an onward ticket and proof of funds. Queues are short compared with Bangkok because only a handful of international flights land each day. Details are on our Thailand visa guide.

After baggage reclaim

  • Transport desks for taxis and shared minibuses are just outside the baggage hall.
  • Hotel transfer staff wait with name boards in the arrivals area.
  • ATMs and currency exchange counters are in the arrivals area. [check] Foreign cards pay a fee of about 220–250 THB per withdrawal at Thai ATMs; always decline "dynamic currency conversion".
  • Mobile operators sell tourist SIMs at arrivals. [check] AIS and True tourist packages cost about 499 THB for 8 days with passport registration; see our SIM card and Wi-Fi guide.

Can you use Grab at the airport?

Grab lists Samui airport as a pickup point with car and bike services, and the fare is shown before you book. In practice, local transport groups resist app pickups at the airport and piers. In May 2026 travellers reported walking more than a kilometre with luggage to meet Grab drivers outside the airport gate. If you book Grab, message the driver, agree the meeting point and expect to walk to the main road. With heavy bags or children, the kiosk taxi is simpler. Departing from Samui

  • When to arrive: aim for 90 minutes before a domestic flight and 2 hours before an international one. Check-in counters close well before departure, and the airport is busy in December and January.
  • Taxi to the airport: hotel desks book fixed-price taxis; from Chaweng the ride is 10–15 minutes, from the west coast up to 55 minutes. Grab drop-offs at the terminal are usually fine.
  • Security: standard liquid limits apply. Vapes and e-cigarettes are illegal in Thailand, so do not carry them.
  • Lounges: Bangkok Airways has long offered its Boutique Lounge to economy passengers, with free snacks, coffee and Wi-Fi. Business passengers and eligible members use the Blue Ribbon Lounge (domestic 04:30–22:00, international 06:30–20:00 [kp_open spec="Mo-Su 06:30-20:00"]); economy passengers can upgrade to it for about 950 THB.
  • Connections in Bangkok: Bangkok Airways checks bags through to many partner airlines from Samui. On a separate ticket, allow at least 3 hours at Suvarnabhumi.

Facilities and accessibility

The terminal is a set of open-sided, thatched pavilions with gardens between them. It is pleasant in dry weather and noisy with rain in November. Facilities are basic but adequate: cafés and snack bars, a few souvenir and duty-free shops on the international side, toilets, ATMs and free Wi-Fi. [check] Luggage storage is limited, so ask at the information desk if you need it. [check] Most areas are on one level, which helps wheelchair users, but the walk between aircraft and terminal can be in the open. Ask your airline for assistance when booking. [check]

Open-air shuttle carts have traditionally carried passengers between the aircraft and the pavilions. [check]

The 2026–2030 expansion

Bangkok Airways is enlarging the airport. The March 2026 plan raises annual capacity to 6 million passengers, adds boarding gates (from 7 to 11), creates 40 more check-in points including self-service kiosks, and enlarges commercial space to 4,000 m². Construction was due to start in the second quarter of 2026, with completion scheduled for 2030. A daily cap on flight movements has kept Samui fares high for years; the expansion is expected to allow more flights over time. Expect some building work around the terminal during 2026–2030.

Weather and disruption

Most delays at Samui are caused by afternoon thunderstorms or knock-on delays from Bangkok. Serious disruption is rare, but tropical storms do hit: when Storm Pabuk struck in January 2019, many flights were cancelled and ferries were suspended. The north-east monsoon (October–December) brings the most rain. Plan sensibly:

  • Leave a buffer day between a boat trip to Koh Phangan or Koh Tao and an international flight home.
  • If you are booked on a combo ticket via Surat Thani, remember the last coaches leave about 16:00.
  • Check the forecast on our Koh Samui weather page and the wind on windy.com.

Why is Samui airport privately owned?

Until the late 1980s Samui had no airport, and visitors came by boat from Surat Thani. Bangkok Airways built the airport itself and opened it in 1989, and it has owned and operated it ever since. Because it owns the airport, the airline controls slots and charges, which is why it has a near-monopoly and why Bangkok–Samui fares are higher than Bangkok–Surat Thani fares on low-cost airlines. The small, garden-style terminal is also part of that private, boutique approach. The Thai government and other airlines have discussed more competition over the years, but in late 2026 Bangkok Airways remains the owner and main operator.

Tips from the island

  • Peak dates: 20 December to 10 January, Chinese New Year and Songkran are the busiest weeks. Kiosk queues can reach 20–30 minutes, so a pre-booked hotel transfer saves time.
  • Late arrivals: the last flights land in the evening. Shared minibuses may already have stopped; the kiosk taxi is the reliable option. Night fares are 20–30% higher after 22:00.
  • Early departures: book your taxi the evening before through your hotel. Grab drivers are scarce before 06:00, especially from the west coast.
  • Children: taxis rarely have child seats. If you need one, ask your hotel to arrange a transfer with a seat in advance. [check]
  • Rain: the open-air pavilions mean you may walk a few metres in the rain between covered areas in October–December. Keep a light rain jacket in your cabin bag.
  • Scams: ignore touts in the car park offering "cheaper" taxis; use the official desk. See our scams guide.
  • Long layover: with 3 hours or more, a taxi to the Big Buddha or Fisherman's Village (5–20 minutes away) for lunch is easy. Leave luggage with your airline if possible and be back 90 minutes before departure.
  • Driving yourself: international car rental desks, including Hertz and Budget, operate at or near the airport. You need your licence plus an International Driving Permit; see our car rental guide.
