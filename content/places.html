Koh Samui is 228.7 km² with a ring road of about 51 km (Route 4169) that takes roughly 90 minutes to drive non-stop, so every sight on the island is within about 45 minutes of Chaweng. The north and east (Chaweng, Bophut, Choeng Mon) hold the busy beaches and the Big Buddha; the south has Hin Ta and Hin Yai, the Guan Yu shrine and Thong Krut pier for Pig Island; the west has the quiet sunset coast and the old capital, Nathon. Most temples and viewpoints are free, and the national park at Ang Thong charges 300 THB per foreign adult.

How the island is laid out

One road does most of the work. Route 4169 circles the island close to the coast through Chaweng, Lamai, Hua Thanon, Na Mueang, Nathon, Maenam and Bophut. Two connectors leave it: Route 4170 in the south-west serves Taling Ngam, Thong Krut and Lipa Noi and is steep, narrow and slippery when wet; Route 4171 in the north-east loops past Bang Rak, the Big Buddha, Plai Laem and Choeng Mon. The interior is jungle-covered hills rising to Khao Pom (635 m), crossed only by a few steep concrete roads, which is why the waterfalls and the Secret Buddha Garden feel remote although they are close on the map.

The island is one city municipality (Thesaban Nakhon Ko Samui) in Surat Thani province, divided into seven tambons (subdistricts). You will see them on addresses and signs: Bo Phut (Chaweng, Bophut, Bang Rak, Choeng Mon; postcode 84320), Mae Nam (84330), Ang Thong (Nathon; 84140), Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Na Mueang (the waterfalls) and Maret (Lamai and Hua Thanon).

Areas and beaches by coast

The east coast faces the sunrise and has the best sand, but it takes the north-east monsoon swell from October to December, with red flags and rip currents. The north coast is calmer, tidal and faces Koh Phangan. The west and south-west are quiet, shallow and tidal, with the island's sunsets. East coast

Chaweng7 km of white sand and the most restaurants, bars and shops; sunrise side Chaweng NoiQuiet cove south of Chaweng; snorkel off the sand Lad Koh ViewpointFree ring-road pull-off; stay off the rocks in big waves Coral CoveRocky snorkelling cove; kayak about 300 THB an hour Silver Beach (Crystal Bay)Boulder-framed cove with clear water; full by 10:00 Lamai4 km beach, slower than Chaweng, daily night market

Chaweng is the island's centre for first-timers, nightlife and shopping (Central Samui and Chaweng Lake are behind the beach road); the north end is quieter and better for families. South of a rocky point, Chaweng Noi is calmer and lined with resorts. The ring road then climbs past the Lad Koh viewpoint to Coral Cove and Silver Beach, two small coves where the water is clearest, before dropping to Lamai, Samui's second beach, which drops off steeply in the centre and is stony at the north end.

North-east coast

Choeng MonSheltered bay, among the calmest for small children Tongson BaySmall bay down a sand track; clear December–April Plai LaemThe island's north-east tip: resorts and Wat Plai Laem Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach)Piers for Koh Phangan and Koh Tao; not for swimming

Choeng Mon is 5–10 minutes from the airport, with soft sand and a sandbar to Koh Fan Noi at low tide. Tongson Bay, between Choeng Mon and the Bang Rak headland, is a quiet half-day escape. Bang Rak is the transport beach: most Koh Phangan and Koh Tao fast boats leave from its piers, and the seafood restaurants on the sand are good, but the muddy shallows are not.

North coast

Fisherman's Village, BophutWooden shophouses, the best dining street, Friday market Bophut BeachDinner on the sand with Phangan views; poor swimming MaenamLong, quiet palm-lined beach; cheapest beachfront stays Bang PorSeafood restaurants and sunsets; swim at high tide only Laem YaiVery quiet north-west beach with snorkelling and sunsets

Fisherman's Village is the reason to come to Bophut: old wooden shophouses turned into restaurants, boutique hotels and the Friday Walking Street. Maenam is the quiet, budget end of the north coast, with the Lomprayah pier for Koh Phangan. Beyond it, Bang Por and Laem Yai (the Four Seasons side) are tidal, local and good for a sunset dinner.

West coast

NathonOld capital and ferry port; daily night market, pier sunsets Lipa NoiVery shallow, calm water for toddlers; car-ferry pier nearby Taling NgamLong village beach with the best sunset over the Five Islands

Nathon has the government offices, the government hospital, old shophouses and the island's most local night market (about 16:00–22:00, busiest on Tuesday). Lipa Noi and Taling Ngam are the sunset coast: wide flats at low tide, few restaurants and 40–50 minutes from the airport.

South coast

Bang KaoQuiet beach with few facilities; high tide only Thong KrutPier village for the Pig Island and Koh Tan boats Hua ThanonMuslim fishing village with a morning fish market

The south is Samui's working coast: fishing boats, local villages, wellness resorts and the pier at Thong Krut, where our Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, 1,250 THB child) leaves every day of the year. Hua Thanon's fish market is best early in the morning, and the village is home to Samui's central mosque, built in 1984.

Temples and shrines

Two more are worth a stop if you are passing: the Chinese Temple of Guanyin at Taling Ngam on the west coast (map), open in daylight, and Wat Samret on the Na Mueang road (map), a temple more than 150 years old. All of these are free; donations are welcome. Dress code at every temple: shoulders and knees covered, shoes off before entering a building, and never point your feet at a Buddha image. A sarong in your bag solves most problems. The full guide is Koh Samui temples.

Waterfalls and nature

Na Muang Waterfall 1Swimmable pool by the car park; 09:00–17:00 Na Muang Waterfall 220–40 minute steep climb with ropes; busy by 10:30 Hin Lad WaterfallJungle trail near Nathon; about 2 hours round trip Secret Buddha GardenHilltop sculpture garden; very steep road Hin Ta and Hin YaiThe Grandfather and Grandmother Rocks at Lamai; free

Na Muang 1 is free (parking 20 THB for a scooter, 60 THB for a car); don't stop at the first car park by the elephant camp. Both Na Muang falls shrink to a trickle from February to April. The Secret Buddha Garden road is so steep that a 4x4 or a jungle safari tour is the sensible way up; the entry fee is a small one paid at the gate. Hin Ta and Hin Yai are free with about 30 THB parking, and open Sunday to Friday 08:30–18:30 and Saturday 08:00–19:00. See our Koh Samui waterfalls guide for the trails.

Viewpoints

The Lad Koh viewpoint is a free 24-hour pull-off on the ring road between Chaweng Noi and Coral Cove, with the view north over Chaweng Bay. Stay off the lower rocks when waves are up: a tourist was swept off them in November 2024. Overlap Stone, in the hills above Lamai, has balanced boulders and a suspension bridge, a steep access road and an entry charge that includes a soft drink; it is open 08:00–18:00. The hilltop Khao Hua Jook Chedi above Chaweng (map) looks over Chaweng Lake and the bay. There is also a ring-road viewpoint above Chaweng Lake, best from 08:00 to 10:00. Pig Island and Koh Tan run all year from Thong Krut; the Koh Madsum landing fee is about 50 THB in cash. Koh Rap, beyond Koh Tan, is quieter still and reached by our three-island private longtail (8,450 THB per boat, up to 8 guests). Ang Thong National Marine Park, about 30 km west, closes every year from 1 November to 15 December and reopens on 16 December; the Ang Thong speedboat tour costs 1,800 THB plus the park fee of 300 THB per foreign adult and 150 THB per child, in cash. The Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip costs 2,200 THB plus the 250 THB Nang Yuan fee.

Practical places

Samui Airport (USM)Open-air terminal run by Bangkok Airways Bangkok Hospital SamuiPrivate, 24 h, near Chaweng Noi; +66 77 429 500 Koh Samui HospitalThe government hospital, outskirts of Nathon Immigration OfficeMaenam (moved from Nathon in 2016); +66 77 423 440 Tourist PoliceHotline 1155, 24 hours, English spoken

Samui has five main hospitals: Bangkok Hospital Samui (ring road near Chaweng Noi; hotline 1719), Samui International Hospital (north end of Chaweng Beach Road, 24-hour emergency), Thai International Hospital (Chaweng, opposite Lotus's), Bandon International Hospital (Bophut ring road near Big C) and the government Koh Samui Hospital near Nathon. The Immigration Office is at 333 Maenam Road, Soi 1, Moo 1, Maenam, usually open Monday to Friday 08:30–16:30; old maps still pin it in Nathon. Central Samui, the island's main shopping mall, faces Chaweng Lake. Emergency numbers: Tourist Police 1155, police 191, ambulance 1669, fire 199. Getting between places

Grab works on Samui but coverage is thin on the west coast: expect about 80–120 THB for a 5 km hop and 200–350 THB to cross the island, with waits of 5–20 minutes and surge pricing in rain. Taxis have no meters in practice, so agree the fare first; a full day with a driver costs about 2,000–3,000 THB. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) run the ring road for 30–80 THB local fares, mostly in daytime, and stop by about 20:00–22:00. A 125 cc scooter costs about 150–350 THB a day, but you need your licence with the motorcycle category plus an International Driving Permit, or your insurance won't pay; the helmet fine is up to 2,000 THB per person, and police checkpoints are common near Chaweng Lake Road and the airport. Don't leave your passport as a deposit. A compact car is about 800–1,200 THB a day. See getting around Koh Samui and Koh Samui roads for safety on the hills.
