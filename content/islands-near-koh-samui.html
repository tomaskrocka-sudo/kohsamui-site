The four best island trips from Koh Samui are Ang Thong Marine Park (42 islands about 30 km west; closed every year 1 November–15 December), Koh Madsum (Pig Island) with Koh Tan from Thong Krut (15–30 minutes by boat, bookable every day), Koh Tao with Koh Nang Yuan (1.5–2 hours each way) and Koh Phangan (20–50 minutes by ferry). Island fees are extra and paid in cash: 300 THB at Ang Thong, 250 THB on Koh Nang Yuan and about 50 THB on Koh Madsum. Day tour boats always return to Samui, so there is no one-way island hopping on a tour.

Map of the islands around Samui

Samui sits in the middle of a ring of islands. Koh Phangan and Koh Tao lie to the north, Ang Thong to the west, and Koh Tan, Koh Madsum and Koh Rap off the south-west tip. Departures are split between the north-coast piers (Bangrak, Big Buddha, Maenam and Bophut) for Phangan and Koh Tao, Thong Krut in the south-west for the southern islands, and Nathon on the west coast for the mainland ferries.

Ang Thong Marine Park

Ang Thong is the big one: 42 limestone islands in a protected national park about 30 km west of Samui. A day trip climbs to the Emerald Lake on Koh Mae Ko (15–20 minutes up wooden stairs; no swimming in the lake), climbs again to the 360° viewpoint on Koh Wua Talab (a steep 25–30 minutes), kayaks along the cliffs and snorkels a sheltered reef. The park fee is 300 THB per foreign adult and 150 THB per child aged 3–14, in cash.

The park closes every year from 1 November to 15 December and reopens on 16 December. We run it six ways: the speedboat day tour (1,800 THB, about 8 hours), the big boat (1,500 THB, about 10 hours), the VIP tour (4,500 THB, only 15 guests on a big speedboat), overnight with a tent (3,600 THB) or a bungalow (4,200 THB), and a private speedboat (33,000 THB per boat). Read the full Ang Thong guide and our speedboat vs big boat comparison.

Koh Madsum (Pig Island)

Koh Madsum is the small island off the south-west coast where domestic pigs roam the beach. Boats leave Thong Krut pier and land in 10–15 minutes by speedboat or 20–30 minutes by longtail. The island fee is about 50 THB per person in cash. Besides the pigs there is a beach restaurant and bar, shade and calm water for swimming.

Because Thong Krut is on the sheltered side of the island, Pig Island trips run every day of the year, weather permitting, including the Ang Thong closure. Our speedboat tour costs 1,450 THB (4–5 hours, with a Thai meal), the sunset tour 1,550 THB with dinner, and the small-group longtail 1,750 THB with lunch on Koh Tan. See the Pig Island guide for all ten options.

Koh Tan

Koh Tan (Koh Taen) is the larger island next to Koh Madsum, home to a few dozen people, and the best snorkelling of the southern pair. The reef along Ao Ok on the east side is shallow and calm on most days, ideal for beginners and children. Expect patchy coral and reef fish rather than big marine life. There is no separate landing fee. Every one of our Pig Island trips stops here; the small-group longtail stays for lunch. Masks and snorkels are provided; bring your own fins if you want them. More in the Koh Tan guide.

Koh Rap and the pink dolphins

Koh Rap is the furthest of the three southern islands, about 6 km from Koh Tan: a long white beach, calm water, a small bar and toilets, with far fewer visitors than Koh Madsum. The island fee is about 50 THB per person. Two private trips include it: the three-island private longtail (8,450 THB per boat, up to 8 guests, kayaks included) and the Koh Rap full-day private boat (9,950 THB per boat).

The pink dolphins are Indo-Pacific humpback dolphins, which turn pink as they age. They feed off Khanom on the mainland coast opposite Samui. Our Pink Dolphins and Pig Island tour (2,250 THB, about 6 hours) spends the morning looking for them, then snorkels Koh Tan and visits Koh Madsum, with lunch on a mainland beach. Sightings are frequent but never guaranteed, boats keep their distance, and there is no swimming with the dolphins.

The Five Islands (Koh Ha) off Taling Ngam

The Five Islands are a cluster of small, steep islets off Samui's south-west coast. Most people know them as the silhouette behind the sunset from Taling Ngam beach, one of the best sunset spots on the island. They are best seen from the water on a sailing or sunset trip, or from the beach at Taling Ngam. Our Boutique Yachting day (4,500 THB per person, about 8 hours on a sailing yacht, with breakfast and a buffet lunch) visits the Five Islands and Pig Island.

Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan

Koh Tao is 1.5–2 hours north by fast boat, past Koh Phangan, and is famous for diving and clear water. Just off its north-west coast, Koh Nang Yuan is three small islands joined by sandbars, with a viewpoint 10–25 minutes up steps and rocks. Koh Nang Yuan is privately run. Day visitors pay 250 THB per adult and 120 THB per child in cash, and may visit only between 10:00 and 15:00, so every day trip, ours included, times its Nang Yuan stop inside that window. Plastic bottles and cans are not allowed on the island, fins are banned, and drones may not be flown.

Our Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB adult, 1,600 THB child) crosses the sandbar, climbs the viewpoint, snorkels the Japanese Gardens reef, has a Thai lunch on Koh Tao and snorkels two Koh Tao bays chosen for the calmest water. It is the longest crossing of our day trips and can be bumpy in a north-east wind. If you want to stay on Koh Tao, take the ferry instead: see Koh Samui to Koh Tao.

Koh Phangan

Koh Phangan is Samui's neighbour to the north, about 17 km away. Roughly 40 scheduled ferries and speedboats a day cross in 20–50 minutes, mostly to Thong Sala, for 200–500 THB. The Haad Rin Queen runs direct from Big Buddha pier to Haad Rin, the Full Moon Party beach. The next Full Moon Party is on Tuesday 24 November 2026, the same night as Loy Krathong.

For a party night, our Full Moon Party speedboat costs 1,400 THB return with hotel pickup on Samui; entry to the party (about 200 THB) is paid at the gate. For a day on Phangan's beaches, see the ferry guide Koh Samui to Koh Phangan and our Koh Phangan overview.

Koh Som, Koh Fan and Koh Fan Noi

Three small islands off Samui's north-east coast are easy to confuse:

  • Koh Fan is the islet that carries the Big Buddha, joined to Samui by a causeway; no boat needed.
  • Koh Fan Noi is the islet at the north end of Choeng Mon beach. At low tide a sandbar runs out to it, but watch the tide so you are not cut off.
  • Koh Som is a small island off the north-east coast that private charters pair with Tongson Bay for a half-day swim and snorkel.

None of these has a tour of its own. A private speedboat from 19,000 THB per boat can combine Koh Som, Tongson Bay and the north-east coast in a few hours.

Planning several islands in one trip

Because tour boats always come back to Samui, the simplest way to see several islands is to stay on Samui and take day trips, then move on by ferry if you want nights elsewhere. A week that works well outside the closure:

  1. Day 1 Arrive on Samui; beach afternoon.
  2. Day 2 Ang Thong by speedboat or big boat.
  3. Day 3 A free day on Samui, or a spare day if the sea cancelled day 2.
  4. Day 4 Pig Island and Koh Tan in the morning; south-coast temples in the afternoon.
  5. Day 5 Ferry to Koh Phangan (20–50 minutes) for two nights.
  6. Day 7 Fast boat to Koh Tao (about 1–1.5 hours from Phangan) or back to Samui for your flight.

From November to mid-December, swap Ang Thong for the Koh Tao day trip and keep a spare day before any flight, because monsoon ferries can be delayed.

One trip that doesn't work as a day out: Khao Sok National Park on the mainland is 4–6 hours each way from Samui by ferry and road, so it needs at least one night.

Getting to the departure piers

Every one of our island tours includes a hotel transfer from most areas of Koh Samui, so you don't need to find the pier yourself. If you are travelling independently or meeting a private boat, these are the departure points:

  • Thong Krut (south-west): Pig Island, Koh Tan, Koh Rap and our southern private trips. About 30 minutes from Lamai and 45–60 minutes from Chaweng, Bophut or Maenam.
  • Bangrak and Big Buddha piers (north-east): most fast boats to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, and the Haad Rin Queen to Haad Rin. Close to the airport, Chaweng and Choeng Mon.
  • Maenam (Pralarn pier): Lomprayah services to Koh Phangan, Koh Tao and a morning boat to Koh Nang Yuan.
  • Nathon (west): ferries to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, and the car ferries to Donsak on the mainland.

Several piers on the north coast share the name "Bangrak", so check the exact pier name on your ticket and allow for traffic in Chaweng at rush hour.

Island-hopping rules and ferries

Tour boats return to the island they left from. You can't join an Ang Thong tour from Samui and get off on Koh Phangan, or leave the Koh Tao trip to stay on Koh Tao. For multi-island travel, use the scheduled ferries and buy separate tickets.

Island trips for non-swimmers

You don't need to swim to enjoy an island day. Every stop on our trips can be done from the boat or the beach, life jackets come in adult and child sizes, and guides keep non-swimmers close to the boat while they float with a mask. The easiest choices are Pig Island (short crossing, wade-ashore landings, shallow water at Koh Tan) and the Ang Thong big boat, where the Emerald Lake and the viewpoint are walks rather than swims. Kayaks on Ang Thong are two-seaters, so a non-swimmer can paddle with a partner in a life jacket.

Bring cash for every island: Ang Thong's park fee, the Koh Nang Yuan fee and the small Koh Madsum and Koh Rap fees are all paid in Thai baht on arrival. Don't rely on finding an ATM on the smaller islands, and carry small notes, because change can be short.

For taxis, car hire and scooters to reach the piers on your own, see getting around Koh Samui.

Seasickness, safety and weather cancellations

The short Thong Krut crossings rarely upset anyone. The Koh Tao run is the longest and roughest, and an Ang Thong speedboat can bounce for an hour in a swell. If you get seasick, take a tablet 30–60 minutes before you board, sit at the back of a speedboat or the middle of a big boat, and watch the horizon. The Ang Thong big boat is the gentlest ride.

Children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn on our boats. Use reef-safe sunscreen, wear a rash vest and keep clear of boat traffic when snorkelling. Box jellyfish are rare but dangerous in these waters: vinegar for at least 30 seconds on a sting, no rubbing, no fresh water, and call 1669.

We cancel when the sea isn't safe, and you choose a full refund or a new date. If you cancel yourself, day tours are refunded in full 48 hours or more before departure, 50% at 24–48 hours and nothing under 24 hours; Ang Thong tours are refunded in full 48 hours or more before departure and nothing under 48 hours.
