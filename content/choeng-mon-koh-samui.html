Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay of about 1 km on Koh Samui's north-east tip, 5–10 minutes from the airport. It is among the island's calmest swimming beaches and our first pick for families with small children: soft white sand, a gentle slope and usually flat water. At low tide a sandbar links the south-eastern end to the islets of Koh Fan Noi and Koh Fan Yai. It is quiet after dark.

The beach and swimming

Choeng Mon is a crescent of fine white sand held between two headlands, which is why the water stays calm when the open east coast is rough. The bottom slopes gradually and the water is warm (28–30 °C) and usually clear. The centre of the bay is the best place to swim; the western end is rocky underfoot. Unlike the north-coast beaches, it doesn't turn into mudflats at low tide, though the south-eastern end by the islets dries out.

It is not immune to the monsoon. From October to December, strong north-east winds can push waves into the bay, and the lifeguards and resorts will fly a red flag on those days. In practice Choeng Mon is often the best east-side beach in November, but check the flag every time. Box jellyfish are rare but recorded around Samui and Koh Phangan, and calm, warm water is where they are most likely. Avoid swimming at dusk or at night, and look for the vinegar stations most resorts keep on the beach. First aid is vinegar for at least 30 seconds, no rubbing and no fresh water; call 1669.

The Koh Fan Noi sandbar

Off the south-eastern end of the bay sit two small islets, Koh Fan Noi and Koh Fan Yai. At low tide the water drains off a sandbar and you can walk across. It is one of the nicest short walks on Samui with children, but treat it with respect.

  • Check the tide first. Go on a falling tide and start back before it turns. The sandbar floods quickly and the channel is not a place to wade in rising water with a child on your shoulders. [check]
  • Wear water shoes. There are rocks, shells and the odd sea urchin.
  • Stay on the sand. The islets are home to a private resort (Cape Fahn Hotel), so don't wander into the grounds.
  • Not to be confused with Big Buddha's "Koh Fan", the islet on a causeway at Bang Rak where the golden Buddha sits.

Choeng Mon for families

Choeng Mon gets the family vote because the basics all work: the water is calm and shallow near the shore, the sand is soft, restaurants and toilets are on the beach, and the airport is 5–10 minutes away when you are travelling with tired children. It is also quiet at night.

A family day at Choeng Mon

  1. 08:00 Arrive before the heat and the midday crowd; park at the public entrance opposite the Café Amazon.
  2. 08:30 Swim in the centre of the bay while the water is flattest.
  3. 10:30 If the tide is low, walk the sandbar to Koh Fan Noi in water shoes; be back before it turns.
  4. 12:00 Lunch in the shade at a beach restaurant; avoid the sun between 11:00 and 15:00.
  5. 15:00 Second swim, or a short drive to Wat Plai Laem to feed the fish.
  6. 17:30 Dinner on the sand before tired children melt down; you are 5–10 minutes from most hotels on the north-east tip.

What to bring

  • Water shoes for the rocky west end and the sandbar.
  • Sun hats, a beach tent or umbrella (shade is limited) and reef-safe sunscreen.
  • Cash for loungers, drinks and massages; card machines are not universal on the sand.
  • A small bottle of vinegar if your resort does not have a vinegar station on the beach.

Where to eat and nightlife

A row of beach restaurants and resort dining rooms lines the sand, serving Thai food, seafood and international plates at resort-area prices. The lane behind the beach and the main road have a 7-Eleven, cheaper Thai restaurants, a few bars with happy hours and massage shops (a one-hour Thai massage costs about 300–500 THB). FishHouse at the Kimpton Kitalay resort on Choeng Mon has been listed in the MICHELIN Guide. [check]

Nightlife is a cocktail at a beach bar and an early night. For anything louder, Chaweng is 15–20 minutes away and the beach clubs of Bang Rak and Plai Laem are closer. See Koh Samui restaurants and Koh Samui nightlife.

Where to stay in Choeng Mon

Choeng Mon is one of Samui's more expensive areas, because much of it is upscale resorts with private pools. Mid-range doubles cost about 3,000–7,000 THB a night in the shoulder season; peak season adds 30–80 %.

  • Luxury: pool-villa resorts from about 7,000–14,000 THB at SALA Samui Choengmon and 8,000–20,000 THB at The Tongsai Bay. Cape Fahn Hotel occupies the Koh Fan islets.
  • Mid-range: beachfront and near-beach resorts along the bay.
  • Budget: a few guesthouses and small resorts on the road behind the beach, from about 1,000 THB.
  • Wellness: Absolute Sanctuary is on the Choeng Mon side, about 5 minutes from the airport. See wellness resorts.

For a private pool villa for a family or group, see Koh Samui villas; to compare areas, read where to stay on Koh Samui.

Aircraft noise

The airport is 5 km away, so you will hear planes at times, mostly in the daytime. Choeng Mon is far quieter than Bang Rak (Big Buddha Beach), which sits right under the approach. If noise matters to you, ask your resort which side of the bay your room faces.

Safety at Choeng Mon

Choeng Mon's calm is its selling point, but calm water still needs watching. Keep toddlers within arm's reach even in the shallows. Check the flag every day from October to December. Don't get caught on the sandbar by a rising tide. Avoid swimming at dusk and at night because of the small box jellyfish risk, and know where your resort's vinegar station is. Theft from beach bags is uncommon but not unknown; keep phones out of sight while you swim. For any emergency call 1669 (ambulance) or 1155 (Tourist Police); the nearest private hospitals are in Chaweng and Bophut, 15–20 minutes away. See Koh Samui beach safety.

Things to do near Choeng Mon

  • Tongson Bay: a small, quiet bay just north-west, clear and shallow December–April; the last stretch is a sand road.
  • Wat Plai Laem: the lakeside temple with the 18-armed Guanyin, about 06:00–18:00, free.
  • Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai): 07:00–18:30, free; cover shoulders and knees.
  • Bang Rak: beach clubs, seafood on the sand and the ferry piers.
  • Chaweng: shopping at Central Samui and the Thursday–Saturday walking street.

Getting there and parking

Choeng Mon is reached by Route 4171, the road that loops around the north-east tip past Big Buddha and Plai Laem. The main public entrance is opposite the Café Amazon, next to Tops Daily, signposted towards Bay Beach Resort, with parking there. Grab covers the area well. Songthaews run less often here than on the main ring road.
