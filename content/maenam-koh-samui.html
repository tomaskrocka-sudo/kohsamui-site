Maenam is the long, quiet, palm-lined beach on Koh Samui's north coast, 20–30 minutes from the airport. It has the cheapest beachfront stays on the island (mid-range about 1,000–2,500 THB a night), calm water in the dry season that reaches chest depth about 5 m out, a small Chinatown, the Koh Samui Immigration Office and Lomprayah's Pralarn pier for Koh Phangan and Koh Tao. Nights are quiet.

The beach and swimming

Maenam's beach runs for several kilometres along the north coast, backed by coconut palms, low-rise bungalow resorts and beach bars. The sand is golden and coarser than Chaweng's in places. It feels like Samui did before the big resorts: unhurried, uncrowded and local, with fishing longtails pulled up between the sunbeds.

In the dry season (January–September) the sea is usually calm. The edge is shallow, then the bottom drops so that you are chest-deep about 5 m out, which suits swimmers but means you need to watch small children closely. High tide narrows the beach to a thin strip in places; at low tide the water pulls back, as on all north-coast beaches. The water is not as clear as the east coast.

From October to December the north-east monsoon brings rain and, on windy days, chop and small waves. Maenam is less exposed than Chaweng or Lamai and is often swimmable when they are flagged, but take any red flag seriously. Box jellyfish are rare but recorded in Samui waters; avoid swimming at dusk or at night, and treat stings with vinegar for at least 30 seconds, no rubbing and no fresh water, then call 1669.

The beach, east to west

The eastern end, towards the headland that separates Maenam from Bophut, has the larger resorts and the W Koh Samui. The middle stretch, around Chinatown and the Pralarn pier, is the most local, with fishing longtails, family-run bungalows and cheap beach restaurants. Heading west, the beach gets quieter still and runs on towards Ban Tai and Bang Por. Walking the sand from end to end at low tide takes well over an hour; at high tide you will have to step round a few sea walls.

Chinatown and the Chinese temple

Maenam's small Chinatown sits around the ring road at Moo 1, marked by a Chinese gate. It is a working village, not a tourist street: shophouses, a fresh market, noodle shops and the colourful Chinese shrine San Chao Jao Eng Sae. At Chinese New Year (Saturday 6 February 2027) Maenam stages lion dances and celebrations at the shrine that are among the most family-friendly on the island. See Chinese New Year on Koh Samui.

Markets

J&J Street Food, on the ring road opposite the Chinese gate, runs daily from about 12:00 to 24:00, busiest in the evening. Maenam's old Thursday walking street in Chinatown has not been confirmed as running in 2026: one careful 2025 source says it disappeared after the pandemic, while older listings still show it. We'll update this page once we have checked it on the ground; until then, don't plan a trip around it. For a reliable weekly market, the Fisherman's Village Friday Walking Street is 10–15 minutes east. See Koh Samui markets.

Koh Samui Immigration Office

The island's immigration office is in Maenam, not Nathon (it moved in 2016, and some map pins are still wrong).

  • Address: 333 Maenam Road, Soi 1, Moo 1, Maenam 84330.
  • Phone: +66 77 423 440.
  • Hours: usually Monday–Friday 08:30–16:30; call ahead before you go.
  • Extensions: a 30-day extension of a visa-exempt stay costs 1,900 THB (form TM.7). Since 15 September 2026 most visa-exempt nationalities get 30 days on arrival.

Eating and nightlife

Maenam eats local: beach restaurants with plastic chairs, noodle shops in Chinatown, J&J Street Food in the evening and a handful of Western cafés for long-stayers. Bang Por Seafood (Takho), listed in the MICHELIN Guide Thailand 2026, has a Maenam address on the western side towards Bang Por and opens daily 10:00–22:00.

Nightlife is a beach bar with a sunset beer. The exception is Cape Away's Sunday party, with DJs, dancers and fire performers (open about 10:00–22:00). The W Koh Samui's Woobar is the upscale sunset option. For more, see Koh Samui restaurants and Koh Samui seafood.

  • Fisherman's Village: the Friday Walking Street, 10–15 minutes east.
  • Ban Tai beach: a quiet, shallow 1 km stretch just west of Maenam, good for small children at high tide.

Staying in Maenam

Maenam is where long-stayers, budget travellers and families who want a quiet base end up. Mid-range rooms cost about 1,000–2,500 THB a night in the shoulder season, and budget pool villas can be found from about 1,200–2,500 THB. Monthly deals are common.

  • Budget and mid-range: bungalow resorts directly on the sand, many family-run.
  • Luxury: the W Koh Samui, Santiburi and Napasai are on this stretch of coast, from about 10,000 THB a night.
  • Downside: quiet nights, few restaurants within walking distance of some resorts, and 25–30 minutes to Chaweng.

Families should note the drop-off about 5 m out; for toddlers, Choeng Mon is gentler. Compare areas in where to stay on Koh Samui and see longer stays in Koh Samui long stay.

Long stays in Maenam

Maenam is the north coast's long-stay base. Many bungalow resorts and villa owners offer monthly rates, and everyday costs are lower than in Chaweng.

  • Scooter: about 2,000–4,500 THB a month for a 125 cc bike; you need an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category for your insurance to pay.
  • SIM: tourist SIMs from AIS and True cost about 1,199 THB for 30 days with unlimited data.
  • Money: foreign-card ATM withdrawals cost 220–250 THB; AEON machines at Big C and Lotus's charge 150 THB.
  • Health: the nearest private hospitals are in Bophut and Chaweng, 15–30 minutes away; in an emergency call 1669.
  • Visa: the immigration office is on your doorstep for the 30-day extension (1,900 THB).

Safety in Maenam

Maenam is one of the safest-feeling parts of Samui, but the usual island risks apply. The ring road through Maenam is fast and has few pavements, so take care walking or riding at night; wear a helmet and don't ride after drinking. Songthaews thin out after about 20:00, so arrange a taxi or Grab for late returns. On the beach, swim near others, watch children at the drop-off and stay out on red-flag days. Mosquitoes bite at dusk, and dengue is present on the island, so use repellent. Emergency numbers: ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155.

Getting there and parking

Maenam sits on the ring road (Route 4169) on the north coast. Parking is easy almost everywhere, which is one of its quiet advantages. Grab works in Maenam but is thinner than in Chaweng; songthaews run along the ring road in the daytime (Chaweng to Maenam about 50–60 THB for locals, 70–100 THB tourist).
