Chaweng suits first-timers and nightlife: it has the most restaurants and bars on Koh Samui and is 10–15 minutes from the airport, with mid-range rooms at about 2,000–4,500 THB a night. Lamai is calmer and better value (about 1,500–3,500 THB), with a 4 km beach, a daily night market and the Hin Ta and Hin Yai rocks, but it is 25–30 minutes from the airport. Both are on the east coast, so both fly red flags from October to December.

The verdict

Pick Chaweng if…

  • It is your first time on Samui and you want everything within walking distance.
  • Nightlife matters: clubs, beach bars, fire shows, Muay Thai.
  • You have a short stay or an early flight (10–15 minutes to the airport).
  • You want the softest, whitest sand and the gentlest swimming slope (North Chaweng).
  • You like choice: hundreds of restaurants, a mall, three walking-street nights.

Pick Lamai if…

  • You want a beach town with a slower pace and fewer crowds.
  • Value matters: rooms are typically 20–30 % cheaper for the same standard.
  • You are a confident swimmer (the centre drops off quickly).
  • You like a nightly market dinner and a pub with a band rather than a club.
  • You want to be close to the south of the island: Hin Ta and Hin Yai, the waterfalls, the Red Temple and the road to Thong Krut for Pig Island boats.

The beaches compared

Chaweng

Chaweng is Samui's showpiece: a long bay of fine, pale sand with clear water. North Chaweng is the quietest section and the best for swimming, with a slope so gentle you can wade a long way out. Central Chaweng is the busiest, with ARKbar, the beach clubs, sunbeds, jet skis and nightly fire shows. South Chaweng is quieter, running down to the rocky point before Chaweng Noi, a small cove with coarser sand and some snorkelling off the rocks.

Lamai

Lamai is about 4 km of fine sand on the south-east coast. The centre is the best part for swimming, but the bottom shelves steeply, so you are out of your depth quickly; strong swimmers like it, parents of small children need to watch closely. The north end is shallow and stony, so wear reef shoes. The south end has the Hin Ta and Hin Yai rock formations. Overall Lamai feels more open and less built-up at the waterline than central Chaweng.

Swimming and safety on both

Both beaches face the open Gulf. From January to September they are among the best swimming beaches on Samui. From October to December the north-east monsoon brings surf, rip currents and red flags to both, and winter wind surges can do the same: on 19 February 2026, 1–2 m waves closed the water on Chaweng and Lamai. A red flag means no swimming. Box jellyfish are rare but recorded in Samui waters; avoid swimming at dusk and at night and know the first aid (vinegar for at least 30 seconds, no rubbing, no fresh water, call 1669). In the monsoon, sheltered Choeng Mon is the better swim from either town. See Koh Samui beach safety.

Nightlife and markets

Chaweng

  • Soi Green Mango: the club lane; The Green Mango Club is open nightly about 21:00–04:00.
  • ARKbar Beach Club: on the sand, open about 10:00–02:00, with free fire shows and DJs nightly.
  • Bar Solo: live cover bands most nights.
  • Chaweng Walking Street: Thursday, Friday and Saturday, about 16:00/17:00–22:00/23:00, by Chaweng Lake opposite Central Samui; Si Khao Night Market daily 17:00–23:00.
  • Muay Thai: both regular stadiums are in Chaweng: Samui International (Tuesday, Friday, Sunday) and Phetchbuncha (Monday, Wednesday, Saturday), fights from about 21:00, tickets about 1,500–2,500 THB. No fights on Thursdays. See Koh Samui Muay Thai.

Lamai

  • Lamai Night Market: daily from about 17:00 on Beach Road just before the bridge, with food stalls and live music; best 18:00–20:00.
  • Shamrock Irish Pub: a Lamai institution since 2005, with a show band nightly from about 21:30.
  • Beach bars: Rock Bar and Teddy Weed Beach for fire shows and reggae.
  • Status notes: Beach Republic in south Lamai was largely closed in 2025–2026, and the old Lamai Sunday walking street has not been confirmed as running in 2026. We'll update this page if either returns.

Lamai's nightlife is relaxed and older; Chaweng's is bigger, louder and later. Read Chaweng nightlife, Lamai nightlife and Koh Samui nightlife.

In the rainy season

From October to December neither town is at its best: both beaches face the north-east monsoon, so the sea is often rough and flagged, and November averages about 445 mm of rain. Chaweng copes better on wet days because it has the Central Samui mall and far more indoor restaurants and bars. Lamai's outdoor night market can be rained off. If your trip falls in these months and swimming matters, consider the sheltered bays of the north-east or the west coast instead, or read Koh Samui in November.

Families vs couples

Families

With small children, North Chaweng is the safer swim of the two because of the gentle slope, and it is quiet at night. Central Chaweng is loud until about 02:00. Lamai's steep centre and stony north end are less suitable for toddlers, but older children who swim well enjoy it, and Lamai's lower prices stretch a family budget. If calm water is the priority, look past both to Choeng Mon. See where to stay with kids.

Couples

Couples who want to go out, try different restaurants every night and be close to the airport do best in Chaweng (or quiet Chaweng Noi, 5–10 minutes away). Couples who want quiet evenings, better value and a more local feel do best in Lamai. For something more romantic, consider Bophut or a private pool villa.

Getting around and traffic

Chaweng and Lamai are about 12 km apart, 20–25 minutes by car, along the coast road past Chaweng Noi, the Lad Koh viewpoint and Coral Cove.

  • Songthaew Chaweng to Lamai: about 30–40 THB for locals; 50–60 THB is a fair tourist price. They mostly stop by 20:00–22:00 and take cash only.
  • Grab: works well in both, best in Chaweng; about 200–350 THB between them.
  • Airport: taxi to Chaweng about 400–600 THB; to Lamai approx. 400–700 THB depending on the zone.
  • Traffic: Chaweng Beach Road crawls from late afternoon until late. Lamai's Beach Road is busier on market evenings but rarely gridlocked.
  • Scooters: police checkpoints run on Chaweng Lake Road; the helmet fine is up to 2,000 THB per person since 1 June 2025.

Why not both?

The two towns are only 20–25 minutes apart, so the choice is less final than it seems. Many visitors stay in one and spend an evening in the other: Lamai people go to Chaweng for a big night out, Chaweng people go to Lamai for the night market and Hin Ta and Hin Yai. On a longer trip, splitting your stay (for example, three nights in Chaweng, then four in Lamai) gives you both. Coral Cove, Silver Beach and the Lad Koh viewpoint make a good stop on the drive between them.
