Nathon is Samui's old capital and its main west-coast port, 24 km (30–40 minutes) from Chaweng. Seatran car ferries sail roughly hourly to Donsak on the mainland (about 1.5 hours, around 170–180 THB for a foot passenger at the pier), and Lomprayah, Songserm and Boonsiri boats run to Koh Phangan (from 200 THB) and on to Koh Tao. The town has the island's government offices and the government hospital, and a waterfront night food market every day from about 16:30 to 22:00, busiest on Tuesdays. It is a transit and day-visit town, not a beach base, and its pier is a free sunset spot.

Map of Nathon

Nathon is a compact grid of streets behind a long waterfront road (Chonwithi Road). The main pier sticks out into the bay in the middle of town, with a second ferry jetty a few hundred metres north. The night market sets up near the pier, and the old shophouse streets run parallel to the seafront.

Arriving at Nathon pier

When a ferry docks, taxis and songthaews wait at the pier. Agree the price before you get in; taxis have no meters. As a guide for 2026, a taxi from Nathon to Chaweng costs about 500–750 THB, and a songthaew about 60–80 THB at the local fare (visitors are often asked 80–120 THB). Grab works in Nathon in the daytime, but fewer drivers wait on the west coast than in Chaweng. Some ferry tickets include a minibus transfer to your hotel; check when you book.

Night market and food

Nathon's night food market sets up near the pier and the waterfront every evening, roughly 16:30–22:00. It is mostly a local market: tables in the middle, Thai food stalls around the edges, and few tourists. Tuesday is the busiest night. Popular stalls run out later in the evening, so come around 17:00–18:00, and bring cash.

Old town and Chinese heritage

Nathon was Samui's administrative centre long before the beach towns grew, and it still has rows of two-storey wooden and concrete shophouses, small family businesses and the island's district offices. Many of Samui's Chinese families have Hainanese roots, and the Hainan shrine in Nathon is the focus of their festivals. At Chinese New Year (Saturday 6 February 2027), Nathon holds the island's most traditional celebration, which has included ritual face-piercing by spirit mediums; it is striking but may not suit children. Maenam's Chinatown celebrations are more family-friendly. Walk the streets in the early morning or late afternoon, when it is cooler, and dress respectfully at the shrine. Festivals and events in Nathon

  • Chak Phra and Thod Pha Pa: at the end of Buddhist Lent each year (October or November), decorated temple floats from about 16 Samui temples are pulled through Nathon to the pier, with a fair of about nine days. Recent editions ran 18–26 October 2022 and 6–14 November 2023; the procession day brings heavy traffic around the pier. Dates are set locally each year.
  • Songkran: Ang Thong subdistrict (Nathon) was one of three official Songkran zones on Samui in 2026, with events from 12 April. The 2027 public holidays are 13–15 April. See Songkran on Koh Samui.
  • Red Cross Fair: held around late February (22 February in 2026) with a traditional costume parade, traditionally in Nathon.
  • Chinese New Year: 6 February 2027 at the Hainan shrine.

No swimming beach in Nathon

Nathon has no beach you would want to swim from. The town faces the sea along a sea wall, with ferries, fishing boats and piers in the bay, so it is harbour water, not a swimming spot. The nearest swimming beaches are Lipa Noi, 6 km south (very shallow and calm, swim at high tide), and the north coast beyond Laem Yai. The west coast is sheltered from the October–December north-east monsoon waves, though November is still the wettest month island-wide, at about 445 mm.

Sunset on the pier

Nathon faces west, and the end of the main pier and the waterfront are free places to watch the sun go down over the sea, with ferries and fishing boats in the foreground. Sunset ranges from about 17:55 in November to 18:45 in July. Combine it with the night market, which is in full swing by sunset. Other west-coast viewpoints are on Koh Samui sunset spots.

Nearby places

  • Hin Lad waterfall: about 2 km inland, next to Wat Namtok Hin Lad; a jungle trail of roughly two hours return, steep and slippery after rain.
  • Lipa Noi: 6 km south; shallow sunset beach and the Raja ferry port.
  • Laem Yai: 5 km north; a quiet sunset shore.
  • Taling Ngam: 11 km south; Five Islands sunset and Baan Suan Lung Khai.
  • Maenam: 14 km east; long swimming beach and the immigration office.
  • Koh Samui waterfalls: Na Muang 1 and 2 are about 20 minutes away.

Staying in Nathon: transit only

Stay in Nathon only if you need to: for an early ferry, an appointment at the government offices, or a very cheap base on a budget. There are a few simple hotels and guesthouses in town, but there is no swimming beach, little evening life beyond the market, and the main beaches are 30–40 minutes away. For a quiet west-coast stay, Lipa Noi or Taling Ngam are better; for most first visits, stay on the east or north coast.
