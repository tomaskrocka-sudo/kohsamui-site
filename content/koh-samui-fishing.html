The shared fishing day

Our shared day trip uses a traditional wooden Thai fishing boat for up to 20 guests from Big Buddha pier on the north coast. Pickup is from about 09:00, the boat leaves around 10:00 for the first spot, and lunch is served on board at about 12:00 with fish from the morning's catch cooked by the crew. After a second session at a new spot you are back at the pier between 16:00 and 17:00. Rods, bait and lures, an English-speaking guide, soft drinks, life jackets and accident insurance are included, with return hotel transfer from the main areas; Bang Por, Nathon, Lipa Noi, Lipa Yai, Taling Ngam and Phang Ka add 500 THB per person. It is bottom and line fishing, relaxed and social, not big-game: the catch is not guaranteed. Trolling is for private trips only.

The Gulf of Thailand is heavily fished by commercial boats, so expect plenty of small and medium fish rather than trophies. The skipper's local knowledge matters more than the gear.

Night squid fishing

Squid fishing is one of the most Thai things you can do on the water. After dark the boat anchors in a calm bay and switches on strong lamps. The light draws plankton, small fish follow, and the squid come up to hunt them. You drop a light hand line with a coloured jig, let it sink, and lift it gently in short pulls; when a squid grabs it, wind steadily. The crew cook the catch on board, often grilled or stir-fried with garlic. It is easy enough for children and complete beginners, and calm, dark nights, away from the full moon, usually fish best. Our night squid fishing trip on the King 9 catamaran runs 19:00–23:00 with dinner, at 17,500 THB for 2 guests and 1,500 THB per extra guest up to 10; transfer isn't included (a private van for up to 10 costs 3,000 THB). Night trips can also be arranged on the shared Thai fishing boat on request.

Private fishing charters

A private boat suits families, groups of friends and anyone who wants to choose between trolling, bottom fishing and a swim.

Thai fishing boat from the south coast

A private traditional fishing boat with captain and crew, about 6 hours on the water: trolling along the coast, snorkelling and lunch or a BBQ at Koh Tan, an optional stop at Koh Madsum (Pig Island), and line fishing on the way back. Rods, tackle, bait, fruit and soft drinks and snorkel masks are included, with hotel transfer from the main areas. Prices per boat: 10,500 THB for 1–3 guests, 12,000 for 4–5, 13,000 for 6–7, 14,500 for 8–9 and 15,500 for 10–11. The south coast is sheltered from the north-east monsoon, so this trip runs on more days from October to December than north-coast boats.

Sport-fishing speedboat

A 36 ft twin-engine boat with sonar and a toilet, for a full day of trolling and bottom fishing from Bang Rak pier, 08:30–17:00. It costs 32,400 THB for up to 10 guests (500 THB per extra guest up to 14), with rods, reels, bait and tackle, fruit, snacks and soft drinks; lunch is extra. Transfer is included from Bophut, Choeng Mon and Chaweng.

Rules and responsible fishing

  • No fishing in Ang Thong. Ang Thong is a national marine park, and taking fish or any marine life inside it is prohibited. Boats fish outside the park boundary.
  • Catch and release. Keep what the crew will cook for lunch and let the rest go, especially large breeding fish and anything undersized.
  • No coral damage. Anchors and lines snag reefs; skippers should anchor on sand or drift.
  • Protected species. Turtles, sharks and rays that are hooked by accident go straight back.
  • Rubbish. Old line and hooks go back to the pier in a bag, never over the side.

Season and sea

February to September is the best time to fish from Samui, with the calmest seas from February to April. From October to December the north-east monsoon brings rain and swell; north-coast trips are cancelled more often, while the sheltered south coast keeps fishing on most days. Calm mornings fish best, and afternoon wind makes bottom fishing harder. Check the sea before you book: [kp_sea_now]. Read more in our sea conditions guide. If we or our partner cancel for unsafe seas, you choose a new date or a refund.

What to bring

  • A hat, sunglasses and reef-safe sunscreen; there is little shade on a fishing deck.
  • A rash vest or long-sleeved shirt and light trousers.
  • Seasickness tablets, taken before you board, if you're prone to it. Anchored boats roll.
  • Swimwear if your trip includes a snorkel stop.
  • Cash for drinks, tips for the crew and the Koh Madsum landing fee (about 50 THB) if you stop there.
  • Your own favourite lures or reel if you have them; rods and bait are provided.

Fishing with children

Children enjoy fishing as long as something bites. The shared day trip has a child price of 1,300 THB, and night squid fishing is the most reliable for young anglers because squid are easy to catch on light lines. Choose an early finish (the 19:00–23:00 catamaran trip rather than a trip past midnight), bring a jumper for the breeze, and make sure every child wears a life jacket on deck.

Which fishing trip should you choose?

  • A relaxed, sociable day on a budget: the shared Thai fishing boat (1,800 THB), with lunch cooked from the catch.
  • A family or small group who also want to snorkel: the private Thai fishing boat from the south coast, from 10,500 THB for up to 3 and 15,500 THB for up to 11, with Koh Tan and a BBQ.

Where the fishing boats leave from

Shared and sport-fishing trips leave from Big Buddha (Bang Rak) pier on the north coast; private fishing boats to the southern islands leave from the south coast near Thong Krut. To see the island's working fishing fleet and buy the morning catch, visit the fish market at Hua Thanon. For seafood without catching it yourself, see our restaurants guide.
