Tongson (also spelled Thongson) Bay is a small, sheltered, quiet bay of white sand on Koh Samui's north-east coast, between Choeng Mon and the Plai Laem headland, 10–15 minutes from the airport. It is clear and shallow from December to April and rougher from May to October. The north end is better for swimming, the rocky south end for snorkelling, and the last stretch in is a sand road. Facilities are minimal, which is the point.

The bay

Tongson is a short, curved bay tucked into the north-east corner of Samui, framed by green headlands. It is small enough to see end to end from the sand. The beach is white and the water, in the right season, is a clear turquoise over sand, with granite rocks at the southern end. A handful of resorts, villas and one or two restaurants sit behind it; there is no beach-bar strip, no jet skis and no traffic noise. It is the kind of place locals take visiting friends for a quiet afternoon.

Tongson is not part of Choeng Mon, though it is only a few minutes away by road. Its name is often confused with Tongsai Bay, the private bay of the resort called The Tongsai Bay, so check the spelling when you search a map. [check]

Swimming and snorkelling

From December to April Tongson is clear, shallow and calm, and a lovely place to swim. The northern part of the beach has the better sandy bottom for swimming. The southern end is rocky, which makes it poor for wading but the best part for a snorkel: small reef fish, urchins and some coral around the boulders. Bring your own mask and water shoes; there is no rental desk.

From May to October the bay is rougher, and in the north-east monsoon (October–December) strong winds can push waves in. There are no lifeguards. Swim near others, don't swim alone in rough water, and stay out on red-flag days or when you see waves breaking on the rocks.

Box jellyfish are rare but recorded around Samui and Koh Phangan, most often in calm, warm water and after rain, and near shore at dusk. Swim in daylight. First aid is vinegar for at least 30 seconds, no rubbing, no fresh water; then call 1669. With no shop on the beach, bring a small bottle of vinegar if you have children with you. The access road

Tongson is reached from Route 4171, the road that loops around the north-east tip. A side road heads towards the bay, and the last stretch is a sand and dirt track. In the dry season a scooter manages it with care; after rain it becomes slippery and rutted, and a car with low clearance may struggle. Ride slowly, keep to the firm sand and don't attempt it on a scooter if you are not an experienced rider. Parking is limited to the end of the track, so don't block private gates or resort entrances.

Grab and taxis will drop you near the bay, but don't count on finding a car for the return; agree a pickup time or keep the driver's number. Read Koh Samui roads before you ride, and remember the helmet fine is up to 2,000 THB per person since June 2025.

Facilities

Facilities are thin, and that is the trade-off for the quiet. Thongson Bay Restaurant has loungers you can use if you eat or drink there. There are no public toilets, showers, lifeguards or shops on the beach, and little shade beyond the trees at the back. Bring water, sunscreen, snacks, a hat and cash. The nearest 7-Eleven, cafés and pharmacies are at Choeng Mon or on the ring road near Big Buddha, 5–10 minutes away. Mobile signal is generally fine. [check]

A half day at Tongson Bay

  1. 08:30 Ride or drive in while the track is dry and the light is soft; park at the end of the sand road without blocking gates.
  2. 09:00 Swim at the north end while the sea is flat.
  3. 10:00 Snorkel the rocks at the south end in water shoes; stay close to shore.
  4. 11:30 Lunch and a lounger at the beach restaurant, or move on to Choeng Mon for more choice.
  5. 13:00 Head out before the midday sun; visit Wat Plai Laem or Big Buddha on the way back.

What to bring

  • Water, snacks and cash; there is no shop on the beach.
  • Mask, snorkel and water shoes.
  • Sunscreen and a hat; shade is limited to the trees at the back.
  • A charged phone and your driver's number for the return.

Safety at Tongson Bay

Tongson is quiet, which is lovely, but it also means nobody is watching the water. There are no lifeguards and no flags. Use common sense: don't swim alone, don't swim after drinking, keep children within arm's reach, and leave the water if waves start breaking on the rocks. The sand track is the other hazard: most scooter accidents on Samui involve tourists on unfamiliar roads, so go slowly and wear a helmet. For emergencies call 1669 (ambulance) or 1155 (Tourist Police). Read safety on Koh Samui.

With small children, Choeng Mon is easier. For a couple's quiet morning with a mask and snorkel, Tongson wins. They are 5 minutes apart, so you can do both in a day. Early risers get the sunrise over the sea from either beach, roughly 06:00–06:40 depending on the month.

Where to stay

A few small resorts and private villas sit around the bay. Melati Beach Resort & Spa and Thongson Bay Bungalows are the best-known, and villa developments on the headlands rent sea-view houses. Staying here means very quiet nights and a short drive for dinner. If you want more restaurants on foot, base yourself in Choeng Mon (mid-range about 3,000–7,000 THB) and visit Tongson for an afternoon. For pool villas see Koh Samui villas, and compare areas in where to stay on Koh Samui.

Eating and nightlife

Eat at the beach restaurant or your resort. For more choice, Choeng Mon's beach restaurants and the lane behind them are 5–10 minutes away, and Bang Rak's seafood strip and beach clubs are 10–15 minutes. There is no nightlife at Tongson; that is the reason to come. See Koh Samui beach clubs for the nearest lively option.

Seen from the sea: Koh Som and Tongson Bay

Many people see Tongson for the first time from a boat. A private speedboat or catamaran from the north coast can anchor off the bay for a swim, then run out to Koh Som, the small island off Samui's north-east, or across to Koh Phangan's beaches. It is one of the best short charters on the island in the calm months, from February to April.

  • Private speedboat: from 19,000 THB a boat (30 ft, 4 guests base, up to 8).
  • Catamaran charter: sailing catamarans from 20,000 THB a boat.

Things to do nearby

  • Choeng Mon: the calm family bay with the sandbar to Koh Fan Noi, 5 minutes south.
  • Wat Plai Laem: the lake temple with the 18-armed Guanyin; about 06:00–18:00, free.
  • Big Buddha: 07:00–18:30, free; dress for a temple.
  • Plai Laem and Bang Rak: beach clubs, seafood and the ferry piers.
