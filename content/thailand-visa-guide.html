Since 15 September 2026, visitors from the 60 visa-exempt countries, including the UK, US, Canada, Australia and all EU states, get 30 days on arrival, not 60. You can extend once, by up to 30 days, at Koh Samui Immigration in Maenam (form TM.7, 1,900 THB). Every foreign arrival must file the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) within 3 days before landing, and no tourist entry fee is being collected as of November 2026.

Last checked: November 2026, against the Royal Gazette notices of 31 August 2026, the Tourism Authority of Thailand, the Thai Immigration Bureau and Thai embassy notices. Visa rules change at short notice. Check the official pages linked at the end of this guide before you book flights.

What changed on 15 September 2026

On 31 August 2026, the Royal Gazette published four Ministry of Interior notices. They repealed the 60-day visa exemption that had covered 93 countries since July 2024 and replaced it with three narrower schemes from 15 September 2026:

  • a 30-day visa exemption for 60 countries and territories, for tourism only;
  • a 15-day visa exemption for Mauritius and Seychelles;
  • visa on arrival for just 3 nationalities (Azerbaijan, Belarus and Serbia), down from 31.

The old scheme also allowed short business trips. The new exemption is for tourism. If you are coming to Koh Samui for meetings, a conference or work, ask the Thai embassy which visa you need.

If you entered before 15 September 2026

Anyone who entered Thailand on or before 14 September 2026 keeps the permission to stay stamped in their passport, including a full 60-day stamp. Your next entry falls under the new rules. If you leave Samui for a weekend in Kuala Lumpur and fly back, you get 30 days, not 60.

Countries that lost visa-free entry

Dozens of countries were on the old 93-country list but are not on the new 60-country list. Some of them are still covered by a bilateral agreement (Brazil, for example, still gets 90 days). Others now need a visa or, for three countries, a visa on arrival. If your passport was visa-free in 2025, don't assume it still is. Check the tables above before booking.

Visa on arrival: only 3 nationalities

Visa on arrival now applies only to passports from Azerbaijan, Belarus and Serbia. Travellers who used visa on arrival before September 2026 and are no longer on the list must apply for a visa online before they fly. Samui Airport is an international airport, but most visitors arrive via Bangkok, so you will usually clear immigration at Suvarnabhumi before your domestic flight to the island.

Visa required: how to apply online

If your passport isn't covered by an exemption, a bilateral deal or visa on arrival, apply through the official Thai e-Visa portal at thaievisa.go.th. It is run by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and replaces paper applications at most embassies. Apply at least a few weeks before departure, upload a passport scan, photo, flight booking, accommodation and bank statement, and pay the fee online. Only use the .go.th address. Agencies that sell "Thai e-visa" services on other domains charge extra and some are outright scams.

Before you fly

TDAC: the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card

Every foreign national arriving by air, land or sea must complete the TDAC online. It replaced the paper TM.6 card on 1 May 2025. File it within the 3 days before you arrive at tdac.immigration.go.th. You will need your passport details, flight number, the address of your first night's stay (your Samui hotel or villa is fine) and the purpose of your visit. Keep the confirmation email or QR code on your phone.

The official TDAC is free. In March 2026 the Immigration Bureau said at least 10% of foreign arrivals had registered through look-alike websites, some of which charge fees and harvest passport data. If a site asks for a card payment, you are on the wrong site. Transit passengers who don't pass through Thai immigration don't need a TDAC.

Every traveller needs their own TDAC, children and babies included. If your plans change after you file, check the details on the official site before you fly, and file again if needed, still free of charge.

Where you clear immigration on the way to Samui

You clear Thai immigration at your first Thai airport, not necessarily on Samui:

  • Via Bangkok (Suvarnabhumi): the most common route. You pass immigration in Bangkok, then take a domestic flight to Samui (about 1 hour 5 minutes). The Samui arrival is domestic, with no passport check.
  • Direct international flight to Samui: from Singapore, for example. You clear immigration at Samui Airport's small international terminal, which is quick but can queue when two flights land together.
  • Overland or by ferry: if you enter Thailand at a land border (for example from Malaysia), that crossing counts towards your 2 land entries a year. The ferry from Surat Thani (Donsak) to Samui is domestic, so there is no check at Nathon or Lipa Noi piers.

See Bangkok to Koh Samui and Samui Airport for routes and timings.

Passport validity and onward ticket

Your passport should be valid for at least 6 months from the day you arrive, with at least 1 blank page. Airlines check this at check-in and can refuse boarding. You may also be asked for proof of onward or return travel within your permitted stay. A ferry ticket from Samui to Koh Phangan doesn't count, because it doesn't leave Thailand.

Proof of funds

Immigration can ask to see at least 10,000 THB per person or 20,000 THB per family, in cash or equivalent. The UK government's travel advice notes that British nationals have been refused entry without this evidence. Checks are random, but carry a recent bank statement on your phone and some cash.

The tourist entry fee: proposed, not in force

A flat 450 THB tourism fee for every foreign arrival has been proposed, replacing an older plan of 300 THB by air and 150 THB by land or sea. A public consultation took place on 6 October 2026. The draft still has to pass the National Tourism Policy Committee and Cabinet and then be published in the Royal Gazette. Collection from air arrivals would start up to 180 days after publication, with land and sea arrivals deferred by about a year. Officials have floated the first quarter of 2027 as a target. Nothing is collected as of November 2026. Ignore any website that asks you to pre-pay a "Thailand entry fee". We re-check this every month.

Extending your stay on Koh Samui

Koh Samui Immigration is at 333 Maenam Road, Soi 1, Moo 1, Maenam 84330, on the north coast. It moved from Nathon in 2016, so older map pins in Nathon are wrong. Phone +66 77 423 440. It usually opens Monday to Friday, 08:30–16:30, and closes on weekends and Thai public holidays. Call ahead before a long drive. What to bring for a 30-day extension

  • Your passport, plus signed copies of the photo page, the entry stamp and your TDAC confirmation.
  • A completed TM.7 form (available at the office or from the Immigration Bureau website).
  • One 4×6 cm photo taken within the last 6 months.
  • 1,900 THB in cash.
  • Your Samui address: hotel booking or rental contract, and the TM.30 receipt if you rent privately.
  • A flight out of Thailand, in case the officer asks.

Allow most of a morning, and more in the December–April high season. Arrive soon after opening with every copy already made and signed: you can't count on a photocopier at the office. From Chaweng it is roughly 15 km along the ring road (about 25 minutes by scooter or taxi), from Bophut about 6 km, from Lamai about 25 km and from Nathon about 13 km. Wear long trousers or a knee-length skirt and covered shoulders; government offices expect neat dress, and staff can ask you to come back properly dressed.

Apply in the last week or so of your 30 days. The new permission runs from the end of your current stamp, so applying early doesn't cost you days, but don't leave it to your final day in case the office is closed for a holiday.

Rules while you are in Thailand

90-day reporting (TM.47)

Anyone who stays in Thailand for more than 90 consecutive days must report their address to immigration on form TM.47. Holiday visitors on a 30 + 30 day stay never reach 90 days. DTV holders on a 180-day entry do. You can report from 15 days before to 7 days after the due date. The first report must be made in person in Maenam; later reports can usually be filed online at tm47.immigration.go.th. Leaving Thailand resets the count. A late report costs 2,000 THB.

Address notification (TM.30)

The owner or manager of the place where a foreigner stays must notify immigration within 24 hours. Hotels and licensed resorts do this automatically at check-in. If you rent a private villa or condo on Samui, ask the landlord for the TM.30 receipt. Immigration usually asks for it when you extend. Landlords who don't file can be fined up to 2,000 THB.

Re-entry permit

If you hold an extension of stay (for example retirement, marriage or a DTV extension) and fly out without a re-entry permit, the extension is cancelled. Apply at Koh Samui Immigration on form TM.8 before you leave. A single re-entry permit costs 1,000 THB and a multiple one costs 3,800 THB. Visa-exempt tourists don't need one.

Overstay fines and bans

Overstaying costs 500 THB per day, capped at 20,000 THB, paid at the airport or at immigration. Longer overstays also bring a ban on returning. The ban depends on whether you hand yourself in or get caught.

Common visa mistakes on Samui

  • Counting 60 days out of habit. Visitors who came in 2025 often book 6-week trips without an extension plan. On a 30-day stamp, a 6-week stay needs the TM.7 extension or a tourist visa.
  • Leaving the extension until the last day. If your stamp ends on a Saturday or a public holiday, the office is shut. Go a few days early.
  • Using a paid TDAC site. It wastes money and exposes your passport data. Use tdac.immigration.go.th only.
  • Working on an exemption. Teaching yoga, crewing a boat or selling villas for a Samui business needs a work permit, even if you aren't paid in Thailand.
  • Trusting old map pins. The immigration office is in Maenam, not Nathon.
  • Booking a flight home that leaves after your stamp ends. Even 1 day over costs 500 THB and goes on your record.

Visa runs in 2026

Leaving Thailand and coming straight back gives you a fresh 30 days, but repeated runs attract questions. Land crossings are capped at 2 visa-exempt entries per calendar year, so the classic minibus run to the Malaysian border can only be used twice a year. Flying out of Samui to Singapore (direct flights) or via Bangkok to Kuala Lumpur is the usual option. Officers at Bangkok and Samui airports can refuse entry to travellers who appear to be living in Thailand without a proper visa. If you want to stay for months, get a DTV or another long-stay visa instead.

If your passport is lost or stolen on Samui:

  1. Report it to the Tourist Police (1155) or the nearest police station and get a written police report.
  2. Contact your embassy in Bangkok for a new passport or emergency travel document. Most embassies need an appointment, and it can take several working days.
  3. Take the new document and the police report to Koh Samui Immigration in Maenam (or immigration at your departure airport) so your entry record and permitted stay can be linked to the new passport before you fly.

Keep a photo of your passport photo page and entry stamp on your phone and in your email. It speeds up every step, and it is also what Koh Samui Immigration asks to see when you extend.

Plan your Samui days around your visa

With 30 days on a visa exemption, most visitors spend 1–2 weeks on Samui. Pig Island runs all year: book the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult). Ang Thong National Marine Park is closed from 1 November to 15 December and reopens on 16 December, so book the Ang Thong speedboat day tour (1,800 THB adult) for later dates. The Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB adult) is the other big day out. Hotel pickup is included from most areas of Koh Samui; +400 THB per person return from Lipa Noi, Nathon, Bang Kao, Thong Krut and other remote areas.
