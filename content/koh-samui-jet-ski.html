Beach jet ski rental on Chaweng and Lamai is where most Koh Samui jet ski complaints start: riders are blamed for "damage" that was already there and pressured to pay. Never hand over your passport as a deposit (the UK government's travel advice says exactly this), film the jet ski all round before you ride, and agree the price and terms in writing. The safer way to ride is guided: a 1-hour guided ride from Maenam starts at 3,500 THB per jet ski, a 3-hour crossing to Koh Phangan at 6,500 THB, and our guided Jet Ski Safari to Pig Island costs 9,950 THB per jet ski for a driver and a passenger.

The damage scam and how it works

You rent a jet ski by the hour on a busy beach. When you bring it back, the operator points to a scratch, crack or dent and says you caused it. The demand can be a few thousand to tens of thousands of baht. If you left your passport as a deposit, they keep it until you pay. More staff or "friends" may turn up to add pressure. Some damage was there before you rode; some machines are deliberately rented out with old damage hidden by water or angle. The UK government's travel advice tells visitors not to hand over passports as a guarantee to jet-ski rental businesses, and notes that companies may hold on to passports against claimed damage. The pattern is old, it hasn't disappeared, and it is why we sell only guided rides. Our Koh Samui scams guide covers the other common traps.

Red flags before you ride

  • The operator wants your passport, not a cash or card deposit.
  • No price board, or a price that changes when you ask what's included.
  • Jet skis with taped-over patches, scuffed hulls or fresh paint in odd places.
  • No life jackets, or life jackets that don't fasten.
  • The operator discourages you from filming the jet ski.
  • Pressure to go out "now, quickly" without a briefing.

How to rent safely: 8 steps

  1. Film everything. Walk slowly round the jet ski with your phone, including the hull below the waterline, the seat and the handlebars, with the operator in the shot. Do it again when you return.
  2. Get the price in writing: the time, the price per jet ski and what happens if you are late.
  3. Never leave your passport. Offer a photocopy and a cash deposit you can afford to lose, and get a receipt. A hotel or tour desk may help you agree a fair deposit.
  4. Check the life jackets fit both riders and are fastened before you start.
  5. Ask where you may ride: stay outside the swimming area and the buoy lines.
  6. Stay sober. Alcohol and jet skis cause most serious accidents, and insurers refuse claims.
  7. Know the number: save the Tourist Police (1155) in your phone before you go out.
  8. Prefer a guided ride with a named operator and written terms.

Rules and safety on the water

  • Swimmers first. Keep well clear of swimmers, snorkellers and anything inside a buoyed swimming area; slow right down near the shore.
  • Boats and longtails have right of way in practice. Watch for fishing lines and nets around the islands.
  • Distance. Never chase or cut across another jet ski's wake at speed; most injuries are collisions.
  • Passengers hold the driver or the strap, not the seat edge, and keep feet on the footrests.
  • Sea state. From October to December the north-east monsoon brings swell and red flags to Chaweng and Lamai; don't ride when the flag is red.
  • Sun and spray. Wear a rash vest and sunglasses with a strap. An hour at speed burns more than a day on the beach.

Licences, age and insurance

Tourists renting a jet ski on Samui's beaches or joining a guided ride are not normally asked for a boat licence. That makes the briefing and the guide more important, not less. On our Pig Island safari the driver must be 18 or older and passengers 8 or older. Travel insurance is the weak point: many policies exclude jet skis, or exclude them unless you hold a licence or ride with a licensed operator, and almost all refuse claims if you had been drinking. Read your policy's water-sports section before you ride, and check whether it covers liability if you injure someone else.

If something goes wrong

  1. Stay calm. Don't argue on the beach and don't hand over cash under pressure.
  2. Show your video from before the ride.
  3. Call the Tourist Police on 1155 and ask them to attend. They speak English and handle jet ski disputes regularly. Our Tourist Police guide explains what they can do.
  4. If you must pay to get your passport back, get a written receipt with the operator's name, then report it to the Tourist Police.
  5. Contact your embassy if your passport is withheld and the police can't resolve it.
  6. If anyone is hurt, call 1669 for an ambulance first; see hospitals on Koh Samui.

From a jet ski centre on Maenam beach, a guide on his own jet ski leads 1-hour rides to Koh Som, 1.5-hour sunset rides, and 3 or 4-hour crossings to Koh Phangan with three or four beach and snorkel stops. Jet skis and children

Children ride as passengers only: 8 or older on our Pig Island safari, with a fitted child life jacket. They should be able to hold on with both hands for long stretches over chop. Younger children are happier on a boat: the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, 1,250 THB child) visits the same islands, and a private speedboat (11,950 THB for up to 10) keeps a family together on one boat.

Best time of year for jet skiing

February to September is the calmest time on Samui, with flat mornings and the occasional windy afternoon. The south and west coasts, where our safari runs, are sheltered from the north-east monsoon, so the safari runs most days even from October to December, though the guide may shorten the route. If we cancel for weather, you choose a full refund or a new date. Mornings are usually calmer than afternoons all year.
