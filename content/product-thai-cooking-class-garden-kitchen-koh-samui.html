Why go: cook in a Samui home garden, not a hotel kitchen

Many cooking schools on Koh Samui run large classes in purpose-built rooms. This one is small and personal: up to four guests in an open-air kitchen surrounded by the herbs and vegetables that go into the dishes. You get more time at the wok, more questions answered, and a better idea of how Thai families actually cook.

What you'll do

The class starts with a walk through the garden picking lemongrass, basil, chillies and kaffir lime leaves, with an optional market stop beforehand in the morning. You then pound a curry paste, prepare a starter, a soup or salad and a main, learning to balance sweet, sour, salty and spicy. At the end you sit down to eat everything you cooked and take home a recipe booklet.

Who it suits

  • Couples and small groups of friends; solo travellers who want a sociable morning.
  • Vegetarians and anyone with mild food allergies, as the small format makes changes easy.
  • A rainy-day plan: the kitchen is covered.

Who should choose something else

  • Groups larger than 7: ask us about a bigger school.
  • Families with young children may prefer an activity with more movement, such as the capybara café trip.

Good to know

  • Spice: you decide how hot your dishes are; the cook will show you how to adjust.
  • Allergies: peanuts, shellfish and fish sauce are common in Thai cooking; tell us in advance.
  • Mosquitoes: the garden is green and open; repellent helps, especially in the afternoon.

Before you book

Choose the morning class if you want the optional market stop, and tell us about allergies and how spicy you like your food. Because classes are capped at four, popular dates in the December to April high season fill a few days ahead. Private classes for five to seven guests are arranged on WhatsApp.
