Why go: watching elephants behave like elephants

Samui's best elephant visits are the ones where you mostly watch. This sanctuary sits on the hillside above Chaweng Noi and keeps a small group of elephants retired from logging and trekking work. A visit starts with a short film about where the animals came from. You then watch from a raised platform while they feed, help hand out fruit and vegetables, and walk a few metres behind the herd as they move through the forest. Lunch is a Thai set menu on the rooftop, looking down towards the coast.

The rules here are the ones that matter for animal welfare. There is no riding, no bathing with the elephants, no painting and no shows. The elephants decide how close they come. Guides explain each animal's history and health, and that is often the part of the visit people remember most.

Who it suits, and who should choose something else

  • Good for: families with children aged 4 and up, first-time visitors who want an ethical elephant visit, and anyone short of time. The 3-hour visit fits into a morning or an afternoon.
  • Choose something else if: you want hands-on bathing or mud sessions. We don't sell those for welfare reasons. If you'd like a longer programme or an elephant museum, see the elephant museum and jungle walk.
  • Limited mobility: the platform and restaurant are easy, but the forest walk is on uneven ground. Guests can skip it and wait at the platform.

Good to know

  • Morning or afternoon: the 09:00 session is cooler and the elephants are usually more active. The 14:00 session suits late risers and families with toddlers who nap.
  • Rainy season: from October to December paths can be slippery. Wear shoes with grip.
  • Behaviour: follow the guide's distance rules. Don't touch an elephant's trunk or ears unless the guide invites you to, and don't bring your own food.
  • Photos: phones are fine. Drones are not allowed over the animals.
