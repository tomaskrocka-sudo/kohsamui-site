May brings Koh Samui's hottest days, with highs of 33 °C and lows of 26 °C, and the first short afternoon showers: about 132 mm of rain over 11 rain days. The 30 °C sea stays calm on most days, Ang Thong Marine Park is open, and rooms are at shoulder prices before the summer holidays. In 2027 the Full Moon Party moves to Friday 21 May because of Visakha Bucha, and the 24th Samui Regatta runs off Chaweng from 22 to 29 May.

Good to know: many websites list the Full Moon Party on Thursday 20 May 2027. That is Visakha Bucha, an alcohol-ban day, and there is no party that night. The party is on Friday 21 May; our shared return speedboat costs 1,400 THB.

The verdict

May is a good month for value and boat trips, as long as you plan around the heat and the afternoon showers.

Good for:

  • Shoulder-season room rates before the July–August peak
  • Calm water for Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao, best in the morning
  • Diving, with visibility of 15–30 m
  • Quieter boat days: Ang Thong is noticeably less busy than in high season
  • The Samui Regatta and the Full Moon Party in the same week

Not for:

  • Long midday walks: May is the hottest month of the year
  • A kitesurfing trip: May is a light-wind transition month
  • Midday swims on the north and west coasts, where low tide falls around noon
  • Waterfalls, which have only a modest flow

If you want the same calm sea with less heat and almost no rain, April is the better month, at higher prices over Songkran. See Koh Samui weather by month.

May has about twice February's rain but less than a third of November's. The rain falls differently too: short showers rather than the long grey spells of the monsoon.

What May weather looks like

May is the start of Samui's hot season with short showers, which lasts until September. From May the wind comes from the south-west, across the mainland, so the island is largely sheltered while Phuket and Krabi get the south-west monsoon. Expect sunny mornings, building heat and humidity, and on about 11 days a short afternoon shower or thunderstorm followed by sunshine.

Thunderstorms are the one thing to watch on the water. A May squall can bring 30-knot gusts for half an hour on an otherwise calm day, which is why captains make the final call on the morning and turn back if a squall builds. Morning boat trips are the safer bet.

At 33 °C, midday is heavy going. Walk or sightsee before 10:00 or after 16:00, and drink plenty of water. Mosquitoes are worse after rain, and dengue is present in Thailand all year and peaks in rainy periods, so use repellent at dusk. The risk of box jellyfish is higher in the rainy months (the UK government says May to October nationally), after heavy rain and in calm, warm water in the evening. Swim inside netted areas where they exist. For a sting, pour vinegar on it for at least 30 seconds and call 1669. See beach safety.

Visakha Bucha on Thursday 20 May marks the Buddha's birth, enlightenment and death. Every temple holds an evening candle procession. It is an alcohol-ban day: 7-Eleven, supermarkets and unlicensed restaurants can't sell alcohol, but since May 2025 licensed hotels and bars in tourist areas may still serve. See temples and drugs and alcohol law.

The Full Moon Party moves to Friday 21 May 2027 because of the ban. The date is provisional until the Haad Rin organisers confirm it, and we re-check it about 30 days ahead. Our shared return Full Moon Party speedboat costs 1,400 THB per person with hotel pickup and drop-off from most areas of Samui; a private speedboat for up to 10 costs 19,500 THB. The Samui Regatta brings a week of keelboat racing off Chaweng Beach, with beach parties and a closing gala. Watching from the beach is free. For a closer look, or a day out on the water that week, see private speedboats.

Where to stay in May

  • Chaweng is the base for Regatta week, with the racing in the bay and the evenings ashore. The east coast is calm and good for swimming.
  • Lamai is the best-value east-coast beach, with mid-range rooms from about 1,500 THB.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay 5–10 minutes from the airport, good for families.
  • Maenam, Bophut and the west coast are tidal: in May low tide falls around midday and the beaches are very shallow, so swim early or late in the day.
  • Bang Rak is fine for a night before an early boat, but the water is murky in April and May.
  • For sunsets, the west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) and Bang Por in the north-west face the sun as it sets, at about 18:33 in mid-May. See sunset spots.
  • Wherever you stay, pick a hotel with a pool or good shade for the hottest hours.

Diving, snorkelling and water sports in May

May is a very good diving month. Visibility around Koh Tao and Sail Rock is typically 15–30 m, the sea is flat in the morning, and whale sharks are possible (Koh Tao dive centres point to March to May) but never guaranteed. Snorkelling at Ang Thong, Koh Tan and Koh Nang Yuan is very good; go early, before any afternoon storm. See diving and snorkelling.

Kitesurfing is poor in May: the wind is light and variable as the seasons change, and lessons move to Nathon on the west coast. E-foil and SUP are the usual alternatives. See kitesurfing and kayak and SUP.

Prices in May

May is shoulder season, with rooms well below the peaks of mid-December to February and July to August, when rates run 30–80% higher. Along with June and September, it is one of the best months for good weather at moderate prices.

Daily costs don't change with the season: street dishes cost 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage about 300–500 THB and a scooter 150–350 THB a day. Ang Thong and Koh Nang Yuan fees are cash only. Full price list: is Koh Samui expensive?

Getting here and around in May

Flights and ferries run normally. Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day and from Phuket about 7–8 times a day. Phuket and Krabi get the south-west monsoon from about May to October, which makes Samui the better choice of the two in summer. See how to get to Koh Samui.

Most scooter crashes on Samui happen on wet roads. If an afternoon shower is pouring, wait it out in a café: it is usually short. Wear a helmet (fines up to 2,000 THB) and carry an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles. Grab prices surge in rain. See getting around Koh Samui.

What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes, and a cover-up for temples.
  • A compact umbrella for afternoon showers.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin, especially for dusk.
  • A rash vest or full-length stinger suit for snorkelling and evening swims.
  • Seasickness tablets if you are prone to it.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches and shallow tidal flats.
