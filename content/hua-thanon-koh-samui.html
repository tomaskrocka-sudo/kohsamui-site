Hua Thanon is Samui's Thai-Muslim fishing village on the south-east coast, 3.6 km and 5–10 minutes south of Lamai. Its covered fish market is listed as open daily 07:00–18:00, but the best of the catch is sold by mid-morning, so go between 07:00 and 09:00. The village is home to the Koh Samui Central Mosque (Masjid Nurul Ihsan, built 1984), and the 16 m bronze Guan Yu statue stands on the hillside just above it. Entry to everything is free; dress modestly.

The village

Hua Thanon is Samui's main Muslim village. Fishing families here supply much of the island with seafood, and the shore in front of the village is lined with colourful wooden longtails and small trawlers. Lanes are narrow, houses are close together, and life runs on the rhythm of the boats and the five daily prayers. It is a residential village, not a tourist attraction, so visit quietly and briefly, the way you would visit any small community at home. The local language is southern Thai, and English is limited: a smile and "sawasdee" go a long way.

The fish market

The market is a covered hall run by and for the local fishing community. The day's catch is laid out on ice and tables early, alongside meat, fruit and vegetables from nearby farms. Floors are wet and the smell is strong, so wear shoes you don't mind getting wet.

Village and mosque etiquette

The Koh Samui Central Mosque, Masjid Nurul Ihsan, was built in 1984 in a Neo-Mughal style. It is a working place of worship. You may be able to look inside outside prayer times if someone invites you in, but always ask first and never enter during prayers.

During Ramadan (expected to run from about 8 February to 9 March 2027, depending on the moon sighting), avoid eating, drinking and smoking in the street in daytime; food stalls open around sunset. Background on customs is in Thai culture and traditions.

The Guan Yu Shrine above Hua Thanon

On the hill just north of the village stands a 16 m bronze statue of Guan Yu, the Chinese general revered as a god of loyalty and protection, with a shrine and a view over the coast. It is open 09:00–17:00 and free; a donation is welcome. The shrine reflects Samui's Hainanese Chinese heritage and holds celebrations at Chinese New Year (Saturday 6 February 2027). Dress respectfully and do not climb on the statue. Details on our Guan Yu Shrine page; other temples are on Koh Samui temples.

The beach and swimming

Hua Thanon's shore is a working fishing beach. Boats are pulled up on the sand, nets dry on the shore and the water is shallow with a muddy, stony bottom at low tide. It is not a swimming beach, and beach towels in front of people's boats are out of place. For a swim, Lamai's central beach is 5–10 minutes north (red flags common October–December), and the south coast's quieter beaches such as Bang Kao work at high tide. On the south-east coast, the north-east monsoon (October–December) brings wind and rain, and November is the wettest month with about 445 mm; the market runs regardless.

Good to know: the coast road through Hua Thanon is narrow and shared with children, dogs and fishing trucks. Ride slowly. The nearest 24-hour hospitals are in the Chaweng area, about 20–25 minutes away; ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155.

Food in Hua Thanon

All food in the village is halal, and it is one of the best places on Samui for southern Thai Muslim cooking. Look for:

  • Khao yam: a southern herbal rice salad with toasted coconut, lemongrass and fish sauce dressing, a breakfast dish.
  • Roti with curry: flaky flatbread with a bowl of chicken or beef curry, sold from morning stalls.
  • Southern curries: gaeng tai pla and fish curries, served with khanom jeen rice noodles.
  • Grilled and fried fish: whatever came in that morning.

Expect stall prices of roughly 40–70 THB a dish. Stalls cluster near the market and the main lane in the morning; opening hours vary by vendor, so go early. The village has no bars. For a sit-down seafood dinner, Lamai and its seafront restaurants are 5–10 minutes away; see Koh Samui restaurants.

Where to stay near Hua Thanon

There are few places to stay in the village itself; most visitors come from Lamai. Along the coast between Lamai and the south, and on the hills of Maret, there are small resorts, guesthouses and pool villas, typically cheaper than Chaweng. If you want this local, quiet side of the island with easy access to restaurants, stay in south Lamai (1,500–3,500 THB a night for mid-range) and walk or ride to Hua Thanon at 07:00. For the wellness resorts further south see Bang Kao and the south coast; for all areas, where to stay on Koh Samui.

Nearby sights

  • Guan Yu Shrine, 0.5 km: the 16 m bronze statue, 09:00–17:00, free.
  • Red Temple (Wat Ratchathammaram), 1.5 km: carved terracotta-red temple, 07:00–17:00, free.
  • Hin Ta and Hin Yai, 3 km: the famous rocks at the south end of Lamai.
  • Lamai, 3.6 km: beach, night market and bars.
  • Wat Khunaram, 3 km inland: the mummified monk Luang Pho Daeng.
  • Laem Sor Pagoda, on the south coast towards Bang Kao.

A good morning loop: Hua Thanon market at 07:30, breakfast roti, the Guan Yu Shrine at 09:00, the Red Temple, then Hin Ta and Hin Yai before the tour buses arrive.
