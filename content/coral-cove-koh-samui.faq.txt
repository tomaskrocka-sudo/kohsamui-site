Is Coral Cove good for snorkelling? | It is the easiest shore snorkel on Samui: you swim straight from the beach to the boulders on either side and see small reef fish, but the coral is patchy and was reported dead in places in January 2026, so boat trips to Koh Tan are better.
How much is a kayak at Coral Cove? | About 300 THB per hour, rented on the beach; paddle only on calm days and stay close to the rocks.
Do I need water shoes at Coral Cove? | Yes, the sand is coarse and crystalline and the seabed has rocks, coral rubble and occasional sea urchins, so water shoes make it far more comfortable.
Is there parking at Coral Cove? | There is no car park, only roadside pull-ins on the ring road; park fully off the road because this curving stretch has fast traffic, and come by scooter if you can.
Is the coral still alive at Coral Cove? | Partly at best: a review dated 6 January 2026 described dead coral and litter, while the same visitor's friends had seen live coral the previous May, so expect fish and rocks rather than a healthy reef.
Is Coral Cove good for kids? | For older children who can snorkel, yes on calm days; for toddlers the coarse sand and rocky entry are less suitable than Choeng Mon or Lipa Noi.
What are Coral Cove's opening hours? | The beach is public, and the rentals and beach bar are listed as open 08:00–20:30; arrive before 10:00 for the calmest water.
How far is Coral Cove from Chaweng and Lamai? | It sits roughly halfway, about 6.5 km by road from each, 10–15 minutes by scooter or taxi.
Can I snorkel at Coral Cove in November? | Usually not: November is the roughest and wettest month on the east coast, waves break on the rocks and visibility is poor, so snorkel from February to September instead.
Is Coral Cove the same as Chaweng Noi? | No, Chaweng Noi is the next cove north, about 2.5 km away; Coral Cove is smaller and rockier, between the Lad Koh viewpoint and Silver Beach.
