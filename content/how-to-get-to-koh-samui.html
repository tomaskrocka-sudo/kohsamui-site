The fastest way to Koh Samui is a direct flight to Samui airport (USM): about 1h05–1h15 from Bangkok Suvarnabhumi, with roughly 20–25 flights a day, usually 2,800–6,800 THB one way with a checked bag. The cheapest is a low-cost flight or the night train to Surat Thani, then a coach-and-ferry combo ticket (330–850 THB) to Nathon or Lipa Noi, which takes 5–7 hours from Bangkok airport to island. From Koh Phangan the crossing takes 20–50 minutes (200–500 THB), and from Koh Tao 1.5–2 hours by fast boat (about 700 THB).

Koh Samui has no bridge. Every visitor arrives either by air at the island's own airport in the north-east or by boat at one of a dozen piers. This page compares every route with 2026 prices and times, explains which pier is which, and tells you what can go wrong in the monsoon. Detailed route guides sit underneath it: Bangkok to Koh Samui, Surat Thani to Koh Samui, Phuket and Krabi to Koh Samui, Koh Samui to Koh Phangan, Koh Samui to Koh Tao and the Samui airport guide.

Good to know: timetables and fares on this page were last checked on 1 November 2026. Ferry operators change times by season and fuel surcharges moved Donsak fares twice in March 2026, so re-check on the day at the pier or ticket office. We name operators so you can find them, but we do not link to them or sell their tickets.

Fly direct to Samui (USM)

Samui International Airport is privately owned and run by Bangkok Airways, which has operated it since it opened in 1989. That ownership explains the higher fares: competition is limited, and the only foreign carrier with scheduled flights in late 2026 is Scoot on the Singapore route. Every other route is flown by Bangkok Airways. The airport sits about 2 km north of Chaweng, so most hotels are 10–50 minutes away by road. Full details, from taxi zones to lounges, are on our Koh Samui airport page.

Fares and when to book

  • Bangkok–Samui one way usually costs 2,800–6,800 THB, with a checked bag included on Bangkok Airways economy fares.
  • The most expensive weeks are 20 December–10 January, Chinese New Year, Easter, Songkran (13–15 April) and July–August. Book 6–10 weeks ahead for these.
  • Outside the peaks, fares often drop below 3,500 THB if you book 3–4 weeks ahead and fly mid-week.
  • Connecting from a long-haul flight at Suvarnabhumi: allow at least 2 hours on a separate ticket, more if you must collect bags and re-check them.

Fly to Surat Thani or Nakhon Si Thammarat, then ferry

This is the budget favourite. Thai AirAsia, Nok Air and Thai Lion Air fly from Don Mueang to Surat Thani (URT) about 12 times a day, roughly 06:50–19:15, usually for 1,000–2,000 THB. A few flights also leave from Suvarnabhumi. In the URT arrivals hall, desks sell combined coach-and-ferry tickets that take you to Donsak pier and across to Samui. The whole trip from URT to the island takes about 3–4 hours. Read the full breakdown on our Surat Thani to Koh Samui page.

Nakhon Si Thammarat (NST) also has low-cost flights from Don Mueang. The ground leg from NST to Samui is about 3.5 hours via Donsak, and combo tickets are fewer, so it only makes sense when URT flights are full or much dearer.

Bangkok overland: train, bus and boat

Ten trains a day run from Krung Thep Aphiwat Central Terminal (formerly Bang Sue Grand) to Surat Thani station, which is actually in Phun Phin, 14–16 km west of Surat Thani town and about 80 km from Donsak pier. The most useful overnight train for Samui is Express 85, because it arrives at a sensible hour for the morning ferries.

  1. 19:50 Express 85 leaves Krung Thep Aphiwat. 2nd-class air-con berth about 755 THB (upper) to 825 THB (lower); 1st-class cabin berth about 1,265–1,465 THB.
  2. 06:23 Arrive Surat Thani (Phun Phin). Combo desks on the station forecourt sell coach + ferry tickets (Phantip/Seatran about 420 THB; Raja coaches from 05:30).
  3. 07:30–09:00 Coach to Donsak pier (about 1.5 h).
  4. 09:00–10:30 Car ferry to Nathon or Lipa Noi (about 1.5 h).
  5. 10:30–11:30 On Samui. Add 40–60 minutes by songthaew or taxi to Chaweng or Lamai.

Bus-and-boat joint tickets avoid any changes you need to arrange yourself:

  • Lomprayah from Khao San Road: buses at 06:00, 21:30 and 22:00, 1,250–1,750 THB, 11.5–13.75 h. These go via Chumphon, not Surat Thani, and land at Pralarn pier in Maenam or at Bangrak.
  • Boonsiri from Khao San Road: 22:00 bus, arrives Bangrak about 11:30 the next morning, about 1,450 THB.
  • Government coach (Transport Co.) from the Southern Bus Terminal or Mo Chit with the Seatran ferry: about 620 THB (express) to 960 THB (24-seat VIP) at the counter, 12–16 h, landing at Nathon.

Car and passenger ferries from Donsak

Two companies run big roll-on, roll-off ferries from Donsak (about 70 km east of Surat Thani town) to Samui. They carry foot passengers, motorbikes, cars, trucks and buses, and they are the backbone of every combo ticket. Both crossings take about 1.5 hours. The two Donsak terminals are about 1 km apart, so check which one your ticket uses.

From Koh Tao

Fast boats from Mae Haad on Koh Tao take 1.5–2 hours to Samui, most calling at Thong Sala on Phangan on the way, and cost about 600–700 THB. Slow ferries (Songserm high-speed ferry, Lomprayah's express) take 2.5–4.5 hours for roughly 300–550 THB. The first boats leave Koh Tao at about 07:00 and the last scheduled departures are around 15:30–16:30. Boats land at Bangrak, Pralarn (Maenam) or Nathon depending on the operator. Detailed times are on our Koh Samui to Koh Tao page.

From Phuket, Krabi and Khao Lak

There is no ferry between Phuket and Samui: they are on opposite coasts. Bangkok Airways flies Phuket–Samui about 6–8 times a day in about an hour, with fares from roughly 3,700 THB in low season and 5,000 THB or more in December. Krabi–Samui flights run 1–2 times a day in about 45–50 minutes.

Overland, van-and-ferry or bus-and-ferry tickets go via Donsak. From Phuket they take about 6.5–10 hours and cost roughly 450–1,500 THB depending on operator; Lomprayah's coach + catamaran (about 6.5–7 h) is the quickest. From Krabi, van + ferry tickets take about 5–6.5 hours. Khao Lak has no airport, so fly from Phuket or take a van across Route 401 via Khao Sok. Full options are on our Phuket and Krabi to Koh Samui page.

Weather, delays and cancellations

Samui's rough season is the north-east monsoon, roughly October to December, with the heaviest rain in November (about 445 mm on average). North-east swell makes the crossings to Phangan and Tao bumpy, and speedboat services are the first to be cancelled in strong wind. The big car ferries from Donsak rarely stop, but they do halt in tropical storms: when Storm Pabuk hit in January 2019 ferries were suspended and many flights cancelled. February to April has the calmest sea of the year.

  • Leave a buffer day between your last boat day and an international flight home, especially from October to January.
  • Check the live sea state before booking a speedboat crossing: [kp_sea_now] More detail is on our sea conditions guide and weather by month page.
  • Wind forecasts on windy.com and warnings from the Thai Meteorological Department at tmd.go.th are the most useful free tools.
  • If an operator cancels, ask for a refund or transfer to the next sailing at the counter, and keep your ticket stub.

Check timetables before you travel

Every operator changes times at least twice a year, usually around the October/November and April/May schedule changes, and adds extra boats for Full Moon Parties and the 20 December–5 January peak. Treat every time on this page as "last checked 1 November 2026" and confirm it at the pier, the operator's town office or your hotel desk the day before.

  • Buy at the pier for most ferries outside peak weeks. Prices are the same, and you avoid paying a booking site fee.
  • Book ahead for flights in high season, overnight train berths (sales open 90 days ahead for most trains) and any boat on a Full Moon date.
  • Keep your ID handy: some operators ask for a name and passport number on the ticket.
  • Arrive 30–45 minutes early for catamarans; check-in closes before departure and luggage is loaded first.
  • Check holidays: trains, buses and ferries fill up around Thai public holidays such as Songkran (13–15 April), the long weekends in May and July, and 31 December–2 January. Ferry offices also get crowded on the mornings after the Full Moon Parties of 24 November, 24 December and 31 December 2026, when thousands of people return from Phangan at once.

Sample door-to-door days from Bangkok

Real journeys rarely match the headline flight time. These timelines start in central Bangkok and finish at a hotel in Chaweng.

Direct flight

  1. 08:00 Leave central Bangkok by taxi or Airport Rail Link (45–60 min).
  2. 09:00 Check in at Suvarnabhumi; Bangkok Airways passengers can use its lounge before boarding.
  3. 10:30 Flight departs; about 1h05–1h15 in the air.
  4. 11:45 Land at USM, collect bags (domestic arrivals are quick).
  5. 12:15 Kiosk taxi to Chaweng (10–15 min). At the hotel by about 12:30.

Low-cost flight to Surat Thani

  1. 05:30 Leave central Bangkok for Don Mueang.
  2. 07:00 Low-cost flight to URT (about 1h10–1h20).
  3. 08:30 Land at URT, buy a combo ticket in arrivals.
  4. 09:30 Coach leaves for Donsak (about 1.5 h).
  5. 11:00 Board the ferry or catamaran to Nathon.
  6. 12:30–13:00 Land at Nathon; songthaew or taxi to Chaweng (40–60 min). At the hotel by about 14:00.

Night train

  1. 19:00 Arrive at Krung Thep Aphiwat; buy food before boarding, as the dining car is limited.
  2. 19:50 Express 85 departs; berths are made up after departure.
  3. 06:23 Arrive Surat Thani (Phun Phin) if on time; delays of 30–60 minutes are common.
  4. 07:30 Combo coach to Donsak, then ferry.
  5. about 10:30–11:30 Land on Samui; hotel in Chaweng by about midday.

Entry formalities if you are flying in from abroad

If Samui is your first stop in Thailand (for example on a Singapore or Hong Kong flight), you clear immigration at Samui airport. Every foreign arrival must complete the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card online within 3 days before arrival at the official site, tdac.immigration.go.th. Ignore look-alike sites that charge a fee. Since 15 September 2026, visa-exempt visitors from countries including the UK, US, EU states and Australia get 30 days, not 60. Our Thailand visa guide explains the current rules and extensions. Domestic arrivals from Bangkok or Phuket go straight to baggage reclaim with no checks.

Common mistakes on the way to Samui

  • Booking the wrong "Bangrak pier". The Lomprayah pier, the Seatran pier and Petcherat Marina are all sold as Bangrak and are a few minutes apart. Check the operator name and put the exact pier into Google Maps before you leave.
  • Assuming Surat Thani station is in Surat Thani. The station is in Phun Phin, 14–16 km west of town. Taxis from Phun Phin straight to Donsak cost far more than a combo ticket bought on the forecourt.
  • Landing at Surat Thani after 16:00. The combo desks close, and a taxi to the Donsak car ferry is the only way across that evening.
  • Buying "VIP" bus tickets from street agents in Bangkok. Some Khao San agency buses are minibuses with transfers in the middle of the night, and thefts from hold luggage on tourist buses are a long-running problem. Buy from the operator's own desk or the bus terminal and keep valuables with you.
  • Trusting old blog fares. Ferry prices rose in March 2026 with fuel costs, and many websites still show 2023–2024 figures.
  • Planning a boat day the day before a flight home. A cancelled return boat from Phangan or Tao in the monsoon can mean a missed flight. Do island trips earlier in your stay.
  • Getting into an unlicensed taxi at the pier. Agree the fare before loading bags, and use the airport kiosk rather than touts in the car park. Our scams guide lists the tricks to watch for.

Luggage, surfboards, bikes and pets

  • Flights: Bangkok Airways economy fares include a checked bag. Low-cost airlines to Surat Thani charge for bags, and the cheapest fares often include only 7 kg of cabin baggage, which can wipe out the saving for a family.
  • Combo coaches and catamarans: large bags travel in the hold or a luggage rack. Keep passports, money and medication in a small bag on your lap, never in the hold, especially on overnight buses.
  • Car ferries: the easiest way to carry bicycles, diving gear or a lot of luggage. Bikes and motorbikes are charged as vehicles.
  • Pets: airlines have strict rules on animals in the cabin; most people bring pets by car on the Donsak ferries.
