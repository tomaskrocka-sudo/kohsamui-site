Na Muang 2 is Koh Samui's tallest waterfall, a chain of at least five tiers about 80 m high in total, 1 km above Na Muang 1 in the south-central hills. The climb is free on the signed left-hand path, takes 20–40 minutes each way over roots and boulders with fixed ropes, and the site is open 09:00–18:00. It runs best December–January, is a trickle February–April, and has a serious safety record: at least four visitors have died in falls here since 2019, so stay off the edges.

Which path to take

Two routes start from the car parks at the top of the hill road, and visitors often take the wrong one.

  • Left-hand path (recommended): signed as a free jungle walk. It goes downhill about 100 m past a small shop to a wooden bridge over the stream, then climbs beside the falls. This route reaches the pools and the main views.
  • Right-hand route: leads through a private café and tropical garden with coloured water slides. Reviewers rate its views lower, swimming at the top is not allowed there and it may involve a charge.

Rule of thumb: if you have not crossed a wooden bridge within the first few minutes, you are on the wrong path.

The climb step by step

  1. 0 min Park at the top car parks beyond Na Muang 1. Use the toilets and buy water at the small café; there is nothing on the trail.
  2. 2–5 min Take the left fork downhill past the small shop and cross the wooden bridge.
  3. 5–15 min The trail climbs steeply through forest on roots and rock steps. Pace yourself: the humidity makes it harder than it looks.
  4. 15–30 min Rope sections begin on the steepest boulders. Test each rope, keep three points of contact and go one at a time.
  5. 20–40 min Reach the upper tiers and pools. Sit well back from the lip of each drop. Swim only in the shallow pools away from the edge.
  6. Descent Allow as long as the way up. Face the rock on the rope sections and step down slowly; most slips happen going down.

Fit walkers in dry conditions do it in about 20 minutes each way; families with teenagers or anyone unused to scrambling should allow 40 minutes or more.

What you see at the top

The stream drops over a staircase of granite slabs and ledges, with small, cold plunge pools between them and views out over the forest towards the south coast. In December and January the full width of the rock is covered in water; in March and April you may find a thin ribbon and dry, dark rock. There are no railings, no lifeguards and no shop at the top.

The safety record

Na Muang 2 is the most dangerous tourist site on Samui for falls. The pattern is consistent: wet rock on the upper tiers, someone moving to the edge for a photo, and a fall to the tier below.

DateWhat happened
July 2019A Spanish visitor died after falling on the trail.
October 2019A French visitor died after falling while taking photos at the same spot.
June 2024An Italian visitor was injured in a slip on the rocks.
10 November 2024A 29-year-old Indian visitor died after climbing a cliff for a high view, ignoring a warning sign; heavy rain delayed the search.
May 2025A British visitor was seriously injured; his hospital bill exceeded 4.4 million THB.
5 January 2026A 22-year-old French visitor slipped from the fifth tier while taking photos and died; recovery took about two hours with ropes.

Warning signs have been installed throughout the area. Follow them.

Safety rules for the climb

  • Never step backwards for a photo, and never stand on the lip of a drop. Have someone else take the picture from a safe spot.
  • Stay on the marked trail and the main pools. Don't climb the cliffs beside the falls.
  • Don't climb after heavy rain or when the water is brown. Flash floods can arrive within minutes.
  • No jumping: the pools are shallow and rocky.
  • Wear closed shoes with grip. Flip-flops and smooth-soled trainers are the main cause of slips.
  • Go with someone, and tell your hotel where you are going; mobile signal is patchy in the valley.
  • Check your travel insurance covers hiking: one injured climber's hospital bill on Samui exceeded 4.4 million THB.
  • Emergency: ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155. See hospitals on Koh Samui.

Who should skip it

Na Muang 2 is short, but it is not a casual walk. Be honest with yourself before you start:

  • Young children, or anyone who would need to be carried on the rope sections
  • Anyone with knee, ankle or back problems: the descent is harder than the climb
  • Visitors in flip-flops, slip-on sandals or smooth-soled fashion trainers
  • Anyone who has been drinking, or who is very hungover in the midday heat
  • Everyone, on the morning after heavy rain or when the water is brown

If that's you, swim at Na Muang 1 at the bottom of the hill instead: it has the better pool anyway. For a gentler forest walk, Hin Lad near Nathon is longer but less exposed.

A sample morning

  1. 08:45 Arrive at Na Muang 1 and swim while the pool is quiet.
  2. 09:30 Drive up the hill road to the top car parks; buy water.
  3. 09:40 Start the climb on the left-hand path, across the wooden bridge.
  4. 10:15 Rest at the upper pools, well back from the edges.
  5. 10:45 Descend slowly; the trail is getting busy by now.
  6. 11:30 Early lunch on Route 4169, then Wat Khunaram or the Secret Buddha Garden by 4x4.

Is it worth it?

In December and January, with water across the rock and a dry-ish trail, yes: it is the biggest waterfall on the island and the climb feels like a small adventure. From February to April you climb 30 minutes in the heat for a thin ribbon of water, and we'd only recommend it as exercise. In heavy rain it is not worth the risk at all. Whatever the month, the reward is the forest and the view, not a swim: the pool at Na Muang 1 is far better for that.

Getting there

Follow the signs from Route 4169 to Na Muang, then continue past the Na Muang 1 car park up the steep hill road to the top car parks. Don't stop at the first lot beside the elephant camp, even if an attendant waves you in. A 125 cc scooter can carry two people up the hill, but it is steep: ride in low gear, brake before bends and take it slowly on the way down.
