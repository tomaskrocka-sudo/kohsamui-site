For a first visit, stay in Chaweng: it has the most restaurants and is 10–15 minutes from the airport; for quiet with Chaweng nearby, choose Chaweng Noi or Choeng Mon. Couples and food lovers do best in Bophut and Fisherman's Village, though the beach there isn't for swimming. Lamai is the best value (about 1,500–3,500 THB a night mid-range), Maenam suits budget and long stays, and the west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) has the sunsets and villas but sits 40–50 minutes from the airport.

Map of Koh Samui's areas

The east coast (Chaweng, Lamai) faces the sunrise and has the best sand; the north coast (Maenam, Bophut, Bang Rak) faces Koh Phangan and has the ferry piers; the west and south are quieter, with sunsets and fishing villages. The ring road (Route 4169) runs about 51 km around the island and takes about 90 minutes non-stop, so nowhere is truly far, but evening taxis between areas add up.

Area by area

Chaweng

Samui's main resort strip, with about 7 km of fine white sand on the east coast. It splits into three parts. North Chaweng is the quietest, with a gently sloping beach that suits families who still want restaurants nearby. Central Chaweng is the busiest: beach clubs such as ARKbar, fire shows, Soi Green Mango and the walking street by Chaweng Lake (Thursday to Saturday). South Chaweng is a mix of mid-range resorts and rocky sections. Mid-range rooms cost about 2,000–4,500 THB, and budget guesthouses back from the beach 500–950 THB. The downsides are traffic on Beach Road in the evening and noise near the bars. Central Samui mall and three of the island's private hospitals are in or next to Chaweng. More on Chaweng.

Chaweng Noi

A small, quiet cove just south of a rocky point from Chaweng, lined mostly with resorts. The water is good for swimming and you can snorkel off the sand around the rocks. It suits couples who want calm days with Chaweng's restaurants 5 minutes away by taxi. Sea lice are occasionally reported. See Chaweng Noi.

Silver Beach, Coral Cove and Tongson Bay

Three small coves with only a handful of hotels each, for travellers who want a quiet base with good water. Silver Beach (Crystal Bay), between Chaweng Noi and Lamai, is a boulder-framed cove with pale sand and clear water for swimming and snorkelling; it fills up by about 10:00 with day visitors. Coral Cove, just north, has coarse crystalline sand (wear water shoes) and kayak hire at about 300 THB an hour. Tongson Bay, between Choeng Mon and the Bang Rak headland, is small and sheltered, clear and shallow from December to April and rougher from May to October; the last stretch is a sand road. All three need a car or taxi for dinners out.

Lamai

Samui's second beach, about 4 km long, with the same fine white sand as Chaweng and far fewer people. The centre is the best part for swimming, but the seabed drops steeply; the north end is shallow and stony, so bring reef shoes. Like Chaweng, it faces east and gets red flags in the October–December monsoon. Mid-range rooms run about 1,500–3,500 THB, which makes Lamai the best value on the east coast. There is a daily night market by the river bridge, Hin Ta and Hin Yai at the south end, and a slower, slightly older crowd at night. See Lamai and Chaweng vs Lamai.

Bophut and Fisherman's Village

The north-coast village with the island's best dining street: old wooden shophouses converted into restaurants, boutique hotels and bars, with views across to Koh Phangan. Mid-range rooms cost about 2,500–5,000 THB. The Friday walking street (about 17:00–23:00) is the island's biggest weekly market, with smaller markets on Monday and Wednesday. The catch is the beach: the sand is narrow and brownish and the water often cloudy, so it is not a swimming beach. Stay here for food and atmosphere, and take a 15-minute ride to Chaweng or Choeng Mon to swim. On Friday the road closes from about 16:30 and illegally parked scooters are towed (500 THB or more to release). See Fisherman's Village.

Maenam

A long, quiet north-coast beach backed by palms, with the cheapest beachfront rooms on the island (about 1,000–2,500 THB mid-range) and many monthly rentals. The water is calm in the dry season, but the beach narrows at high tide and gets chest-deep about 5 m out. Maenam has Pralarn pier for Lomprayah boats to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, a small Chinatown and the Samui immigration office. Nights are very quiet. See Maenam.

Choeng Mon

A sheltered bay in the north-east, 5–10 minutes from the airport, with soft white sand and some of the calmest water on Samui. The centre of the bay is best for swimming, and at low tide you can wade across a sandbar to the islet of Koh Fan Noi. Mid-range rooms are about 3,000–7,000 THB; this is resort and pool-villa territory. It is our first choice for families with small children and for honeymooners who want quiet. Downsides: it gets busy at midday, and there is little nightlife. See Choeng Mon.

Bang Rak and Plai Laem

The north-east corner around Big Buddha, right next to the airport and the piers for Koh Phangan and Koh Tao (the Lomprayah, Seatran and Petcherat Marina piers, all called "Bangrak pier", and Big Buddha pier for Haad Rin). Bang Rak beach is not good for swimming: muddy shallows, murky in April and May, and glass has been reported in the sand. Aircraft noise is noticeable. It works for a night before an early boat or flight, and the seafood restaurants on the sand are good. See Big Buddha Beach and Plai Laem.

West coast: Lipa Noi, Bang Por, Laem Yai and Taling Ngam

The quiet side of the island, with the best sunsets. Lipa Noi is very shallow and calm, with no coral, which makes it the safest beach for toddlers at high tide. Bang Por, in the north-west, has local seafood restaurants; swim only at high tide because a stony flat stretches 50 m or more at low tide. Laem Yai, near the Four Seasons, is rocky with clear water for snorkelling. Taling Ngam is a long village beach with fishing boats and cliff-top luxury resorts, looking out to the Five Islands at sunset. Mid-range to villa prices run about 1,500–6,000 THB. The trade-off is distance: 40–50 minutes to the airport, few restaurants within walking distance, and a car or driver needed for most outings.

The south: Bang Kao, Laem Set, Thong Krut and Hua Thanon

Local Samui: fishing villages, quiet beaches and wellness resorts such as Kamalaya near Laem Set. Thong Krut is the pier village for Pig Island (Koh Madsum) and Koh Tan boats. Hua Thanon is a Muslim fishing village with a morning fish market. Bang Kao is very shallow with coral pieces; swim at high tide in water shoes. This area suits wellness stays and long-term visitors who want quiet, but it is 35–45 minutes from the airport and far from nightlife. See Bang Kao, Thong Krut and Hua Thanon.

Nathon

The old capital and main ferry port on the west coast, with government offices, banks, a daily night food market and a free sunset view from the pier. It is not a beach base. Stay here only if you arrive late on the ferry. See Nathon.

Where to stay by traveller type

First-timers

Chaweng, ideally North Chaweng for a calmer beach with the centre a short walk away. If you want quiet, Chaweng Noi or Choeng Mon put you 10 minutes from the restaurants without the noise.

Couples and honeymooners

Bophut for dinners and atmosphere; Choeng Mon or Chaweng Noi for a resort with a swimming beach; a west-coast pool villa in Lipa Noi or Taling Ngam for privacy and sunsets. See Koh Samui honeymoon.

Families with small children

Choeng Mon (calmest bay, short airport transfer) or Lipa Noi (very shallow, calm much of the year). North Chaweng works if you want restaurants nearby. Avoid Bophut and Bang Rak for swimming. Our where to stay with kids guide goes into detail, and Koh Samui with kids covers activities.

Nightlife

Central Chaweng, within walking distance of ARKbar, Soi Green Mango and the bars on Beach Road. Lamai has a quieter bar scene. For the Full Moon Party, see the section below.

Budget travellers

Maenam (beachfront from about 1,000 THB) or Lamai. In Chaweng, guesthouses back from the beach start around 500–950 THB. Songthaews run along the ring road by day for 30–80 THB.

Wellness

The south-east around Laem Set and Lamai's hills (retreats and jungle spas) or Choeng Mon. See Koh Samui retreats.

Long stays and digital nomads

Maenam, Bophut and Lamai have the most monthly rentals, cafés and coworking spaces. Scooters cost about 2,000–4,500 THB a month. Remember that the visa-free stay is now 30 days (extendable once by 30 days); see the Thailand visa guide.

Swimming, tides and beaches by area

The east coast (Chaweng, Chaweng Noi, Lamai, Silver Beach) has deeper water and the best swimming from January to September. The north and west coasts are shallow and tidal: at low tide the water can be ankle-deep for 50 m or more and the seabed stony, so plan swims around high tide. Check the live tide before you go:

[kp_tide_now]

  • Best for swimming: North and Central Chaweng, central Lamai, Choeng Mon, Chaweng Noi, Silver Beach.
  • High tide only: Maenam, Bang Por, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao.
  • Not for swimming: Bophut, Bang Rak, Nathon.
  • Snorkelling from the shore: Chaweng Noi rocks, Silver Beach, Coral Cove (wear water shoes), Laem Yai.

Box jellyfish occur in Gulf waters: swim where hotels display vinegar stations and treat any sting with vinegar for at least 30 seconds. Full beach-by-beach notes are in Koh Samui beaches, tide tables on Koh Samui tides and safety on beach safety.

Hotels, resorts or villas?

Hotels and guesthouses cluster in Chaweng, Lamai and Maenam and are the easiest choice for short stays: walkable restaurants, taxis outside and simple check-in.

Resorts line Choeng Mon, Chaweng Noi, Bophut and the west coast. Luxury rooms start around 10,000 THB a night. Names you will see include the Four Seasons (Laem Yai), SALA Samui Choengmon, W Koh Samui (Maenam), Banyan Tree and Silavadee (Lamai), Anantara Bophut, Santiburi and Napasai (Maenam), The Tongsai Bay (Choeng Mon), the Conrad (Taling Ngam) and Six Senses Samui. Season 3 of the TV series The White Lotus, which aired from February to April 2025, was filmed largely at the Four Seasons Koh Samui and pushed up demand and prices for the island's top resorts. See our White Lotus Koh Samui guide.

Pool villas are good value for families and groups, especially in the hills above Chaweng, Bophut, Choeng Mon and on the west coast. Check three things before you book: the access road (many are steep concrete drives that are hard on a scooter and alarming in rain), the distance to the nearest shop and beach, and how electricity is charged. See Koh Samui villas for holiday and long-term options.

Prices and booking tips for 2026–2027

  • Peak seasons: rates rise 30–80% from mid-December to February and in July and August. Christmas, New Year and Songkran (13–15 April 2027) sell out first.
  • Shoulder seasons: March–April, May–June and September offer good weather at lower prices.
  • Low season: October and November have the lowest rates and the most rain.
  • "++" pricing: resort restaurant and spa prices marked "++" add 10% service charge and 7% VAT.
  • Electricity: villas and monthly rentals often bill electricity per unit on top of the rent. Public tariffs are about 4–7 THB per unit, but some landlords charge more, so ask before you book.
  • Road access: ask for a photo of the access road if a villa is on a hillside, and whether a car can reach the door.
  • Location over luxury: a simpler hotel near the restaurants often beats a grand resort that needs a taxi for every meal.

Arriving late or leaving early

Flights land at Samui into the evening, and Raja car ferries from Donsak run until about 19:00, landing at Lipa Noi around 90 minutes later. For a late arrival, book your first night close to where you land: Chaweng, Bophut, Choeng Mon or Bang Rak are 5–15 minutes from the airport, whereas the west coast is 40–50 minutes away on dark, winding roads. Taxis at the airport counter charge roughly 400–700 THB per car depending on the zone.

If you arrive by ferry at Nathon or Lipa Noi late in the day, a night in Nathon saves a long drive in the dark. For an early boat to Koh Phangan or Koh Tao, Bang Rak and Maenam put you minutes from the piers. For an early flight, anywhere in the north-east works.

Questions to ask before you book

  • Is the beach in front of the hotel good for swimming at low tide, or only at high tide?
  • How far is the nearest restaurant on foot, and is the walk lit at night?
  • Is the access road steep or unpaved, and can a taxi reach the door?
  • For villas: is electricity included, and if not, what is the rate per unit?
  • Does the price include breakfast, service charge and 7% VAT, or is it "++"?
  • Is the hotel inside the free pickup zone for boat tours (Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao and Thong Krut add 400 THB per person, return)?
  • Is there a pool, for red-flag days on the east coast from October to December?

Where to stay for boat trips

Hotel pickup is included from most areas of Koh Samui. Nathon, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam, Bang Kao and Thong Krut (and other remote areas) add 400 THB per person, return. Surcharge zones can differ by departure pier, and each tour page shows its own pickup rules at checkout.

  • Koh Phangan and Koh Tao ferries leave from the north coast (Bangrak piers, Big Buddha pier, Pralarn pier in Maenam) and from Nathon, so Bophut, Bang Rak and Maenam are the most convenient bases for island hopping.

Where to stay for the Full Moon Party from Samui

The Full Moon Party is at Haad Rin on Koh Phangan; the next dates are 24 November, 24 December and 31 December 2026. If you plan to take a public boat, stay on the north-east coast near Big Buddha and the Bangrak piers: the Haad Rin Queen leaves from Big Buddha pier and is the only direct public service to Haad Rin. Return boats on party night are crowded and late-night transport back to a west-coast hotel is expensive.

The simpler option is our Full Moon Party speedboat: 1,400 THB per person return, with hotel pickup and drop-off on Samui, so you can stay anywhere on the island. We sell the boat only; party entry (about 200 THB) is paid at the gate. For groups, the private Full Moon speedboat leaves when you want. See Full Moon Party from Koh Samui.

Honest downsides by area

  • Chaweng: evening traffic on Beach Road, bar noise in the centre, touts and jet-ski operators on the beach. See scams to avoid.
  • Lamai: the seabed drops steeply in the centre and the north end is stony; 25–30 minutes from the airport.
  • Bophut: a lovely street with a poor swimming beach; Friday towing of scooters.
  • Maenam and Choeng Mon: very quiet after dark; you will take a taxi for nightlife.
  • Bang Rak: aircraft noise and murky water.
  • West coast and south: 35–50 minutes to the airport, few restaurants within walking distance, and the +400 THB tour pickup surcharge from remote areas.
  • Hillside villas: steep roads, power cuts in storms and electricity billed per unit.
