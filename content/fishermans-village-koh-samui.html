Fisherman's Village is Bophut's old shophouse strip on Koh Samui's north coast, 10–15 minutes from the airport. It is now the island's best dining street and hosts the Friday Walking Street, from about 17:00 to 23:00, with the beach road closed to traffic from about 16:30; smaller markets run on Monday and Wednesday. The beach in front is narrow and not recommended for swimming, but dinner on the sand with Koh Phangan on the horizon is one of Samui's best evenings.

The village

Bophut was once one of Samui's trading and fishing ports, and Fisherman's Village is what remains of that era: a narrow beach road lined with old two-storey Chinese-Thai wooden shophouses, many with balconies over the street. Over the years most have become restaurants, boutiques, bars, small hotels and galleries, but the street keeps its scale and its timber fronts, which is what makes it the most attractive place on the island for an evening walk.

By day it is quiet: cafés, shops and a few people on the beach. The street comes alive from about 17:00, when the restaurants put tables out and the light turns gold over Koh Phangan. At Wat Bo Phuttharam, the village temple, 10 THB buys a bucket of food for the animals in its moat.

Friday Walking Street and the other market nights

The Friday Walking Street is the island's best-known weekly market. Stalls fill the beach road from The Wharf at the west end to Coco Tam's, with street food, clothes, crafts, cocktail stalls and buskers, and a beer garden at a small roundabout halfway down. The road closes to traffic from about 16:30, and scooters aren't allowed on the walking street from roughly 18:00 to 22:00. It runs all year, weather permitting; in November and December rain sometimes cuts it short.

Friday tips

  • Arrive by 17:30–18:00. After about 18:00 parking runs out.
  • Peak crowds are 19:00–20:30; the last hour is calmer.
  • Stalls take baht only. Foreign-card ATMs charge about 250 THB per withdrawal, so bring cash.
  • Book a restaurant table if you want to sit down to dinner on a Friday in high season.
  • Pickpocketing is rare, but crowds are crowds: front pockets and zipped bags.

Bophut beach: why not to swim here

The beach in front of the village is part of Bophut Bay, about 3 km of narrow, browner sand with a few pebbles. The water is calm and looks inviting, and some guides call it fine for a dip, but we tell guests not to swim here. The water is often cloudy, an outflow is reported near the eastern entrance of the village, and jellyfish are reported. It is also shallow and tidal. Swim at Choeng Mon (15 minutes east) or Chaweng, and come to Bophut for the evening.

Bophut is also one of the 24 Thai beaches where smoking has been banned since 2018, with fines of up to 100,000 THB. More on the beach itself on Bophut Beach.

Bars and fire shows

Coco Tam's is the village's best-known beach bar: beanbags on the sand, cocktails and fire shows at about 19:15–19:30 and 21:00, open from about noon to midnight, with a DJ on Fridays and Saturdays. Some front-row seats have a minimum spend; ask before you sit. Several other beach bars along the sand do happy hours at sunset. For louder nights, Chaweng's clubs are 15–20 minutes away; see Koh Samui nightlife.

Parking, towing and getting there

Parking is the one thing that goes wrong for visitors here. The beach road is narrow, and badly parked scooters are towed, especially on Fridays. Getting a towed scooter back costs 500 THB or more and an evening's hassle.

  • The Wharf (west end): about 20 THB for a scooter, 40 THB+ for a car. On Fridays it is full after about 18:00.
  • Paid lots on the lanes between the ring road and the village: follow the signs and pay the attendant.
  • Grab or taxi: the easiest choice on a Friday; ask to be dropped at the Fisherman's Village sign or The Wharf. Grab from Chaweng costs about 150–250 THB.

Fisherman's Village by season

The village is busiest from December to February, peaking in January, and again in July and August. From June to September the Friday market is noticeably less crowded and evenings are usually dry. October to December is the rainy season: November averages about 445 mm, and a downpour can empty the street within minutes, so keep an eye on the forecast and pick a restaurant with a roof.

Fisherman's Village with children

The village is easy with children on a quiet evening: the beach road is flat, there are plenty of places for ice cream and pancakes, and children can play on the sand while you eat. Fridays are harder: the street is shoulder to shoulder from 19:00, pushchairs struggle and it is easy to lose sight of a small child. Come at 17:00 and leave by 19:00, or choose the smaller Monday or Wednesday market. Keep children well back from the fire shows.

Staying in Bophut

Bophut is the area we suggest to couples and food lovers: you can walk to dinner every night, the atmosphere is relaxed rather than rowdy, and the airport is close. The trade-off is the beach.

  • Boutique hotels in and around the shophouses: mid-range about 2,500–5,000 THB a night in the shoulder season.
  • Resorts on the bay: Anantara Bophut and NH Collection Samui Peace Resort (rebranded in January 2025) are the best-known names.
  • Villas in the Bophut hills behind the village, with sea views; see Koh Samui villas.
  • Peak season (mid-December to mid-January, July–August) adds 30–80 %; Friday nights are the first to sell out.

Events in Fisherman's Village

  • New Year's Eve, Thursday 31 December 2026: the second-biggest fireworks on Samui after Chaweng, with the beach and street packed; resort galas (Anantara Bophut's 2026 gala is priced from 14,500++ THB). Arrive early or come by Grab.
  • Loy Krathong, Tuesday 24 November 2026: krathong floated from the beach; sky lanterns are banned on Samui.
  • Christmas: resort brunches and dinners on 24–25 December.

Things to do near Fisherman's Village

  • Big Buddha and Wat Plai Laem: 15 minutes east, both free.
  • Choeng Mon: the nearest calm swimming beach.
  • Maenam: 10–15 minutes west for a long, quiet beach and the Pralarn ferry pier.
  • Sunset: the north coast faces Koh Phangan rather than the sunset itself; for the sun dropping into the sea, go west to Bang Por or Lipa Noi. See Koh Samui sunset spots.
