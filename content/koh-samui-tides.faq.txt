What time is high tide on Koh Samui today? | The live panel at the top of this page shows the tide now and the next high and low tide for Chaweng, Nathon and Thong Krut. Samui usually has one high tide a day, arriving about 50 minutes later than the day before.
How big is the tide on Koh Samui? | Usually between about 0.4 m on small-tide days and 1.5 m on big-tide days, reaching about 2 m at the extremes in December–January and June–August. The range grows and shrinks in a cycle of roughly two weeks.
When is low tide in the daytime on Koh Samui? | From March to September, and most noticeably from May to August, when low tide falls between about 07:00 and midday. From October to February low tides mostly fall in the evening or at night, so the beaches are full by day.
Which Koh Samui beaches are good for swimming at low tide? | Chaweng, the centre of Lamai, Silver Beach and the middle of Choeng Mon slope enough to swim at most tides. Bophut, Maenam, Bang Rak, Bang Por, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam and Bang Kao go shallow, so swim there near high tide.
Why does the sea go out so far at Bophut and Maenam? | Samui's north and west coasts are fringed by wide, almost flat sand and mud flats. A fall of a metre in the water level on such a gentle slope exposes tens of metres of seabed, while the steeper east-coast beaches barely change.
Is the biggest tide on the full moon on Koh Samui? | Not usually. Samui's tide is mainly once a day, and its biggest ranges come when the moon is furthest north or south of the equator, which often falls a few days away from the full or new moon.
Do tides affect boat trips to Pig Island? | Only a little. Trips run at any tide, but at very low tide the boat anchors further out at Koh Madsum, the wade ashore is longer, and the captain may swap the order of stops so the Koh Tan reef is snorkelled at a better depth.
Can I use these Koh Samui tide times for navigation? | No. They are predictions from a global tide model for beach planning, and wind, pressure and rain can shift the real water level by 10–30 cm. Skippers should use official nautical charts and tide tables.
