The panel uses the Open-Meteo marine forecast for the waters off Samui: total wave height, the wind-driven part of it (wind waves), the longer swell from distant weather, and the forecast wind. Wave height is the significant wave height, the average of the highest third of waves; individual waves can be up to about twice that.

How to read the verdict:

  • Calm: small waves and light wind. Every boat trip runs and speedboat crossings are smooth.
  • Choppy: trips usually run, but speedboats bounce and spray, and the longer crossings (Koh Tao, Ang Thong) are uncomfortable for anyone prone to seasickness.
  • Rough: expect cancellations on exposed routes, red flags on east-coast beaches and ferry delays.

As a working guide, the verdict reads Calm below about 0.5 m, Choppy from 0.5 to 1.25 m and Rough above 1.25 m. [check]

How we decide whether boats run

The captain and the operations team decide on the morning of each trip, not days ahead. They look at the Thai Meteorological Department's marine forecast and any warning in force, the wave and wind forecast for the route, what the sea is actually doing at the departure pier and on the crossing, and reports from other captains already out. A route can be safe while another is not: on a monsoon morning the Thong Krut crossing to Pig Island may be flat while the run to Koh Tao is not.

What happens next:

  • If we cancel because the sea is unsafe, you choose a full refund or a new date. We contact you by WhatsApp or email as early as possible, usually the evening before or at dawn.
  • If the park or the authorities close the sea, as Ang Thong did for one day on 8 January 2026 when 2–3 m waves were forecast, the same applies.
  • Rain alone doesn't cancel a trip. A grey, wet day with a flat sea still runs.
  • If you cancel yourself because you don't like the forecast, the normal policy applies: day tours are refunded in full 48 hours or more before departure, 50% at 24–48 hours and nothing under 24 hours; Ang Thong tours in full 48 hours or more before, nothing under 48 hours.

Overloading, poor maintenance and rough seas are behind most fatal boat accidents in Thai waters, so we would rather cancel and lose the day than take a risk.

The north-east monsoon explained

Samui's sea and weather run the opposite way to Phuket's. From May to September the south-west monsoon soaks the Andaman coast, but Samui sits in the lee of the Thai peninsula and stays mostly dry, with a westerly breeze. From about October the wind swings round to the north-east, blowing across hundreds of kilometres of open Gulf straight onto Samui's east coast. That brings:

  • Onshore surf on Chaweng, Chaweng Noi, Lamai and the east-facing coves, with rip currents and red flags. Swimming is often banned on the worst days.
  • Heavy rain, peaking in November, often as long grey spells rather than short tropical showers.
  • Choppier ferry crossings to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao, with delays and occasional cancellations.
  • The Ang Thong closure from 1 November to 15 December, for visitor safety and to rest the reefs.
  • Cold-air surges later in the season: on 19 February 2026, for example, 1–2 m waves brought red flags and swimming bans to Chaweng and Lamai.

Not every monsoon day is rough. Long calm, sunny spells happen in November and December too, and the west and south-west coasts stay sheltered. It is a season for flexible plans, not for staying away.

Reading a marine forecast yourself

If you check Windy or the TMD forecast before a boat day, three numbers matter:

  • Wave height (significant): the average of the highest third of waves. Below 0.5 m is calm for any boat; 0.5–1 m is fine for big boats and bumpy in a speedboat; above 1.5 m most tour routes are cancelled.
  • Wave period: the seconds between waves. Short-period wind chop (3–5 seconds) feels rougher in a small boat than a longer swell of the same height.
  • Wind speed: 10 knots is a pleasant breeze; 15–20 knots raises whitecaps and chop; above about 25 knots, small boats stay in. In the monsoon, check the gusts as well as the average.

Forecast models smooth out squalls. Thunderstorms in October and May can bring 30-knot gusts for half an hour on an otherwise calm day, which is one reason captains make the final call on the morning, and turn back if a squall builds.

Sea temperature

The sea around Samui stays warm all year: about 28 °C from December to February and 29–30 °C from March to November. Most snorkellers need no wetsuit; divers doing several dives a day may want a thin one in December and January, when run-off and wind can cool the surface. Warm, calm water after rain is also when jellyfish are most likely near the shore.

Seasickness: boats, routes and tips

Seasickness depends on the boat, the route and you. Speedboats are fast, so you spend less time at sea, but in a swell they slam and bounce, which also matters if you are pregnant or have back problems. Big boats are slower but roll more gently. On the Ang Thong run our big boat takes about 1.5 hours against the speedboat's hour, and is the better choice for anyone who knows they get seasick. See Ang Thong: speedboat or big boat.

  • Take a tablet (dimenhydrinate or similar, from any pharmacy) 30–60 minutes before departure. It doesn't work once you feel sick.
  • Sit at the back of a speedboat or low in the middle of a big boat, where the motion is smallest.
  • Look at the horizon, keep fresh air on your face and avoid reading your phone.
  • Eat a light breakfast; skip alcohol the night before.
  • Choose short crossings on choppy days: Thong Krut to Koh Tan is 10–30 minutes.

Ferries and weather cancellations

Ferries between Samui, Koh Phangan, Koh Tao and the mainland run in most weather; they are bigger than tour boats and the routes are short. Storms do cause delays and cancellations, most often from November to January. In storms the longer Koh Tao services are often the first to stop, while the short Samui–Phangan crossings (20–50 minutes) usually keep going. Tropical Storm Pabuk in January 2019 closed Ang Thong and cancelled ferries for days.

Two rules for monsoon travel: leave a spare day between an island hop and an international flight from Samui or Surat Thani, and buy flexible ferry tickets where you can. Our Full Moon Party speedboat runs only when the sea is safe; if we cancel, you choose a full refund or a new date. Ferry details are in Koh Samui to Koh Phangan and Koh Samui to Koh Tao.

Storms and official warnings

Tropical storms are rare in the lower Gulf but not unknown. Tropical Storm Pabuk (January 2019) is the benchmark: it closed marine parks, stopped ferries and caused flooding across the south. In December 2025 the Thai Meteorological Department warned of 2–3 m waves in the lower Gulf, and in January 2026 a strong wind warning forecast 2–3 m waves and closed Ang Thong for a day.

When the TMD issues a marine warning for the Gulf, small boats are told to stay ashore, and all tours on the affected routes are cancelled. Official sources:

Monsoon travel checklist

  • Book boat trips for early in your stay, so a cancelled day can move to a later one.
  • Leave a spare day between any island hop and an international flight.
  • Choose south-west departures (Pig Island from Thong Krut) on windy days.
  • Check this page and the TMD forecast the evening before, and watch for our message.
  • Pack a dry bag, a light rain jacket and seasickness tablets.
  • On the east coast, swim only where the flag is green or yellow, or use the hotel pool.

Swimming and surf safety

Several drownings on Samui have followed swimmers ignoring red flags on the east-coast beaches in rough sea. Rules that save lives:

  • Never swim when a red flag is flying, and don't climb on rocks in surf: a tourist was swept off the rocks at the Lad Koh viewpoint in November 2024.
  • If a rip current pulls you out, don't fight it: float, then swim parallel to the beach and come in where the waves break.
  • Swim where you can stand, and never after drinking.
  • Leave the water at the first thunder.
  • In calm water after rain, watch for jellyfish; vinegar for at least 30 seconds on a sting, and call 1669.

Low tide matters too: the north and west coasts turn to shallow flats, which is safe but frustrating. See Koh Samui tides and our full beach and swimming safety guide.
