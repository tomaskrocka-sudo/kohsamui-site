Around 40 boats a day cross from Koh Samui to Koh Phangan, taking 20–50 minutes and mostly costing 200–500 THB. The main route is Lomprayah from Bangrak, Maenam (Pralarn pier) or Nathon to Thong Sala, for 350 THB. For Haad Rin, take the Haad Rin Queen from Big Buddha pier (200 THB, 10:30 and 17:00); groups and Full Moon nights are easier by shared or private speedboat with hotel pickup.

Good to know: the timetable below was last checked on 1 November 2026 against operator boards from late September 2026. Times change with the season and extra boats run on Full Moon dates. Re-check at the pier or operator office the day before you travel.

Coming back: Phangan to Samui

Return boats from Thong Sala run from about 07:00–08:00 until late afternoon. Typical departures are around 08:00, 10:30, 11:00, 12:00, 12:30, 15:30, 16:00 and 16:30, across Lomprayah, Seatran, Boonsiri and Songserm. Echo speedboats return from Baan Tai, and the Haad Rin Queen from Haad Rin. Lomprayah sells a Thong Sala to Samui airport ticket for about 500 THB, roughly hourly 07:30–18:00. If you have a flight, take a boat that lands at least 3 hours before departure.

Which Samui pier?

This is where most people go wrong. "Bangrak pier" on a ticket can mean any of three different piers a few hundred metres to a couple of kilometres apart, each used by different companies. A taxi driver told "Bangrak pier" may take you to the wrong one. Name the operator and the pier.

Thong Sala to Haad Rin is about 10 km and roughly 20 minutes by songthaew or taxi on a paved road. Thong Nai Pan in the north-east is 40–50 minutes from Thong Sala over a hilly road. If your hotel is in Haad Rin, the Haad Rin Queen saves you this taxi; if it is anywhere else on the island, Thong Sala is almost always the better choice because it has far more boats and onward transport. A Koh Phangan day trip from Samui

Phangan is close enough for a day out, but plan around the ferry times.

Plan 1: Haad Rin and Thong Sala by public ferry

  1. 10:30 Haad Rin Queen from Big Buddha pier (200 THB).
  2. 11:20 Arrive Haad Rin. Walk across to Haad Rin Nok, swim and have lunch.
  3. 14:30 Songthaew or taxi to Thong Sala (about 20 minutes).
  4. 15:00 Walk around Thong Sala and the market.
  5. 16:00–16:30 Last good return boats from Thong Sala to Bangrak or Maenam (350 THB).

Plan 2: Than Sadet, Thong Nai Pan and Bottle Beach by private boat

The east and north coasts of Phangan are slow to reach by road. A private speedboat from Samui can visit Than Sadet's waterfall and bay, the twin Thong Nai Pan beaches and Bottle Beach (Haad Khuad) in one day and bring you back to Samui. This only works in calm seas, which on the east coast usually means February to September.

Good to know: tour boats follow a simple rule in high season: a boat returns to the island it left from. We do not run one-way island-hopping on tour boats, so if you are moving hotels to Phangan, use the ferry or a private transfer.

Safety: the UK Foreign Office warns about drink spiking, assault and theft at Full Moon Parties. Stay with your group, watch your drink, carry only what you need and agree a meeting point. Never get on an overloaded boat.

Weather and safety

The Samui–Phangan strait is short but exposed to north-east swell from October to December, when crossings can be rough and some speedboats are cancelled. February to April is the calmest period. The UK Foreign Office notes that boats in Thailand have sunk or collided, linked to overloading, poor maintenance and rough seas, especially in the monsoon.

  • Choose a large catamaran or the Seatran Discovery over a small speedboat on choppy days.
  • Count the life jackets and do not board a boat that is clearly overloaded.
  • Keep passports and phones in a dry bag; spray reaches the open decks.
  • On our own boats, we cancel when it isn't safe, and you choose a full refund or a new date.
  • Check live conditions: [kp_sea_now] More on our Koh Samui sea conditions page.

Practical tips for the crossing

  • Arrive 30 minutes early. Catamarans board in order and luggage goes on first. At the Lomprayah pier, you swap your voucher for a boarding sticker at the counter.
  • Sit at the back and low down if you get seasick; the bow moves most. Take seasickness tablets 30–60 minutes before departure, following the pharmacist's directions.
  • Cash helps. Most pier counters take cash only, and ATMs at the piers can run out on busy days. Thong Sala has plenty of ATMs; Haad Rin has fewer.
  • Taxis on Phangan wait at Thong Sala and Haad Rin piers. Shared pickup trucks charge per person by area; agree the price before you load bags.
  • Morning boats are smoother. Wind tends to build in the afternoon, especially from October to December.
  • After Full Moon nights, the first morning boats from Thong Sala and Haad Rin are packed. If you have a flight from Samui that day, take a private boat or leave Phangan the afternoon before.
  • Do not buy Full Moon "packages" from street touts. Some sell boat tickets on boats that are overloaded or do not exist. Buy from the operator's counter or a company with a licence.
  • Keep your return ticket safe. Open-dated returns must be confirmed at the operator's office a day ahead on some lines.

Do I need to book ahead?

Usually not. Outside peak dates you can buy tickets at the pier or the operator's office 20–30 minutes before departure. Book ahead in these cases:

  • Full Moon Party nights and the days either side (24 November, 24 December and 31 December 2026).
  • The Christmas and New Year peak, roughly 20 December–5 January.
  • Half Moon Festival nights, if you want a package with transport.
  • Any private speedboat, so the boat and crew are reserved.

Online booking for our tours and transfers closes at midnight Thai time the day before departure; same-day requests go to WhatsApp.

Vehicles, luggage and children

  • Rental scooters and cars: Samui rental contracts almost never allow taking a vehicle to Phangan, and insurance is void on the ferry. Rent again on Phangan instead; scooters there cost about the same.
  • Your own car: car ferries to Thong Sala run from Donsak on the mainland (Raja and Seatran, about 2.5 hours); a direct Samui–Phangan car ferry schedule was not confirmed at the time of writing. [check]
  • Luggage: on catamarans, large bags go in a pile at the stern or in the hold and are unloaded onto the pier, so keep valuables with you. On speedboats, bags sit in the open and can get wet: use a rain cover.
  • Children: children's life jackets are provided on our boats and must be worn. On public ferries, ask for a child's jacket when boarding; large catamarans have indoor seating, which suits small children better than an open speedboat.
  • Accessibility: pontoons and gangways move with the tide and swell. Crew will help, but wheelchair users should choose a large catamaran from the Lomprayah or Seatran pier and call ahead.

Island-hopping routes: Samui, Phangan and Tao

The three islands sit in a line, so the classic route runs Samui → Phangan → Tao or the reverse, and onward to Chumphon for the mainland.

  • Samui → Phangan: 20–50 minutes, about 40 boats a day.
  • Phangan → Koh Tao: about 1–2 hours by catamaran or speedboat from Thong Sala, around 600–700 THB.
  • Koh Tao → Chumphon: catamarans to the mainland with bus connections to Bangkok.
  • Through tickets: Lomprayah and Boonsiri sell Samui → Koh Tao tickets that stop at Thong Sala, so you can break the journey on Phangan with two separate tickets.

A balanced 10-day plan: 4 nights on Samui (flights in and out, boat trips such as Pig Island or, from 16 December, Ang Thong), 3 nights on Phangan, and 3 nights on Koh Tao, returning to Samui for the flight home with a buffer day. Samui or Phangan?

Choosing where to base yourself? Samui has the airport, international hospitals and a wider range of hotels; Phangan is quieter outside party nights, with a more backpacker and wellness feel and cheaper rooms.
