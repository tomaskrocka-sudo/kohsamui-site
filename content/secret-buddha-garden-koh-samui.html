The Secret Buddha Garden (Tarnim Magic Garden) is a hillside sculpture garden in Koh Samui's central highlands, begun in 1976 by a local farmer and opened to visitors by his family after he died in 1990. Entry is 100 THB per adult in recent reports (some sources still say 80 THB) and it is open 09:00–18:00. The access road is very steep and narrow with a rough final stretch: go by 4x4 or on a tour, not on a rental scooter. Allow 45–60 minutes inside.

What you'll see

The garden is a steep hillside of forest, bamboo and fruit trees with a stream running through it, scattered with concrete and stone sculptures, many now softened by moss.

  • The sculptures: dozens of figures, often said to number over 100: Buddhas in different postures, Hindu deities, Thai mythological figures, animals, musicians and scenes from village life. Many are grouped as small tableaux along the paths.
  • The stream: a cool stream with little cascades and pools runs down through the garden; after the rains it is one of the prettiest spots on the island.
  • The views: from the upper paths you look out over the forested hills to the sea.
  • The creator's memorial: the founder's memorial is in the garden.
  • Shade and cool air: the altitude and forest make it noticeably cooler than the coast, a welcome break on a hot day.

Paths are rocky, steep in places and slippery when wet. Most people walk a loop of 30–60 minutes and then rest at the café.

A short history

The garden was started in 1976 by a local fruit farmer who began placing sculptures on his land in the hills, and worked on it until he died in 1990. His family then opened it to visitors and still run it. Different sources give different names for the creator and different accounts of his inspiration, so we only report what is consistent: the dates, the fact that he was a local farmer, and the mix of Buddhist, Hindu and Thai folk imagery.

The road: why not to ride a scooter

The garden's biggest problem is getting there. The access road climbs very steeply from the Na Mueang area into the hills; it is narrow, winding and, in its final stretch, rough and broken. Reviewers who rode scooters up describe some sections as very steep and advise checking your transport can handle it.

  • Most rental scooters are 125 cc automatics with weak brakes. On the way down they overheat, and that is when riders crash.
  • In the rain (especially October–December) the road is slippery and 4x4 is strongly recommended.
  • Rental insurance usually won't pay if you don't have an International Driving Permit with the motorcycle category.
  • Passengers on the back make the steep sections much harder.

Our advice: unless you are an experienced rider on a dry day, take a 4x4 taxi or a tour. If you do ride, use low gear, stop on the way down to let the brakes cool, and never descend with a passenger on the steepest sections. Read Koh Samui roads and motorbike rental first.

By road the garden is roughly 30 minutes from Lamai, 45 minutes from Chaweng and 35–40 minutes from Nathon, with the last section slow going. Grab and regular taxis rarely go up the final road. Ask us about a jungle safari or a private 4x4: [kp_wa]

Best time to visit

January to April is best: the road is dry and the stream still has some water in January. Go early, 09:00–10:00, before the jungle safaris arrive late morning. In October–December the stream and greenery are at their best, but the road and paths are slippery and fog or rain can close in on the hills. Check the weather: [kp_weather_now]

Elephants on the way

Several reviewers of the garden mention elephants tied up nearby and swaying, a sign of stress. We don't recommend stopping to feed or ride elephants anywhere in the hills. If you want to see elephants, read our elephant sanctuary guide and choose a no-riding, no-bathing venue.

Safety inside the garden

  • Paths are steep and rocky with no railings in places; watch children near the stream and drops.
  • Wet rock by the stream is slippery; don't climb on the sculptures.
  • Mosquitoes are common in the forest; use repellent.
  • Emergency: ambulance 1669, Tourist Police 1155. Mobile signal can be weak in the hills.

What to wear and bring

  • Shoes with grip for rocky, wet paths
  • Cash for the entry fee and the café
  • Water, insect repellent and sunscreen
  • A light layer: the hills are cooler than the coast
  • A sarong or shirt if you are combining it with Wat Khunaram

After the hills, the sea

The garden's views reach the sea; the next day, get out on it. The Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB adult, 1,250 THB child) runs every day from Thong Krut, and the Pig Island longtail tour (1,750 THB adult) is the slower, more traditional way to go. Hotel transfers are included from the main areas; some remote areas pay a surcharge. Guided ATV rides are another way to explore the hill tracks with a guide.
