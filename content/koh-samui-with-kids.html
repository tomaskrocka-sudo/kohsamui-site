Koh Samui is the easiest Gulf of Thailand island for families: direct flights from Bangkok, Singapore and Hong Kong, private hospitals with paediatric departments, and calm seas from January to September (best February to April). On our boat trips children aged 3–11 pay a child rate, under-3s travel free and children's life jackets are always provided. The best first boat day is the Pig Island speedboat tour: 4–5 hours door to door, 1,450 THB per adult and 1,250 THB per child, and it runs on any date of the year, weather permitting.

When to go to Koh Samui with kids

Samui's weather runs opposite to Phuket's. The north-east monsoon brings most of the year's rain between October and December, while the rest of the year is mostly dry with short showers. For families, the sea matters more than the sky: calm water means easy swimming, comfortable boat crossings and no red flags on the east-coast beaches.

Where to stay with kids

Choose the beach first, because Samui's beaches differ more than its hotels. Choeng Mon on the north-east tip is the calmest bay on the island, with soft sand, a gentle slope and a sandbar to the islet of Koh Fan Noi at low tide. North Chaweng has the same easy water when the monsoon is not blowing, with restaurants you can walk to. Lipa Noi on the west coast is very shallow and calm, which toddlers love, but it is 40–50 minutes from the airport and our east-side boat trips add a transfer surcharge there. Avoid Bophut and Bang Rak for swimming: the water is shallow and often cloudy. Our full comparison, with hospital distances and tour pickup zones by area, is in where to stay on Koh Samui with kids.

For larger families, a pool villa with a fenced pool can work out cheaper per person than two hotel rooms; low-season rates for a 2–3-bedroom villa start at about 8,000 THB a night for the whole house. Browse Koh Samui villas and check the road: many hillside villas sit on steep concrete lanes that are hard work with a pushchair.

Pig Island: the best first boat day

Boats leave from Thong Krut in the south-west, so the crossing to Koh Madsum (Pig Island) takes only 15–30 minutes. Children meet the pigs on the beach, then snorkel or float in a life jacket over the shallow reef at Koh Tan, the better of the two snorkelling stops. A Thai meal on the beach is included. Bring about 50 THB per person in cash for the Koh Madsum island fee. Because neither island is in a national park, the trip runs every day of the year, including the Ang Thong closure. Let children watch the pigs and let the pigs come to them: no chasing, lifting or feeding crisps. More background on the islands is on our sister site pigislandkohsamui.com.

Ang Thong Marine Park by big boat

For children of about 6 and up, the big boat is the more comfortable way to see Ang Thong: a smoother ride than a speedboat, shade, toilets and room to move. The day includes the Emerald Lake on Koh Mae Ko, kayaking, snorkelling and the viewpoint on Koh Wua Talab. Be honest with yourself about that viewpoint: it is a steep 25–30 minute climb on stairs and rocks, and many families let one parent go up while the other stays on the beach with younger children. The national park fee is paid in cash at the park: 300 THB per foreign adult and 150 THB per child. The park is closed 1 November–15 December 2026 and reopens on 16 December. Read the Ang Thong guide before you book.

Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan for older children

This is a long day on open water, so it suits confident children of about 8 and older who don't get seasick. The reward is clear water at Koh Tao's snorkel sites and the sandbar viewpoint at Koh Nang Yuan (landing fee about 250 THB, paid on the island). It runs all year when the sea allows.

Private boats for families

With toddlers, a private boat removes the stress of fitting other guests' timetables: you leave when the children are ready, stay longer at the beach and turn back when they are tired. A private longtail costs 5,950 THB per boat for up to 8, and a private speedboat 11,950 THB for up to 10. Larger groups can ask us about private catamarans. Speedboats bounce in a chop, so with babies or pregnant mothers choose a longtail or a catamaran.

Elephants: no riding, no bathing

Children love elephants, and Samui has several sanctuaries where visitors prepare food, feed the animals and watch them forage, wallow and dust-bathe. Choose a place with no riding, no bathing with tourists and no shows: those activities need trained, controlled animals. We only sell visits that meet that standard; our elephant sanctuary guide explains how to check a venue. The skywalk visit is the most hands-off option, with a raised walkway above the elephants; the Bophut hills visit includes feeding and walking with the herd. Explain to children before you go that these are large wild animals: stay behind the guide, never stand behind or between elephants, and keep food in the basket until you're told. Skip the chained-elephant feeding stands you may see at roadside viewpoints.

Temples, the Big Buddha and fish feeding

The Big Buddha on the Koh Fan islet in Bang Rak is free, open about 07:00–18:30, and the climb up the dragon staircase is short enough for small legs. One kilometre east, Wat Plai Laem has a lake full of catfish and carp that crowd the edges when children feed them (fish food costs a few baht from machines). Both temples expect shoulders and knees covered, including for older children, and shoes off on the upper platforms and in halls. Go before about 09:30 to avoid tour vans and the midday heat.

Na Muang 1 waterfall

Na Muang 1 is an 18 m waterfall with a pool you can swim in, 5–10 minutes' walk from the car park. It is free, open about 09:00–17:00 and fullest between October and January; from February to April it can thin to a trickle. The rocks are slippery, so wear sandals with grip and keep children out of the pool after heavy rain, when the current gets strong. Skip Na Muang 2 with small children: it is a steep 20–40 minute climb on fixed ropes.

Capybaras, ziplines, go-karts and horses

The capybara café in Lamai is one of the most requested family stops: a garden where the calm, oversized rodents wander among visitors, with a café for parents. Entry is about 400 THB for adults and 300 THB for children aged 3–12. Our private van trip waits while you visit. For older children, the treetop zipline near Tan Rua waterfall takes ages 6–70 at the same price as adults, and the go-kart track lets children drive from 9 years and 1.3 m, with a two-seater for younger ones. Guided horse rides on the south and west coasts take riders from 5.

Cooking class and markets

A small garden cooking class (2,000 THB, up to 4 guests; see our cooking class guide) works well for children who like to help in the kitchen; tell the teacher in advance so they can tone down the chilli. On Friday evenings the Fisherman's Village Walking Street in Bophut (about 17:00–23:00) has food stalls, toys and buskers. It gets crowded after 19:00, so arrive early with a pushchair, or use a carrier.

Rainy-day ideas

Showers on Samui usually pass in an hour or two outside October–December. For a properly wet day:

  • Central Samui in Chaweng: an air-conditioned shopping mall with a food court, shops and supermarkets.
  • A cooking class under cover, or a morning at the capybara café, which has shaded areas.
  • The elephant sanctuaries run in light rain; heavy storms may move the session time.
  • Waterfalls are at their best after rain, but don't swim when the water is brown and fast.
  • Pig Island trips run in most weather; the captain decides on the morning, and if we cancel for sea safety you choose a full refund or a new date.

Attractions to skip or check first

Older guidebooks and map pins list family attractions that we have not been able to confirm as open in 2026, or that we don't recommend on welfare grounds. Phone before you go, and never pay a driver in advance for them.

  • Samui Aquarium and Tiger Zoo: no confirmed opening in 2026, and welfare concerns about captive tigers.
  • Samui Butterfly Garden, Coco Splash waterpark and Samui Snake Farm: reported closed or unconfirmed in 2026.
  • Namuang Safari Park: old listings advertise elephant rides; the site now trades under another name and we have not confirmed that riding has stopped.
  • Elephant riding or bathing anywhere, and chained elephants at roadside stops.
  • Monkey shows and photo props with macaques or other wild animals.
  • Overlap Stone 2: reported closed since a forestry raid in July 2026.
  • Jet ski rental from the beach without photos and a written price: a common damage-claim scam, and children should not ride them.

Health and safety with children

Heat and sun

Daytime highs are 29–33 °C all year and the UV index is very high. Keep the hours between about 11:00 and 15:00 for the pool, shade or a nap. Use rash vests and hats rather than relying on sunscreen alone, and choose reef-safe mineral sunscreen: oxybenzone and similar filters are banned in national parks including Ang Thong. Children dehydrate quickly; oral rehydration salts are sold in every pharmacy and 7-Eleven.

The sea: flags, tides and jellyfish

From October to December the east-coast beaches (Chaweng and Lamai) get big waves and rip currents, and red flags mean no swimming. Few beaches have lifeguards. On the north and west coasts the problem is the opposite: at low tide the water can retreat a long way over flats, so swim at mid to high tide. Box jellyfish are rare but dangerous in the Gulf of Thailand; of the first 10 deaths recorded in Thailand since 2002, 9 were off Samui and Koh Phangan, and a 13-year-old boy died after a sting on the mainland coast in August 2026. Swim in netted areas where they exist, avoid the water at dusk and after storms, and know the first aid: pour household vinegar over the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub and don't rinse with fresh water, then call 1669. Many hotels keep vinegar stations on the beach. Full advice is in beach safety on Koh Samui.

Mosquitoes and dengue

Dengue fever is present on Samui all year, with peaks in the rainy months. The mosquitoes that carry it bite in the daytime. Use a repellent suitable for your child's age, light long clothes at dawn and dusk, and plug-in repellents in rooms. See a doctor promptly for a high fever, especially with a rash, vomiting or bleeding gums. Ask your doctor or travel clinic at home about vaccinations before you travel.

Dogs, monkeys and other animals

Rabies is present in Thailand. Teach children not to touch stray dogs, cats or monkeys, and never to feed them. Most beach dogs are calm, but packs can be territorial at night. If anyone is bitten or scratched, wash the wound with soap and running water for 15 minutes and go to a hospital the same day for rabies treatment.

Hospitals and pharmacies

Bangkok Hospital Samui on the ring road near Chaweng Noi has a paediatric department and a 24-hour emergency room (+66 77 429 500; 1719 from a Thai phone). The other private hospitals (Samui International, Thai International, Bandon International and Wattanapat) and the government Koh Samui Hospital near Nathon run children's clinics. Private hospitals ask for a deposit or insurance guarantee, so carry your policy details. Pharmacies are everywhere in the tourist areas and sell children's paracetamol, rehydration salts and insect repellent. Details, phone numbers and costs are on our hospitals page, and pharmacies in every tourist area stock children's basics.

Good to know: save 1669 (ambulance) and 1155 (Tourist Police, English spoken) in your phone before you land, and write your villa or hotel address in Thai for taxi drivers.

Getting around with kids

  • No scooters with children. Samui's roads are steep in places and accidents are common. If you do ride as an adult, a helmet is the law, with fines of up to 2,000 THB per person since June 2025.
  • Taxis and Grab: child car seats are rarely available. Bring a lightweight travel seat or booster, or ask for one in advance.
  • Private van with driver: the easiest way to do a day of sights with small children, from 2,500 THB per van. Book our private van hire and ask about a child seat when you book.
  • Hire car: compact cars cost about 800–1,200 THB a day; you need your licence plus an International Driving Permit.
  • Pushchairs: pavements are patchy and beaches are soft sand, so a carrier is more useful than a big buggy. A light umbrella stroller works in malls and Fisherman's Village.

More on taxis, Grab and fares is in getting around Koh Samui.

Food for children

Thai food is easy for children once you know what to order. Ask for dishes "mai phet" (not spicy). Reliable choices are khao pad (fried rice), kai jeow (Thai omelette over rice), gai yang (grilled chicken) with sticky rice, pad see ew (stir-fried wide noodles), chicken satay and fresh fruit such as mango, pineapple and watermelon. Every resort and most beach restaurants also serve pasta, pizza and chips. Drink bottled or filtered water; ice in restaurants is made from purified water and is generally safe. 7-Eleven and the supermarkets at Central Samui, Big C and Lotus's sell nappies, formula and baby food, though brands may differ from home.

A five-day family plan

  1. Day 1 Arrive, swim in the hotel pool, early dinner on the beach at Choeng Mon or North Chaweng.
  2. Day 2 Pig Island speedboat in the morning (pickup about 08:30, back about 14:15), nap, pool.
  3. Day 3 Big Buddha and Wat Plai Laem before 09:30, beach in the afternoon, Fisherman's Village if it's Friday.
  4. Day 4 Elephant sanctuary morning session, lunch, Na Muang 1 waterfall swim if the water is clear.
  5. Day 5 Ang Thong by big boat (from 16 December) for children 6+, or a cooking class and the capybara café for younger ones.
