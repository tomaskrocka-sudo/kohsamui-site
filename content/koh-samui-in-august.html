August is one of the most reliable months for weather on Koh Samui: about 102 mm of rain over 10 rain days, highs of 32 °C, lows of 25 °C and a 29 °C sea. The sea is calm on most days, so Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao all run well. The catch is the crowd. European school holidays make it peak season, and room rates run 30–80% above the shoulder months. The Full Moon Party falls on Tuesday 17 August 2027.

Good to know: August is a busy month for boat trips, so book rooms and boats early. Put your main boat day early in your stay, so there is time to move it if the weather turns. The Ang Thong speedboat tour costs 1,800 THB plus the 300 THB park fee in cash, and the Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip 2,200 THB.

The verdict

August suits families, first-timers and anyone who wants reliable boat days and a lively island. It does not suit travellers looking for low prices or empty beaches.

Good for:

  • Calm sea on most days for Ang Thong, Pig Island and Koh Tao
  • Swimming on Chaweng and Lamai, which are calm from January to September
  • Short showers rather than long rainy spells
  • Combining Samui with Koh Phangan and Koh Tao
  • Diving, with the Gulf in season while the Andaman coast has its monsoon
  • The Full Moon Party on Tuesday 17 August 2027

Not for:

  • Low hotel rates or last-minute villa bookings
  • Quiet beaches and empty restaurants
  • Midday swims on the north and west coasts, which are shallow at low tide
  • Waterfalls at full flow (they run fullest from October to January)

For a summer trip, Samui usually beats Phuket, which has its monsoon from about May to October. If you can move your dates, September brings much the same weather with fewer people and lower prices; see Koh Samui in September and Koh Samui weather by month.

What August weather looks like

From May to September the wind blows from the south-west, and the Thai peninsula shelters Samui from the monsoon that soaks Phuket and Krabi. Expect around 10 rain days in August, most of them a short afternoon shower followed by sunshine.

The heat matters more than the rain. Highs of 32 °C and high humidity make midday heavy going, and nights stay warm at about 25 °C. Plan temples and viewpoints for the morning, spend 12:00–15:00 in the shade or the sea, and drink more water than you think you need.

Tides: on the north and west coasts (Maenam, Bang Por, Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam), low tides fall in daylight from March to September, and the lowest water of the year comes from June to August, in the morning. Plan swims there for the late afternoon or evening, around high tide. Tide times are on Koh Samui tides.

Box jellyfish: the risk is higher in the rainy months (the UK government says May to October across Thailand), after heavy rain, in calm, warm water and in the evening. On 1 August 2026 a 13-year-old boy died after a sting at Sichon, on the mainland coast south of Samui. Swim inside netted areas where they exist and wear a full-length rash vest. For a sting, pour vinegar over it for at least 30 seconds and call 1669. See beach safety.

Where to stay in August

  • Chaweng and Lamai are at their best. Both are among the best swimming beaches on Samui from January to September. Chaweng suits first-timers and nightlife; Lamai is the best value on the east coast and quieter at night.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay 5–10 minutes from the airport and our first choice for families with small children. Expect it to be busy with families in the school holidays.
  • The north and west coasts are shallow at low tide, and August has the lowest daytime water of the year. Lipa Noi is the safest beach for toddlers at high tide. From May to August the sun sets further north, which favours Bang Por and Laem Yai for sunsets.
  • Bophut and Fisherman's Village are good for dinners and the Friday market, but the beach is not for swimming.
  • Villas: July and August book out months ahead. Ask for a photo of the access road if the villa is on a hillside.

Prices in August

August is peak season. From mid-December to February and in July and August, room rates run 30–80% above shoulder-season prices. In the shoulder months a mid-range double costs about 2,000–4,500 THB in Chaweng and 1,500–3,500 THB in Lamai, so budget well above that now. Book flights 6–10 weeks ahead for July and August; fares rarely drop at the last minute.

Daily costs do not change with the season: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB (400–600 THB in Chaweng), a scooter 150–350 THB a day. For similar weather at lower rates, come in May, June or September. The full price list is in is Koh Samui expensive?

Getting here and around in August

Direct flights from Bangkok Suvarnabhumi take about 1h05–1h15, with roughly 20–25 a day. The cheapest seats go first in July and August. The budget route is a flight to Surat Thani and a ferry; see how to get to Koh Samui. Ferries to Koh Phangan and Koh Tao run normal timetables, and May to September is usually good for the Koh Tao crossing.

On the island, evening traffic on Chaweng Beach Road is the main delay. Grab costs about 80–120 THB for a 5 km hop. If you hire a scooter, carry an International Driving Permit that covers motorcycles and wear a helmet (fines up to 2,000 THB). See getting around Koh Samui.

Diving and snorkelling in August

August is in the Gulf diving season, which runs from February to October. Whale sharks visit Sail Rock and Chumphon Pinnacle. In July 2026 the Koh Tao Tourism Association named June to September as the peak for sightings, but treat one as a bonus. The best snorkelling is offshore: Koh Tan on the Pig Island tour, Ang Thong, and Koh Nang Yuan by Koh Tao (250 THB landing fee). Visibility is clearest from February to April, so expect good rather than perfect water. See snorkelling and diving.

What to pack

  • Reef-safe sunscreen (SPF 50), a hat and sunglasses.
  • Light cotton or linen clothes and a cover-up for temples (shoulders and knees covered).
  • A compact umbrella for afternoon showers.
  • Insect repellent with DEET or picaridin: dengue is present all year and peaks in rainy periods.
  • A full-length rash vest for snorkelling, against sunburn and jellyfish.
  • Water shoes for stony beaches and low-tide flats.
  • Seasickness tablets if you are prone to it.
  • A light layer for air-conditioned boats and buses.
