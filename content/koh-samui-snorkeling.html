Snorkelling straight off Koh Samui's beaches is modest: Coral Cove, Silver Beach, the Chaweng Noi rocks and Laem Yai are the best of it. The good reefs are offshore: Koh Tan on the Pig Island trip (all year, from 1,450 THB), Ang Thong Marine Park (16 December–31 October, park fee 300 THB) and Koh Nang Yuan by Koh Tao (250 THB landing fee). Visibility is best from February to April, Ang Thong is closed from 1 November to 15 December, and the east-coast beaches are rough from October to December.

Snorkelling from the beach

None of Samui's beaches has a reef to match the islands offshore, and several of the best-known beaches (Bophut, Bang Rak, Maenam, Lipa Noi) have no reef at all, just sand or mud flats. The spots below have rocks and some coral within swimming distance. All of them are best on calm mornings from February to September; from October to December the east-coast spots are often rough, cloudy and flagged red.

Coral Cove

A small rocky cove on the ring road between Chaweng Noi and Lamai, and the island's best-known shore snorkel. The sand is coarse and crystalline, so wear water shoes. Kayaks rent for about 300 THB an hour, and there are facilities above the beach. Be honest with yourself about expectations: a January 2026 visitor review reported dead coral and litter, and the fish life is modest. It is still a pleasant hour on a calm day.

Silver Beach (Crystal Bay)

A small cove framed by granite boulders, a little south of Coral Cove. The best snorkelling is around the boulders at either end. It fills up fast, so arrive before 10:00 for space on the sand and calmer water. More in our Silver Beach guide.

Chaweng Noi rocks

The quieter cove south of Chaweng has rocks at its north end where you can snorkel straight off the sand. Occasional sea lice (tiny jellyfish larvae that cause an itchy rash) are reported here. Rinse off after swimming.

Laem Yai

On the north-west tip, near the luxury resorts, the water is often clearer than on the east coast, with rocks and pebbles rather than sand. It is poor at low tide, when the rocks are close to the surface, so time it around high tide.

Choeng Mon and Koh Fan Noi

Choeng Mon is the calmest bay on the north-east coast. Snorkel around the rocks at the ends of the beach and around Koh Fan Noi, the islet at the north end. At low tide a sandbar runs out to Koh Fan Noi; watch the tide so you don't get cut off.

Tongson Bay

A small, sheltered bay between Choeng Mon and the Bang Rak headland, reached by a sand road. It is clear and shallow from December to April. Bring everything you need, as facilities are minimal.

Snorkelling by boat

This is where Samui's snorkelling gets good. All our boat trips include masks, snorkels and life jackets, and a guide who chooses the spot on the day for the calmest, clearest water.

Koh Tan and Koh Madsum

From Thong Krut on the south-west coast, the boat reaches Koh Tan in 10–15 minutes by speedboat or 15–30 minutes by longtail. Koh Tan is the better reef of the two: a shallow reef along Ao Ok beach with patchy coral and reef fish, ideal for beginners and children. Koh Madsum next door is about the pigs and the beach, not the snorkelling. Because Thong Krut is sheltered from the north-east monsoon, these trips run all year, weather permitting. Our Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) snorkels Koh Tan for about an hour; the small-group longtail (1,750 THB) adds lunch on Koh Tan. See the Pig Island guide.

Ang Thong

Ang Thong's reefs fringe the sheltered sides of the islands; the guide usually picks Koh Sam Sao, Koh Wao or Koh Tai Plao. The snorkelling stop lasts 45 minutes to an hour, after the Emerald Lake, the viewpoint and kayaking. Water clarity is best in March and April and worst after heavy rain, when run-off clouds the water. The park fee (300 THB adult, 150 THB child) is paid in cash, and the park is closed from 1 November to 15 December. Our speedboat tour costs 1,800 THB.

Koh Nang Yuan and Koh Tao

The clearest water within a day trip of Samui. At Koh Nang Yuan the reef known as the Japanese Gardens lies off the sandbar. The island is privately run: day visitors pay 250 THB per adult (120 THB per child) in cash, may visit only between 10:00 and 15:00, and may not bring plastic bottles or cans or use fins. Our Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB) snorkels Nang Yuan and then two Koh Tao bays, chosen on the day from places such as Mango Bay, Hin Wong and Aow Leuk. The crossing is 1.5–2 hours each way and can be bumpy in a north-east wind.

Koh Rap

Koh Rap is the quietest of the southern islands, with a long white beach and calm water. Its draw is the empty beach and the kayaks rather than a big reef. Visit on the three-island private longtail (8,450 THB per boat, up to 8 guests), which also snorkels Koh Tan, or the Koh Rap full-day private boat (9,950 THB per boat).

Private boat snorkel routes

A private boat lets you snorkel longer, start earlier and skip the parts you don't care about.

  • Southern islands, half day: private longtail to Koh Tan and Koh Madsum, 5,950 THB per boat for up to 8, 5–6 hours, private transfer included. Stay on the reef as long as you like.
  • Southern islands, faster: private speedboat, 11,950 THB per boat for up to 10, with Koh Rap or another bay if time and sea allow.
  • Ang Thong your way: private speedboat, 33,000 THB per boat for up to 10. Ask the captain for two snorkelling stops and skip the viewpoint if you prefer.
  • North-east coast: a private speedboat charter from 19,000 THB per boat can combine Koh Som and Tongson Bay in a few hours.

When visibility is best

Visibility follows the monsoon. The calm months between the monsoons give the clearest water; heavy rain from October to December washes run-off into the sea and stirs up the shallows. What you might see

We would rather under-promise. On the shallow reefs around Samui, Koh Tan and Ang Thong, expect hard corals in patches, sea urchins and common reef fish. Coral cover varies a lot from bay to bay, and some has been damaged by past dynamite fishing, storms and warm-water bleaching. Turtles and reef sharks are rare at snorkelling depth.

The bigger marine life of the Gulf, the schools of barracuda, batfish, groupers and trevally that Koh Tao dive centres describe, lives on deeper pinnacles such as Sail Rock and Chumphon Pinnacle. To see those you need to dive; see our Koh Samui diving guide.

Gear: rent, bring or use ours

Every one of our boat trips includes a mask and snorkel for each guest and life jackets in adult and child sizes. Children's life jackets are always provided and must be worn on the boat. Fins are not included: bring your own if you like them, but leave them behind for Koh Nang Yuan, where they are banned.

  • Mask fit: press the mask to your face without the strap and breathe in gently through your nose. If it stays put, it fits. A moustache breaks the seal; a little petroleum jelly helps.
  • Fogging: rub a drop of baby shampoo or anti-fog inside the lens and rinse, before every snorkel.
  • Full-face masks: we don't recommend them. They can build up carbon dioxide and are hard to clear if water gets in.
  • Water shoes: essential at Coral Cove and anywhere with rocks or urchins.
  • Rash vest: better than sunscreen for a long snorkel, for your back and for the reef.

For snorkelling from the beach, dive shops and beach stalls in Chaweng and Lamai sell basic sets; buy your own mask if you plan more than one trip.

Reef rules

  • Don't stand on coral. Rest on sand or float with a life jacket. Coral cuts infect easily.
  • Don't touch or collect anything: coral, shells, sea urchins and starfish all stay where they are.
  • No fish feeding. Bread and biscuits make fish aggressive and change the reef.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen: since 2021 Thailand has banned sunscreens containing oxybenzone, octinoxate, 4-methylbenzylidene camphor (4-MBC) or butylparaben in its marine national parks, including Ang Thong, with fines of up to 100,000 THB. Check the label, apply it 20 minutes before you swim, or wear a rash vest.
  • Cash for fees: Ang Thong 300 THB (child 150), Koh Nang Yuan 250 THB (child 120), Koh Madsum and Koh Rap about 50 THB each.
  • Take your litter home. Koh Nang Yuan bans plastic bottles and cans entirely.

Safety

Box jellyfish. Rare but serious: of Thailand's 10 recorded box jellyfish deaths since 2002, 9 happened in the waters off Samui and Koh Phangan. Risk is higher in calm water and after rain. Many hotels have vinegar stations. If someone is stung, get them out of the water, pour vinegar on the sting for at least 30 seconds, don't rub it or rinse with fresh water, and call 1669 for an ambulance.

Currents and red flags. From October to December the north-east monsoon brings surf and rip currents to the east coast, and red flags go up on Chaweng and Lamai beaches. Don't snorkel when the flag is red; a tourist was swept off the rocks at the Lad Koh viewpoint in November 2024. If a current pulls you out, swim parallel to the beach.

Boat traffic. Longtails and speedboats use the same bays as snorkellers. Stay inside the area the guide shows you, near the boat, and use a brightly coloured snorkel or float off the beach.

Sun. Your back burns fast while you float face down. Wear a rash vest or T-shirt and snorkel before 11:00 or after 15:00 from the shore.

Seasickness. Snorkelling straight after a bumpy crossing makes it worse. Take a tablet before you board, and float on your back between looks if you feel queasy. More in beach and swimming safety and sea conditions.

Snorkelling or Discover Scuba

If you enjoy snorkelling and want to go deeper, a Discover Scuba Diving session lets non-divers try scuba with an instructor, without a full course. On Koh Tao it costs about 2,500–3,500 THB in 2026. You need to be reasonably comfortable in the water and to fill in a medical questionnaire. It is the only way to see the pinnacles where the bigger fish gather. Compare options, prices and seasons in our diving guide, and see freediving on Koh Samui if you would rather hold your breath than carry a tank.
