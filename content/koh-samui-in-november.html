November is Koh Samui's wettest month, with about 445 mm of rain over 16 rain days, highs of 30 °C, lows of 24 °C and a 29 °C sea, and Ang Thong Marine Park is closed from 1 November to 15 December, so it is a poor choice for a boat-first holiday. It is green, quiet and good value, and Pig Island tours run on any date, weather permitting. On Tuesday 24 November 2026 the Full Moon Party and Loy Krathong fall on the same night.

Good to know: Ang Thong Marine Park is closed every year from 1 November to 15 December. Season reopens 16 December. Book now for later dates. In November, book the Pig Island speedboat tour (1,450 THB) or the Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan day trip (2,200 THB), sea permitting.

The verdict

November suits flexible travellers who want low prices and don't mind rain. It does not suit a short trip built around beach days and Ang Thong.

Good for:

  • Low hotel rates before the Christmas peak
  • Quiet beaches, restaurants and roads
  • Green hills and full waterfalls
  • Pig Island and Koh Tan, bookable on any date
  • Loy Krathong and the Full Moon Party on 24 November 2026
  • Spa, cooking classes, yoga and wellness stays

Not for:

  • Ang Thong Marine Park (closed 1 November–15 December)
  • Guaranteed sunshine or long swims on Chaweng and Lamai
  • A trip with only one or two possible boat days
  • Calm crossings to Koh Tao on every day

If your dates are fixed in November, come, but plan two or three flexible days and choose your area carefully. If you can move your trip, February to April has the calmest sea and least rain; see Koh Samui weather by month.

What November rain looks like

November rain comes with the north-east monsoon. With 16 rain days on average, roughly half the month stays dry, but when it rains it often comes in long, heavy bursts rather than a short tropical shower, and some days are wet from morning to evening. Years vary a lot: some Novembers are mostly sunny, others bring a week of grey skies. Low-lying roads can flood after the heaviest downpours, and hill roads become slippery.

The bigger issue for most visitors is the sea. The north-east wind blows onshore on the east coast, so Chaweng and Lamai get surf, rip currents and frequent red flags. When the red flag is up, stay out of the water; swimmers have drowned after ignoring them. Waves also break over the rocks at the Lad Koh viewpoint, so keep back from the edge.

Check the hourly forecast on Windy or Open-Meteo rather than the daily icon, and watch for warnings from the Thai Meteorological Department. Our sea conditions page shows live wave height.

Loy Krathong falls on the full moon of the 12th lunar month, Tuesday 24 November 2026 (some English-language sites wrongly give 25 November). People float krathongs, small decorated rafts with a candle and incense, to thank the water spirits. On Samui the classic spots are Chaweng Lake and Big Buddha, plus beaches and hotel ponds. Sky lanterns are banned on Samui because of the fire risk. Choose a krathong made of bread or banana leaf, not foam. It is not a public holiday and there is no alcohol ban. Where to stay in November

  • Avoid choosing a hotel only for its east-coast beach. Chaweng and Lamai are at their roughest, with frequent red flags. If you stay there, pick a hotel with a good pool.
  • Choeng Mon is a sheltered bay in the north-east, 5–10 minutes from the airport, and a good all-round November base.
  • The west coast (Lipa Noi, Taling Ngam) lies on the far side of the island from the north-east wind, with quiet sunsets, but it is 40–50 minutes from the airport and very shallow at low tide.
  • Bophut and Fisherman's Village are good for rainy evenings, with covered restaurants and bars close together.
  • Villas: look for a covered terrace or sala, so a wet afternoon is still pleasant, and check that the access road is paved, as steep concrete drives are slippery in the rain.

Things to do on a wet November day

  • A Thai cooking class or a long spa session; a one-hour Thai massage costs about 300–500 THB.
  • Central Samui mall in Chaweng for shops and food.
  • Muay Thai fights every night except Thursday (tickets 1,500–2,500 THB).
  • Temples between showers: Big Buddha, Wat Plai Laem and Wat Khunaram.
  • Na Muang Waterfall 1 at full flow, from the viewing area; stay out when the water is brown and fast.
  • A yoga or wellness day; see retreats.

Prices in November

November is the cheapest month of the year for rooms, along with October. Hotels that charge 30–80% more from mid-December to February often run their lowest rates now, and villas are easier to book at short notice. Restaurants, massages, scooters and taxis cost the same as the rest of the year: street dishes 50–120 THB, a one-hour Thai massage 300–500 THB, a scooter 150–350 THB a day. Getting here and around in November

Flights to Samui run as normal; Bangkok Airways flies from Bangkok about 20–25 times a day. Car ferries from Donsak to Lipa Noi and Nathon keep running, though the crossing can be rough. Ferries between Samui, Koh Phangan and Koh Tao are the services most likely to be delayed or cancelled when strong north-east winds blow, so don't plan a tight connection to a flight on the same day. Tropical storms are uncommon but possible from late October to January; buy travel insurance that covers weather disruption.

On the island, take extra care on scooters: wet roads, flooded dips and sand on bends cause many accidents. Grab surges in heavy rain, so allow time. See getting around Koh Samui.

Diving and snorkelling in November

November is outside the main Gulf diving season, which runs from February to October with the clearest water in March and April. With Ang Thong closed, snorkelling trips in November mean Koh Tan on the Pig Island tour, or Koh Tao and Koh Nang Yuan when the sea allows. Expect more stirred-up water than in the dry season, and check the forecast the day before. See snorkelling and diving.

If you can choose, late December or January gives much better odds of sunshine and calm water, at higher prices. The first half of December is a compromise: less rain than November, low prices, but Ang Thong stays closed until the 16th.

What to pack

  • A light rain jacket or poncho and an umbrella.
  • Quick-dry clothes and sandals that grip on wet tiles.
  • A dry bag for your phone and passport on boats and scooters.
  • Insect repellent: mosquitoes are worst after rain, and dengue peaks in rainy periods.
  • Seasickness tablets for boat days.
  • Reef-safe sunscreen: the sun is strong between showers.
  • A light layer for air-conditioned malls and buses.
